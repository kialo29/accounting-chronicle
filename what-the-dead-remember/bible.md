# WHAT THE DEAD REMEMBER — Story Bible

Star Wars fan fiction. Adult. Literary, slow burn, dark where it must be. Read `notes/canon.md` alongside this; it holds the dates, places and what each character knows.

## Premise
Three dead people wake in their own younger bodies, years apart, none knowing the others are there.
- **Obi-Wan Kenobi** died on the Death Star (0 BBY) and woke four years ago (30 BBY) in a 27-year-old body, two years into training an 11-year-old Anakin. He has spent four years quietly raising the boy who will become the thing that killed him, lying to the Council, and trying to change small things.
- **Ahsoka Tano** left the collapsing Sith temple on Malachor (3 BBY) and woke seven years ago (33 BBY) in a 3-year-old body on Shili, days before Plo Koon found her. She has spent seven years as a youngling with an adult mind, watched from the back of every room, and waited.
- **Anakin Skywalker** died at Endor (4 ABY), redeemed, and wakes tonight (early 26 BBY) in his 15-year-old Padawan body in the Jedi Temple. The book begins here.

Obi-Wan and Ahsoka both believe Anakin fell for good; neither knows of his redemption, Luke's hand on the mask, or the Emperor's death. Anakin knows everything, and knows that the two people he loved most saw him only as the monster. Palpatine is six years into his Chancellorship. The clones are growing on Kamino. Nobody has told anyone anything.

## The central mystery (what the reader keeps reading for)
Two questions braid through the whole book and must be alive in every chapter:
1. **Who else is here?** Anakin discovers that things are already wrong: this Obi-Wan is warmer than the one he remembers and does things that Obi-Wan never did; a ten-year-old Togruta youngling holds her training saber backwards and looks at him as if she is checking something; an access log shows Obi-Wan searched the Archives for a planet that does not exist; a green convor sits on the Temple roof. Anakin cannot tell whether his memory is rotten after twenty-three years in the suit, whether this timeline was never his, or whether someone else came back. He prods. The people he prods prod back. Everyone lies.
2. **What has the Chancellor noticed?** Sidious has walked in the boy's mind for six years. Overnight it closes behind a wall that is old, scarred, and built by someone who knows Sith discipline. Sidious concludes someone else has had the boy, suspects Dooku, and sets the Sith machinery moving: Dooku is sent to look; a trap is laid for the master to isolate the apprentice; a Mandalorian hunter brings back a recording that shows a fifteen-year-old fighting like a Sith Lord. The Jedi Council, meanwhile, senses a new shadow in the Temple and hunts it, not knowing it is a boy.

The two hunts close on the same child from opposite sides. The discoveries between the trio are the heart; the Sith and the Council are the clock.

## Structure and reveal rules (hard)
34 chapters in four parts. Reveal one (Anakin and Ahsoka) lands at the end of chapter 13 and is lived through in 14. Reveal two (Obi-Wan) lands at the end of chapter 23 and is lived through in 24.

**Before a character's reveal, the reader must never be told explicitly that they came back.** Obi-Wan POV chapters (5, 11, 16, 22) and Ahsoka POV chapters (3, 8, 20) before their reveals follow these rules:
- Never use a noun or name from after the POV character's present: no clone, chip, Vader, Empire, Order 66, Mustafar, Rex, Luke, Leia, Kanan, Ezra, Malachor, Fulcrum, Death Star, Tatooine-as-exile, Ben, Inquisitor. (Ahsoka can know Barriss and Caleb as children who exist now; she must not think about what they do later in those words.)
- Their feelings may run far hotter than the situation explains. Dread, grief, recognition, "not again", "of all the children in the Temple, that one", a hand that shakes, a name almost said. Memories can be referred to only as unnamed places or unnamed people ("a place she would not name", "a man he had buried", "a dream of a desert and a farm").
- Behavioural tells are allowed and encouraged: Obi-Wan's letters policy, his refusal to let Anakin be alone with the Chancellor, his lists, his habit of saying "my friend", his looking too long at the Tano child; Ahsoka's reverse grip, her silence when other children cry, the way she flinches from a Pau'an Temple Guard, her questions to Master Plo about dreams that are true.
- A reader should finish each of these chapters more suspicious than they started and still unable to prove it. Late ones (16, 22) may be heavy; the last line before each reveal is allowed to all but say it.

After a character's reveal, their POV is fully open and their future knowledge is on the page.

Anakin's POV is open from page one. He is the reader's anchor and the reader knows everything he knows.

## Voice and style (hard)
- **British spelling** throughout (colour, realise, grey, centre, travelling, storey, kerb, armour).
- Close third person, past tense. One POV per chapter unless the outline says otherwise. Name the POV character in the first paragraph.
- Literary prose with a spine. Concrete sensory detail; short sentences under pressure; longer ones in reflection. No purple. No melodrama in the narration; let the events be the melodrama.
- **Grounding rule:** in Temple scenes use the words *children*, *child*, *youngling*, *initiate*, *boy*, *girl* often, and physical reminders of size (a training saber too long for a wrist; a tunic hem; a boy's hands; a girl who has to look up). Their minds are adult; their bodies are not. Anakin's body is fifteen: narrow, growing, clumsy at the edges, quick to tire, quick to flush. Ahsoka's is ten: small, strong for her age, short montrals and lekku barely past her shoulders. Never let the reader picture the Clone Wars versions.
- **Flashbacks** are set in *italics* as a block, or triggered mid-scene as a single italic line that cuts in. Keep them vivid, physical and unsoftened: the Temple on fire, the boy's face, Drallig's blade, the lava, the respirator's first breath, Luke's eyes. A flashback never resolves a present problem; it interrupts it.
- Scene breaks: a line containing only `* * *`.
- Dialogue is sharp and in character. Obi-Wan: dry, courteous, deflecting with wit; warmth under it; "my friend"; "Hello there" at most once in the book. Anakin: direct, impatient, affectionate when he forgets himself; in a boy's register when performing, in a man's register when alone. Ahsoka: quick, wry, blunt; adult when alone with Anakin, a careful child in public. Palpatine: avuncular, patient, every sentence a hand on the shoulder. Dooku: formal, precise, old-world courtesy with contempt beneath. Yoda: his syntax, sparingly. Windu: clipped, direct.
- Themes to keep surfacing: deceit (every protagonist is lying to everyone, including each other, and the lies are mostly loving); trust (who gets to be believed; a child is not; a Padawan is not; a Council that was wrong will be wrong again); memory (whether a thing remembered is true; what the dead remember); the body as a prison and a second chance; whether a person can be forgiven by people who saw only the worst of him.
- Do not explain the mechanism of the return. The convor is the only hint; Ahsoka connects it to the Daughter late in the book.
- No sex. No romance for Anakin with anyone. Padmé appears once, in chapters 17–18, and the scene must hurt.
- Violence is adult and specific but never gloating.
- No author's notes, no headings inside chapters other than the chapter title, no epigraphs.

## Characters

**Anakin Skywalker, 15 (mind: 45).** Padawan braid behind his right ear, short sandy hair, no scar yet, growing fast, two flesh hands. Memories of twenty-three years in the suit sit in his body: he reaches for a respirator control that isn't there; he is startled by breath, by cold, by the feeling of water. His Force presence is enormous and he must sandbag it constantly. He fights like Vader (Form V, heavy, one-handed overhand strokes, economical, terrifying) and must fight like a talented boy instead; instinct wins when provoked. He can shield his mind with Sith discipline, which a Jedi healer reads as "a wall, old and scarred, built over years". He has Vader's skills: slicing, piloting, command, and a precise knowledge of how Sidious's mind feels, because he lived inside it. He has grudges he knows are wrong: Windu (the distrust; the window), Obi-Wan (the bank of the lava river; the legs), Yoda, the Council, Dooku (the arm; the kneeling execution). He has guilt that would kill a normal man: the Temple, the younglings, Padmé, Ahsoka on Malachor, Fives, the Inquisitors, the galaxy. He loves Luke and Leia, whom nobody here knows. He keeps a coded list on a datapad in Vader's own cipher: *Things that are different.* He lies well. He wants two things: to keep anyone from ever becoming what he became, and to never again be looked at the way Obi-Wan looked at him on Mustafar. He does not believe he deserves to be here.

**Obi-Wan Kenobi, 31 (mind: 57).** Short beard, Soresu, tea. In four years he has: let Anakin exchange letters with Shmi (a quiet scandal the Council tolerates because he argued that a wound in the open heals); arranged for Anakin to assist in the initiates' saber classes "because it is good for him" (he knows what Anakin did to younglings); never once let the Chancellor have Anakin for a whole day; searched the Archives, uselessly, for Kamino (28 BBY), and asked Dex about Kaminoans; made a private list of every Jedi who dies in the purge and where; tried and failed to reach Qui-Gon; told Yoda nothing, because he watched the Council fail at every turn and because he believes the boy fell through isolation and fear, and a Council that knows will watch him like a bomb, and a watched boy becomes the bomb. His guilt is that he did everything right once and it was not enough, and that he left his brother to burn. He is warmer than canon Obi-Wan of this age, deliberately, and it costs him. He suspects, from the night the bond went opaque, that something has happened to Anakin; he does not let himself think the word. He fears the Tano child without knowing why she frightens him. He will, when the time comes, lie to the Council without hesitation.

**Ahsoka Tano, 10 (mind: 33).** Small, strong, blue-eyed, white facial markings, short montrals, lekku to her shoulders. Hawkbat Clan. Seven years of being a child with a woman's mind: she learned to let other children win, to cry on schedule, to be "intense" rather than strange. She has nearly broken cover twice: at five she asked a healer whether a dream can be a memory (it is in her file). She trusts the Council not at all and Master Plo completely, and tells him almost nothing. She fights Jar'Kai with a reverse grip when no one is watching and sometimes when they are. She has a list of her own in Togruti. She watches Caleb Dume, 7, as if he were already blind. She cannot look at Barriss Offee, 12. She felt Anakin arrive the night he came back, "like a struck bell", and since then has been trying to get close enough to the Padawan to be sure, terrified of what being sure would mean, because the last time she saw his face it was through a cracked black mask and he had told her she would die. She intends to become his Padawan again, four years early, and does not know how. The convor found her on Shili; she has never told anyone.

**Sheev Palpatine / Darth Sidious, 58.** Six years Chancellor. He has walked in Anakin's mind weekly since the boy was nine; it is his garden. When it closes he feels, with real pleasure, the first surprise in years. He does not consider time travel; he considers poaching, and Dooku, and a third possibility he does not say aloud. He never raises his voice. He wants the boy, and if he cannot have him, he wants the boy's master dead and the boy grieving in his office. He is also running the Republic: Outer Rim trade, the Senate's rising reformers (Amidala, Organa, Mothma), the groundwork of a crisis to extend his term.

**Count Dooku / Darth Tyranus, 76.** Publicly a Count and a philanthropist; privately Sidious's apprentice six years in. Proud, cultured, a believer in his own reasons. He has never met Qui-Gon's boy. When he touches the boy's mind at the reception he finds a hatred aimed at him personally, with a history it should not have, and it unsettles him more than anything has in years. He is obedient to Sidious and privately beginning to count the cost. He will arrange Oba Diah through the Pykes and send Jango to oversee it.

**Supporting (canon):** Yoda (senses the shadow; watches Anakin on Ilum; says little and the right thing); Mace Windu (distrusts Anakin, is right to and wrong about why); Plo Koon (loves "little 'Soka", suspects she is more than she shows, protects her); Cin Drallig (battlemaster; sees a boy use a stroke he has never taught); Jocasta Nu (keeps the logs); Vokara Che (chief healer; walks Anakin's fake dream); Padmé Amidala (20, Senator; appears once); Jango Fett (overseer at Oba Diah; his helmet records everything); Lom Pyke; Dex (Obi-Wan's friend); Caleb Dume, 7 (follows Ahsoka about); Barriss Offee, 12 (quiet, earnest, a clan above Ahsoka); Cliegg, Owen and Shmi (chapter 31). Huyang aboard the *Crucible*. Mas Amedda, Sly Moore.

- **Master Tahlin Vos-Kerrick**, a Chalactan Jedi who teaches the Padawans galactic politics. (No relation to Quinlan Vos.)

## World rules for this book (keep consistent)
- The **training bond** between Obi-Wan and Anakin is a felt presence and mood, not words. Anakin shields it the night he wakes; to Obi-Wan the bond goes opaque "like a door closing". Anakin keeps it thin, not closed, after the first week, because closed would be a confession. Obi-Wan's pain at Oba Diah blows through it.
- Anakin and Ahsoka had a bond once; it does not exist here until they deliberately make a thin one after Ilum, and they keep it faint because a Padawan bonded to a youngling would be noticed.
- Anakin's nightmares spill through the Force when he sleeps unshielded; younglings wake crying across the Temple; Yoda feels it. This is what brings the healers and the Council's "shadow in the Temple".
- Sidious can try the boy's mind only in person; he does so in every audience. Anakin lets him find a constructed surface: a fifteen-year-old's resentments, Shmi, speeders, a crush he invents. Beneath it, a wall.
- The Chancellor's standing audience with Anakin is every tenth day. Obi-Wan has never managed to end it.
- Anakin's coded list and Ahsoka's Togruti list and Obi-Wan's list of the dead are three physical objects. The book's last chapters bring the three lists together.
- The convor appears to Ahsoka (always), to Anakin (chapters 9, 13, 34), to Obi-Wan (chapter 5). It is never explained.
- Dates: the book runs about seven months of 26 BBY. Reveal one is roughly two months in; the reception at month four; Oba Diah at month six.

## Hard constraints
- Target 3,800–4,500 words per chapter; interludes (12, 19, 29) 2,800–3,200; big set pieces (13, 14, 18, 23, 24, 30) may run to 5,500.
- Chapter files begin with `## Chapter N: Title` and nothing else before the prose.
- Use canon lines from `notes/canon.md` at most once each across the whole book; the ledger records which have been spent.
- No events from the outline paragraph may be skipped; no major events may be added that the outline does not contain. Small scenes may be invented to land the beats.
- Never reveal a returned character explicitly before their reveal chapter. Never let Anakin or Ahsoka tell anyone else before chapter 25. Palpatine must not die. Padmé must not fall in love. Nothing may contradict `notes/canon.md` for events before the book opens.
