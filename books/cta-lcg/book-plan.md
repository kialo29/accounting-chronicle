## 1. Title and central questions

**Working title:** *The Living Law: Groups and Borders.* Print subtitle: *A story-led guide to the CTA Advanced Technical paper, Taxation of Larger Companies and Groups.*

Alternatives offered for Kian's choice: *Many Companies, One Purpose*; *The Group and the World*. The recommended title keeps the series family (*The Living Law*), and "Groups and Borders" names the paper's two great themes: the group as a set of separate taxpayers that behave as one business, and the border across which profits, people, money and companies move.

**Central questions**
1. **One taxpayer or many?** UK law taxes each company separately, yet a group plans, borrows, invests and reports as one. How does the law bridge that gap (group and consortium relief, no gain/no loss transfers, roll-over, the shareholding exemption, the corporate interest restriction), and where does it refuse to?
2. **Where are profits made, and what stops them drifting?** Residence, permanent establishments, double tax relief and the branch exemption decide where profits are taxed; transfer pricing, CFCs, hybrid rules, the interest restriction, the unassessed transfer pricing profits charge and the global minimum tax stop them moving to where they are not.
3. **What does the law follow: the accounts, the deal or its own purpose?** Tax starts from the accounts (loan relationships, intangibles, deferred tax), respects commercial transactions (reorganisations, demergers), but overrides both with purpose tests (unallowable purpose, the recast share exchange rules, value shifting, main purpose TAARs).
4. **How does a large group govern its tax, and what must an adviser spot?** The paper's own aim is that candidates "identify what the potential tax issues are" and know specialist areas well enough to coordinate specialists (exam-intel §5). The book asks, in every chapter, what the tax function must do, by when, and what a good adviser flags.

## 2. Scope

- **Syllabus:** the LCG Advanced Technical column of the CIOT **2026** syllabus grid (`research/lcg-grid-extract-v2.txt`): corporation tax including CT on chargeable gains; capital allowances; stamp duty, SDRT and SDLT (or LBTT) **at awareness level**; plus the "all taxes" rows (disclosure and discovery, DOTAS, APNs and follower notices, avoidance and evasion, GAAR, SAO, tax strategy, corporate criminal offence, penalties and interest, ADR, treaties, DTR). The LCG page's narrative syllabus adds accounting issues relevant to tax (deferred tax, profit recognition, share schemes), financing transactions, exchange gains and losses and the functional currency election, and UK reporting for transfer pricing (exam-intel §5). Note 2 of the grid expects knowledge of the Law, Professional Responsibilities and Ethics and Principles of Accounting manuals' terms (exam-intel §0).
- **Depth:** grade **1 (core)** items get full Advanced Technical depth, with worked computations; grade **2 (non-core)** items get working knowledge with the main computation (migration, Part 6 loan relationships, derivatives, wasting assets, indexation, s 48–52, cross-border transfers of business, international movements of capital, Part 14B, CA VAT adjustments); grade **3 (awareness)** items get principles and recognition (Patent Box, DPT and UTPP, multinational and domestic top-up tax, notification of uncertain tax treatment, ADR, sale and leaseback, stamp taxes, CA anti-avoidance, freeports). **Weighting** follows grade **and** exam frequency (exam-intel synthesis table): heavy chapters for the CT computation, capital allowances, property gains and leases, SSE and reorganisations, group and consortium relief, international (residence, PE, branch exemption, migration), CFCs, transfer pricing, CIR, compliance and governance, R&D, tax accounting and deferred tax, loan relationships, and investment companies.
- **Law:** FA 2026 (Royal Assent 18 March 2026), FY2026. Every story computation applies FY2026 law whatever year the story has reached. Enacted later changes (for example withholding on yearly interest at the **22% savings basic rate from 2027/28**, FA 2026 ss 5–6) and **not-yet-law** proposals (the draft Finance Bill 2026-27 published 13 July 2026, including a Pillar Two "side-by-side" package and reform of the foreign PE exemption; the Autumn Budget of 28 October 2026; the June 2026 distributions consultation; the 2026 UTT consultation) are flagged "coming next" or "proposed".
- **Angle:** why the rule exists, how it works, where it bites, what a tax function does about it, and how the examiner tests it.
- **Deliberately left out or kept brief:**
  - **Income tax and NIC** (except corporate deductions for employment costs, withholding by companies, and a line on individual sellers such as Jess Hartley); **VAT, customs and excise** (not in LCG; a one-line VAT signpost where a group transaction obviously raises it, for example VAT grouping on an acquisition); **IHT**.
  - **Owner-manager topics** (close company loans, extraction): LCG scenarios are listed groups "with no individual controlling shareholders" (exam-intel §1). Close companies appear only where the grid needs them (late interest under s 375 CTA 2009; the 2028 grid adds close companies, which is outside the 2027 sitting).
  - **Overseas tax law**: "Candidates will not be examined on the overseas tax aspects of cross-border mergers" (exam-intel §5). The book uses invented countries (Vallaria, Marrovia) with stated invented rates, and real Ireland only where TKS already used it (Irish trading rate 12.5%, verified in TKS; the UK–Ireland treaty as modified by the MLI).
  - Pre-FA 2002 intangibles (Part 8 Chapter 16 is excluded by the grid), Part 22 Chapters 3 and 8 and ss 990–995 CTA 2010, CAA Parts 3, 3A, 4, 4A, 5, 9, 10 (excluded rows), insurance and banking specialities, ring fence trades.
  - Question practice.
- **The exam technique layer:** chapter 32 teaches the AT craft (closed book with online legislation only, RM Assessment Master and its spreadsheet rule, timing, layouts, examiner messages). Every chapter's Exam lens box feeds it.

## 5. Structure: chapter briefs

Each brief gives: what the chapter **covers and owns**; what it **must not tell** (owned elsewhere: one-line signpost only); **syllabus anchors** with LCG grades from the v2 grid; **story leads** (real cases, history and people, to verify at writing time); **analogies**; the **misconception**; the **planning angle** (Going further); the **exam lens** notes (past papers and traps); the **running-case step**, with the numbers it must fix or use (all canonical in `calder-ledger.md`); **key sources** (statute, HMRC manuals, cases with citations as verified in the law sheets); the **glossary terms it owns**; and the **target word count**. **Global rules for every brief.**
- The running case is invented: say so on first appearance in each chapter ("our invented group, Tarnmoor"). Tarnmoor's story years are **Group Years** ("Group Year One", GY1 in the reading edition), not calendar years (section 6 explains why). Every computation uses FY2026 law.
- Where a teaching point turns on a real commencement date (for example FA 2026 rules for "accounting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026"), teach it with a **labelled real-calendar example**, never with a Tarnmoor story year.
- Recaps of TKS: one to three sentences, naming the TKS chapter.
- The story is the spine but not the cage: labelled hypotheticals ("suppose...") are encouraged where the exam tests a variant the story does not reach. They must never be reused as story facts.

### Prologue: One loan, two answers. — `00-prologue.txt` — about 2,200 words

**Recommended moment:** *BlackRock HoldCo 5, LLC v HMRC* [2024] EWCA Civ 330, decided **11 April 2024** by the Court of Appeal (Peter Jackson, Nugee and Falk LJJ) [V]. A US group's acquisition: LLC5, a Delaware LLC resident in the UK, borrowed about **$4bn** from the group to buy preference shares. The taxpayer **won on transfer pricing** (the FTT found an independent lender would have lent, with covenants that would have been forthcoming) but **HMRC won on unallowable purpose**: LLC5 had a tax-advantage main purpose, and on the "but for" approach all the debits were attributable to it (paras 97, 186–187, 193; Nugee LJ at [192] quotes Falk LJ's "sole raison d'être" phrase) [V]. The Supreme Court refused permission (listed with *Kwik-Fit* and *JTI*) [V, with a flag: the SC page prints the date 13 October 2024 and the citation [2024] EWCA Civ 419; Find Case Law has [2024] EWCA Civ 330: **use 330** and check the SC date].
**Why it opens the book:** one loan, two rules, opposite answers. Transfer pricing asks "what would independent parties have done?"; unallowable purpose asks "why did this company do it?" Between them sit the book's four questions: the group as one business or many companies, where profits are taxed, whether the law follows the deal or its own purpose, and what a tax function must spot. The prologue tells the case and stops, promising chapters 12 (unallowable purpose), 27 (transfer pricing) and 28 (the interest restriction).
**Alternative moments (if the writer prefers):** (a) *DSG Retail Ltd v HMRC* [2009] UKFTT 31 (TC), released 31 March 2009, which the tribunal called "the first transfer pricing case to have come to the Tribunal": Dixons' extended warranties insured or reinsured by its Isle of Man captive, the retailer's "point of sale advantage", and the move to a profit-split approach [V]; (b) the October 2021 agreement by "more than 135 countries" on a 15% global minimum tax (GOV.UK Pillar 2 collection) [V for those words only: any further detail needs sources]; (c) *Cadbury Schweppes plc v IRC* (Case C-196/04), judgment 12 September 2006, "wholly artificial arrangements" [V as quoted in *Vodafone 2*].
**Verify:** the judgment's facts and paragraph numbers; the SC permission list date; no invented scene detail (no courtroom description beyond the record).
**Owns:** the *BlackRock* story (chapters 12, 27, 28 analyse it and refer back).
**Key sources:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2024/330 ; https://supremecourt.uk/news/permission-to-appeal-aug-sep-2024 ; law sheet 1 §2 and §10; law sheet 5 teaching notes.

### 6.2 Story time: Group Years, not calendar years

The Tarnmoor story runs in **Group Years (GY1, GY2, ...)**, Tarnmoor's accounting years to 31 December, with **no calendar year named**.
