## 3. Research rules (lessons from TKS)

- **The law sheets are a head start, not a substitute.** Re-open the primary source for every rule you state, unless the law sheet marks it **V** and you are only restating it. Anything marked **S** or **U** (or "[verify]" in your brief) must be checked before you teach it, or labelled in the text ("HMRC's guidance says...", "the point is not settled") and listed in your flags.
- **Search budget:** assume **no WebSearch budget** (TKS exhausted it). Use **WebFetch on known URLs** only. If the session does have budget, use at most 5 WebSearch calls per chapter.
- **Sources that work:**
  - legislation.gov.uk: `https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/8/section/371BC` (TIOPA = 2010/8; CTA 2010 = 2010/4; CTA 2009 = 2009/4; TCGA = 1992/12; CAA = 2001/2; TMA = 1970/9; FA 1998 = 1998/36; ITA 2007 = 2007/3; FA 2026 = 2026/11, add `/enacted` for as-enacted text; F(No.2)A 2023 = 2023/30). Raw text: append `/data.xml`. **Check the banner** ("up to date with all changes known to be in force on or before..."; "changes not yet applied") and say when the text may lag. Some formulae are images (TIOPA s 58; CTA 2009 s 879O): use HMRC's manual text.
  - GOV.UK HMRC manuals: `https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/international-manual/intm414020`; content API: `https://www.gov.uk/api/content/hmrc-internal-manuals/international-manual/intm414020`. GOV.UK search: `https://www.gov.uk/search/all?keywords=...`.
  - Find Case Law: `https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2024/330` (pattern court/year/number).
  - tax.org.uk (CIOT), icaew.com, att.org.uk.
- **Blocked or useless:** BAILII (blocks bots: don't try); oecd.org (403: rely on INTM descriptions and the statute, and say so).
- **Commencement dates matter.** FA 2026's international rules mostly apply to "chargeable periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026" (TP, PE, UTPP), the WDA cut from 1 April 2026, CIR reporting companies for periods of account ending on or after 31 March 2026. Teach these with labelled real-calendar examples; the Tarnmoor story periods are simply under FA 2026 law.
- Never rely on memory for a figure, date, section number, case fact or quote. If you can't verify it, leave it out or label it, and flag it.

## 4. Accuracy rules

- **Status words** in the prose: "the law says" (statute), "the courts have held" (case law), "HMRC's view is" (guidance, not law), "this is disputed" or "being litigated", "announced", "proposed, not law" (the June 2026 distributions consultation; the 2026 UTT consultation; the draft Finance Bill 2026-27), and for the story "in our invented case".
- **Law year:** FY2026; say "for the financial year twenty twenty six" at least once per chapter. Flag later changes as "coming next" (for example withholding on yearly interest at 22% from 2027/28).
- **Quotes** from judgments, statute or official documents: short (under about 25 words), exact, attributed in words (who, which court, when). Never invent words for real judges, ministers or officials. No long quotes from textbooks or articles (copyright): paraphrase. Examiners' report remarks may be quoted briefly and attributed ("the examiners reported, after the May twenty twenty six sitting, that...").
- **No invented scene detail** about real cases (no weather, thoughts, feelings, courtroom colour) unless the source records it.
- **Worked examples** use the **invented Tarnmoor group** (and, in chapters 1, 10, 27 and 31, the TKS Hartleys). The script must say they are invented the first time they appear in your chapter ("our invented group, Tarnmoor"). **Run every computation in Python before writing it.** Numbers in the script must match the reading edition and the ledger. Re-run `python3 /home/claude/cta-lcg/ledger-check.py` if you touch canonical numbers (it must still report 0 failures; never edit it: report changes in your notes instead).
- **Story time:** Group Years only ("in Group Year Three"; GY3 in the reading edition). Never name a calendar year for a Tarnmoor event. The Ridgeway year is "some years later".
- **Exam scope:** state the grade of every syllabus item you teach (core, non-core, awareness) in the Exam lens. Don't teach overseas tax law beyond the invented country facts in the ledger; for Ireland use only the 12.5% trading rate and the UK–Ireland treaty with the MLI (both verified).

## 5. Voice

A gripping storyteller with a tax adviser's discipline, now speaking to a trainee who has passed TKS and wants Advanced Technical mastery. Warm, clear, witty where it fits, never flippant about the law. Open each chapter on a real documented moment (a case, a statute's arrival, an examiner's remark), a sharp question, or a labelled Tarnmoor moment. Each section opens with its point or its moment. **Why before how:** the problem the rule solves, then the mechanics, then the edges and the cases, then planning and the tax function's job. One or two strong analogies per section at most, always followed by the precise rule. Tackle at least one misconception per chapter. Name real cases with their stories. Vary pace: slow on hard mechanics (CFC gateways, CIR steps, DTR limits), brisk on connective tissue. Remember the listener hears it once: signpost, recap, repeat key numbers.

Cut filler: "it's worth noting", "delve", "little did they know", "the rest is history", "a pivotal moment", "changed the course of history forever", "it's important to remember", stacked rhetorical questions, repeated "not only... but also". Avoid "Let's" at the start of every section.

**Depth:** this is Advanced Technical. Where TKS stopped at the rule, go to the conditions, the exceptions, the anti-avoidance, the interaction with other regimes, the administration (who files what, by when, with what penalty), and the case law. Explain enough that Kian could "identify the issues and coordinate the specialists" (the CIOT's stated aim) and also compute the answer.

## 13. Lessons learnt from TKS (read these)

- **Canonical facts prevent rewrites.** TKS needed 13 continuity rulings (salaries, a client's identity, loss years, a house price) because chapters invented numbers in parallel. Here the ledger fixes the numbers up front: use them; propose changes in notes, never silently.
- **Scripts and reading editions drift.** Recompute after any late edit and re-check both editions.
- **Wording traps found in TKS reviews:** use "no gain, no loss" (statutory wording), not "nil gain nil loss"; don't call proposals law; don't say a rule "applies from" a date when the statute says "periods beginning on or after"; check penalty amounts against the statute, not GOV.UK summaries (TKS repeated GOV.UK's "£1,000 each" for persistent late filing: the statute says £1,000 / £2,000).
- **Exam facts change.** TKS planned on one syllabus and then had to switch grids. Here: the 2027 LCG grid and tax tables are not yet published; write Exam lens boxes on the 2026 grid and add "check the 2027 grid" to your flags only where a grade change would matter.
- **Independent reviews catch real errors** (TKS reviews A–F found statutory misreadings, wrong dates and arithmetic slips). Expect your chapter to be reviewed; make your notes' source list complete so the reviewer can follow it.
- **Don't edit shared helper scripts or other writers' files.** Report problems in your notes.
