## 13. Teaching notes (purpose, history, landmark cases — citations verified on the National Archives unless stated)

- **Why residence follows the boardroom.** *De Beers Consolidated Mines Ltd v Howe* [1906] AC 455 (HL): a South African-registered diamond company was UK resident because its "real business is carried on where the central management and control actually abides" (Lord Loreburn LC at 458) — the key decisions were taken by directors in London. Verified as quoted in *Wood v Holden*. Incorporation test added later by statute (now s 14 CTA 2009).
- **Influence is not control.** *Wood v Holden* [2006] EWCA Civ 26 (Chadwick LJ; appeal from Park J's order of 8 April 2005): a Dutch company (Eulalia Holding BV) used in a pre-planned share sale was Dutch resident. The court distinguished boards that are "usurped" (stand aside, as in *Unit Construction Co Ltd v Bullock* [1960] AC 351, where a parent's representative in East Africa effectively ran Kenyan subsidiaries) from boards that take decisions themselves under the "proposing, advising and influencing" of an outsider. Story value: the case concerned CGT assessments on Mr and Mrs Ron Wood following the 1996 sale of shares in Ron Wood Greetings Card Holdings Ltd for £23.7 million via a BVI company (CIL) to Eulalia (not the musician; do not conflate).
- **Or is it?** *HMRC v Development Securities plc* [2020] EWCA Civ 1705 (Newey LJ; appeal allowed, FTT decision restored): Jersey companies set up under a 2004 PwC scheme to access indexation relief were UK resident because the real decision to buy assets at an overvalue was made by the UK parent; the Jersey boards merely implemented it. One member of the court expressed "very considerable reservations" about the FTT's reasoning but agreed on the outcome. Useful contrast with *Wood v Holden* for a debate-style teaching passage.
- **Tie-breakers and timing.** *HMRC v Smallwood* [2010] EWCA Civ 778: a KPMG "Round the World" trust scheme via a Mauritius trustee (judgment's own phrase); the majority held the solution to dual residence lay in the treaty tie-breaker (place of effective management) rather than a "snapshot" at the moment of disposal, so the UK could tax. Shows why POEM tie-breakers mattered before the MLI replaced many of them with competent-authority agreement.
- **Treaties are read as treaties.** *Fowler v HMRC* [2020] UKSC 22 (Lord Briggs): a South African diver deemed by UK law to be trading; the treaty's employment article still applied, so the UK could tax. OECD Commentaries carry persuasive force "as the cogency of their reasoning deserves". Good for exam technique on supplied treaty extracts.
- **Beneficial ownership and treaty shopping.** *Indofood International Finance Ltd v JP Morgan Chase Bank NA* [2006] EWCA Civ 158: a Netherlands company ("Newco") interposed to secure a reduced Indonesian withholding rate on interest would, on the balance of probabilities, not be held to be the "beneficial owner" (appeal allowed). Forerunner of the MLI principal purpose test.
- **Treaty credit purpose.** *Bayfine UK v HMRC* [2011] EWCA Civ 304: UK–US convention Art 23 and unilateral relief (s 790 ICTA 1988): the Court of Appeal allowed HMRC's appeal, reading Art 23 purposively ("the primary purposes of the Treaty are ... to eliminate double taxation and ... to prevent the avoidance of taxation") so that the UK did not have to give the credit claimed. Ties to the TIOPA ss 81-95 anti-avoidance story. (Holding summarised from paras 83, 183, 225; read the judgment before teaching detail.)
- **Same profits for DTR.** *Anson v HMRC* [2015] UKSC 44: a UK resident member of a Delaware LLC was entitled to credit for US tax because the income taxed in both countries was the same income. Shows the "same income" requirement (INTM153250).
- **Non-discrimination bites.** *HMRC v FCE Bank plc* [2012] EWCA Civ 1290: UK–US treaty Art 24(5) allowed group relief between UK sister companies with a US parent.
- **DPT: "pay now, argue later".** *Glencore Energy UK Ltd v HMRC* [2017] EWHC 1476 (Admin): judicial review of a DPT charging notice (£21,129,349 plus interest) — the issue was whether alternative statutory remedies had to be used. UTPP keeps the pay-first design (s 217K) while moving the charge inside CT so treaties and MAP apply.
- **Policy arc for the textbook.** Treasury consent for capital movements (ICTA s 765) → reporting regime (FA 2009 Sch 17, from 1 July 2009). Credit for foreign dividends with mixer cap and onshore pooling (FA 2000/2001) → dividend exemption (Part 9A, 1 July 2009) → branch exemption (FA 2011) → CFC reform (FA 2012) → DPT (2015) and BEPS/MLI (UK in force 1 October 2018) → Pillar Two (2023-2025) → FA 2026 consolidation of TP/PE/DPT into OECD-aligned rules inside corporation tax. Theme: the UK moved from taxing foreign profits with credit to territorial exemption, while building anti-diversion tools.

## 14. Traps and examiner favourites

No CIOT LCG examiner reports were accessed (tax.org.uk paper page only: six questions of 10, 15 or 20 marks, 3 hours 30 minutes, pass mark 50%, scenarios built around multinational groups; https://tax.org.uk/taxation-of-larger-companies-groups). The traps below come from the statute and dates verified above, not from examiner commentary.

1. **Dates of the FA 2026 PE/TP/UTPP rules**: "chargeable periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026". A December year-end company's AP from 1 January 2026 is in; a 31 March 2026 year-end company is in only from its AP starting 1 April 2026. An AP starting 1 October 2025 is entirely under the **old** rules (old s 1141(1)(b), old ss 22-32, DPT).
2. **Calling it DPT in 2027 answers**: for APs beginning on or after 1 January 2026 the charge is UTPP, a **corporation tax** charge at CT rate + 6% (31% at main rate); DPT (31% since APs from 1 April 2023; 25% before) only for earlier periods.
3. **Old PE attribution sections**: s 29-32 CTA 2009 deduction rules no longer exist for current periods; candidates quoting "s 32 interest restriction for PEs" are using repealed law.
4. **Treaty v domestic PE**: the UK domestic dependent agent test (principal role) may be wider than the treaty test (UK–Ireland has no MLI Art 12). Where a treaty applies, check the treaty extract's Art 5 before concluding the UK can tax.
5. **Subsidiaries are not PEs** by control alone (INTM153060), but closely related agents acting exclusively for the group are no longer "independent" (s 1142(1A)).
6. **DTR "mixer" confusion**: s 42 is the R × IG limit; the mixer cap is s 58 Step 3, (D + PA) × M%. Onshore pooling and EUFT were repealed from 1 July 2009 — never pool foreign taxes across sources for current periods.
7. **Grossing up with capped underlying tax**: income is grossed up by the full underlying tax even where Step 3 caps the creditable amount (s 31(3)(b); INTM164220).
8. **Exempt dividends carry no credit**; withholding tax on an exempt dividend is a pure cost. A dividend deductible for the payer abroad is **not exempt** and gets **no underlying tax** (s 931D(c); s 57(3)).
9. **Loss/QCD allocation** (s 52) to protect credit: UK income first, then lowest-taxed foreign source.
10. **Withholding on interest**: "yearly" interest only; UK company recipient → excepted (s 933, reasonable belief s 930); non-resident lender → deduct unless HMRC direction (DT Company form) or Treaty Passport/DTTP2 direction in place; quoted Eurobond (s 882) and QPP (s 888A) exemptions. From 6 April 2027 the rate is **22%** (savings basic rate), not 20%. Royalties to non-residents: s 906, but the payer may apply the treaty rate on reasonable belief without clearance (s 911); no such self-help for interest.
11. **EU Interest and Royalties Directive** relief has gone (payments from 1 June 2021).
12. **CT61**: quarterly returns, due within 14 days of the quarter end.
13. **Migration**: s 185 exit charge on all assets; s 187 postponement no longer exists; TMA s 109B notice and approved arrangements are needed before migrating; payment plan = 6 equal annual instalments.
14. **Pillar Two**: register within 6 months of the end of the first qualifying AP even if no top-up tax; first GIR/UK return 18 months (floor 30 June 2026), then 15 months. Revenue threshold is tested on 2 of the previous 4 periods.
15. **Branch exemption**: irrevocable and affects all PEs; foreign tax on exempt profits is not creditable (s 18(3A)); UK-UK TP exemption disapplied where s 18A applies.
16. **Unremittable income** claim deadline: 2 years after end of the AP in which the income arises.
17. **International movement of capital reports**: within 6 months, only if value exceeds £100 million, only for UK corporate parents (reporting bodies).
