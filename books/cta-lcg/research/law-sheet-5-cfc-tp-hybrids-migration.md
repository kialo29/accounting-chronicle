## Teaching notes

### Why the rules exist (story material)

- **CFCs: from "wholly artificial" to "gateway".** The pre-2012 UK CFC regime taxed low-taxed subsidiaries broadly, subject to exemptions. In *Cadbury Schweppes plc v IRC* (Case C-196/04) [2006] ECR I-7995 (judgment **12 September 2006**), the ECJ held that CFC rules restricting freedom of establishment could be justified only if they applied to **"wholly artificial arrangements intended to circumvent national law"** and let the taxpayer show the subsidiary was "actually established" in the host state carrying on "genuine economic activities" (wording as quoted in *Vodafone 2 v HMRC* [2009] EWCA Civ 446). In *Vodafone 2* (Court of Appeal, **22 May 2009**; Chancellor, Longmore and Goldring LJJ) the court allowed HMRC's appeal: the old rules could be read **conformingly** by implying a genuine-establishment exception rather than being disapplied (High Court: [2008] EWHC 1569 (Ch), Evans-Lombe J, 4 July 2008). The 2012 regime (FA 2012 Sch 20, in force 17 July 2012) is the policy answer: tax only profits **artificially diverted** from the UK, defined through the "gateway", with the **SPF/AOA** test borrowed from PE attribution. Citations verified on caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk (Cadbury citation and case number as cited in [2008] EWHC 1569 (Ch) and [2011] EWCA Civ 127).
- **The state aid saga as a courtroom drama.** The finance company "75% exemption" was designed to make the UK attractive as a holding location. The Commission (2 April 2019) called part of it illegal State aid; the General Court agreed (8 June 2022); the Court of Justice (19 September 2024) annulled the decision because the reference framework should have been the **UK corporation tax system as a whole**, not the CFC rules in isolation. HMRC had already collected the money under Sch 7ZA; SI 2024/1307 ordered it repaid, and FA 2026 s 51 added interest on repaid interest. A neat lesson in how a tax rule's "logic" is defined (secondary sources for EU dates; UK statutory steps verified).
- **ATAD fingerprints.** FA 2019 s 20 (from 1 January 2019) added the >50% investment control test (s 371RG) and narrowed Ch 9 to non-UK-SPF profits. FA 2019 Sch 8 (from 1 January 2020) ended the s 187 postponement and turned exit-charge payment plans into the ATAD-style 5-year instalment model. Post-Brexit, these EU-shaped rules remain on the statute book.
- **Transfer pricing: from "business facilities" to OECD consistency.** *DSG Retail Ltd v HMRC* [2009] UKFTT 31 (TC) (Special Commissioners John Avery Jones and Charles Hellier, released **31 March 2009**) was described by the tribunal as "the first transfer pricing case to have come to the Tribunal". Dixons sold extended warranties insured or reinsured by its **Isle of Man captive, Dixons Insurance Services Ltd (DISL)** (with Cornhill Insurance as fronting insurer from May 1986 to April 1997). The tribunal rejected the proposed CUPs because of the retailer's **"point of sale advantage"**, found that DSG provided **business facilities** to DISL below an arm's length price (old ICTA s 770 and Sch 28AA), and pointed to a **profit split/return-on-capital** approach for DISL, adjourning quantum. Story value: functions (who sells), assets (the customer relationship) and risks (insurance capital) drive the answer, exactly what N23 Q6 candidates failed to analyse.
- **Thin cap and the EU.** *Test Claimants in the Thin Cap Group Litigation v HMRC* [2011] EWCA Civ 127 records the ECJ judgment of **13 March 2007** (C-524/04) that the UK thin cap rules restricted freedom of establishment where targeted at intra-group relations. The UK response in 2004 was to extend TP to **UK-to-UK** transactions so the rules were not discriminatory (history: **unverified** from a primary source in this session). FA 2026 s 164A reverses that for most domestic transactions from 1 January 2026, because "the potential removal of UK-to-UK transfer pricing ... would reduce the UK compliance burden" (Corporate Tax Roadmap 2024, para 2.48).
- **TP and unallowable purpose can diverge.** *BlackRock HoldCo 5 LLC v HMRC* [2024] EWCA Civ 330 (Falk, Nugee and Peter Jackson LJJ, **11 April 2024**): **$4bn** of intra-group loan notes; the FTT found an independent lender would have lent with additional **covenants** that would have been forthcoming, so the **TP** challenge failed (taxpayer's appeal on TP allowed); but **100% of the interest debits** were disallowed under the **unallowable purpose** rule (CTA 2009 s 441). A good illustration that TP asks "what would independents have done?", not "why did the group do it?".
- **Hybrids: arbitrage of characterisation.** BEPS Action 2 (5 October 2015) targeted outcomes, not motives: one payment deducted with no inclusion, or one expense deducted twice. The UK rules (1 January 2017) apply a **primary response** in the payer (or investor) jurisdiction and a **secondary response** elsewhere, so that whichever country acts first, the mismatch is neutralised once. FA 2021 softened over-reach found in practice (imported mismatches, dual inclusion income allocation).
- **Migration: the exit tax as a "final bill".** s 185 treats a departing company like a taxpayer leaving the room with its assets, crystallising gains accrued under UK jurisdiction. TMA ss 109B–109E are the "settle up before you go" rules, backed by personal penalties on directors and recovery from group companies.
- **DPT to UTPP.** DPT's "pay now, argue later" design (*Glencore Energy UK Ltd v HMRC* [2017] EWHC 1476 (Admin), law sheet 3) is preserved in UTPP but moved inside CT, so treaties and MAP apply.

### Worked examples (arithmetic checked in Python; FY2026 main rate 25%)

**Example 1: CFC finance company, Chapter 9 75% exemption, apportionment**
Facts: FinCo (resident in a territory with a 5% tax rate) lends £40m to a German group company controlled by the same UK parent; interest receivable **£2,000,000**; funded by a UK capital contribution; business premises condition met; no UK SPFs (s 371EB not engaged). Shareholders: UK plc 80%, an unconnected UK company 15%, a non-UK investor 5%. No entity exemption applies (tax exemption fails: 5% local tax on a 25% UK rate base is 20% of the UK tax, below 75%).
1. Gateway: Ch 5 applies (NTFP); profits fall within s 371EC (UK capital). Claim under Ch 9: s 371ID exempts **75%** = £1,500,000. Chargeable profits = **£500,000**.
2. Creditable tax: DTR attributable to foreign tax on income included in chargeable profits: 5% × £500,000 = **£25,000** (just and reasonable attribution assumed).
3. Apportionment by ordinary shares: 80% / 15% / 5%.
4. Chargeable companies: UK plc (80% ≥ 25%). The unconnected UK company (15%, no connected holders) is **not** chargeable. The non-UK investor is outside the charge.
5. UK plc charge = 25% × 80% × £500,000 − 80% × £25,000 = £100,000 − £20,000 = **£80,000**.
Without the Ch 9 claim: 25% × 80% × £2,000,000 − 80% × £100,000 = **£320,000**. Effective UK rate on the full interest with the claim: 25% × 25% = 6.25% gross, before credit.

**Example 2: Low profit margin exemption (and the M25 trap)**
Distributor CFC: sales £6,000,000; cost of goods £4,200,000 (of which **£1,500,000** shipped direct from the factory to customers outside the CFC's territory, never used there); staff £700,000; **management fee to UK parent £300,000**; rent £200,000; other £150,000; interest £90,000.
- Operating expenditure = £5,550,000; accounting profit after interest = £360,000; **before interest** = **£450,000** (s 371MB(2)).
- Relevant operating expenditure = £5,550,000 − £1,500,000 (goods not used in territory) − £300,000 (expenditure giving rise to income of a related person) = **£3,750,000**; 10% = **£375,000**.
- £450,000 > £375,000, so the **exemption fails**. Typical wrong answers: comparing £360,000 (after interest) with £555,000 (10% of total opex) gives a false "exempt". Next step: test the other exemptions, then the gateway.

**Example 3: Tax exemption with a s 371NC adjustment**
CFC assumed TTP £1,200,000; corresponding UK tax 25% = **£300,000**; 75% = **£225,000**. Local statutory rate 18% on local profits £1,300,000 = £234,000, but local profits include **£100,000** of income not within ATTP, so local tax is reduced by 18% × £100,000 = £18,000 to **£216,000** (72%). **Not exempt.** An unadjusted comparison (78%) would wrongly exempt. On an identical base, the break-even local rate is 75% × 25% = **18.75%**. A tax-holiday CFC has a local tax amount of nil: never exempt under Ch 14.

**Example 4: Short APs and thresholds**
Low profits exemption for a **9-month** CFC AP: £500,000 × 9/12 = **£375,000** and £50,000 × 9/12 = **£37,500**. Excluded territories threshold = greater of 10% of accounting profits and £50,000: accounting profits £300,000 gives **£50,000**; £2,000,000 gives **£200,000**.

**Example 5: The 5% incidental finance profits rule**
Trading CFC with trading profits (before interest and tax) £3,000,000 and NTFP £140,000: 5% = £150,000; NTFP ≤ £150,000, so **Ch 5 does not apply** (s 371CC).

**Example 6: Apportionment mechanics**
Holding 60% for 146 days then 100% for 219 days of a 365-day AP: P% = 60% × 146/365 + 100% × 219/365 = **84%** (s 371QF). Indirect: UK plc holds 80% of X Ltd (non-UK), which holds 70% of the CFC: P% = 80% × 70% = **56%** (s 371QE).

**Example 7: TP adjustments for a UK lender and a UK service provider (M25 Q4 style)**
- Interest-free loan of **£5,000,000** from UK parent to overseas subsidiary; arm's length rate 6%: UK parent's profits increased by **£300,000**; CT **£75,000**. The overseas subsidiary is outside UK tax, so no s 174 claim; relief abroad depends on the treaty (Art 9(2)) and MAP (s 124).
- Management services costing **£800,000** recharged at £600,000; low value-adding services so cost + 5% = **£840,000**: adjustment **£240,000**, CT **£60,000**.
- Is the parent an SME? If the group has **220 staff**, turnover **€60m** and balance sheet **€40m**, it is **medium-sized** (fewer than 250 staff and balance sheet ≤ €43m), so exempt unless HMRC issues a TP notice or the counterparty is in a **non-qualifying territory**.

**Example 8: Methods (N23 Q6 style)**
- Resale price: distributor resells at £1,000; comparable gross margin 25%: arm's length purchase price **£750**.
- Cost plus: manufacturer's cost £400; arm's length mark-up 20%: price **£480**.
- TNMM: distributor sales £10,000,000; operating expenses £1,500,000; arm's length operating margin 3%: operating profit £300,000, so arm's length purchases = **£8,200,000**.
- Residual profit split: combined profit £10,000,000; routine returns A £3,000,000, B £2,000,000; residual £5,000,000 split 60:40 by relative R&D contribution: A **£6,000,000**, B **£4,000,000**.

**Example 9: Thin capitalisation then CIR (M23 Q3 / M26 Q1 style)**
UK subsidiary borrows **£100m** from its US parent at 9% (interest £9,000,000). Arm's length analysis: it could borrow **£60m** at **7%** on its own (no guarantee/implicit-support adjustment): arm's length interest **£4,200,000**; TP disallowance **£4,800,000** (s 147; s 153A if a parent guarantee was needed for the excess). CIR then applies to the remaining £4,200,000: Tax-EBITDA **£12,000,000** (unaffected by the interest disallowance); fixed ratio 30% = £3,600,000; interest capacity = greater of £3,600,000 and the £2m de minimis = **£3,600,000**; CIR disallowance **£600,000**. Total disallowed £5,400,000; CT **£1,350,000**. US parent: no UK compensating adjustment; withholding on the arm's length interest only (law sheet 3).

**Example 10: Hybrid financial instrument (Ch 3)**
- Case 1: UK Co pays a **£1,000,000** coupon to its 100% parent in Territory X, which treats the instrument as equity and exempts the receipt as a dividend: D/NI mismatch from the instrument's features; UK payer's deduction reduced by **£1,000,000** (s 259CD); CT effect **£250,000**.
- Case 2 (under-taxed): Territory X taxes the receipt at a special 5% where its full marginal rate is 20%: mismatch = UTA × (FMR − R)/FMR = £1,000,000 × 15/20 = **£750,000**; UK deduction allowed **£250,000** (s 259CB(11)).
- If instead the **UK** is the payee jurisdiction and X does not counteract, the UK payee is taxed on the relevant amount (s 259CE).

**Example 11: Hybrid entity double deduction (Ch 9)**
UK Sub is treated as transparent by its US parent (US "investor") and incurs **£2,000,000** of expenses deductible in both countries. If the US applies no equivalent rule and they are in the same control group, the UK restricts UK Sub's deduction to **dual inclusion income** (£1,400,000 here): deduct **£1,400,000** now, carry forward **£600,000** against future DII (s 259IC; investor-side primary response is s 259IB).

**Example 12: Migration exit charge and payment plan (N25 Q3 style)**
An Irish-incorporated, UK-resident trading company (so eligible for a Sch 3ZB plan) moves its effective management to Ireland and keeps no UK PE. Immediately before migration:
- Patents (CTA 2009 s 859): market value £5,000,000, TWDV £2,000,000: credit **£3,000,000**.
- Trading stock (ss 41, 162): value £900,000, cost £700,000: **£200,000**.
- Loan asset (s 333): fair value £10,400,000, carrying value £10,000,000: **£400,000**.
- 5% portfolio shareholding (s 185; no SSE): MV £3,000,000, cost £1,200,000 (bought after 2017, so no indexation): **£1,800,000**.
- UK investment property let to third parties (not a PE asset): deemed gain **£2,500,000** **postponed** under TCGA s 187B until actual disposal (unless elected out within 2 years); still within UK tax on disposal as UK land, and the rents stay within CT as UK property income.
Exit profits **£5,400,000**, CT **£1,350,000**. If the migration AP's CT without the exit items is £300,000, CT1 = £1,650,000, CT2 = £300,000, ECPP tax = **£1,350,000**, paid in **6 instalments of £225,000** plus interest, the first 9 months and 1 day after the AP ends. Before migrating: TMA s 109B notice, migration time, statement of tax (CT, PAYE, CIS etc., plus interest) and approved arrangements (or the plan).

## Traps and examiner favourites

Evidence from exam-intel.md (CIOT examiners' reports and marking guides) unless marked "statute".

1. **Apply the shareholding percentage and the 25% test properly.** M25 Q1: candidates "could not apply" the rules; errors included **not applying the shareholding %** and **mishandling creditable tax**. The 25% chargeable company test (s 371BD) counts connected/associated holdings; it is **not** the control test (M24 Q5: candidates "confused the 25% apportionment threshold with the control tests").
2. **Stop when an exemption applies.** M24 Q5: candidates "kept discussing other exemptions after finding one that applied". Order of attack: CFC status (control) → entity exemptions (exempt period, ETE, low profits, LPM, tax) → gateway → apportionment and charge.
3. **Low profit margin arithmetic.** M25 Q1: "Few calculated the low profit margin correctly." Use profit **before interest**; exclude goods not used in the territory and related-party expenditure from ROE (Example 2).
4. **Tax exemption: adjust both sides.** Compare adjusted local tax with UK tax on **ATTP** under the CT assumptions; tax holidays and designer rates fail (M24 Q5 "tax-holiday company"; statute s 371NC–ND).
5. **Gateways are weaker than exemptions** (M24 Q5). Know Ch 3 Conditions A–D for Ch 4, the 5% rule, s 371EC capital investment from the UK, and the Ch 9 75%/full exemptions with the **business premises condition**. N23 Q1(c): a finance company paying 1% tax went through the NTFP gateway.
6. **Adjust for gains and UK dividends in chargeable profits** (M25 Q1: "not adjusting for gains and dividends"). Capital profits are excluded from **accounting profits** (s 371VD) and from the tax-exemption local profits (s 371NB(4)), but chargeable gains are part of assumed TTP; exempt distributions are excluded.
7. **Exempt period is well understood** (M25 Q1), but remember the subsequent-period condition and the 12-month amendment window.
8. **"How CFC legislation works" is an easy mark** (M26 Q5): state the charge on UK companies with ≥ 25% interests, the gateway and exemptions in a few lines.
9. **TP methods: analyse functions and risks.** N23 Q6: "Theory good, application weak: functions and risks were not analysed. Most did not know the low-value services approach or the stewardship point." Match method to entity: manufacturer (cost plus), distributor (resale price/TNMM), contract R&D (cost plus, but check who controls risk).
10. **TP marks are for spotting and explaining** (M25 Q4): interest-free loans to subsidiaries and undercharged management fees; state participation, potential UK advantage, SME status and the adjustment.
11. **SME exemption flips on acquisition** (N24 Q5): a UK SME bought by a large group becomes subject to TP, with Master/Local File obligations if the group revenue is ≥ €750m. Don't over-write TP and miss easier marks.
12. **Don't bring in DPT for loans** (M23 Q3: "Time was wasted on DPT, which does not apply to loan relationships"); for APs from 1 January 2026 the regime is **UTPP**, which also excludes profits arising wholly from excepted loan relationships (law sheet 3).
13. **TP before CIR, and Tax-EBITDA is not reduced by interest disallowances** (M26 Q1 marking guide; statute s 155(6)).
14. **Hybrids need a hybrid** (N25 Q6): "Some thought a low-tax jurisdiction alone made something a hybrid." Identify the mismatch (D/NI or DD), the chapter, the **primary** and **secondary** response, and admin (CT600B, dual inclusion income). N24 Q5: "brought in hybrids without any trigger."
15. **Migration: land is automatically postponed** (N25 Q3: common error "missing the automatic postponement on land"); **don't discuss the disregard regulations**; know payment plan detail (6 equal instalments, acceleration events) and notification (notice, statement, penalties, recovery from group companies and directors).
16. **Don't confuse PE and migration questions** (M26 Q5: "Many wrongly discussed residence or migration of the UK company").
17. **Statute traps for 2027 answers**: s 187 postponement is gone (migrations from 1 January 2020); realisation-method plans are gone; Sch 3ZB is limited to EU/EEA-protected companies moving to a relevant EEA state; s 164A UK-UK exemption applies only from chargeable periods beginning on or after **1 January 2026** and not for CFCs, banks, patent box provision, differing rates or currencies; guarantee rules apply fully only from **1 January 2028** (earlier only for new borrowing or by election).
