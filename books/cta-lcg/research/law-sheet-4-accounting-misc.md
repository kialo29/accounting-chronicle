Researcher brief: CTA Advanced Technical paper *Taxation of Larger Companies and Groups* (LCG). Law assumed: **Finance Act 2026 (c. 11)**, financial year 2026 (1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027). Research date: 9 October 2026.

**Status key:** **V** = verified (I opened and read the statutory text or primary source in this session). **S** = secondary (an HMRC manual paraphrase, a professional body or a firm summary). **U** = unverified (from memory or inference, so check before use).

**legislation.gov.uk currency.** Every legislation.gov.uk page I opened carried the banner "up to date with all changes known to be in force on or before [6 to 9] October 2026", followed by the standard warning that "there are changes that may be brought into force at a future date". The FA 2026 pages said "no known outstanding effects". So the text quoted here should be current to early October 2026. Statutory instruments (SIs) are generally shown on the site as made, not as amended. That matters for SI 2004/3271, covered below.

### 1.4 Leases: IFRS 16 and FRS 102 (2024 amendments), and the lessee tax rules

| Rule | Detail | Ref | Effective / FA 2026 | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accounting change | Under IFRS 16, and now FRS 102 Section 20 as amended, lessees **no longer distinguish finance and operating leases**. Most leases go on balance sheet as a right-of-use (RoU) asset with a lease liability. The P&L shows **depreciation plus interest** instead of rent. Short-term (12 months or less) and low-value leases are exempt. | — | IFRS 16: periods beginning on or after 1 Jan 2019. **FRS 102 (2024 amendments): periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026**, with early adoption permitted | https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/business-leasing-manual/blm50005 ; ACCA In Practice March 2026 | S |

Because the liability usually equals the RoU asset, FRS 102 transitions produce adjustments less often than IFRS 16 did. | FRS 102 paras 1.45 to 1.48, as cited by HMRC | — | https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/business-leasing-manual/blm17050 (updated 3 September 2025); BLM52005 | S |
| Tax principle | The underlying tax law is unchanged. HMRC's position is that most rentals remain revenue despite on-balance-sheet accounting, and that every lease must still be tested to see whether it is a long funding lease. The FA 2019 Sch 14 changes "were designed to ensure that there is no tax advantage or disadvantage to lessees". Typically the depreciation and interest are deducted. Where a lease was already a finance lease that qualified for capital allowances, the existing treatment continues. Capital elements of the RoU asset must be stripped out: premiums, SDLT, fit-out, payments to the previous tenant, dilapidations. | — | — | https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/business-leasing-manual/blm50005 ; /blm50010 ; ICAEW 23 March 2026 | S |

**How the exam has asked it (verified, November 2025 LCG paper Q1, 3 marks of 20).** "Calculate the total tax charge required for the profit and loss account (current year tax charge and deferred tax charge), and the closing deferred tax balance."

The suggested answer had two parts:
- **Fixed asset timing differences.** Qualifying NBV of £31,250k (after excluding £92,000k land and buildings) less TWDV of £2,990k gives £28,260k. At 25% that is a DTL of £7,065k, down from £8,375k, a credit of £1,310k.
- **Short-term timing differences.** "Tax provisions" of £475k (from £275k) give a DTA of £118.75k at 25%, a credit of £50k.

The total deferred tax credit was £1,360k. The current tax charge was shown before and after DTR, with foreign tax added back.

**Examiner:** "only a small number of candidates calculated the deferred tax charge, with reference only being made to the fixed asset timing differences without including short term timing differences … candidates … typically only referred to the tax balances, rather than calculating the actual charges."

| Rule | Detail | Ref | Effective / FA 2026 | Source | Status |

## FA 2026 changes affecting these topics (all verified on legislation.gov.uk, ukpga/2026/11)

1. **CT rates for FY2027 (ss 11, 12).** The main rate is 25%. The small profits rate is 19% with a marginal relief fraction of 3/200. **Deferred tax** balances expected to reverse in FY2027 are therefore measured at 25% on an enacted basis.
2. **Main-rate WDA cut from 18% to 14% (s 28).** It applies to CT chargeable periods beginning on or after **1 April 2026**. A straddling period uses the hybrid rate X = 18 × BRD/CP + 14 × ARD/CP, rounded up to 2 decimal places. This affects capital allowance timing differences, deferred tax and long funding lease lessee allowances.
3. **New 40% FYA, CAA s 45U (s 29).** It covers unused, not second-hand, **main-rate** plant (not special rate) incurred **on or after 1 January 2026**. There is a TAAR excluding disqualifying arrangements (s 45V). **General exclusion 6 (plant for leasing) is disapplied** where the lessee uses the plant wholly or almost wholly to earn income within the charge to tax, or is UK resident and not using it significantly for non-taxable overseas income (new s 46(4B) to (4G)). This is relevant to lessors and to the interaction with long funding leases. I have not verified whether a long funding lease lessee's deemed s 70A expenditure qualifies, so treat that as **U**.
4. **New CTA 2009 s 1305B (s 63), in Part 20 general calculation rules.** It denies deductions for losses, expenses or debits on a **liability recognised alongside assets that remain on the transferor group's balance sheet** after a transfer to a **securitisation company** or a party to the same capital market arrangement, where a main purpose of any party is a tax advantage. The denial is on a just and reasonable apportionment. It has effect for **APs beginning on or after 26 November 2025** (Budget day), with straddling periods split.
5. **Charities.** **s 54:** gifts by will to charitable companies are within the CT charge (new CTA 2010 s 474A) but exempt so far as applied for charitable purposes, on a claim, for gifts received on or after 6 April 2026. **s 55:** approved charitable investments get an "allowable purpose" test (CTA 2010 s 511), for investments made on or after 6 April 2026. **s 56 and Sch 9:** the tainted donations outcome test (see §11), which matters most to **company donors**.
6. **Distributions (s 42).** The notional tax credit for non-resident recipients is abolished from 2026-27. This is income tax, awareness only.
7. **No FA 2026 provision found** on these topics: change of accounting basis, FRS 102 lease or revenue transition, management expenses, Part 12 share relief, EBCs, pension spreading, Part 14 change of ownership, transactions in securities, Part 8ZB, Part 9A, liquidation, or Part 22. This is based on a scan of all FA 2026 section headings. Schedules were not read line by line apart from those named above. The FRS 102 lessee transition relies on the **existing FA 2019 Sch 14 spreading**.

## Teaching notes (purpose, history, landmark cases)

### Accounts as the starting point
- **The courts insist that tax profits follow commercial accounting, unless the law says otherwise.**
  - In *Odeon Associated Theatres Ltd v Jones* [1971] 48 TC 257, Salmon LJ said "the ordinary principles of commercial accounting must, as far as practicable, be observed", subject to the law (quoted in BIM31095).
  - *Gallagher v Jones* [1993] 66 TC 77 (CA) and *Johnston v Britannia Airways Ltd* [1994] 67 TC 99 settled that taxable profits should not be computed on a basis "divorced from the principles of commercial accountancy", and that GAAP is a question of fact that evolves (BIM31095, S).
  - CTA 2009 s 46 is the modern codification (V).
- **HMRC v NCL Investments Ltd [2022] UKSC 9** (V, read on Find Case Law). The IFRS 2 debits for share options, granted by a parent's EBT to the companies' employees for accounting periods to 30 April 2010 to 2012, were **deductible**. HMRC lost on every ground: s 46 "adjustment required by law", s 54 wholly and exclusively, s 53 capital, and s 1290 EBCs. The court said "What matters is the character of the Debits, not that of any corresponding credit… these were revenue in nature."
  - Story value: the court refused to let tax ignore an accounting debit with no cash outflow.
  - Context: **FA 2013 s 40** separately substituted s 1038 and inserted s 1038A, which block deductions for shares and options inside Part 12 territory (V).
- **GDF Suez Teesside Ltd v HMRC [2018] EWCA Civ 2075** (V). Teesside Power transferred near-worthless-in-its-accounts Enron claims to a non-resident subsidiary for shares. The accounts were GAAP-compliant, but the old FA 1996 s 84(1) "fairly represent" requirement was **overriding**, so a credit equal to the claims' value had to be recognised. Appeal dismissed.
  - Story: the "Enron claims" case, about the limits of tax following the accounts.
  - The 2016 reforms then refocused the LR rules on amounts in profit or loss (CFM76010, S).
- **Union Castle Mail Steamship Co Ltd v HMRC [2020] EWCA Civ 547** (V). This was a derivative contracts scheme: a £39.1m debit from derecognising FTSE put options, set against A share issues and debited to equity. Union Castle's and Ladbrokes' appeals were both dismissed, with the court relying on "arise from" and "fairly represent".
  - Story: an investment-trust group trying to turn an equity entry into a tax loss.

### Lease accounting
- In 2011 Parliament **froze** lease tax rules against accounting change (FA 2011 s 53).
- When IFRS 16 arrived, FA 2019 Sch 14 **repealed the freeze** (para 11) and instead **spread** the transition adjustment over the weighted average remaining lease term (paras 12 to 17).
- HMRC's stated aim was that there should be "no tax advantage or disadvantage to lessees" (BLM50010).
- The same rules now catch the FRS 102 (2024) adopters from 1 January 2026, so seven years of IFRS 16 experience transfer directly. This makes a good narrative of tax law choosing either to ignore or to follow the accountants.

### Deferred tax
- The IAS 12 Pillar Two exception (May 2023) was an emergency carve-out. The standard-setters admitted they could not yet say how to account for a top-up tax that depends on jurisdictional ETRs.
- Pair it with the UK's own multinational top-up tax.
- The N25 paper shows the examiners care about the mechanics: NBV versus TWDV and short-term differences.

### Employee benefit trusts
- In *Macdonald v Dextra Accessories Ltd* [2005] UKHL 47, contributions to an EBT were potential emoluments, so the deduction waited until they were paid out. HMRC says this applies to contributions made **before 27 November 2002** (IHTM42959, S); the statutory rule replaced it (now CTA 2009 s 1290).
- *RFC 2012 plc (in liquidation) v Advocate General for Scotland* [2017] UKSC 45, the Rangers EBT case (V, title verified), led into the disguised remuneration era.
- F(No.2)A 2017 s 37 then added the **5-year cut-off** and the "tax and NIC paid within 12 months" condition (V).

### Management expenses
- *HMRC Inspector of Taxes v Camas plc* [2004] EWCA Civ 541 (V, title) and *Dawsongroup plc v HMRC* [2010] EWHC 1061 (Ch) (V, title) are the classic "what is an expense of management" cases. Both are discussed in Centrica, together with *Sun Life*.
- **Centrica Overseas Holdings Ltd v HMRC [2024] UKSC 25** (V). About £2.53m of Deutsche Bank, PwC and De Brauw fees on the sale of the Dutch Oxxio business were expenses of management, **but capital**. The capital test in s 1219(3)(a) is the **same as s 53** for traders, and is a question of law. Fees were incurred once a commercial decision to sell an identifiable capital asset had been taken. Appeal dismissed. The court also explained that the capital exclusion was **first enacted by FA 2004 s 38**.
  - Story value: a big-name energy group and a Dutch disposal.

### Change of ownership
- Loss-buying rules target the purchase of "tax shells".
- *Williams v Peeters Picture Frames Ltd* (1983) 56 TC 436, as HMRC's CTM06370 spells it (the case is often cited as **Willis** v Peeters, so check the name, U). The company switched to selling via a group distributor to the same end customers. On the special facts this was **not** a major change in customers, outlets or markets.
- *Purchase v Tesco Stores Ltd* (1984) 58 TC 46: "major" means more than significant but less than fundamental.
- *Rolls-Royce Motors Ltd v Bamford* (1976) 51 TC 344: the village grocer analogy for gradual change.
- **SP10/91** sets out HMRC's practice (CTM06370, S).
- The 2017 loss reform widened the windows: 3 to 5 years for trades, and 8 years for investment companies (V, amendment annotations).

### Transactions in securities
- These are ancient anti-avoidance rules aimed at turning income into capital.
- *IRC v Parker* (1966) 43 TC 396, *IRC v Joiner* (1975) 50 TC 499, *Williams v IRC* (1979) 54 TC 257, and *IRC v Laird Group plc* [2003] UKHL 54 (a dividend alone is not a transaction in securities), all from CTM36810 (S).
- FA 2010 Sch 12 removed circumstance A (V).
- Note for the narrative: *IRC v Cleary* [1968] AC 766, the classic income-tax TiS case, was **not verified** in this session.

### Transactions in land
- Part 8ZB, from FA 2016 and in force from 5 July 2016, rewrote the old "artificial transactions in land" code. It brought **offshore developers** into the UK charge after avoidance through offshore structures and treaties. FA 2016 s 81(4) to (12) counteracted arrangements made between 16 March and 5 July 2016 (V).
- *Page v Lowther* (1983) 57 TC 199, the old s 776 case, was not verified (U).

### Liquidation
- *Ayerst v C & K (Construction) Ltd* (1974) 50 TC 651: liquidation strips beneficial ownership (CTM36125, S).
- The informal strike-off concession ESC C16 was put on a statutory footing by SI 2012/266 with a **£25,000 cap** (V).

### Distributions
- Part 9A (FA 2009) replaced the old UK-only dividend exemption with a **worldwide exemption**, a response to EU-law challenges. The EU-law motivation is from recollection (U).
- Company law background: *Progress Property Co Ltd v Moorgarth Group Ltd* [2010] UKSC 55 (V, title only), on whether an undervalue transfer is a distribution. Holding not verified (U).

### Leasing
- The long funding lease regime (FA 2006 Sch 8) moved capital allowances from lessor to lessee for long, finance-type leases. It was an answer to lessor-driven allowance schemes.
- *Barclays Mercantile Business Finance Ltd v Mawson* [2004] UKHL 51 is the landmark finance-lease capital allowances case. It is not on Find Case Law, so the citation is from recollection (U).

## Traps and examiner favourites

**Evidence base.** The trap list mixes two kinds of evidence. Items marked "(N25)" come from the CIOT November 2025 LCG paper, its suggested answers and the examiners' report, which I read for this session. The other items are traps inferred from the verified statute.

1. **Deferred tax, the whole of it (N25).** Candidates did fixed asset timing differences only. They must also include short-term timing differences (unpaid remuneration, pensions, provisions) and compute the charge or credit, not just the balances. Exclude non-qualifying land and buildings from the fixed asset difference. Use 25%.
2. **Pension contributions (N25).** These are on a **paid** basis (FA 2004 s 196). Accrued but unpaid contributions are added back, and prior-year accruals paid this year are deducted. **Spreading was wrongly applied** by many candidates. It needs CCCP above 210% of CPCP **and** an excess over 110% of at least £500k (s 197). Note that the N25 suggested answer's short explanation ("do not exceed the prior period's contributions by more than £500,000") is a simplification of the statutory test.
3. **Bonuses and LTIPs (N25).** Remuneration unpaid **9 months** after the period end is deductible only when paid (s 1288). An amount paid 15 April is fine; an amount paid the following 1 January is not. The same 9-month idea applies to EBCs (s 1290), with the extra 5-year and 12-month tax-paid rules.
4. **Long funding lease plant (N25).** Lessee asset additions under a long funding lease go to the **main pool**, not special rate. Remember the lessee's s 70H return election and the 7-year short lease cut-off.
5. **Share schemes.** Add back the IFRS 2 / FRS 102 s 26 charge (s 1038). Deduct the Part 12 amount, which is **MV less amount paid**, in the AP of **acquisition** (exercise), not grant. Scheme set-up and administration costs remain deductible. Lapsed options get nothing (s 1038A).
6. **Change of accounting basis.** The Ch 14 adjustment is taxed in full on **day 1** of the first new-basis period, with **no spreading** for revenue (IFRS 15 / FRS 102 s 23). Lease transition **is** spread (FA 2019 Sch 14). LR and derivative transition amounts are spread over **10 years** only where they are "prescribed" (COAP reg 3A). Do not mix these up. A change of view on the law **is** a change of basis (s 180(6)(b)).
7. **FRS 102 2026 knock-ons.** Transition profits can trigger QIPs or very-large-company QIPs and the £5m loss allowance (ICAEW). This is a likely planning or "advise the CFO" angle.
8. **Management expenses.**
   - Capital costs of acquiring or disposing of investments are not deductible once a decision to proceed is made (*Centrica*).
   - Management expenses come off total profits **first**.
   - Carried-forward amounts **need a claim** and fall within the 50% restriction.
   - An investment company's excess QCDs are carried forward as management expenses, which is unusual.
9. **Investment company change of ownership.** The capital increase test is **both** at least £1m **and** at least 125%. The major change window is 8 years starting 3 years before. Check the s 719 definition carefully (more than 50%, with 5% holdings aggregated).
10. **Transactions in securities.** Candidates should name the **circumstance** (C, D or E) and the **relevant company** test (5 or fewer controllers, or unquoted). The defence is commercial reasons **and** no main object of a CT advantage. Clearance runs on **30-day** clocks, is void on non-disclosure, and has a 6-year limit for counteraction.
11. **Part 8ZB.**
    - It applies to non-residents.
    - Gains are taxed as **trading** profits even if capital.
    - The indirect rule uses a **50%** value test.
    - Profit accrued before the intention to develop is carved out.
    - Effective for disposals from 5 July 2016.
12. **Liquidation APs.** The AP ends immediately **before** winding up starts, then runs in 12-month periods. For administration, it ends the day before entry. Beneficial ownership is lost on liquidation, so group relief is affected, but gains groups are kept (s 170(11)). **s 1030A:** the £25k limit is on the total of pre-dissolution distributions. If dissolution does not happen within 2 years, everything is an income distribution.
13. **Distributions received.** Large companies need an **exempt class**. Watch E and F distributions (not exempt), foreign-deductible dividends (not exempt), the 40/55 JV rule, the portfolio test (all three limbs under 10%), and the 2-year s 931R election to tax a dividend to secure foreign tax credits.
14. **Charitable donations.**
    - QCDs are deducted after other reliefs but before group relief.
    - They cannot create a loss.
    - Excess is surrenderable as group relief but not carried forward.
    - Watch the benefits limits (25%, then £25 plus 5%, aggregate £2,500).
    - Since 6 April 2026, check the **tainted donations outcome test** where the charity lends or invests back into the donor's circle.
15. **Part 22.**
    - On a Ch 1 trade transfer, no s 39 carry back for the predecessor.
    - The successor inherits losses only to the extent of the net assets left behind (the L − A restriction).
    - Capital allowances pass at TWDV.
    - Ch 4 tax refund surrender must be notified **before** the refund is made.
    - Ch 7 lets HMRC chase group companies for a non-resident's unpaid CT after 6 months.
16. **Corporate partner.** Compute the firm's profit using **CT rules** (s 1259), not income tax rules. Allocate by profit-sharing ratio. Mixed membership allocations to a corporate partner may be reallocated to individuals (ITTOIA s 850C; CTA 2009 s 1264A).
17. **Unremittable income.** The claim is due within 2 years. The income is taxed when it becomes remittable, at the value at that date.
18. **Grid scope reminders.** Sale and leaseback is **awareness (3)**. Part 14B is **non-core (2)**. Part 22 Chs 3 and 8 and ss 990 to 995 are **excluded**. Group relief (and so consortium relief) is examined at "Awareness module, excluding consortia", so for JVs teach the consortium definition in outline only.
