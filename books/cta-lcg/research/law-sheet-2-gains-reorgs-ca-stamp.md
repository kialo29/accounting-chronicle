# LCG Law Sheet 2: company chargeable gains, reorganisations, capital allowances and stamp taxes (FY2026, Finance Act 2026)

Compiled 9 October 2026 for the CTA Advanced Technical paper *Taxation of Larger Companies and Groups* (LCG). Assumed law: Finance Act 2026 (2026 c. 11, Royal Assent 18 March 2026), financial year 2026 (1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027).

**Method.** Statute was read as raw text from legislation.gov.uk's XML (`/data.xml`), with each provision's amendment notes ("commentaries") checked for recent changes. HMRC manuals and GOV.UK guidance were read through the GOV.UK content API. Judgments came from Find Case Law (caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk). Facts already verified in the TKS project (`/home/claude/cta-tks/rates-2026-27.md`, `book-bible.md` and chapter notes 16, 23, 24 and 30) were reused rather than re-verified, and are marked **R**.

**Currency of legislation.gov.uk.** Every provision below that FA 2026 amended already showed the FA 2026 text, with FA 2026 amendment notes: TCGA s 2B, s 135(6), s 136(6), s 137, s 138, s 139, Sch 1A, and CAA s 56(1) at 14%, s 45U, s 45V and s 46(4B). The "unapplied effects" flags on the CAA 2001 and TCGA records refer to other provisions, for example a 2026 Senedd Act amending CAA s 436. None of them touches the provisions used here. Even so, any text read on legislation.gov.uk could lag behind later amendments. Where that matters, it is flagged.

**Status key.**
- **V**: verified in a primary or official source opened on 9 October 2026.
- **R**: verified earlier in the TKS project files and not re-opened.
- **S**: secondary source only.
- **U**: unverified, so do not use it as fact without checking.

**Abbreviations.** TCGA = Taxation of Chargeable Gains Act 1992. CAA = Capital Allowances Act 2001. CTA 2009/2010 = Corporation Tax Acts. TMA = Taxes Management Act 1970. FA = Finance Act. LGU = legislation.gov.uk.

## 16. FA 2026 changes affecting this sheet

1. **TCGA s 137 recast (FA 2026 s 37).** The bona fide commercial and main-purpose double test is replaced by a single main-purpose test with *just and reasonable counteraction*, which can partly disapply ss 135 and 136. The **5% small-holder exception (old s 137(2)–(3)) is abolished**, so minority holders in listed takeovers are now in scope. s 138 clearance is reworded to "without arrangements in respect of which section 137 applies". This applies to share or debenture issues **on or after 26 November 2025**, with transitional clearance protection to 26 January 2026 or 60 days. [V]
2. **TCGA s 139 recast (FA 2026 s 38).** There is a new main-purpose test with counteraction for reconstructions involving a transfer of business. It now covers **income tax as well as CGT and CT**, and the s 139(5) clearance is reworded. This applies to transfers of business assets **on or after 26 November 2025**. [V]
3. **TCGA s 103K (CIS reconstructions) recast (FA 2026 s 36).** Same model; awareness only. [V]
4. **Main rate WDA cut to 14% (FA 2026 s 28)** for chargeable periods beginning on or after 1 April 2026 (CT). Day-weighted hybrid rate rounded up to 2 decimal places. [V]
5. **New 40% FYA (FA 2026 s 29, CAA ss 45U–45V, 46(4B)–(4G))** from 1 January 2026, for main-rate new plant. Unlike full expensing, it **covers leasing** (but not overseas leasing) and unincorporated businesses. There is no special balancing charge. [V]
6. **Zero-emission car / EV charge point 100% FYA** extended to 31 March 2027 (FA 2026 s 30). [R]
7. **Non-resident company gains via a UK PE (FA 2026 Sch 7 para 28)**, for chargeable periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026: the "relevant connection to the PE" wording and s 2C(2) are removed, and attribution under CTA 2009 ss 20 and 24. This is part of the PE reform (CTA 2010 s 1141 also changed, per the bible). [V]
8. **Sch 1A cell companies (FA 2026 s 40)**: each cell is treated as a separate company for the 75% UK-land-rich test, for disposals on or after 26 November 2025. [V]
9. **SDRT UK listing relief (FA 2026 s 85, FA 1986 s 89C)**, 3 years from first listing, for listings from 27 November 2025. [R]
10. **Not FA 2026, but 2026 developments:** the HMRC distributions consultation (23 June to 14 September 2026) proposes liberalising statutory demergers and freezing share capital on new holding companies. These are proposals only. The VAT capital goods scheme thresholds changed from 29 July 2026 (affecting CAA ss 234–240 adjustments). [V / R]
11. **No FA 2026 change found to:** SSE (Sch 7AC), s 171/179/Sch 7A, s 184A–I, s 190, demergers (CTA 2010 Part 23 Ch 5), full expensing (s 45S), AIA, SBA, fixtures, LFLs, FA 1930 s 42 or SDLT Sch 7 (based on the FA 2026 contents list). [V]

## 17. Teaching notes (purpose, history, leading cases)

**Indexation frozen in time.** FA 2018 added s 53(1B): no indexation for RPI movements after December 2017. Indexation also cannot create or increase a loss (s 53(2A)). TKS chapter 23 already tells the "thermometer froze" story and the stated aims: alignment with individuals and with trading disposals, and simplification. For LCG the twist is the s 110 indexed pool. Its formula now hard-codes "RE is the retail prices index for December 2017", so a pool's indexed rise stops dead. [V]

**Paper for paper and the long fight over purpose (ss 135–138; *Delinian*).**
- s 135 lets a takeover happen without forcing a tax charge on shareholders who receive only paper. s 137 has always been the gatekeeper.
- **Story: *Delinian Ltd (formerly Euromoney Institutional Investor plc) v HMRC* [2023] EWCA Civ 1281 (3 November 2023).** Euromoney sold Capital Data to Diamond Topco for about US$80.44m. The roughly US$21m cash element was restructured into redeemable preference shares in the buyer, redeemed in January 2016 for US$21.2m. A Group Managing Director's email said: "The preference shares are the mechanism to avoid paying tax on the capital gain for the cash element of the transaction."
- The FTT found avoidance was *a* purpose of the arrangements as a whole, but not a *main* purpose. The UT ([2022] UKUT 205 (TCC)) and the Court of Appeal (Vos MR, Snowden and Whipple LJJ) upheld that approach: the arrangements are "the whole of the scheme or arrangements undertaken by the taxpayer in question, not a selected part". Euromoney won.
- FA 2026 s 37 then rewrote s 137 for issues from 26 November 2025. The new wording targets "arrangements relating to" the exchange and counteracts only the avoidance, "just and reasonably". It also scraps the 5% exception. The link between *Delinian* and the rewrite is an inference: the TIIN does not name the case. There is **no case law yet on the new wording.** [R, from the TKS ch 16 notes; s 37 V]

**The reconstruction family (s 136, s 139, Sch 5AA; stamp s 75 and SDLT Sch 7).**
- Sch 5AA (FA 2002 origin, not re-verified) replaced the old case-law notion of "reconstruction" with four statutory conditions. Note that the 2026 consultation describes HMRC's practice for clearances as sometimes more generous than the statute.
- Teaching angle: the same commercial event (splitting a business between shareholder groups) is tested four times, by four slightly different rule-books. Those are CGT for shareholders (s 136), CT for the company (s 139), stamp duty (FA 1986 s 75) and SDLT (Sch 7 para 7). Each has its own purpose test, and s 139 now reaches income tax too. [V]

**Deferred consideration (*Marren v Ingles*).**
- Story: the taxpayer sold shares in a private company for an immediate payment plus a further sum payable if the company later obtained a Stock Exchange quotation and its first-day price exceeded a set level. Both happened.
- The courts held that the right to the further, unascertainable sum was a separate asset (a chose in action). Its value is included in the original proceeds, and each later receipt is a disposal of that right, not the payment of a debt.
- Citation: *Marren v Ingles* 54 TC 76; [1980] STC 500; [1980] 1 WLR 983; [1980] 3 All ER 95 (ACCA summary; CG14990). The court (commonly given as the House of Lords) was not stated in the sources opened. Companion case: *Marson v Marriage* (54 TC 59), Fox J, on s 48 and ascertainable contingent consideration.
- For companies, s 48(2)–(3) sends rights under a creditor relationship to the loan relationship rules at fair value. s 138A rescues share earn-outs into paper-for-paper treatment, with an irrevocable election out (2 years after the end of the accounting period for a company).
- Exam point: later receipts get none of the original reliefs (CG14970: no roll-over on the right; SVM107160). [V / S]

**Value shifting (ss 29–31).**
- s 29(2) is the old "control and value passing" rule. s 30 targets schemes that depress an asset's value while handing a tax-free benefit to someone.
- For companies selling shares, FA 2011 Sch 9 replaced the old ss 31–34, which were notoriously complex, with a single targeted rule: s 31, a main purpose of avoiding CT on gains, and just and reasonable uplift. It carves out "exempt distributions" within CTA 2009 s 931H. That carve-out is the key teaching point: an ordinary pre-sale dividend from genuine profits is fine, but a manufactured value strip is not. [V]

**Groups: separate entities that share a gains "pool" (ss 170–175).**
- s 171 lets assets move at no gain, no loss. s 171A (from FA 2009, date not re-verified) lets a group match gains and losses *without* moving the asset. That replaced the old practice of intra-group transfers just before sale.
- The 75% / effective 51% test means a group can be wider than the parent's direct holdings but never leaks to minority-held companies. s 190 uses a 51% group, so collection reaches further than relief. [V]

**The degrouping charge and the 2011 reform (s 179; *Johnston Publishing*).**
- The charge (FA 1968 origin per CG45400, single source, R) stops a group from moving an asset into a subsidiary at no gain, no loss and then selling the subsidiary's shares rather than the asset.
- *Johnston Publishing (North) Ltd v HMRC* [2008] EWCA Civ 858: the majority held that the old "associated companies leaving together" exception required association at the time of the acquisition. Tuckey LJ, para 46: "one must start by trying to give meaning to all the words used." FA 2011 Sch 10 rewrote s 179(2) into Conditions A and B, which now expressly look back to acquisition.
- The bigger FA 2011 change: where the company leaves on a share sale by a group member, the degrouping gain is added to the share sale proceeds. So it is **exempt if the share sale is within the SSE**, which aligns the charge with the policy of not taxing share sales twice. Applies from 19 July 2011 (or 1 April 2011 by election). [R / V]

**SSE: Brown's 2002 Budget and the 2017 reforms.** This is in the TKS ch 23 story (verified). For LCG, add:
- the hive-down rule in para 15A (FA 2011), illustrated by *M Group Holdings Ltd v HMRC* [2023] UKUT 213 (TCC). A single company that hived down a trade and sold the new subsidiary 11 months later could not count the month before the subsidiary existed, because "a group needs more than one company". The gain was about £53.2m and the tax about £10.6m (R).
- the para 5 anti-avoidance rule ("sole or main benefit");
- the para 4 priority over s 127, which stops a company using paper-for-paper to defer a gain it would rather have exempt, or to preserve a loss. [V / R]

**Pre-entry losses and gain buying (Sch 7A; ss 184A–184I).**
- The 1993 pre-entry loss regime, with time-apportionment and market-value elections, was slashed back by FA 2011 Sch 11 to a simple rule: only losses actually realised before entry are ring-fenced. Para 7 says where they may still be used.
- The old pre-entry *gains* regime (Sch 7AA) was repealed in 2006 and replaced by purpose-based anti-buying rules (s 184A for losses, s 184B for gains) built on a "qualifying change of ownership".
- Teaching angle: the move from mechanical ring-fences to purpose tests is also visible in s 137, s 139 and s 31. [V]

**Exit charges after Brexit (s 185; TMA Sch 3ZB).**
- s 185 taxes unrealised gains when a company migrates. The old s 187 postponement for 75% subsidiaries of UK companies was repealed by FA 2019 Sch 8.
- Payment plans (6 annual instalments with interest) came in after the *National Grid Indus* line of EU case law (that link was not verified here). On legislation.gov.uk they are still limited to migrations to "relevant EEA states". This is a nice exam nuance and worth re-checking against the latest text. [V, history U]

**Demergers.**
- The demerger provisions (GOV.UK still cites Statement of Practice 13 (1980), which suggests a 1980 origin; the originating Act was not verified) exist to let a trading group split without a dividend charge for shareholders, a capital gain for the company, or degrouping charges.
- The June 2026 consultation says the relief "is not currently well-used" and proposes liberalising almost every condition. Meanwhile it proposes shutting the capital-reduction and liquidation (Insolvency Act 1986 s 110) demerger routes that practitioners use instead.
- Good material for a "law in motion" box. Students must answer on current law (conditions A–M). [V]

**Capital allowances: the investment-incentive arc.**
- Full expensing (100%) and the 50% special rate FYA have been in place for companies since 1 April 2023 and are permanent.
- FA 2026 cut the main rate to 14% and introduced a 40% FYA that reaches leasing and unincorporated businesses.
- The policy story is in the policy paper "Capital allowances: new first-year allowance and reducing main rate writing-down allowances" (rates file, R).
- Teaching angle: full expensing's special balancing charge (ss 59A, 59B) shows the price of 100% relief. The disposal proceeds are taxed in full (or half for 50% assets), not netted in the pool. [V]

**Fixtures: making allowances follow the building.**
- Before FA 2012, buyers and sellers could both claim the same fixtures. ss 187A and 187B require the seller to have pooled (from April 2014) and the parties to fix the value (from April 2012), usually by s 198 election within 2 years. Otherwise the buyer's claim is nil for every future owner.
- This is a classic due-diligence point in property deals. [V]

**Succession and *Kenmir v Frizzell*.**
- Where the question is whether a *business* (rather than assets) has passed, for example for the CAA s 265–267 succession rules, CTA 2010 Part 22 Ch 1 or VAT TOGC, tribunals still quote Widgery J in *Kenmir Ltd v Frizzell* [1968] 1 All ER 414 at 418: "the vital consideration is whether the effect of the transaction was to put the transferee in possession of a going concern, the activities of which he could carry on without interruption".
- Verified via the FTT in *Haymarket Media Group Ltd v HMRC* [2022] UKFTT 168 (TC), which quotes it.
- It was originally an employment case, so use it as a test of "business transfer", not as CA authority. [V via citing judgment]

**Stamp taxes: why groups get relief and why it claws back.**
- FA 1930 s 42 (as tightened in 1967, 1995 and 2000) and SDLT Sch 7 exist so intra-group reorganisations are not taxed. The "control of the transferee but not the transferor" arrangements test, and the 3-year SDLT clawback, stop "enveloping" land in a subsidiary and selling the shares free of SDLT.
- The SDLT acquisition relief (0.5% cap) survives, although stamp duty's equivalent (FA 1986 s 76) was repealed by FA 2012. [V]

**Cases checked but not used.** *Prudential* (degrouping): no relevant LCG judgment was verified (the TKS *Prudential* is the 2013 legal professional privilege case). *Gallaher* (s 171, EU freedom of establishment): not found on Find Case Law with the queries tried. Do not cite either without checking.

## 18. Traps and examiner favourites (evidence: statute text; no examiner reports were opened)

1. **No 5% exception any more.** A minority shareholder (company or individual) in a share exchange is within new s 137 for issues from 26 November 2025. Do not reproduce pre-2026 study texts. [FA 2026 s 37]
2. **s 139 now reaches income tax avoidance;** s 137 does not (CGT and CT only). [s 137(1), s 139(4A)]
3. **s 138 clearance must be given *before* the issue,** and it does not confirm that s 135 applies, or that loan notes are or are not QCBs (CG52631). For companies, loan notes are QCBs (s 117(A1)), so s 135 never gives paper-for-paper on them: s 116 applies instead.
4. **10-day rule, not 30 days, for companies**, and matched shares stay out of the pool. The order is same day → previous 10 days → s 104 pool. [s 105, s 107]
5. **Stock dividend received by a company:** no added cost (bonus-issue treatment). s 142's cash-equivalent cost is for individuals. [CTM17005; CG58750]
6. **SSE losses are not allowable,** so a group wanting a loss must fail the SSE. The SSE is automatic, not a claim. The investee must still trade immediately after the sale **only if the buyer is connected** (or para 15A is used). [Sch 7AC paras 1, 19]
7. **Degrouping gain on a share sale goes onto the seller's proceeds** (then usually SSE-exempt). The transferee's asset is still rebased to market value at the transfer date. Six-year window, measured from the intra-group *acquisition*. [s 179(3), (3A)–(3D)]
8. **Associated companies leaving together** escape only if the 75% / effective 51% relationship existed **from the date of the intra-group acquisition** (*Johnston Publishing*). [s 179(2ZA)–(2ZB)]
9. **s 171A election deadline: 2 years** from the end of the accounting period of the transferring company. Any payment above the gain or loss can be a distribution or taxable. [ss 171A(5), 171B(6)]
10. **Gains group vs SSE group vs s 190 group:** 75% plus effective 51% for gains; 51% for the SSE (para 26); 51% for recovery (s 190(13)). Stamp duty, SDLT and group relief tests also need 75% of profits and assets.
11. **s 31, not s 30, for company share disposals;** dividends from genuine profits (CTA 2009 s 931H exempt class) are carved out. [s 30(2), s 31(1)(c)]
12. **Depreciatory transactions reduce losses only** (with a capped later gain reduction). Negligible value claims count. [s 176(4)–(8)]
13. **Pre-entry losses:** only losses that had already accrued before entry are restricted. They can still be used against gains on assets held at entry, or new assets used in the same trade. [Sch 7A para 7]
14. **Exit charge payment plan:** apply within 9 months of the end of the migration period; 6 equal annual instalments; interest runs; EEA destination only (as legislated). [TMA Sch 3ZB]
15. **Demerger conditions:** the distributor must be a trading company or a member of a trading group, and each subsidiary demerged must trade. A pre-arranged sale or change of control breaks condition D, and a chargeable payment within 5 years is taxed as income and revives s 179. [CTA 2010 ss 1081, 1086; TCGA s 192(4)]
16. **Hybrid WDA rounding is *up*** to 2 decimal places, using days not months (for example 16.00% for an accounting period of 1 October 2025 to 30 September 2026). [FA 2026 s 28(5)]
17. **Full expensing excludes cars, second-hand assets and leasing** (except excluded leases of background plant). The 40% FYA covers leasing but not special rate assets or cars. Neither is available in the year of cessation. On disposal of a 100% asset, the special balancing charge is the full proceeds proportion (s 59A); for a 50% asset it is half (s 59B). [ss 45S, 45U, 46, 59A–59B]
18. **One AIA for a group:** a parent undertaking (Companies Act meaning) and its subsidiaries share £1m. So do related companies under common control. [ss 51C–51E]
19. **LLA £100,000 is divided by (associated companies + 1)** and is not increased for a long period of account. If it is exceeded, *all* the LLA spend is special rate. [s 99]
20. **Short-life asset cut-off: 8 years** from the end of the period of expenditure. The election is due within 2 years for CT. Not available for cars or special rate expenditure. [ss 85, 86, 84]
21. **Fixtures in a property purchase:** no pooling by the seller, or no s 198 election within 2 years, means a nil claim for the buyer. The s 198 figure cannot exceed the seller's original cost. [ss 187A, 198]
22. **Long funding lease:** a term of 7 years or less is a short lease, not an LFL. For an LFL the *lessee* claims and the lessor does not. [ss 70A, 70I]
23. **Connected-party P&M purchase:** no AIA or FYA, and qualifying expenditure is capped at the seller's disposal value. On a trade succession between connected parties, the s 266 election (2 years) transfers at TWDV. [ss 217, 218, 266–267]
24. **Business entertainment use of plant** (for example a corporate hospitality boat) is non-qualifying use. [s 269]
25. **SDLT group relief is denied up-front** if there are arrangements for the purchaser to leave the group, or for outside consideration. It **claws back** if the purchaser leaves within 3 years, charged on market value at the original date. The stamp duty share-sale exit itself costs 0.5%. [FA 2003 Sch 7 paras 2–3]
26. **SDLT connected company rule:** a transfer to a connected company is at market value even if it is a gift, unless a relief such as group relief applies. [FA 2003 s 53]
27. **Stamp duty s 77 needs consideration of shares *only*** (no cash or loan notes) and a mirror-image shareholding. SDLT acquisition relief allows cash up to 10% of the shares' nominal value and has a 0.5% cap. [FA 1986 s 77; FA 2003 Sch 7 para 8]
