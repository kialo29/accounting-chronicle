## 10. Teaching notes (why the rules exist; story material)

**Corporate interest restriction.** HMRC's manual frames the rule as the UK's implementation of OECD BEPS Action 4 ("Limiting Base Erosion Involving Interest Deductions and Other Financial Payments"; interim report 2014, final October 2015, update December 2016). The Action 4 executive summary, quoted at CFM95130, opens: "It is an empirical matter of fact that money is mobile and fungible", and identifies three risks: more third-party debt in high-tax countries, intragroup loans producing deductions above the group's real third-party interest, and debt funding tax-exempt income. Each piece of CIR answers one of those: the 30% fixed ratio caps leverage against taxable earnings; the debt cap stops UK deductions exceeding the group's real external interest; tax-EBITDA counts only UK-taxed earnings. The de minimis (£2m) and the group ratio (for highly leveraged sectors whose real third-party gearing is high) are the safety valves. CIR replaced the worldwide debt cap from 1 April 2017 (CFM95110). The FA 2026 reporting company changes are an administrative-burden fix: groups were losing elections because of the old 12-month notice deadline (policy paper, 26 November 2025: "simplify administration"). Sources: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/corporate-finance-manual/cfm95120 ; /cfm95130.

**Loan relationships: follow the accounts.** Since FA 1996 the code has taxed debt as income following GAAP, removing the capital/revenue distinction for corporate debt. Two pressures followed: schemes engineered accounting results (*Fidex*, accounting-transition; *Greene King*, interest strips; *Union Castle* for derivatives), and Parliament answered with the unallowable purpose rule and targeted TAARs. The connected-party rules exist because a group controls both sides of a debt: forcing amortised cost and denying impairment and release adjustments stops a group from creating a deduction in one company with no matching income in another. The corporate rescue exceptions (2015 onwards) exist because taxing a distressed company on the release of debts it cannot pay would defeat rescues.

**Unallowable purpose: the 2024 trilogy.** In 2024 the Court of Appeal decided *BlackRock* (11 April), *Kwik-Fit* (3 May) and *JTI* (13 June), all for HMRC on s 441, and the Supreme Court refused permission in all three on the same list (13 October 2024). Common threads: the company's purpose can be the purpose of the group's plan in which it was placed (*BlackRock*: LLC5's "sole raison d'être" was to take the loans to obtain tax advantages for the group, per Falk LJ as quoted by Nugee LJ at [192]); a tax advantage for **another person** counts (s 442(5)); and on apportionment the "but for" test from *Fidex* (2016: "But for this tax avoidance scheme there would have been no debit at all") can attribute all the debits to the unallowable purpose even where there is a commercial acquisition (*JTI*: the tax advantage was "bolted on"). *Kwik-Fit* adds a time dimension: once the target losses were used, HMRC accepted continued denial would not be just and reasonable. *BlackRock* is also a transfer pricing case, and the taxpayer **won** on TP: a useful contrast for students (one rule can fail while another bites). *Syngenta* (FTT 2024, HMRC won; UT permission granted October 2025) is the live case to watch.

**Intangibles: from 2002 to the goodwill saga.** The regime began on 1 April 2002 and taxes intangibles as income following the accounts. Relief for goodwill and customer-related intangibles was withdrawn for acquisitions from **8 July 2015** (the date written into s 879D) and partly restored from **1 April 2019** at a fixed 6.5%, but only where the business acquisition includes qualifying IP and only up to six times the IP spend (ss 879B, 879M). Related-individual acquisitions (incorporations) remain blocked (s 879K). The 2020 pre-FA 2002 change and the FA 2026 TP alignment both show the regime being bent towards arm's length outcomes on cross-border and related-party transfers.

**R&D: the merged scheme.** The policy rationale on GOV.UK: "to support and incentivise R&D, helping overcome a market failure (positive externality) which causes underinvestment in R&D". The merged scheme (APs from 1 April 2024) combined the old RDEC and SME schemes; the lower 19% notional tax rate at step 2 for small-profit and loss-making companies is the main design concession to SMEs (CIRD112100). The overseas restriction and the claim notification and additional information requirements are the anti-fraud and "UK benefit" responses to the error and fraud figures already in the TKS bible (§3.13).

**Losses: the 2017 bargain.** Relaxation (carried-forward losses usable against total profits and surrenderable to the group) was paid for with restriction (50% above £5m) and the tougher change-in-ownership windows (5 years, 8 years). Osborne's Budget 2016 speech and the TIIN of 5 December 2016 are in the TKS sources (ch 22 notes). For LCG the story moves to groups: the single group allowance needs a nomination, and pre-acquisition losses of a joiner are fenced for 5 years.

**Large business governance.** SAO (FA 2009, from financial years beginning on or after 21 July 2009), tax strategy (FA 2016 Sch 19), UTT (FA 2022, returns from 1 April 2022) and the corporate criminal offence (30 September 2017) form a ladder of accountability: a named individual certifies systems; the board publishes an attitude to tax; uncertain positions over £5m are flagged; and the company itself is criminally liable for failing to prevent facilitation of evasion. The 2026 UTT consultation shows the ladder still being extended.

## 11. Traps and examiner favourites (with evidence)

1. **CIR de minimis is a floor on capacity, not an allowance on top.** Interest capacity is the higher of (allowance + available b/f allowance) and £2m (s 392). A group with £2.5m net interest and tiny EBITDA loses £0.5m, not nothing.
2. **The debt cap bites groups with low external gearing.** Fixed ratio allowance = lower of 30% tax-EBITDA and the fixed ratio debt cap (s 397); intragroup-funded UK subsidiaries of lowly geared groups are restricted by the debt cap even below 30%.
3. **Interest income increases the allowance** (s 396(1) B) but net interest expense is computed first; do not double count.
4. **Abbreviated returns kill carried-forward allowance** unless replaced by a full return (s 393(5); GOV.UK guidance).
5. **FA 2026 reporting company rules:** "more than half" (not "at least 50%") of eligible companies; no time limit; an IRR is optional unless the group needs to allocate, carry forward, reactivate or elect; a purported filer can be appointed afterwards; £1,000 penalty if not appointed within 18 months (Sch 7A paras 1, 1A, 11A, 11B). Old rules still govern periods ending on or before 30 March 2026.
6. **Disallowed interest has no time limit; unused allowance expires after 5 years.** Do not mix them up (ss 378, 395).
7. **Default disallowance order hits non-trading loan relationship debits first** (s 377), which matters for NTLR deficits and for Part 7ZA streaming.
8. **Tax-EBITDA excludes capital allowances and R&D credits**: full expensing does not reduce tax-EBITDA, and RDEC does not increase it (s 407).
9. **Connected party: no bad debt relief for the creditor, no release credit for the debtor** (ss 354, 358): symmetry. But buying a connected-to-be company's debt cheaply triggers a **deemed release** taxed on the debtor (s 361) unless equity-for-debt or the 60-day corporate rescue exception applies.
10. **Late interest is now narrow:** only close-company participator cases (and pension scheme loans); where the creditor is a company, only if it is in a non-qualifying territory (s 375(4A)). Connected UK companies accrue interest normally.
11. **Unallowable purpose is per loan relationship per period and apportioned**, and a group's tax advantage counts. The 2024 trilogy closed the "commercial acquisition so interest must be allowed" argument (*JTI*).
12. **NTLR deficit carry-back is only against NTLR profits** of the previous 12 months; carry-forward is against total profits (post-2017) with a 2-year claim (ss 463E, 463G).
13. **IFA degrouping charge is switched off by SSE-qualifying share sales** (s 782A), mirroring TCGA s 179 for gains.
14. **Goodwill 6.5%: three restrictions** (pre-2019 assets; no qualifying IP in the deal; related individuals) and the **6× IP cap** (ss 879C–879O). Customer lists and unregistered trade marks are "relevant assets" too.
15. **4% election:** 2 years after the end of the AP of acquisition, irrevocable, all capitalised expenditure on that asset (s 730).
16. **FA 2026: cross-border intangible transfers use the arm's length price, not market value** (s 845(4ZA)).
17. **Merged RDEC notional tax**: 25% where profits are chargeable at the main rate, **including marginal relief companies**; 19% otherwise (CIRD112100).
18. **Payments for surrendered RDEC** within a group are tax-neutral only up to the amount surrendered (FA 2026 s 31).
19. **Claim notification**: a company that last claimed more than 3 years earlier must notify within 6 months after the period of account ends, or the claim is invalid.
20. **Group deductions allowance needs a nomination**; a member allocated nothing has nil allowance (CTM05140).
21. **Terminal relief for carried-forward losses (s 45F) is unrestricted by Part 7ZA** but cannot go back into pre-1 April 2017 periods or the loss-making period itself.
22. **Change in ownership window**: any 5-year period starting up to 3 years before the change (s 673), wider 8-year window for non-trading reliefs under Ch 2A; and the gradual-change rule.
23. **Consortium relief**: 75%/5% test; ownership proportion is the **lowest** of four ratios including voting power (s 143); link company route (s 133).
24. **Liquidation versus administration**: AP ends immediately before winding up **starts** (resolution date) but immediately before the **day** administration begins (CTA 2009 ss 10, 12).
25. **Late filing penalties (FA 2026)**: statute says £200 then **£400** (not a further £400); persistent £1,000 / **£2,000**; tax-geared 10% at 18 months after the AP end, **20%** if more than 2 years after (Sch 18 paras 17, 18). GOV.UK's "another £200" wording equals the statutory £400 total.
26. **Enquiry window for group companies** that are not in a small group runs from the **filing date**, not delivery (Sch 18 para 24(6)).
27. **SAO and UTT thresholds both use £200m / £2bn but aggregate differently**: SAO aggregates UK-incorporated group companies; UTT aggregates 51% group members within the charge to CT on income; tax strategy adds the €750m CbCR route. SAO penalties are fixed £5,000 with no mitigation.
28. **UTT has two triggers in law, not three** (Sch 17 para 10); the third is only proposed (2026 consultation).
