# Chapter twenty six: Controlled foreign companies.

## A rule on trial.

On **19 September 2024** the Court of Justice of the European Union gave judgment in three joined appeals (Cases C-555/22 P, C-556/22 P and C-564/22 P). The appellants were the United Kingdom, the broadcaster ITV, and companies in the London Stock Exchange group. The defendant was the European Commission. The subject was a single exemption in the UK's rules on controlled foreign companies: the exemption for group finance companies.

Five years earlier, on **2 April 2019** (Decision (EU) 2019/1352), the Commission had decided that part of that exemption was unlawful State aid. HMRC had been made to collect the money from the groups that had used it. The General Court had upheld the Commission on **8 June 2022** (Cases T-363/19 and T-456/19). Now the Court of Justice set that judgment aside and annulled the Commission's decision. *(The EU dates and case numbers come from the Court's press release as reproduced by professional sources; the British steps that followed are in UK statute and are verified. We meet them later in the chapter.)*

The legal error was a choice of baseline. To call a tax rule State aid, you must show that it departs from the normal rules. The Commission had treated the CFC rules themselves as the normal rules, and the finance company exemption as a departure from them. The Court of Justice held that the right reference framework was the UK's corporation tax system as a whole. Look at the whole system, and a rule that chooses not to tax some foreign profits is not a gift: it is a decision about where the UK's taxing reach should stop.

That is the question underneath this chapter. A UK company owns a subsidiary abroad. The subsidiary is a separate person, resident in another country, taxed there. Its profits are not the parent's profits. **When, if ever, should the UK tax them? And if it does, on whom, and how much?**

This is chapter 26, in Part Four (profits that cross borders). Controlled foreign companies are **core (grade 1)** on the LCG Advanced Technical paper, and the examiners set full 20-mark CFC questions in May 2024 and May 2025. *The Living Law* (TKS), chapter 26, met the choice between a branch and a subsidiary abroad and the exemption for foreign dividends, and only signposted these rules. Chapter 25 of this book took the branch side of that choice; here we take the subsidiary side to Advanced Technical depth. Throughout, the law is that for **FY2026**, under Finance Act 2026.

The rules are in **TIOPA 2010 Part 9A** (ss 371AA–371VJ), "the International Act" in the audio edition. "CTA 2010" is the Corporation Tax Act 2010.

## Why the rules exist.

Every growing group faces the choice. A UK company that sells abroad can do it through a **branch** or through a **subsidiary**. Chapter 25 showed that a branch is part of the UK company, so its profits are UK profits unless the company elects for the branch exemption (CTA 2009 s 18A). A subsidiary is its own taxpayer. If it is resident abroad, the UK taxes nothing it earns abroad; it taxes the parent only on what comes back, and since **1 July 2009** most dividends that come back are exempt (CTA 2009 Part 9A).

That is a sensible design for genuine foreign business. A factory in Vallaria (an invented country, home of our story's main foreign subsidiary) should pay Vallarian tax on Vallarian profits. But the design opens a door. Suppose a UK group moves money, a valuable patent or a profitable contract into a subsidiary in a country with a very low tax rate, with few people and no reason to exist except the rate. Profits that used to arise in the UK now arise there. That is not a foreign business. It is UK profit wearing a foreign coat.

A controlled foreign company regime looks through the coat. Where a non-UK company is controlled from the UK and some of its profits look as though they belong in the UK, the regime taxes those profits on the UK companies that own it.

**The old regime (1984 to 2012).** The UK first introduced CFC rules in **1984** (later ICTA 1988 Part 17 Ch 4, ss 747–756). Broadly, the old regime cast a wide net over controlled companies taxed at a low level abroad and then released those that passed one of a set of exclusions. HMRC's manual (INTM254220) lists them: the **acceptable distribution policy** (paying broadly 90% of net chargeable profits home as dividends within 18 months: INTM254610), the **exempt activities** test, the **de minimis** exclusion (£50,000, pro-rated), the **excluded countries** regulations, the **motive test**, and a territorial exclusion. The ADP exemption was abolished for periods beginning on or after **1 July 2009**, when the dividend exemption arrived, because a dividend home no longer produced UK tax.

**Europe.** In *Cadbury Schweppes plc v IRC* (Case C-196/04), judgment of the Grand Chamber on **12 September 2006** on a reference from the Special Commissioners, the Court of Justice held that rules of this kind restrict freedom of establishment and can be justified only where they target **"wholly artificial arrangements"** intended to escape the national tax normally payable. A subsidiary actually established in another member state and carrying on genuine economic activities there could not be taxed this way, even if tax had been a reason for putting it there. The Court pointed to objective factors that third parties can verify, such as premises, staff and equipment.

The UK statute did not say that. In *Vodafone 2 v HMRC*, the High Court (Evans-Lombe J, [2008] EWHC 1569 (Ch), 4 July 2008) held that no conforming interpretation was possible, so the rules had to be disapplied for EU subsidiaries. The Court of Appeal reversed that on **22 May 2009** (*Vodafone 2 v HMRC* [2009] EWCA Civ 446; the Chancellor of the High Court, Longmore and Goldring LJJ): the legislation could be read as containing an additional exception for a CFC actually established in another member state and carrying on genuine economic activities there. Vodafone 2 Ltd's Luxembourg subsidiary had held the Mannesmann shares bought in 2000 (secondary summaries of the judgment). The rules survived, but with a patch sewn on by the courts.

A regime that caught too much and then excused too much was ready for replacement.

## The twenty twelve answer.

**FA 2012 s 180 and Sch 20** inserted Part 9A into TIOPA 2010 and repealed the old rules. The new regime applies to **CFC accounting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2013** (FA 2012 Sch 20 paras 49–50; a company already a CFC on that date began a new-regime AP then; a straddling old-regime period ran on to its end). Its starting idea is the reverse of the old one: instead of catching everything and excusing some, it aims to tax only profits **artificially diverted** from the UK, and it defines them by rule.

The defining device is the **gateway**. Picture a sieve over the UK tax base. A foreign subsidiary's profits are poured in; most fall away, because they belong where they were earned; only profits that belong in the UK drop through. Beside the sieve are doors: a company that passes through a door walks past the sieve entirely.

**The precise rule.** Under s 371BA(2) a CFC charge arises for a CFC's AP only if (a) the CFC has **chargeable profits** and (b) **none of the entity exemptions** (Chapters 10–14) applies. Chargeable profits are the CFC's **assumed taxable total profits** (ATTP: profits computed as if the CFC were UK resident, under the "CT assumptions" in Ch 19, ss 371SB–371SR) so far as they pass through the gateway (s 371BA(3), s 371BB). The doors are the entity exemptions; the sieve is the gateway.

**The five charging steps (s 371BC).**

| Step | What happens | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify the relevant persons and their **relevant interests**; stop if none is a UK resident company | Ch 15 |
| 2 | Compute the CFC's **creditable tax** | Ch 16, s 371PA |
| 3 | **Apportion** chargeable profits and creditable tax | Ch 17 |
| 4 | Identify the **chargeable companies** (≥ 25% with connected and associated persons) | s 371BD |
| 5 | Charge = appropriate rate × P% × chargeable profits − Q% × creditable tax, as CT of the chargeable company for its AP **in which the CFC's AP ends** | s 371BC(1), (3)–(4) |

The **appropriate rate** is the main rate for the chargeable company's AP (averaged if the AP straddles a change): **25% for FY2026**.

**The theory of the gateway.** The regime borrows its central test from the OECD's **2010 Report on the Attribution of Profits to Permanent Establishments** (the AOA): where are the **significant people functions** (SPFs), the people who take the decisions about assets and risks? If they are in the UK, the profits they generate belong to the UK (s 371DA(3)). *(The OECD report was not opened for this book; the statute's reference to it was.)*

## Who is a controlled foreign company.

A **CFC** is a company that is **not UK resident** and is **controlled by a UK resident person or persons** (s 371AA(3)). The controllers need not be companies, although only companies are charged. The Part does not displace the ordinary charge on a non-resident company's UK permanent establishment (s 371AA(11)).

| Test | Rule | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Legal control | Power, through shares, votes or the articles, to secure that C's affairs are conducted as P wishes | s 371RB(1) |
| Economic control | P would receive **> 50%** of the proceeds on a sale of all the share capital, or of income distributed, or of assets on a winding up (lending banks' rights ignored) | s 371RB(2)–(6) |
| Joint control | Two or more persons together meeting either test | s 371RB(7) |
| **40% rule** | One UK and one non-UK controller: C is a CFC if the UK person holds **≥ 40%** and the non-UK person holds **≥ 40% but ≤ 55%** | s 371RC |
| Attribution | Future rights, nominees' rights and (for UK persons) rights of connected UK persons | s 371RD |
| Accounting control | P is C's parent under the relevant accounting standard, **and** the **50% condition**: at least 50% of chargeable profits would be apportioned to P and its UK subsidiaries | s 371RE |
| **> 50% investment** | A UK resident company, alone or with associated enterprises (25% investment links), directly or indirectly has **more than a 50% investment** in C | s 371RG (FA 2019 s 20(4)); CFC APs beginning on or after **1 January 2019** |

The 40% rule matters for joint ventures. Without it, it is not obvious that a company controlled jointly by a UK person and a foreign person is "controlled by UK resident persons". The rule answers that: a 50:50 venture between a UK group and a French group is a CFC (UK 50% ≥ 40%; partner 50% ≤ 55%); a company run by a foreign partner with 60% is not.

The > 50% investment rule was the UK's implementation of the EU **Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive** (ATAD) control test. It remains UK law after Brexit.

**CFC residence and accounting periods.** A CFC is resident in the territory where it is liable to tax by reason of domicile, residence or place of management, with tie-breakers where there is more than one (ss 371TA–371TC). A CFC's AP begins when it becomes a CFC and ends on events such as ceasing to be one, becoming or ceasing to be liable to tax in a territory, or a relevant interest holder ceasing to have any interest; otherwise CTA 2009 s 10 applies (s 371VB). The charge falls in the chargeable company's AP in which the CFC's AP ends.

## Doors past the sieve.

The five **entity exemptions** are in Chapters 10–14. If any applies for an AP, there is no charge for that AP: no gateway, no apportionment. Test them in order and stop at the first that works.

### The exempt period (Ch 10, ss 371JA–371JG)

When a company first comes under UK control (typically on acquisition), the group gets **12 months** of grace to restructure. HMRC (INTM224200) summarises the starting conditions: the company exists, is resident outside the UK, is controlled from the UK and has at least one chargeable company, and for the preceding 12 months it had no chargeable companies and the charging condition was not met. A CFC AP that **ends** in the exempt period is exempt (INTM224175) if:

- the **subsequent period condition** (s 371JB(2)) is met: the company must not cease to be a CFC before an AP beginning after the exempt period, and the CFC charge must not apply to that following AP. The grace is for groups that use the year to put things right; and
- the **chargeable company condition** (s 371JB(3)): only original chargeable companies, or companies connected with them, benefit.

Profits of a straddling AP that arise in the exempt period are removed (s 371JE). Anti-avoidance applies to finance and IP arrangements and artificially short APs (s 371JF). Because whether the subsequent period condition is met is known only later, a return may be amended up to **12 months after the filing date** for the AP in which the first post-exempt-period CFC AP ends (s 371JG). HMRC may extend the exempt period by notice given before it ends.

> **Worked example 26.1: exempt period (labelled hypothetical, not story)**
> Suppose our invented group, Tarnmoor (introduced below), bought a Marrovian distributor on **1 March**, year end 31 December. Exempt period: 1 March to the end of the following February. First CFC AP: 1 March–31 December, ending inside the exempt period: **exempt**, provided the subsequent period condition is met. Next AP (1 January–31 December) straddles the end of the exempt period: only profits arising after the exempt period are in the frame (s 371JE).

### Excluded territories (Ch 11, ss 371KA–371KJ)

A CFC resident in a territory listed in the **Controlled Foreign Companies (Excluded Territories) Regulations 2012** (SI 2012/3024, Schedule Part 1; in force 1 January 2013) is exempt if its categories A–D income (broadly, income benefiting from exemptions, incentives, refunds or notional interest) does not exceed a **threshold: the greater of 10% of accounting profits and £50,000** (pro-rated for short APs), plus an **IP condition** and no main-purpose tax arrangement. For **Australia, Canada, France, Germany, Japan and the USA**, HMRC's manual says the income and IP conditions need not be met if residence and no-foreign-PE requirements are met (INTM225100, page dated 2016; the regulations themselves were not opened for this book: check them).

### Low profits (Ch 12, ss 371LA–371LC)

Exempt if accounting profits **or** ATTP are **≤ £50,000**; or **≤ £500,000** with **non-trading income ≤ £50,000**. All figures are **pro-rated** for APs under 12 months. Anti-avoidance: arrangements to fall within it; UK intermediary services businesses; the £50,000 limbs are unavailable where the group mismatch scheme rules (CTA 2010 Part 21B) bite.

> **Worked example 26.2: thresholds in short periods (labelled)**
>
> | | £ |
> |---|---|
> | Low profits, 9-month AP: £500,000 × 9/12 | 375,000 |
> | Non-trading income limit, 9-month AP: £50,000 × 9/12 | 37,500 |
> | Excluded territories threshold, accounting profits £300,000: greater of £30,000 and £50,000 | 50,000 |
> | Excluded territories threshold, accounting profits £2,000,000: greater of £200,000 and £50,000 | 200,000 |

## The low profit margin and the tax exemption.

### Low profit margin (Ch 13, ss 371MA–371MC)

For routine, low-margin activity (a distributor, a service centre). A CFC is exempt if its **accounting profits are no more than 10% of its relevant operating expenditure** (ROE). Two details decide almost every answer:

1. Profits are measured **before interest** (s 371MB).
2. ROE is operating expenditure in the accounts **excluding** (a) the **cost of goods purchased that are not used in the CFC's territory**, and (b) expenditure that **gives rise to income of a related person** (s 371MC). A management fee to the UK parent is the classic (b) item.

"Accounting profits" are pre-tax profits under an acceptable GAAP, ignoring exempt distributions, property business profits or losses and capital profits; Part 4 transfer pricing applies, but a difference of **£50,000 or less** is ignored (ss 371VC–371VD).

> **Worked example 26.3: low profit margin (labelled; the M25 trap)**
>
> | £ | |
> |---|---|
> | Sales | 6,000,000 |
> | Cost of goods (of which 1,500,000 shipped direct to customers outside the CFC's territory) | (4,200,000) |
> | Staff | (700,000) |
> | Management fee to UK parent | (300,000) |
> | Rent | (200,000) |
> | Other | (150,000) |
> | **Operating expenditure** | **(5,550,000)** |
> | Profit before interest | **450,000** |
> | Interest | (90,000) |
> | Profit after interest | 360,000 |
>
> | Relevant operating expenditure | £ |
> |---|---|
> | Operating expenditure | 5,550,000 |
> | Less goods not used in the territory | (1,500,000) |
> | Less expenditure giving rise to a related person's income (fee) | (300,000) |
> | **ROE** | **3,750,000** |
> | 10% of ROE | **375,000** |
>
> £450,000 > £375,000: **the exemption fails**. Wrong method: £360,000 (after interest) against £555,000 (10% of total opex) gives a false "exempt". Next: test the tax exemption, then the gateway.

### The tax exemption (Ch 14, ss 371NA–371NE)

A CFC is exempt if its **local tax amount is at least 75% of the corresponding UK tax**. The corresponding UK tax is CT on the CFC's ATTP under the CT assumptions, ignoring DTR for the local tax and less any UK income tax or CT actually suffered on that income. At 25%, the break-even local rate on an identical base is 75% × 25% = **18.75%**.

Both sides are adjusted. Local tax is tax paid in the CFC's territory on its **local chargeable profits** (excluding capital gains), **reduced** where the local base includes income not in ATTP, where ATTP deducts expenses the local system does not, and by **repayments or credits** to any person (s 371NC). Tax under **designer rate** provisions does not count (s 371ND). A company on a tax holiday has a local tax amount of nil and never passes.

> **Worked example 26.4: tax exemption with an adjustment (labelled)**
>
> | £ | |
> |---|---|
> | ATTP | 1,200,000 |
> | Corresponding UK tax at 25% | 300,000 |
> | 75% hurdle | **225,000** |
> | Local tax: 18% × local profits 1,300,000 | 234,000 |
> | Less 18% × 100,000 of local income not in ATTP | (18,000) |
> | **Local tax amount** | **216,000** (72%) |
>
> **Not exempt.** The unadjusted comparison (234,000 / 300,000 = 78%) would wrongly exempt it.

The five doors share a logic: each is Parliament's advance judgement that a whole company is unlikely to be a vehicle for diverted UK profit: a new acquisition, a company in a respectable system, a small company, a low-margin company, a company paying proper tax at home.

## Which gateway chapters apply.

The gateway has two stages (s 371BB). **Chapter 3** decides which of Chapters 4–8 apply; each applicable chapter then decides how much passes. If none applies, nothing passes: no chargeable profits.

### Chapter 4: profits attributable to UK activities

Ch 4 applies **unless** any one of Conditions A–D is met (s 371CA):

| Condition | Met if... |
|---|---|
| **A** | The CFC holds no assets and bears no risks under arrangements a **main purpose** of which is to reduce UK tax and which are expected to make it more profitable (and would not have been made but for the expected tax reduction) |
| **B** | The CFC holds **no UK-managed** assets or risks |
| **C** | The CFC itself has the **capability** to ensure its business would be commercially effective if UK management stopped |
| **D** | Its assumed total profits consist **only** of non-trading finance profits and/or property business profits |

A genuine foreign trading company run by local management usually escapes Ch 4 through A or B.

**The eight steps (s 371DB)** where Ch 4 applies:

1. Identify the CFC's assets and risks.
2. Exclude negligible ones.
3. Identify the SPFs relevant to them, treating the **CFC group as one company**.
4. Split UK and non-UK SPFs; **stop if there are no UK SPFs**.
5. Hypothesise a UK PE carrying on the UK SPFs; attribute to it the assets and risks they manage.
6. Exclude assets and risks where UK activity is the minority (s 371DC: amount A ≤ 50% of amount B).
7. Recompute the CFC's profits without the PE-attributed assets and risks ("provisional Chapter 4 profits"); the difference passes...
8. ...subject to the exclusions in ss 371DD–371DF.

**The exclusions:** **economic value** (arrangements create substantial non-tax value: s 371DD); **independent companies** (independent parties would have made the same arrangements on the same terms: s 371DE); and the **trading profits safe harbour** (s 371DF), all five conditions:

| Condition | Test | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Business premises | Premises in its territory with a reasonable degree of permanence; activities wholly or mainly from there (building sites only if ≥ 12 months) | s 371DG |
| Income | ≤ **20%** of relevant trading income from UK residents or UK PEs (banks ≤ 10%) | s 371DH |
| Management expenditure | UK-related management expenditure ≤ **20%** of total (or the 50% per-asset alternative) | s 371DI |
| IP | No significant IP transferred from UK related persons in the AP or the **previous 6 years** | s 371DJ |
| Export of goods | ≤ **20%** of trading income from goods exported from the UK (other than to the CFC's territory) | s 371DK |

Anti-avoidance stops reorganisations designed to meet the tests (s 371DL). The safe harbour turns on facts a group can see and manage, so that a well-run foreign trading company need not commission a people-functions study every year.

## Finance profits.

Money is the easiest thing to move, so it has its own chapters.

### Chapter 5: non-trading finance profits (NTFP)

Ch 5 applies where the CFC has NTFP (s 371CB), but NTFP from funds held for the purposes of the CFC's own trade or property business are left out, unless the funds are held for something else (future dividends, acquisitions, contingencies, or a tax-reduction purpose).

**The 5% incidental rule (s 371CC):** if the CFC has trading or property profits, Ch 5 does **not** apply where its NTFP are **≤ 5%** of its trading and property profits (before interest and tax). A similar rule protects mainly-holding companies with exempt distribution income (s 371CD).

> **Worked example 26.5: the 5% rule (labelled)**
> Trading profits before interest and tax £3,000,000; NTFP £140,000. 5% = £150,000. £140,000 ≤ £150,000: **Ch 5 does not apply.**

**What passes where Ch 5 applies (ss 371EA–371EE):**

| Route | Catches | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| UK activities | NTFP attributable to UK SPFs (the Ch 4 test applied to finance profits) | s 371EB |
| **Capital investment from the UK** | NTFP earned on funds derived from **capital contributions by UK connected companies**, from profits already charged as CFC profits, from s 174 claims, or other funds received from UK connected companies otherwise than for goods or services or by loan (with a deduction for undercharged management costs) | s 371EC |
| Arrangements in lieu of dividends | Arrangements made instead of paying dividends to UK companies, with a tax main reason | s 371ED |
| Finance leases | Finance leases to UK connected companies in lieu of purchase | s 371EE |

Put plainly: if a UK parent puts equity into a foreign finance company and the finance company lends it on, the interest is caught by s 371EC.

### Chapters 6–8

- **Ch 6, trading finance profits** (s 371CE, s 371FA): pass only so far as they arise from **excess free capital** (more than a non-51%-subsidiary would hold) derived from UK connected capital contributions. A **group treasury company** may elect, by notice within **20 months** after the AP, to treat trading finance profits as NTFP (so that Ch 9 can apply).
- **Ch 7, captive insurance** (s 371CF, s 371GA): insurance of UK connected risks.
- **Ch 8, solo consolidation** (s 371CG, s 371HA): bank capital structures. Recognise; rarely examined.

## The finance company exemptions.

Chapter 9 (ss 371IA–371IJ) is where the State aid saga began, and where Tarnmoor's CFC lives.

**Entry conditions (s 371IA):** Ch 5 applies; the NTFP include profits of **qualifying loan relationships** (QLRs); and the CFC meets the **business premises condition** (s 371DG). A chargeable company may then **claim** (in its CT return; s 371IJ).

**QLR (ss 371IG–371IH):** a creditor relationship whose **ultimate debtor** is a **qualifying company** (connected with the CFC and controlled by the same UK persons). It is **not** a QLR if the debtor's debits reduce the profits of a **UK PE** or a UK property business, if the debtor is a **UK company** without a s 18A election, or if they reduce another CFC's chargeable profits.

**QLR profits (s 371IA(4))** are QLR profits within **s 371EC and not within s 371EB**: since **FA 2019 s 20(2)**, for CFC APs beginning on or after **1 January 2019**, profits from UK SPFs cannot be exempted.

| Exemption | Rule | Effect at 25% | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Full exemption** | X% exempt if X% of the principal is funded **wholly out of qualifying resources** (e.g. profits from lending to group companies in the debtor's territory used there; funds from shares in group members derived from local business profits, pre-acquisition funds or external non-redeemable ordinary share issues); debtor resident in one territory | Nil | ss 371IB, 371IC |
| **75% exemption** | Where the full exemption is not claimed for a QLR: **75%** of its profits exempt | 25% passes: 25% × 25% = **6.25%** before credit | s 371ID |
| **Matched interest** | Remaining QLR profits ("matched interest profits") exempt so far as the UK share exceeds the worldwide group's **aggregate net tax-interest expense** (CIR, TIOPA Part 10) | Nil if ANTIE is nil; nothing if ANTIE is large | s 371IE |

The current s 371IE (substituted by F(No.2)A 2017 for worldwide group periods of account beginning on or after 1 April 2017): if ANTIE is **nil**, matched interest profits are **fully exempt** (s 371IE(3)); otherwise, where the **relevant proportion** (each relevant chargeable company's step 5 percentage × matched interest profits, summed: s 371IE(5)) exceeds ANTIE, the **excess** is exempt (s 371IE(4)). HMRC's example (INTM219380): matched interest profits 25, ANTIE 9: **16 exempt**. ANTIE for this purpose ignores banking and insurance amounts (s 371IE(8)). *(Statute and INTM219380 seen in search extracts; the older INTM219250 formula predates 2017 and should not be used.)*

> **Going further: designing a finance company that works**
> - **Premises and people first.** Ch 9 needs the business premises condition, and since FA 2019 only profits outside s 371EB qualify. A finance company whose credit decisions are taken in the UK has UK SPFs: those profits pass through s 371EB and cannot be exempted. Local directors with real authority, local staff who assess and monitor the loans, and minutes that show it are the substance.
> - **Choose the borrowers.** Loans to foreign group companies controlled by the same UK persons qualify; loans to UK companies (without a s 18A election), to borrowers whose interest reduces UK PE or UK property profits, or to other CFCs whose chargeable profits it would reduce, do not.
> - **Funding.** Equity from the UK puts the profits inside s 371EC (that is why Ch 9 is needed at all). Funding from qualifying resources (local profits of the debtor's territory, pre-acquisition funds, external ordinary share issues) can unlock the full exemption: track the source of funds loan by loan.
> - **Matched interest.** Only useful to groups whose UK net interest expense is small; check ANTIE each year (chapter 28).
> - **The price of a claim.** The 25% that passes still raises the parent's s 105(3A) threshold; model that with the group relief plan.
> - **Trading groups.** For a foreign trading subsidiary, aim for Ch 3 Condition A or B (no tax-motivated arrangements; no UK-managed assets or risks), and keep the safe harbour's five facts (premises, UK income, UK management expenditure, UK IP, UK exports) inside the limits.

> - **Grade:** core.
> - **Past appearances:** N23 Q1(c): a finance company paying 1% tax taken through the NTFP gateway (good answers; some strayed into DPT and CIR). M24 Q5: "Gateways weaker than exemptions".
> - **What earns marks:** Ch 3 Conditions A–D and why Ch 4 is or is not switched off; the 5% rule; **s 371EC** (UK equity); the Ch 9 claim with the **business premises condition**; 75% exemption arithmetic (6.25%); QLR exclusions (UK debtor; UK PE).

## From chargeable profits to the charge.

Chargeable profits are not yet tax. Two thresholds must be kept apart: **control** decides whether the foreign company is a CFC; the **25% test** decides which UK companies pay. The examiners reported after **M24** that candidates "confused the 25% apportionment threshold with the control tests".

**Relevant interests (s 371OC).** A UK resident company's interest is relevant **except** an indirect interest held **through another UK resident company**: the charge sits at the **lowest UK company** in the chain.

**Apportionment (ss 371QC–371QG).** Where all relevant persons hold only ordinary shares of one class (and other conditions hold), apportion by **P% of the ordinary shares**; indirect holdings multiply through the chain (s 371QE); holdings that vary are time-weighted (s 371QF). Otherwise, just and reasonable; HMRC may substitute its own basis (s 371UC); anti-avoidance apportionment (s 371QG).

> **Worked example 26.6: chains and time-weighting (labelled)**
> - Indirect: UK plc holds 80% of X (non-UK), which holds 70% of the CFC: P% = 80% × 70% = **56%**.
> - Time-weighted: 60% for 146 days then 100% for 219 days (365-day AP): P% = 60% × 146/365 + 100% × 219/365 = **84%**.

**Chargeable companies (s 371BD).** A UK resident company is chargeable only if the percentage apportioned to it **plus** the percentages apportioned to persons **connected or associated** with it is **at least 25%**. (Exclusions for certain offshore fund managers and participants: ss 371BE–371BG.)

**Creditable tax (s 371PA)** is the sum of: (a) the DTR that would be given to the CFC (on the assumptions) for **foreign tax** on income **included in its chargeable profits**; (b) UK income tax deducted at source on that income (CTA 2010 s 967); (c) UK income tax or CT actually charged on it; (d) other countries' **CFC charges**. Amounts repaid are excluded. It is apportioned like the profits.

> **Worked example 26.7: apportionment and the 25% test (labelled)**
> FinCo (territory rate 5%) lends to a fellow subsidiary; interest **£2,000,000**; funded by UK equity; business premises; no UK SPFs; no entity exemption (5% local tax is 20% of UK tax). Shareholders: UK plc 80%, an unconnected UK company 15%, a non-UK investor 5%.
>
> | £ | |
> |---|---|
> | NTFP through s 371EC | 2,000,000 |
> | 75% exemption (s 371ID) | (1,500,000) |
> | **Chargeable profits** | **500,000** |
> | Creditable tax: 5% × 500,000 (just and reasonable attribution assumed) | 25,000 |
>
> | Holder | % | 25% test | Charge £ |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | UK plc | 80 | 80% ≥ 25%: chargeable | 25% × 80% × 500,000 − 80% × 25,000 = 100,000 − 20,000 = **80,000** |
> | Unconnected UK company | 15 | 15% with no connected holders: **not** chargeable | nil |
> | Non-UK investor | 5 | outside the charge | nil |
>
> Without the Ch 9 claim: 25% × 80% × 2,000,000 − 80% × 100,000 = **£320,000**.
> **Variant:** if the 15% holder were connected with UK plc, its own 15% plus UK plc's 80% would pass the 25% test, and it too would be chargeable on its 15%.

**Three consequences.**
1. **No reliefs against the charge.** s 371UD (relief against the sum charged) was **omitted by F(No.2)A 2015 s 36** for CFC APs beginning on or after **8 July 2015**. Losses, management expenses and group relief cannot reduce it.
2. **The profit-related threshold.** CFC chargeable profits apportioned to a company under step 3 (where it is a chargeable company at step 4) are added to its **profit-related threshold** for surrendering management expenses and other s 99(1)(d)–(g) amounts as group relief (CTA 2010 s 105(3A)): chapter 13.
3. **It is corporation tax** of the chargeable company: returns, enquiries, assessments, interest and penalties all apply (s 371UA).

**The misconception: "every low-taxed foreign subsidiary is caught".** People believe it because the old regime was built that way and because low tax is what the rules are about. But the modern regime asks three questions before anyone pays: does an **entity exemption** apply; do any profits pass the **gateway**; does a UK company hold **at least 25%**? A low-taxed subsidiary that trades with real local management, earns a low margin, or is simply small pays nothing. A highly taxed one never gets past the tax exemption.

## Tarnmoor's annual review.

Now our invented group. **Tarnmoor plc (TPLC)** is a widely held, London-listed engineering group; everything about it, its companies, its people and the countries **Vallaria** and **Marrovia**, is invented for teaching. Every year its head of tax, **Tom Hesketh**, runs a CFC review of each overseas subsidiary before the group's return is prepared. We join the review for **GY5**, running the numbers on FY2026 rules.

## The State aid saga.

The finance company exemption Tom has just claimed is the rule that was on trial.

The 75% exemption was a policy choice. On the usual account of the policy, the government wanted the UK to be an attractive headquarters location, and international groups finance their foreign operations through finance companies; taxing only a quarter of such a company's profits was meant to be competitive without inviting abuse.

| Date | Event | Source status |
|---|---|---|
| 2 April 2019 | Commission Decision (EU) 2019/1352 (SA.44896): the group financing exemption was partly unlawful State aid (2013–2018) where finance profits derived from UK SPFs | secondary |
| 1 January 2019 | FA 2019 s 20(2) had already narrowed Ch 9 for later periods (profits within s 371EB excluded) | verified |
| After the decision | HMRC recovered the aid by **charging notices** and **interest charging notices** under TIOPA 2010 **Sch 7ZA** (inserted by the Taxation (Post-transition Period) Act 2020 Sch 4) | verified |
| 8 June 2022 | General Court (T-363/19 *UK v Commission*, T-456/19 *ITV v Commission*) upheld the Commission | secondary |
| 11 April 2024 | Advocate General Medina: the Commission chose the wrong reference framework | secondary |
| 19 September 2024 | Court of Justice (C-555/22 P, C-556/22 P, C-564/22 P): General Court judgment set aside; **Commission decision annulled**: the CFC rules were not the correct reference framework; the UK CT system as a whole was | secondary (press release as reproduced) |
| 31 December 2024 | **Controlled Foreign Companies (Reversal of State Aid Recovery) Regulations 2024** (SI 2024/1307) in force: HMRC must give reversal notices cancelling charging notices and cancel interest charging notices; consequential adjustments within 12 months after the AP of the notice | verified |
| 2 December 2025 (deemed) | **FA 2026 s 51**: interest (at the ICTA 1988 s 826 rate) on repayments of interest made because interest charging notices were cancelled | verified |

Selectivity, the core of a State aid finding, is judged against the normal tax system. The CFC rules were not a free-standing system: they were part of UK corporation tax, which by design does not tax most profits of foreign subsidiaries. Because the Commission started from the wrong baseline, its selectivity finding could not stand.

Two lessons outlast the saga. Whether an exemption is a gift depends on what you compare it with (the same question runs through the debate on territoriality and on the arm's length principle). And rules shaped by EU law remain in UK statute after Brexit: some disputes that began in Brussels end in Westminster.

## Running the regime.

**Returns.** Each chargeable company self-assesses the CFC charge in its own CT return, with the supplementary pages **CT600B** ("Controlled foreign companies and foreign permanent establishment exemptions, hybrid and other mismatches"), which summarise each CFC and show how the charge is computed. The charge is CT of the chargeable company (s 371UA): returns, enquiries, assessments, appeals, interest and penalties apply. **Instalments:** HMRC's manual includes "tax charged on controlled foreign companies" in the total liability that a very large company pays by instalments (CTM92825). *(Whether a parent with no taxable profits of its own is "large" for the instalment rules is a separate test on its own profits; this book does not resolve it for TPLC.)*

**Positive actions and deadlines.**

| Item | Rule | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Ch 9 exemptions | Claimed by the chargeable company in its return | s 371IA, s 371IJ |
| Group treasury election (TFP as NTFP) | Notice within **20 months** after the CFC's AP | s 371CE |

> **Going further: the tax function's annual cycle**
> - **Register:** every overseas company; control position and date; residence; APs; exemption or gateway conclusion each year.
> - **Timing:** review **before** the year end where possible: substance is easier to fix in advance than to prove afterwards.
> - **Evidence:** board minutes held locally, staff contracts, office leases, credit papers showing who took the lending decisions.
> - **Acquisitions:** check CFC status on completion day: the exempt period clock starts then; diarise the 12-month restructuring plan.
> - **Interaction memo:** pass the s 105(3A) apportionment to the group relief team; pass CFC tax to the Pillar Two team (push-down, chapter 30); flag any loan to a UK borrower (QLR exclusion; hybrids; TP).
> - **Ethics and judgement:** planning is legitimate when it follows real activity: put real people and decisions where the profits are meant to be; design finance companies so their loans qualify and their SPFs are genuinely local. Manufacturing the appearance of foreign management while decisions are taken in the UK is exactly what the gateway catches, and the same facts raise residence questions (chapter 22) and disclosure duties (chapter 4).

## Neighbouring regimes.

**Transfer pricing.** A CFC's accounting profits must reflect arm's length pricing (difference ≤ £50,000 ignored: s 371VD). In computing ATTP the CFC is assumed UK resident, which might seem to let it use the new **UK-to-UK exemption** (s 164A). **FA 2026 Sch 6 paras 16(3), 32(1)** inserted **s 371SD(5A)**: the residence assumption is ignored for s 164A, so a CFC **cannot** use it, for chargeable periods commencing on or after **1 January 2026**. Chapter 27.

**The interest restriction.** Ch 9's matched interest test borrows CIR's ANTIE; and a finance company's lending to foreign group companies is often the other side of debt pushed into higher-tax countries. TVS's interest to TCM is outside the UK interest rules altogether (TVS is not within the charge to UK CT). Chapter 28.

**Hybrids.** In GY4 Tarnmoor rejected **Project Undertow**, under which TFL (the UK treasury company) would have issued perpetual notes to TCM (chapter 29). For this chapter: a loan to a **UK** borrower is not a QLR, so interest flowing from the UK into a CFC finds no Ch 9 shelter.

**The global minimum tax.** Tarnmoor's revenue is far above €750m, so F(No.2)A 2023 applies. Under **s 179** (as amended by FA 2024 Sch 12), a parent's tax under a **CFC tax regime** (including the UK CFC charge) is **allocated to the CFC** in computing that jurisdiction's effective rate, with the allocation for passive or **mobile income** capped at (15% − the local ETR) × that income; for domestic top-up tax, the cap is 15% of the CFC's adjusted profits (FA 2026 Sch 8 para 38). TCM: Marrovian tax £459,000 + CFC charge £204,000 (within the cap of 6% × £5.1m = £306,000) = £663,000 on £5.1m = **13.0%**: still below 15%, so chapter 30 finds a top-up (simplified, about £102,000). **Open point:** whether a foreign **qualified domestic minimum top-up tax** counts as "local tax" for the Ch 14 tax exemption (s 371NB) or as creditable tax (s 371PA) was not found in HMRC's CFC guidance (INTM226150 last updated 2018; INTM230100 does not address it): treat it as unsettled.

## What to take away.

- A **CFC** is a non-UK resident company controlled by UK resident persons: legal, economic, joint, or accounting control (50% condition); the **40% rule**; the **> 50% investment** test (from 2019).
- Part 9A (CFC APs beginning on or after **1 January 2013**) replaced the 1984 regime that *Cadbury Schweppes* (2006) and *Vodafone 2* (2009) had narrowed; it taxes only profits artificially diverted from the UK.
- A charge arises only if there are **chargeable profits** and **no entity exemption**: exempt period (12 months), excluded territories (greater of 10% and £50,000), low profits (£50,000; or £500,000 with non-trading income ≤ £50,000), low profit margin (profit before interest ≤ 10% of trimmed ROE), tax exemption (≥ 75%; 18.75% break-even at 25%, both sides adjusted). Stop at the first that works.
- The **gateway**: Ch 4 (off if any of Conditions A–D; otherwise UK SPFs; safe harbour); Ch 5 (NTFP; 5% rule; **s 371EC** capital investment from the UK); Ch 9 (business premises; full exemption from qualifying resources; **75% exemption**; matched interest against ANTIE).
- **Apportion** by shares (chains, time-weighting); charge only companies with **≥ 25%** with connected and associated persons; charge = 25% × P% × chargeable profits − Q% × creditable tax, in the AP in which the CFC's AP ends; no reliefs; apportioned profits raise the s 105(3A) threshold.
- **Tarnmoor (invented), GY5:** TCM profit £5.1m; chargeable £1,275,000; creditable tax £114,750; **charge £204,000** (GY1–GY4 £132,000 a year). TVS exempt (80%). TIL: no chargeable profits.

The question was when the UK should tax the profits of a foreign subsidiary. The law's answer is narrow: only when the subsidiary is controlled from here, escapes every exemption, earns profits that belong here, and is owned to at least 25% by a UK company.
