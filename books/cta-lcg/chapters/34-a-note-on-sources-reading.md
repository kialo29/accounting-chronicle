# A note on sources.

Every rule in this book came from somewhere. This note says where. It describes the places corporate tax law lives and how to read them, it says plainly what this book could not open while it was being written, and it suggests how to keep up once the exam is behind you. The law described throughout is the law for **FY2026**, and the facts in this note are as they stood in **October 2026**.

## The statute book.

The law itself lives on **legislation.gov.uk**, the free official site run by The National Archives. Every Act this book relies on is there:

| Act | Citation | URL pattern (section) | Short name in the audio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corporation Tax Act 2009 | 2009 c. 4 | legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2009/4/section/441 | the two thousand and nine Act |
| Corporation Tax Act 2010 | 2010 c. 4 | legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/4/section/1141 | the twenty ten Act |
| Taxation (International and Other Provisions) Act 2010 | 2010 c. 8 | legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/8/section/371BC | the International Act |
| Taxation of Chargeable Gains Act 1992 | 1992 c. 12 | legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/12/section/171 | the Gains Act |
| Capital Allowances Act 2001 | 2001 c. 2 | legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2001/2/section/51C | the Capital Allowances Act |
| Taxes Management Act 1970 | 1970 c. 9 | legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1970/9 | the Management Act |
| Finance Act 1998 Sch 18 (company returns) | 1998 c. 36 | legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1998/36/schedule/18 | the company return rules |
| Finance (No. 2) Act 2023 Parts 3–4 (Pillar Two) | 2023 c. 30 | legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/30 | the Pillar Two legislation |
| Finance Act 2026 (Royal Assent 18 March 2026) | 2026 c. 11 | legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/11 (add /enacted for the text as passed) | the Finance Act twenty twenty six |

**Revised and enacted.** The **revised** text shows the law as later Acts have amended it; the **enacted** text shows exactly what Parliament passed. You need both: the revised text tells you what a section says now; the enacted text tells you what an amending Act did and, through its commencement provisions, from when. Those provisions often sit at the end of a Schedule, far from the section they govern (for example, the UK-to-UK TP exemption's start date is in FA 2026 Sch 6 para 32: chapter 2).

**The "Changes to legislation" box.** Read it before you rely on any revised page. It gives the date up to which the text is "up to date with all changes known to be in force", and lists effects the editors have not yet applied. The site itself warns that revised legislation "may not be fully up to date". When the box lists outstanding changes, the words on your screen may not be the law.

**Point-in-time versions.** You can read a section as it stood on a chosen date; for most legislation the record starts on **1 February 1991**. Tax law applies by period, so a 31 March company and a 31 December company can be under different versions of the same section on the same day (chapter 2's three-company example on CTA 2010 s 1141).

**Statutory instruments and treaties** live on the same site: SI 1998/3175 (instalment payments), SI 2004/3256 (Disregard Regulations), SI 2004/3271 (change of accounting practice), SI 2016/237 (CbC), SI 2023/818 (TP records). Treaties reach a company's return through Orders in Council under TIOPA 2010 s 2.

## Judgments.

Court and tribunal judgments are published free by The National Archives through **Find Case Law** (caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk), launched in **April 2022**. Search by case name, neutral citation, judge, court or date. The neutral citation is the quickest key: *BlackRock HoldCo 5, LLC v HMRC* [2024] EWCA Civ 330 is at caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2024/330.

Coverage of older decisions is uneven, so for an older case you may need the court's or tribunal's own pages, or the older free archive, BAILII. Two habits matter more than the source:

1. **Read the judgment, not a summary.** The paragraph that decides your point is rarely the one a newsletter quotes.
2. **Check what happened next.** A tribunal decision can be reversed, and an appeal may still be pending (*Syngenta Holdings Ltd v HMRC* [2024] UKFTT 998 (TC), Upper Tribunal appeal pending when this book was finished).

## HMRC's manuals and guidance.

HMRC publishes its internal manuals on GOV.UK (gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals). The ones this book drew on most:

| Manual | Code | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| International Manual | INTM | Treaties, DTR, PEs, branch exemption, CFCs, transfer pricing |
| Corporate Finance Manual | CFM | Loan relationships, derivatives, the CIR |
| Company Taxation Manual | CTM | Groups, losses, instalments, distributions, demergers |
| Corporate Intangibles Research and Development Manual | CIRD | Intangibles, R&D, Patent Box |
| Capital Gains Manual | CG | Company gains, groups, SSE, reorganisations |
| Capital Allowances Manual | CA | Plant, SBA, fixtures |
| Senior Accounting Officers Guidance | SAOG | SAO |
| Tax Compliance Risk Management | TCRM | HMRC's large business risk ratings |
| Stamp Taxes on Shares Manual / SDLT Manual | STSM / SDLTM | Stamp duty, SDLT |

Each manual has an **updates** page, and many pages carry banners where the law has moved faster than the guidance (for example the INTM transfer pricing pages after FA 2026). Around the manuals sit **policy papers**, **consultation documents and outcomes**, and **tax information and impact notes (TIINs)**, which say what a measure is for and whom it affects.

**Status.** A manual is **HMRC's view, not law**. A tribunal is not bound by it, and where it conflicts with the statute, the statute wins. Where this book relied on a manual it says "HMRC's view is" or "HMRC's guidance says". But departing from HMRC's known position can, for a large business, create a duty to notify under the UTT rules (FA 2022 Sch 17; chapter 4). So the manuals are not law, and they still matter.

## The OECD's texts.

The OECD publishes the **Model Tax Convention and Commentary**, the **Transfer Pricing Guidelines** (2022 edition, published 20 January 2022), the **2010 Report on the Attribution of Profits to Permanent Establishments**, the BEPS reports of 2015 (including Action 2 on hybrids and Action 4 on interest), and the model rules and guidance behind **Pillar Two**.

For most of the code these texts are **persuasive**: in *Fowler v HMRC* [2020] UKSC 22 the Supreme Court said the Commentaries carry persuasive force "as the cogency of their reasoning deserves" (chapter 2). But for chargeable periods beginning on or after **1 January 2026**, FA 2026 writes them into UK law in three places:

| Rule | Statute | OECD text referred to | Later versions? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transfer pricing | TIOPA 2010 s 164 | Model Art 9 (approved 18 November 2025); TP Guidelines 2022 | Yes, "as amended or replaced from time to time", subject to Treasury opt-out |
| Attribution to a PE | CTA 2009 s 20 | Model Art 7 and Commentary; 2010 Attribution Report; TPG 2022; March 2018 guidance | Yes, as above |
| Definition of a PE | CTA 2010 s 1140A | Model Art 5 and Commentary (approved 18 November 2025) | Not confirmed whether later versions are followed (chapters 2, 23) |

The 2025 update to the Model was approved by the OECD Council on **18 November 2025** and published the next day; revised full editions were expected in 2026.

**In the exam** the position is simpler: you are given the **2017 articles** of the Model as an on-screen PDF, and a **supplied treaty extract beats it**.

## What this book could not open.

A source note should be honest about its gaps.

**1. The OECD's own texts.** The OECD's website could not be opened while the book was written. The book has not read the TP Guidelines, the 2025 Model and Commentary, or the 2010 Attribution Report in their full original form. Where it describes them, it relies on the statute that refers to them, HMRC's descriptions (chiefly INTM), and published summaries, and says so where the point matters. The Commentary changes approved in November 2025 (for example, new paragraphs 44.1–44.21 on Article 5 on when a home office can be a PE) are known here only from professional summaries. A reader who needs the exact words of a Guideline or Commentary paragraph should go to the OECD's own publication.

**2. Examination documents not yet published.** As this book was finished, the CIOT had published no **2027 syllabus grid**, no **2027 prospectus** and no **2027 tax tables**. Every grade in the Exam lens boxes comes from the **2026 grid**. The 2025-to-2026 grid comparison was made against the **draft** 2025 grid (January 2024), because the final 2025 grid could not be found. The **transitional rules** for the 2028 CTA changes were also unpublished.

**3. The method of checking.** For this edition, much of the verification relied on **search results that quote primary sources** (legislation.gov.uk, GOV.UK manuals, Find Case Law, CIOT pages), rather than on reading the full text of each source. A search result shows an extract, sometimes truncated. The labels follow from that:

| What was seen | How the book states it |
|---|---|
| Clear extract of statute or judgment | "The law says" / "the courts have held" |
| HMRC manual text only (statute not seen) | "HMRC's view is" / "HMRC's guidance says" |
| Secondary reports only (commentators, press) | Attributed ("commentators put the deductions at about £654m"; "as reported") |
| The book's reasoning from the sources | "On this book's reading" |

Examples to calibrate by (check each against the full text in your own legislation product before relying on it for a client):

- **Associated companies for QIPs counted on the day before the AP begins** rests on HMRC's manuals (CTM92530, COM95001); the amended text of SI 1998/3175 reg 3 was not found (chapters 1, 3).
- **Payments for a surrendered RDEC ignored for tax** rests on HMRC's description of the new rule; the full text of FA 2026 s 31 was not seen (chapter 10).
- **The 3-month window for a required revised CIR return** (TIOPA 2010 Sch 7A para 8(4)) was seen in HMRC's guidance (CFM98645), not in the statutory text itself (chapter 28).

**4. The invented case.** Everything about Tarnmoor, its people, numbers and countries (Vallaria, Marrovia) is invented. Real cases, statutes and dates are real. The invented case never stands in for a real one.

## Keeping up after the exam.

Tax law moves on an annual cycle.

| Stage | When | What to read | 2026 example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Budget | Autumn | Budget documents, policy papers, TIINs | **28 October 2026** (not yet happened as at 9 October 2026) |
| Legislation Day | Usually July | Draft clauses and technical consultation (GOV.UK collection) | **13 July 2026**: "Finance Bill 2026-27 draft legislation and technical tax documents"; consultation closed 7 September 2026 |
| Finance Bill | After the Budget | The Bill, explanatory notes | Finance Bill 2026-27 (the Budget will confirm its final contents) |
| Finance Act | Often spring | Enacted text and commencement provisions | FA 2026: Royal Assent 18 March 2026 |

Watch each measure's commencement words: "periods beginning on or after" a date is not the same as "payments made on or after" it.

**Where to look:**

- **Law:** legislation.gov.uk; the GOV.UK collection for each Finance Bill.
- **Judgments:** Find Case Law.
- **HMRC's thinking:** manual update pages; GOV.UK consultations; HMRC's Large Business pages ("HM Revenue and Customs: Large Business") and the TCRM manual on risk reviews.
- **Professional bodies:** the CIOT's *Tax Adviser* magazine (monthly, with a Technical Newsdesk section) and its weekly technical email to members; CIOT submissions and Finance Bill briefings; the ICAEW Tax Faculty's weekly newsletter **TAXwire** and monthly **TAXline**.
- **Firms:** Budget-day and Legislation-Day briefings. Use them as maps, then go back to the text.
- **International:** the OECD's announcements on Pillar Two and on the Model and Guidelines (FA 2026 now imports later versions of the TP Guidelines automatically, subject to the Treasury opt-out).
- **The exam:** the CIOT's tax tables page, syllabus and prospectus pages (2027 grid and tables), and key dates page.

And whatever the source, keep the habits this book has tried to teach: find the primary text; check the date it applies from; say whether a point is law, HMRC's view, disputed or only proposed; and when a summary arrives from someone else, or from software, read it as the 2028 learning outcome will ask, critically, against the source.

That is the end of the book. The law will keep living. You now know where to find it.
