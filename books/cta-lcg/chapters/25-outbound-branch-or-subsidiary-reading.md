# Chapter twenty five: Outbound: branch or subsidiary.

## A choice that may not last.

On 13 July 2026 HMRC published draft legislation that would take away the choice this chapter is about. For fifteen years a UK company with a branch abroad has been able to decide whether that branch's profits and losses stay inside UK corporation tax or fall outside it. The draft would make the foreign branch exemption **compulsory for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027**. A policy paper published on 21 May 2026 had explained the aim: it was directed at companies using the losses and capital allowances of foreign branches to reduce their UK profits.

That draft is **proposed, not law**. The 2027 sittings test Finance Act 2026, and under that Act the exemption is still an **election**. So the old question is still the live one, and it is one of the oldest in international tax: **when a company goes abroad, should it go as itself, through a branch, or through a new company, a subsidiary? And if it goes as itself, should it ask to have the branch taken out of the UK tax base?**

This is chapter 25, the fourth chapter of Part Four (crossing borders). Overseas branches and subsidiaries sit inside the **core (grade 1)** grid row "Company residence and chargeable profits of non-UK resident companies and concept of permanent establishment/branch". Company distributions received (foreign dividends) and unremittable income are **core**. "Transfers concerning companies of different member States" and international movements of capital are **non-core (grade 2)**. *The Living Law* (TKS), chapter 26, introduced the branch and the subsidiary, the s 18A election and its opening negative amount, the dividend exemption and s 140. Here we take each to Advanced Technical depth.

Statutes: Corporation Tax Act 2009 (**CTA 2009**: Part 2 Ch 3A, ss 18A–18S; Part 9A, ss 931A–931S; ss 173–175; Part 18, ss 1274–1278); Taxation (International and Other Provisions) Act 2010 (**TIOPA 2010**: Part 2, double tax relief); Taxation of Chargeable Gains Act 1992 (**TCGA 1992**: ss 140–140L); Finance Act 2009 s 37 and Sch 17. The law throughout is that for **FY2026**.

## Two ways to go abroad.

A UK resident company is chargeable to corporation tax on all its profits wherever they arise (CTA 2009 s 5). A **branch** is not a separate person: it is the company itself doing business in another country, so its profits are the company's profits and sit in its UK computation, whatever tax the other country also charges.

A **subsidiary** is a new company with its own legal personality. If it is incorporated and managed abroad it is not UK resident, and the UK does not tax its profits as they arise. Chapter 22 explained how residence follows central management and control: a foreign subsidiary whose real decisions are taken in Leeds can find itself resident in Leeds, defeating the purpose of creating it.

*The Living Law*'s picture: a branch is a **spare room** in your own house; a subsidiary is a **separate flat** you own. The spare room is part of the household accounts. The flat has its own meter, bills and tenancy, and money comes back to you only when the flat pays you something.

**The territorial turn.** For most of the last century the UK taxed both routes on a credit basis: branch profits as they arose, dividends from foreign subsidiaries when received, with credit for foreign tax. Then:

| Date | Change |
|---|---|
| 1 July 2009 | Foreign dividends broadly exempt (CTA 2009 Part 9A); onshore pooling and EUFT repealed; ICTA 1988 s 765 Treasury consent replaced by reporting (FA 2009 Sch 17) |
| 19 July 2011 | Foreign branch exemption election (CTA 2009 Part 2 Ch 3A, inserted by FA 2011 Sch 13) |
| 2012–2013 | Reformed CFC regime (FA 2012 Sch 20; CFC APs beginning on or after 1 January 2013); branch anti-diversion rule aligned with it |
| 2026 | FA 2026: s 18S updated to the OECD Model approved on 18 November 2025 (otherwise no change to Ch 3A or Part 9A) |
| 13 July 2026 | Draft Finance Bill 2026-27 clauses: compulsory exemption (**proposed, not law**) |

The UK moved from taxing foreign profits with credit to leaving most of them alone, while building tools (CFCs, the branch anti-diversion rule, transfer pricing) to stop profits being diverted from home.

So a company going abroad has **three** possible positions, not two: (1) a branch taxed in the UK with credit; (2) a branch exempted by election; (3) a subsidiary whose profits come home as exempt dividends.

## The branch taxed as part of the company.

Without an election, the branch is part of the company's business. If the company trades in the UK and carries out a contract abroad through a permanent establishment (PE), the foreign profits and losses are part of one trading computation. The other country taxes the profits attributable to the PE as its treaty allows (chapters 23–24); the UK taxes them again and gives credit.

Two consequences:

1. **A branch loss reduces UK tax at once**, against the company's other profits of the same period.
2. **A profitable branch never pays less than the UK rate overall.** Credit tops foreign tax up to UK tax; it never refunds foreign tax that is higher.

Chapter 24 set out the credit limit (TIOPA 2010 s 42: R × IG, source by source, after allocating deductions under s 52). For trade income, the credit cannot exceed the CT attributable to the income from the particular transaction, arrangement or asset, after a just and reasonable share of deductions (s 44). Two refinements for branches:

- **Carry forward and back** of unrelieved foreign tax on a PE's profits: forward, or back to APs beginning up to 3 years earlier, against UK tax on the **same PE**'s profits (ss 72–78).
- **Foreign use of branch losses** (ss 71A–71B, FA 2018): where a PE loss is used abroad against other persons' profits (for example in a foreign group consolidation), the foreign tax available for credit on the PE's later profits is reduced by the tax saved.

**Calder's Vallarian branch (invented).** Our invented group, Tarnmoor (everything about it is invented for teaching), bought Calder Valve Engineering Ltd (CVE; 31 March year end) on 1 April GY1. In **August GY2** Calder began an installation contract at a refinery in **Vallaria** (invented; CT rate **20%**). The project ran **14 months**, to **September GY3**. The invented UK–Vallaria treaty treats a construction or installation project lasting **more than 12 months** as a PE (chapter 24). So Calder had a Vallarian branch from the start.

> **Worked example 25.1: Calder's branch without an election (invented)**
>
> Vallarian loss relief is ignored (story simplification: in real life Vallaria would probably carry the GY3 loss forward and tax less of the later profit).
>
> | £ | AP 1 Apr GY2 – 31 Mar GY3 | 9-month AP 1 Apr – 31 Dec GY3 | Project life |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | PE result included in Calder's trading profits | (300,000) | 900,000 | 600,000 |
> | UK CT at 25% on the PE result | (75,000) | 225,000 | 150,000 |
> | Vallarian tax at 20% | — | 180,000 | 180,000 |
> | Credit limit (s 42/s 44: UK tax on the contract's income) | — | 225,000 | |
> | DTR credit (lower of foreign tax and limit) | — | (180,000) | (180,000) |
> | **UK tax (relief) attributable to the PE** | **(75,000)** | **45,000** | **(30,000)** |
> | **Total tax: Vallaria + UK** | | | **150,000** |
>
> £150,000 ÷ £600,000 = **25.0%**. Without an election a branch is taxed at the UK rate on its **net** result, with Vallaria taking its share first.

## The election.

CTA 2009 **s 18A**: where a UK resident company has elected, **exemption adjustments** are made in calculating its taxable total profits so that the profits **and losses** of its foreign PEs are left out. The symmetry is the price.

**Mechanics (s 18A(4)–(7)).** For each **relevant foreign territory** (one in which the company carries on, or has carried on, business through a PE) there is a **relevant profits amount** or a **relevant losses amount**. The **foreign permanent establishments amount** = aggregate relevant profits amounts − aggregate relevant losses amounts. It may be positive or negative, and the exemption adjustments remove it either way.

**Measuring the branch profit.**

| Situation | Measure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Full treaty territory** (treaty with a non-discrimination article covering PEs: s 18R) | Profits the treaty's business profits article would attribute to the PE | s 18A; INTM281040 |
| No full treaty | Profits that would be attributed under a treaty in the form of the **OECD Model**, as defined in s 18S (updated by FA 2026 Sch 7 to the Model approved on **18 November 2025**) | s 18A, s 18S |
| Capital allowances | **Notional allowances** (s 18C), given automatically on the most favourable assumptions, so that allowances on exempt PE assets cannot reduce the profits that stay taxable | INTM285020 (HMRC's view) |
| Leasing | Amounts from a lease where the lessor or a connected company claimed UK allowances are left out (s 18C(3)) | INTM285050 |

**The edges.**

- **Investment business**: profits or losses of any part of the business consisting of making investments are left out of the relevant amounts.
- **UK land**: UK property business profits and UK land-linked gains are never exempted (s 18A(2A), (2B)).
- **Small companies**: only PEs in **full treaty territories** count.
- **Chargeable gains** are generally within the relevant amounts (immovable property abroad used for the PE included: s 18B); but **close companies'** gains are excluded (s 18P(2)) unless on assets used **solely** for the PE's trade (s 18P(3)), in which case credit relief applies to excluded gains in the normal way.
- **Shipping and air transport** in international traffic are excluded.

**Interaction.**

- **No credit**: foreign tax on an exempt relevant profits amount is not "tax payable" for credit (**TIOPA 2010 s 18(3A)**).
- **UK-to-UK TP exemption**: s 164A TIOPA 2010 does not apply where s 18A exemption adjustments are made in relation to the activities (chapter 27).
- **Intangibles**: an asset used in an exempt PE cannot pass tax-neutrally within the intangibles group (chapter 11).

**All or nothing.** The election covers **every** foreign PE of the company, present and future. It cannot be made branch by branch. A group wanting different answers for different branches must put them in different companies.

**Timing (s 18F; INTM281020).**

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Effect | Applies to all APs beginning on or after the **relevant day** (s 18F(1)); an AP straddling the relevant day is split (s 18F(4)) |
| Relevant day (UK resident company) | The day on which the company's **next AP** is expected to begin when the election is made (HMRC: objective, by reference to the normal accounting date); if made before the first AP, the start of that AP (s 18F(2)) |
| Before the relevant day | May be **revoked** by the company (s 18F(6), from 1 January 2013) |
| After the relevant day | **Irrevocable**; ends automatically only if the company ceases to be UK resident (s 18F(7)–(8)) |
| Change of accounting date | The election still takes effect from the original relevant day (HMRC) |

> **Misconception: "electing is a no-brainer once the branch is profitable."** It is not. The election is irrevocable, covers every PE in every country forever, and starts only with the *next* AP. A company that elects gives up UK relief for every future branch loss. It is a forecast of the whole future pattern of the company's overseas business, made in advance.

## The opening negative amount.

Without a further rule, a company could have branch losses relieved in the UK, then elect just as the branch turned profitable. The **transitional matching rules** (CTA 2009 **ss 18J–18N**) close that door.

**The look-back (INTM284020).** Look back over the **6 years** before the first AP the election covers. Aggregate the net losses of **all** the company's foreign PEs, net of their profits. Net losses not absorbed by later profits form the **total opening negative amount**. Chargeable gains and losses are ignored. (A historic extension applies to a "large pre-commencement loss" over £50m in one territory; not needed for the exam.)

**Matching (INTM284030).** Until the opening negative amount is fully matched by later relevant profits, those profits are **not exempt**: they stay in charge, with credit relief. Only then does the exemption start.

**Streaming (ss 18L–18N; INTM284040).** By default the negative amount is one pool across all territories, so a profitable branch in one country can be held in charge by old losses in another. A **streaming election** attaches amounts to the territory that produced them. It has effect only if the company specifies, in its company tax return for the **first relevant AP**, how much is to be the streamed opening negative amount for each streamed territory. A streamed amount is reduced only by that territory's relevant profits (s 18M); unmatched profits elsewhere are treated as unstreamed (s 18N(3)).

*The Living Law*'s image: the opening negative amount is **paying back the rent rebate** you enjoyed while the spare room stood empty.

> **Worked example 25.2: the opening negative amount (labelled, not story)**
>
> A company has PEs in two countries, A and B, in the 6 years before its election takes effect.
>
> | £ | Country A | Country B | Total |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Net results over the 6 years | (500,000) | 200,000 | (300,000) |
> | **Total opening negative amount** | | | **300,000** |
>
> The first £300,000 of later relevant profits, from either territory, stays taxable, with credit; only profits above that are exempt. A streaming election (made in the first relevant return) would instead tie the matching to territory A, whose losses produced the amount, so that B's later profits are not held in charge by A's history.

## Calder's decision.

When the refinery contract was signed in GY2, Calder's board compared a branch with a Vallarian subsidiary and chose the branch (below). It did **not** elect then: the forecast showed early losses, the project was a single contract, and Calder's engineers expected more installation work abroad as Project Ashlar's technology sold. An election would have bound every future branch.

In the summer of GY3, with the profit clear, Calder's finance director, **Graham Pike**, asked **Tom Hesketh**, the group head of tax, whether it was too late. It was. Calder was in its 9-month AP (1 April–31 December GY3). An election made then would take effect only from **1 January GY4**, after the project had ended: it would exempt nothing and would still bind Calder's future branches. **Calder did not elect.**

> **Worked example 25.3: hindsight: an election made in the AP to 31 March GY3 (labelled counterfactual, not story)**
>
> Relevant day: 1 April GY3. Six-year look-back: Vallarian PE loss £300,000 (AP to 31 March GY3): total opening negative amount **£300,000**.
>
> | £ | Not exempt (matched) | Exempt | Total |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Relevant profits, 9-month AP to 31 Dec GY3 | 300,000 | 600,000 | 900,000 |
> | UK CT at 25% | 75,000 | — | 75,000 |
> | Vallarian tax at 20% (apportioned pro rata) | 60,000 | 120,000 | 180,000 |
> | Credit | (60,000) | not creditable: s 18(3A) | (60,000) |
> | **UK top-up** | **15,000** | **—** | **15,000** |
>
> | Comparison (£) | No election (actual) | Election from 1 April GY3 |
> |---|---|---|
> | UK relief for GY3 loss | (75,000) | (75,000) |
> | UK top-up on later profit | 45,000 | 15,000 |
> | Vallarian tax | 180,000 | 180,000 |
> | **Total tax over the project** | **150,000** | **120,000** |
> | **Saving** | | **30,000** |
>
> Against £30,000: the loss of UK relief at 25% for **every** future branch loss of Calder, in every country, for ever. For a company about to export a new technology through installation projects that often begin with losses, the board's view that this was a small prize for a large, permanent commitment is defensible. In an exam, say so as a recommendation.

## Guard rails.

**The anti-diversion rule (ss 18G–18I).** An exemption invites diversion: mobile income (interest, royalties) moved into a low-taxed branch. Since FA 2012 Sch 20 (relevant periods beginning on or after 1 January 2013), the rule is aligned with the CFC regime: in outline, and on HMRC's description (INTM286010, INTM286310), a PE's adjusted relevant profits amount (computed without gains) is not exempt so far as it includes profits that would pass through the CFC gateway if the PE were a CFC (s 18H), unless one of the CFC entity exemptions in TIOPA 2010 Part 9A Chs 11–14 (excluded territories, low profits, low profit margin, tax exemption), as adapted, applies (s 18I). The test looks at each AP separately. Pre-2013 periods used an older motive test (not examinable). Chapter 26 teaches the CFC tests.

**Reporting.** FA 2026 s 48 allows regulations for reporting international controlled transactions (ICTS); the power covers transactions relevant to s 18A exemption adjustments. Regulations are **not yet made** (chapter 27).

**Coming next (proposed, not law).** The draft Finance Bill 2026-27 clauses (13 July 2026) would:

- make the exemption **compulsory** for APs beginning on or after **1 January 2027**, with exceptions including UK property business and investment business;
- **repeal the opening negative amount rules** and replace them with transitional rules **restricting the carry forward of losses** attributable to foreign PEs (income losses, capital losses and capital allowances in separate schedules);
- apply anti-avoidance to arrangements made on or after **13 July 2026**, with a rule against short accounting periods.

These points come from the draft and from professional commentary (KPMG; SWG; Paul Hastings), not from enacted law. Reports differ on whether an earlier start for oil and gas survived into the draft. None of it applies to the 2027 sittings (FA 2026). In practice, check the Bill's progress.

## The subsidiary route.

A foreign subsidiary pays foreign tax on its own profits; the UK leaves them alone. Its losses stay with it, for the foreign country's rules. Its dealings with the UK parent must be at arm's length (chapter 27), and the CFC rules (chapter 26) stand guard over diverted profits.

**Dividends: CTA 2009 Part 9A.** Distributions received are charged (s 931A) unless exempt.

| Recipient | Exempt if | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **Small company** | Payer resident only in the UK or a **qualifying territory** (s 931C; broadly a territory with a treaty containing a non-discrimination provision); not an interest-like distribution (CTA 2010 s 1000(1) E or F); no deduction allowed abroad; not a tax advantage scheme | s 931B |
| **Medium or large company** | Within an **exempt class**; not an E or F distribution; **no deduction allowed abroad** | s 931D |

**The exempt classes (one is enough).**

| Class | Detail | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Controlled companies | **Condition A**: the recipient controls the payer. **Condition B** (joint venture): the recipient is one of two persons who together control the payer, holds **≥ 40%**, and the other holds **≥ 40% but ≤ 55%**. Control as in TIOPA 2010 s 371RB (read with s 371RD as modified) | s 931E; INTM653030 |
| Non-redeemable ordinary shares | | s 931F |
| Portfolio holdings | Under 10% | s 931G |
| Transactions not designed to reduce tax | | s 931H |
| Shares accounted for as liabilities | | s 931I |
| Anti-avoidance | ss 931J–931Q (for example s 931J: schemes to bring pre-control profits within the controlled company class) | INTM654020 |

**Deductible dividends.** If the payer gets a deduction abroad, the dividend is not exempt (s 931B(c)/s 931D(c)), **and** no underlying tax credit is given (TIOPA 2010 s 57(3)): only withholding tax is creditable.

**Withholding tax on an exempt dividend is a pure cost.** Tarnmoor Vallaria SA (TVS; 100% owned by TPLC) pays dividends exempt under the controlled company class; Vallaria withholds **15%** under the invented treaty. For this example, suppose TVS pays **£10m**: Vallaria keeps **£1.5m**, which can never be credited because there is no UK tax.

**The election to tax (s 931R).** A company may elect for a distribution not to be exempt, within **2 years** after the end of the AP of receipt. A typical reason (reasoned illustration): a source country grants its lower treaty withholding rate only if the dividend is taxable in the recipient's hands, or credit would cover the UK tax anyway.

> **Worked example 25.4: does an election to tax help on Vallarian numbers? (labelled, not story)**
>
> A Vallarian subsidiary earns £900,000, pays Vallarian tax £180,000 and declares a dividend of £720,000, from which Vallaria withholds 15% (£108,000).
>
> | £ | |
> |---|---|
> | Dividend (gross of withholding) | 720,000 |
> | Underlying tax (Step 2: £180,000; mixer cap (D + PA) × 25% = (720,000 + 180,000) × 25% = 225,000, not restrictive) | 180,000 |
> | Taxable income (grossed up: s 31) | 900,000 |
> | UK CT at 25% | 225,000 |
> | Credit: underlying 180,000 + withholding 108,000 = 288,000, limited by s 42 | (225,000) |
> | **UK tax** | **nil** |
> | Foreign tax unrelieved | 63,000 |
>
> Total tax £288,000 either way: the election achieves nothing.

> - **Grade:** core ("Company distributions received", 1; chapter 19 owns domestic distributions).
> - **Traps:** treating a dividend as taxable with credit when it is exempt; forgetting that a dividend **deductible abroad** is neither exempt nor carries underlying tax relief; trying to credit withholding against nothing; applying the small-company test to a large group; confusing the s 42 limit with the s 58 mixer cap (chapter 24).

## Branch or subsidiary.

In GY2, Tom Hesketh's paper for Calder's board ran the two routes side by side, on the same simplification that Vallaria taxes each period's profit at 20%.

> **Worked example 25.5: Calder's board paper, branch v subsidiary (invented)**
>
> | £ | Branch (no election) | Vallarian subsidiary |
> |---|---|---|
> | Vallarian tax on £900,000 profit at 20% | 180,000 | 180,000 |
> | UK relief for the £300,000 first-year loss | (75,000) | none: loss stays in Vallaria |
> | UK top-up on £900,000 (25% − credit) | 45,000 | none: dividend exempt (s 931E) |
> | Vallarian withholding on dividend of £720,000 at 15% | — | 108,000 (if fully distributed) |
> | **Total tax** | **150,000** | **288,000** (180,000 if retained) |
> | CFC exposure | n/a | Tax exemption met: 20% ÷ 25% = **80%** ≥ 75% (chapter 26) |
>
> For a short project that starts with a loss, the branch wins.

**The general lesson is subtler.** Over a long life, a profitable subsidiary in a lower-tax country keeps its lower rate, provided its profits are reinvested locally or come home without much withholding. A branch without an election is always topped up to the UK rate. **The branch wins early, while losses need relief; the subsidiary, or an elected branch, wins later, when profits are steady.**

| Factor | Branch (no election) | Branch (elected) | Subsidiary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Losses | Relieved in the UK at once | Not relieved in the UK | Stay abroad |
| Rate on profits | UK rate (credit tops up) | Foreign rate only | Foreign rate; dividends exempt |
| Foreign tax credit | Yes (s 42 limits) | No (s 18(3A)) | Only if dividend taxed (s 931R) |
| Withholding on profits sent home | None (same company) | None | On dividends: a pure cost |
| Anti-diversion | — | ss 18G–18I (CFC-shaped) | CFC rules (TIOPA 2010 Part 9A) |
| Pricing | PE attribution (CTA 2009 ss 20–21 for inbound; treaty Art 7) | Same | Transfer pricing (TIOPA 2010 Part 4) |
| Exit / change later | Elect, or incorporate (s 140) | Irrevocable | Liquidate or sell (SSE: chapter 18) |
| Legal liability | Company's own | Company's own | Ring-fenced |
| Residence risk | — | — | Board must manage abroad (chapter 22) |
| Pillar Two | Measured in the foreign jurisdiction in outline (chapter 30) | Same | Same |
| Scope | Company-wide decision | All PEs of the company | Entity by entity |

## Incorporating a branch.

**TCGA 1992 s 140** (core within the PE/branch row; examined N25 Q2 and M23 Q2).

| Condition | Detail |
|---|---|
| Transferor | UK resident company carrying on a trade **outside the UK through a PE** |
| Transfer | The trade, with **all its assets, or all except cash**, to a **non-UK resident company** |
| Consideration | Wholly or partly **securities** (shares, or shares and loan stock) in the transferee |
| Holding | After the transfer, the transferor holds **≥ 25%** of the transferee's ordinary share capital |
| Claim | The net gain is postponed in the proportion **securities ÷ total consideration** |
| Crystallisation | (1) Transferor disposes of the securities (any time): postponed gain added to proceeds; (2) transferee disposes of the transferred assets **within 6 years**: the corresponding part of the postponed gain comes back |

> **Worked example 25.6: s 140 postponement (labelled, not story)**
>
> | £ | |
> |---|---|
> | Net gains on the branch's assets | 1,000,000 |
> | Consideration: shares 3,000,000 + cash 1,000,000 = 4,000,000 | |
> | Postponed: 1,000,000 × 3,000,000 / 4,000,000 | 750,000 |
> | **Chargeable now** | **250,000** |
> | CT at 25% | 62,500 |

**Judgement.** (1) If the company has already elected under s 18A, gains on PE assets generally fall within the exempt amounts, so s 140 matters most for branches never exempt, or close companies (s 18P). (2) The new subsidiary is a foreign company controlled from the UK: CFC rules apply; plant transferred gives balancing adjustments; intangible fixed assets may give realisation credits (chapter 11). N25 Q2's examiners noted that few candidates saw that the incorporation gain could be postponed.

**ss 140A–140L (non-core).** A family of reliefs born in EU law (F(No.2)A 1992 ss 45–49; SI 2007/3186 for mergers):

| Section | Relief (in outline) |
|---|---|
| s 140A (with s 140B anti-avoidance) | Transfer of all or part of a **UK business** between companies resident in different **relevant states**, **wholly** for shares or debentures, assets remaining in the UK charge: no gain, no loss, on a joint claim. CAA 2001 s 561 follows through for capital allowances |
| s 140C (with s 140D) | A UK company transfers a business carried on through a **PE in a member State** to a company resident in a **member State**, for securities: gains and losses netted; notional foreign tax credit under TIOPA 2010 s 122 where appropriate (CG45713) |

Vallaria is not a member State, so none of these reliefs helps Calder; Ireland is (chapter 31).

## Unremittable income.

Two **separate** reliefs; choosing the right one is the first mark.

| | Trading receipts | Other foreign income |
|---|---|---|
| Statute | CTA 2009 **ss 173–175** (Part 3) | CTA 2009 **Part 18, ss 1274–1278** |
| HMRC guidance | BIM42750 | CFC version: TIOPA 2010 s 371SN (INTM255820) |
| Test | Amount brought into account as a trade receipt is unremittable because, and only because, of foreign exchange restrictions (local law, executive action, impossibility of obtaining transferable currency). HMRC: such a debt is **not** a bad or doubtful debt | **Condition A**: income cannot be transferred to the UK because of local law, executive action of its government, or impossibility of obtaining transferable currency; **Condition B**: not realised outside the territory for sterling or a transferable currency (s 1274) |
| Relief | Deduction from trading profits, **not so as to create a loss**; excess carried forward (s 173) | On a **claim**, income not taken into account (s 1275); **claim within 2 years after the end of the AP in which the income arises** |
| Withdrawal | Amount ceases to be unremittable, is exchanged for a remittable amount, is used to finance expenditure or investment outside the UK, an impairment is made, or (debts) insurance is received: treated as a trade receipt of that period (s 175) | Relief withdrawn when the income ceases to be unremittable (s 1276); income charged after the source has ceased (s 1277); valuation (s 1278) |

> **Worked example 25.7: TEL's blocked receipt (labelled example; amounts for illustration)**
>
> In GY6 a Marrovian customer of Tarnmoor Engineering Ltd (TEL) pays **£400,000** for pumps into a local bank account; new Marrovian exchange controls (Marrovia is invented) stop it leaving.
>
> - The sale is a receipt of TEL's **UK trade**, so the relief is **s 173**, not a Part 18 claim.
> - GY6: £400,000 included in turnover; s 173 deduction **(£400,000)** (TEL's trading profits are far larger, so no loss limit bites).
> - GY7: controls lift and the money is converted: relief withdrawn (s 175); £400,000 treated as a trading receipt of GY7.
> - Effect: **£100,000** of CT deferred one year. Any exchange difference on conversion is a separate accounting and tax point.
>
> Contrast: if the blocked amount were **interest** on a Marrovian deposit of a UK company with no trade there, Part 18 would apply, with a claim by the second anniversary of the end of the AP.

> **Exam lens: unremittable income**
> - **Grade:** core (1). Not seen as a main topic M23–M26: expect a few marks inside an overseas question.
> - **Traps:** treating a blocked trade debt as a bad debt; using Part 18 for trading receipts; missing the 2-year Part 18 claim; forgetting that the relief defers, it does not exempt.

## International movements of capital.

Until 2009, **ICTA 1988 s 765** required **prior Treasury consent** for certain transactions moving capital outside the UK (for example, a UK company causing or permitting a foreign subsidiary to issue shares or debentures); s 765A required information for movements within Europe; s 766 made breach an offence. **FA 2009 s 37 and Sch 17** repealed them and substituted **reporting**, for events and transactions on or after **1 July 2009**.

| Rule | Detail | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| UK corporate parent | UK resident body corporate controlling one or more non-resident bodies corporate, not itself controlled by UK resident companies | Sch 17 para 5 |
| Reporting body | Not foreign-controlled; or foreign-controlled with no other UK corporate parent; or nominated where a foreign parent has several | Sch 17 paras 5–7 |
| Reportable | Value **exceeding £100m** and: issue of shares or debentures by a foreign subsidiary; transfer of shares or debentures of a foreign subsidiary by or caused by the reporting body; a foreign subsidiary becoming or ceasing to be a controlling partner; others prescribed | Sch 17 para 8 |
| Excluded | Ordinary course of trade; all parties resident in the same territory; security given to a foreign subsidiary's bankers; certain investment of funds; regulations | Sch 17 para 9 |
| Deadline | **Within 6 months** of the event or transaction | Sch 17 para 4 |
| Penalty | TMA 1970 s 98 (special returns) | Sch 17 para 10 |

> **Worked example 25.8 (labelled hypothetical, not story).** TVS issues **£120m** of new shares to TPLC to fund a new plant. TPLC is a UK corporate parent and, not being foreign-controlled, the reporting body. £120m > £100m; the parties are in different territories; an issue of shares by a foreign subsidiary is not in the ordinary course of trade. **TPLC reports within 6 months.**

> **Exam lens: international movements of capital**
> - **Grade:** non-core (2). Expect 1–2 marks: who reports, the £100m threshold, 6 months, s 98 penalty.

## Going further.

- **PE register.** List every foreign PE, including construction sites and dependent agents that might create one (chapter 23): an election would capture all of them.
- **Model before each AP.** The election can only start with the next AP. If made, schedule the **opening negative amount** and make any **streaming** election in the first relevant return.
- **One company, one answer.** The election belongs to a company, not a group: loss-making ventures and mature branches can sit in different UK companies, one elected and one not. Some years later in our invented story, Tarnmoor buys Jess Hartley's Ridgeway Holdings, whose subsidiary has a Dublin showroom branch with no election: chapter 31 decides it.
- **Repatriation.** Dividends: usually exempt, but withholding is a cost. Interest, royalties and management charges: deductible abroad, taxable at home with credit for withholding, at arm's length. TVS pays interest to TFL (£120m at 6%) and TCM, a royalty to Calder (from GY4; 5% withholding: chapter 24) and a management charge to TPLC; it can pay dividends (15% withholding).
- **Deadlines.** s 931R election and Part 18 claims: 2 years after the AP end. IMC reports: 6 months. Streaming: first relevant return.
- **Record the reasoning.** Minute a decision not to elect with the forecast behind it (as Calder did), so a later finance director knows why the company is where it is. Using elections Parliament offers, as intended, is ordinary tax management, not avoidance.
- **Watch the draft Bill.** If enacted, the compulsory exemption would turn every branch decision into a question of transitional loss restriction for APs beginning on or after 1 January 2027.

## How the examiner tests it.

The topic appears often, usually inside a larger international question: **M23 Q2** (15), **N25 Q2** (15), **M26 Q5** (20) (details in the Exam lens boxes above). The favourite combination is **PE + branch exemption + incorporation of the branch + CFC**. The grades: branches and subsidiaries, foreign dividends and unremittable income core; cross-border transfer reliefs and international movements of capital non-core.

A good answer to a branch question sets out the three options in turn (taxable branch: credit relief and loss timing; elected branch: irrevocable, all PEs, opening negative amount; subsidiary: residence kept abroad, dividend exemption, withholding as a cost, CFC test), compares the tax cost over the periods given, and **ends with a recommendation**. Allow about 2.1 minutes a mark (about 42 minutes for 20 marks). Keep each table in £ or £000, not both.

## What to take away.

- A UK resident company is taxed on its profits wherever they arise (CTA 2009 s 5). **Without an election**, branch losses are relieved at once and branch profits are taxed at 25% with credit capped at UK tax on that income (TIOPA 2010 ss 42, 44): the branch's **net** result is taxed at the UK rate.
- **s 18A election**: removes profits **and** losses of **every** foreign PE, territory by territory, measured under the full treaty or the OECD Model (s 18S, updated to 18 November 2025); notional capital allowances. Applies from the **relevant day** (start of the next AP); revocable only before then; then **irrevocable**. **No credit** for foreign tax on exempt profits (s 18(3A)). Small companies: full treaty territories only; close companies' gains excluded unless assets used solely for the PE (s 18P); investment business and UK land excluded; anti-diversion rule (ss 18G–18I) shaped like the CFC rules.
- **Opening negative amount** (ss 18J–18N): 6-year look-back; net PE losses must be matched by later PE profits before exemption; streaming per territory in the first relevant return.
- In our invented case, Calder's Vallarian branch lost **£300,000** (AP to 31 March GY3; UK relief £75,000), then made **£900,000** (9-month AP to 31 December GY3): Vallarian tax **£180,000**, UK CT **£225,000**, top-up **£45,000**; project total **£150,000** (25% of £600,000). **Calder did not elect**: by the time the profit was clear, an election could start only after the project ended. Hindsight: electing in the earlier AP would have saved **£30,000** at the price of binding every future branch.
- **Subsidiary**: own profits, losses and rate. Dividends exempt if in an **exempt class** (controlled companies incl. the **40/55** joint venture limb; non-redeemable ordinary shares; portfolio < 10%; transactions not designed to reduce tax; shares accounted as liabilities), unless **deductible abroad** (then no exemption and no underlying tax credit). Withholding on exempt dividends is a pure cost (TVS: 15%). **s 931R** election: within 2 years.
- **s 140**: postponement on incorporating a foreign PE trade for securities, transferor ≥ 25%, in proportion securities ÷ consideration; crystallises on disposal of the securities, or of the assets within 6 years. ss 140A–140L: non-core; "relevant state" = UK or a member State.
- **Unremittable**: trading receipts under ss 173–175 (no loss created; withdrawn when remittable or used abroad); other foreign income under Part 18 (claim within 2 years).
- **International movements of capital**: reporting body; > £100m; within 6 months; TMA s 98.
- **Proposed, not law**: a compulsory exemption for APs beginning on or after 1 January 2027 (draft of 13 July 2026).

**So, branch or subsidiary?** A branch while losses need relief; a subsidiary, or an elected branch, when profits are steady; and a recommendation either way. Under Finance Act 2026 it is still a choice.

Once a subsidiary exists, the UK still watches what it earns. Chapter 26, on controlled foreign companies, explains how.
