Conclusion.

The book began with one loan and two answers. A company called L L C Five borrowed about four billion dollars from its own group. Transfer pricing asked whether strangers would have lent on those terms, and said yes. The unallowable purpose rule asked why the company borrowed, and took every pound of the deductions away. The Court of Appeal decided it in twenty twenty four, and the Supreme Court refused permission to go further.

Thirty two chapters later, that case looks less like a puzzle and more like a summary. A larger company lives under several rules at once, each asking its own question. The adviser's job is to know which questions are being asked, and to ask them all.

This conclusion does four things. It answers the four questions the book set out with. It revisits the debates the book has carried. It looks at what is coming, separating what is law from what is only proposed. And it asks what a large group's tax function will look like in the years after your exam. The law it describes is the law for the financial year twenty twenty six, and the facts about what is coming are as they stood on the ninth of October, twenty twenty six, when this book was finished.

One taxpayer or many.

The first question was whether a group is one taxpayer or many. The law's answer has not moved since Salomon's case in eighteen ninety seven. A company is a person distinct from its members. Each company in a group computes its own profits, files its own return and pays its own tax, on its own timetable. There is no group return.

What the law does is build bridges, each with its own guest list. Group relief carries current losses between seventy five per cent group companies, measured for the overlapping period and limited to the lower of what one can give and the other can take. Assets move at no gain, no loss inside a gains group. A group can roll a gain from one company's disposal into another company's new asset. The shareholding exemption looks through to the group's holding. One annual investment allowance, and one five million pound deductions allowance, are shared out.

The law also refuses, and the refusals are where the marks are. Carried-forward losses travel only after their owner has used what it can, and never for a newcomer's losses from before it joined. A change of ownership and a major change in the trade can wipe out a target's losses, as eight point six million pounds of Brackenwell's were in our invented case. And Tarnmoor's parent learned that management expenses can be surrendered only above a threshold that includes its share of a controlled foreign company's profits. Eight hundred and twenty five thousand pounds a year was stranded.

Yet the newest rules lean the other way. The interest restriction treats the worldwide group as its unit and asks how much interest the whole group may deduct in the United Kingdom. The global minimum tax measures the group's tax rate country by country, across all its companies there. For chargeable periods beginning on or after the first of January, twenty twenty six, most dealings between United Kingdom companies are taken out of transfer pricing altogether, because inside one country the price between relatives usually changes nothing. So the honest answer to the first question is this. Many taxpayers for computing and paying. One business for an increasing number of the limits.

Where profits are made.

The second question was where profits are made, and what stops them drifting.

The law's first answers are about presence. A company is resident here if it was incorporated here, or if its central management and control is here. Our invented Irish company found that leaving costs money, through an exit charge on its assets. A foreign company is taxed here only where it leaves a footprint, and for periods beginning on or after the first of January, twenty twenty six, that footprint includes a person who habitually plays the principal role in concluding its contracts, even one who never signs. Treaties then share the taxing rights and limit what each country takes, and double tax relief, or the branch exemption, stops the same profit being taxed twice.

The second set of answers is about drift. Transfer pricing prices each dealing as if strangers had agreed it. Calder's know-how, sold abroad for six million pounds, was re-priced at eight million, with relief in Vallaria through the mutual agreement procedure. The controlled foreign company rules tax a foreign subsidiary's profits here only where they have been diverted from the United Kingdom. In Group Year Five, Tarnmoor's parent paid a charge of two hundred and four thousand pounds on the profits of its Marrovian finance company. The interest restriction caps net interest at about thirty per cent of tax E B I T D A, earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. The hybrid rules switch off deductions that two countries see differently, which is why Project Undertow never left the drawing board. The unassessed transfer pricing profits charge, at thirty one per cent, now backs transfer pricing for periods beginning on or after the first of January, twenty twenty six, where diverted profits tax once stood.

And then there is the floor. Pillar Two does not decide where profits are made. It decides that, wherever they are made, a large group pays at least fifteen per cent there, or someone collects the difference. Tarnmoor paid one hundred and two thousand pounds of top-up tax on its Marrovian profits in Group Year Five.

So profits are taxed where the activity and the decision-makers are, and the law has built a series of fences against their drifting elsewhere. The latest fence is not a wall at the border. It is a minimum, set by agreement among many countries.

What the law follows.

The third question was whether the law follows the accounts, the deal or its own purpose. The answer is all three, in layers.

The accounts come first. Section forty six of the Corporation Tax Act two thousand and nine, which I will call the two thousand and nine Act, starts trading profits from generally accepted accounting practice. Loan relationships, derivatives and intangible assets follow the profit and loss account. N C L Investments, in the Supreme Court in twenty twenty two, held that a properly computed accounting debit counts unless a statute removes it. So a change of accounting standard can change taxable profit without a Finance Act.

The deal comes second. A share exchange is not a disposal. A scheme of reconstruction passes assets across without a charge. A demerger can hand a subsidiary to the shareholders without a dividend or a sale. The law respects the form of a commercial transaction, provided its conditions are met.

Purpose comes last, and it overrides both. The unallowable purpose rule disallows debits whose purpose is tax, as BlackRock, Kwik-Fit and J T I showed. After the Euromoney group won in the Court of Appeal in twenty twenty three, Parliament rewrote the share exchange purpose test, for issues from the twenty sixth of November, twenty twenty five. Value shifting, the anti-avoidance rules on buying losses and gains, the loss-refreshing rule, the treaty principal purpose test, and the tax design condition in the new transfer pricing charge all ask why. If the book has one trend to name, it is this. Mechanical rules are steadily acquiring purpose tests.

Governing the tax, and spotting the issue.

The fourth question was how a large group governs its tax, and what an adviser must spot.

The law gives everyone a part. The senior accounting officer answers for the systems and certifies them. The board publishes the group's tax strategy. The company tells H M R C, His Majesty's Revenue and Customs, about uncertain treatments above five million pounds. And the company answers in the criminal courts if someone acting for it facilitates tax evasion and it had no reasonable procedures to prevent it. Around those duties sits a calendar, of instalments, returns, interest restriction returns, transfer pricing files, country-by-country reports and Pillar Two filings.

The adviser's part is the one the Institute names. Identify the issues, explain them precisely, and coordinate the specialists. Chapter thirty one showed what that means. One modest acquisition, of Ridgeway from Jess Hartley, touched stamp duty, instalments, transfer pricing, the senior accounting officer, the interest restriction, Pillar Two, research and development, deferred tax, a retention bonus and a foreign branch. None of those points was hard alone. The skill was to find them all, and then to answer the question Jess had left open. Ridgeway Cycles elects for the branch exemption.

The debates, revisited.

The book has tried to give each side of its debates at its strongest, and to say where the law or the evidence stands, without offering a verdict on live politics. Here they are once more, briefly.

Is a group a set of separate entities or a single economic unit? The separate entity view gives certainty and respects legal form. The unit view matches how groups really work. The law still starts from separate entities, but the interest restriction and Pillar Two show the unit view gaining ground.

How far does the unallowable purpose rule reach? Taxpayers argue that ordinary acquisition finance with a tax benefit attached is common and should be safe. H M R C argues that the rule asks a factual question about each company's purpose. After the twenty twenty four cases, the courts have sided with H M R C on those facts, and Syngenta's appeal, still pending before the Upper Tribunal, may refine the line.

Is the arm's length principle the right test? Its defenders say it is the only test countries share. Its critics say it treats integrated groups as if they were strangers. The United Kingdom's answer, for now, is arm's length across borders, and a fresh exemption for most dealings at home.

Should anti-diversion taxes stand outside corporation tax? Diverted profits tax was designed to sit outside the treaties and to make groups pay first and argue later. Its replacement, the unassessed transfer pricing profits charge, keeps the pay-first design but sits inside corporation tax.

Is a global minimum tax the end of a race to the bottom, or a loss of fiscal sovereignty and of useful incentives? Supporters point to the floor. Critics point to complexity and to countries that have not joined. The proposed side-by-side package, which would let some countries' own minimum tax systems run alongside the global rules, is part of that argument. It is proposed, not law.

Is a thirty per cent earnings ratio the right way to limit interest? It is simple and internationally shared. But it treats debt and equity differently, and it has been costly to administer, which is why the Finance Act twenty twenty six simplified the appointment of a reporting company.

Is research and development relief worth its cost, given the fraud and error that followed it? The merged scheme, with its claim notification and additional information rules, is the government's attempt to keep the incentive and close the doors.

Did the controlled foreign company rules survive European law? The twenty twelve regime was built partly in answer to Cadbury Schweppes, and its finance company exemption spent years in a State aid dispute, which ended, as reported, with the Commission's decision annulled by the Court of Justice in twenty twenty four.

Should tax follow the accounts? It does, mostly, and the cases show both the benefits and the price.

Does transparency change behaviour? Senior accounting officers, published strategies and uncertain tax treatment notifications assume that it does. The evidence is still being gathered, and a consultation earlier in twenty twenty six proposed widening uncertain tax treatment reporting. That too is proposed, not law.

Should the United Kingdom exempt foreign branches, or tax them with a credit? It moved from credit to exemption for dividends in two thousand and nine and offered an elective branch exemption from twenty eleven. The draft legislation described below would make that exemption compulsory.

And finally, certainty or fairness? Main purpose tests reach transactions mechanical rules miss, but they make the answer depend on evidence of motive. Clearances are the law's partial answer.

What is coming.

First, what is law but not yet in force. From twenty twenty seven to twenty eight, income tax deducted from yearly interest rises from twenty per cent to the new savings basic rate of twenty two per cent, under the Finance Act twenty twenty six. And the transfer pricing rules on guarantees, rewritten by that Act, reach all borrowing, not just new borrowing, for periods beginning on or after the first of January, twenty twenty eight.

Second, what is only proposed. On the thirteenth of July, twenty twenty six, the government published draft clauses for the next Finance Bill, the Finance Bill twenty twenty six to twenty seven, with a technical consultation that closed on the seventh of September. Two corporate measures matter most for this book. One would make the foreign branch exemption compulsory, for accounting periods beginning on or after the first of January, twenty twenty seven, with the opening negative amount rules replaced by restrictions on carrying forward branch losses. Ridgeway's election in chapter thirty one would then become a rule rather than a choice, though on its facts the result would be the same. The other would implement the side-by-side package for Pillar Two. Both are proposed, not law. Neither is examinable on the Finance Act twenty twenty six.

Around them sit consultations. On reporting international controlled transactions, under a power in the Finance Act twenty twenty six, where the regulations have not been made. On the taxation of distributions and demergers. On widening uncertain tax treatment. And on a standard, fully tagged format for corporation tax computations, which H M R C has proposed to pilot from October twenty twenty seven and require from September twenty twenty eight. Every one of these is proposed, not law.

Third, the Budget. The Chancellor's Autumn Budget is due on the twenty eighth of October, twenty twenty six. As this book was finished, on the ninth of October, it had not happened. The government has said the Budget will confirm the final contents of the Finance Bill, so the draft clauses may change, be dropped or be joined by others. The government's corporate tax roadmap of October twenty twenty four committed to capping the main rate at twenty five per cent for this Parliament. A commitment is not a statute, so watch the Budget for anything that touches it. And the Bank of England's next decision on Bank Rate, due on the fifth of November, twenty twenty six, will move the interest rates on late and early tax payments. Whatever the Budget brings, your twenty twenty seven sitting should be examined on the Finance Act twenty twenty six, and the paper's rubric says candidates who answer by reference to more recently enacted law will not be penalised.

Fourth, the exam itself. From May twenty twenty eight the Chartered Tax Adviser qualification changes shape. This paper continues, as a three and a half hour paper of four to six questions. On the twenty twenty eight grid, the interest restriction, hybrid mismatches and intangible assets fall from core to non-core, and gains on leases and the intensive research and development scheme for loss-making small companies leave the syllabus. A new learning outcome asks candidates to critically evaluate and communicate the output of information generated by other parties or by artificial intelligence. None of that applies to a twenty twenty seven sitting. It shows where the profession thinks the work is going.

The tax function to come.

What will the tax function of a group like Tarnmoor look like over the next few years? The rules already enacted give a fair picture.

It will be a data function. Pillar Two returns need figures from every company in every country, fifteen months after the year end, and eighteen for the first. Country-by-country reports, master files and local files need the same discipline. If the international controlled transactions regulations are made, transaction data will travel with the return. And if H M R C's tagged computations arrive, the computation itself will become data that H M R C's systems read before any person does.

It will be a governance function. The senior accounting officer's certificate, the published strategy and the uncertain tax treatment rules put the tax function's controls in front of the board and in front of H M R C's customer compliance manager. The corporate criminal offence puts its procedures in front of the courts.

It will be a function of specialists, coordinated. No single person in a large group knows every regime in this book at depth. The group head of tax, like our invented Tom Hesketh, knows enough of each to see the issue, to ask the right specialist, and to put the answers together into one recommendation for the board.

And it will be a function that checks. Much of the first draft of tax work, the research memo, the summary of a new rule, the first pass at a computation, will increasingly come from software, including artificial intelligence. The Institute's new learning outcome is a fair description of the job that remains. Evaluate that output critically. Go back to the statute. Check the date the law applies from. Say whether a point is law, H M R C's view, disputed or proposed. Those habits are what this book has tried to build.

The living law.

The book's title is a claim. Corporate tax law is not a finished code. It is a set of answers to questions that keep changing, as groups grow, borders matter in new ways, accounts are rewritten and governments agree new floors. The four questions will still be asked after the rules that answer them today have changed.

So for the exam, keep the law for the financial year twenty twenty six, and use it precisely. After the exam, keep the questions. Is this one taxpayer or many? Where was this profit made? What is the law following here? And what must this group do, by when, and what would a good adviser flag?

The last piece of this book is a note on sources. It says where the law in these chapters lives, what this book could not open, and how to keep up once the exam is behind you.
