# Chapter thirty-two: The Advanced Technical craft.

## One sentence in the instructions.

In the Candidate Instructions for the October 2026 sitting, the Chartered Institute of Taxation (CIOT) printed a sentence every candidate should learn by heart: **"Calculations left in the spreadsheet and not copied to the answer box will not be marked."**

That sentence arrived with new exam software. From October 2026 the CIOT's papers are sat on **RM Assessment Master**, which replaced Exam4 (every question paper from May 2023 to May 2026 still told candidates to type their answers "as indicated by the Exam4 guidance"). The new system puts a spreadsheet on the screen, right under the place where you type your answer. It is a gift and a trap: you can add up a capital allowances pool in seconds, and if you leave it there the marker never sees it.

The sentence is a fair summary of the whole paper. The Advanced Technical paper does not reward what you know. It rewards what you put in front of the marker, in a form the marker can credit, within three and a half hours.

This chapter is about that craft. It is not question practice: you have the CIOT's past papers and your own question bank for that. It is the set of habits that turns thirty-one chapters of law into marks. The chapter gathers what every **Exam lens** box in this book has said, adds the facts about the paper and the day, and checks them against the CIOT's own documents. The law for a 2027 sitting is almost certainly **FY2026 law under FA 2026**, which is the law this book teaches.

Chapter 2 began this work with "Research in the exam room". Here we finish it. There is no syllabus grade for this chapter, because its subject is the paper as a whole. Every grade quoted comes from the **2026 syllabus grid**, and every comment about past papers comes from the question papers, suggested answers and examiners' reports the CIOT publishes for each sitting (sittings are abbreviated M23 = May 2023, N23 = November 2023, and so on). Where a figure is the book's own arithmetic rather than the CIOT's statement, it is labelled "derived".

**The question for the chapter:** what does this paper reward, and how do you give it that in 210 minutes?

**Who the paper is about.** The CIOT says the scenarios "will typically be based around multinational groups and larger companies that may be listed, with no individual controlling shareholders". > **Exam lens: the paper as a whole**
> - **Grades:** the 2026 grid (its page headers still say "2025 sittings", a left-over header); note 2 expects knowledge of the Law, PR&E and Principles of Accounting manuals' terms. **No 2027 grid has been published** (October 2026): check every grade when it appears.
> - **Style:** technical prose and computations; 0.5–1 mark per point; no letter format.
> - **Trap:** writing for a client (letters, pleasantries, executive summaries). Write for a technical marker.

## Inside RM Assessment Master.

**The screen.** On the left, the question scenario, with exhibits as thumbnails. The requirements appear at the bottom of the scenario and again at the top right. Under the requirements is the **answer box**. Below the answer box is a **spreadsheet** (SpreadJS, Excel-like). There is an on-screen calculator, a **flag** (bookmark) function, and a **timer that turns red and flashes 10 minutes before the end: the only time warning**. (CIOT Candidate Instructions, October 2026, pp 2–3.)

**The rule.** Workings must be copied into the answer box (Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V). "Calculations left in the spreadsheet and not copied to the answer box will not be marked", and **examiners cannot see cell formulae**. A cell showing £412,000 tells the marker nothing about where it came from, so every working must be shown in words and figures: proceeds, less cost, less indexation, equals the gain; not just the gain.

**Practise.** The CIOT says candidates must have practised with the software before the test centre, and puts a **practice test** on its website before each sitting. Use it more than once: find out how a table pastes, how long it takes and how it looks once pasted.

**The misconception.** *"The marker will see my working because the computer holds it."* The marker sees only the answer box. A perfect computation in the spreadsheet scores nothing; a rough computation, copied across with a one-line reason beside each figure, can score most of the marks even if the final number is wrong.

The examiners said as much before the software changed. After **N23 Q5** (SAO) they told candidates to show workings, because marks can be earned even when the conclusion is wrong; after **M25 Q4** (CT computation with TP) they made the same point. The software change makes their advice sharper: working that is not visible does not exist.

> **Exam lens: working in RM**
> - **Habit:** do the arithmetic wherever is quickest; before moving to the next requirement, copy it into the answer box, label each line, and check it pasted cleanly.
> - **Habit:** put each requirement's answer under that requirement; use the flag for anything you mean to revisit.
> - **Trap:** a spreadsheet full of correct numbers and an answer box with conclusions only.
> - **Trap:** relying on formulae to "show" the working. They are invisible to the examiner.

## The tax tables.

Behind the "open book" icon are three resources: the **tax tables** (PDF), the **OECD Model Tax Convention** (PDF), and URL links to **Croner-i and Tolley** student legislation. The FAQs are blunt: "The exams are closed book … Candidates cannot access anything other than Online Legislation and the Tax Tables." The Model sits inside RM for this paper; nothing else comes in. (The TKS paper's pre-prepared research exception does not apply to LCG.)

**What the 2026 tables contain** (hard copy on the desk plus PDF in RM):

| In the tables | Not in the tables (your marks) |
|---|---|
| FY2025 and FY2024 CT rates and limits | Associated company counting rules and dates |
| EU SME definition, with R&D and TP notes | Linked and partner enterprise tests applied to facts |
| RDEC 20%; ERIS 186% and the 14.5% credit | The RDEC step order; 25% v 19% notional tax; claim notification |
| CA rates: AIA £1m, **18%**/6% WDAs, SBA 3%, full expensing and 50% FYA | Which assets qualify; second-hand and leasing exclusions; HP timing; special balancing charges |
| HMRC interest rates (including QIP rates) | QIP thresholds, divisors and due dates |
| **Lease percentage table** | Short v long; grant v assignment; the premium income element |
| **RPI** for indexation | Indexation frozen at December 2017; no loss creation |
| Stamp duty/SDRT and SDLT/LBTT rates | Which tax, who pays, when, reliefs and clawbacks |

**The 2027 trap.** As at October 2026 the CIOT tables page offers only "Tax Tables 2026", which reflect FA 2025 (main pool WDA **18%**). An FA 2026 paper uses **14%** for chargeable periods beginning on or after 1 April 2026, with hybrid rates for straddling periods (by days, rounded up), and the new **40% FYA** (CAA 2001 s 45U, from 1 January 2026). The 2027 tables, when published, should show the FA 2026 figures (expected around January 2027; check). Chapter 8 did the hybrid arithmetic (1 January–31 December 2026: 14.99%).

Practise with the tables you will have: learn where each figure sits, so that finding the percentage for a lease with 27½ years left is a ten-second job.

> **Exam lens: tax tables**
> - **Trap:** using the 2026 tables' 18% for an FA 2026 sitting.
> - **Trap:** spending time memorising rates the tables give, and too little on the conditions they do not.
> - **Habit:** state the rate you are using and, for a straddling period, show the day-weighting (chapter 8).

## Legislation and the treaty.

**The legislation.** Online only, from **Croner-i** or **Tolley's Digital Library**: student products bought in advance; work subscriptions will not work. **You cannot make notes in either.** Highlighting and the products' built-in features are allowed. Tags and folder headings may contain a section number and/or a topic name (the CIOT's example: "Corporate rescue exception = CTA 2009 (s 361D)"). They must **not** reproduce pro formas or formulae, and invigilators may spot-check.

So the legislation is a map, not a crib. Its value is speed: open the right section, confirm a condition or time limit, and cite it.

**The treaty.** The OECD Model is available **only as an on-screen PDF**, no hard copy; the document published is the **2017** Model articles. It is a fallback. When a question supplies a treaty extract, **the extract is the treaty for that question**. After **N23 Q1** (20 marks: residence of an Anglo-Belgian parent, with a supplied tie-breaker using place of effective management), the examiners reported that some candidates ignored the supplied article and used the Model text instead. Read the extract, quote its article number, and apply its words to the facts.

**Guidance.** The paper rewards statute. HMRC's manuals are its view, not law, and you will not have them. If you rely on a known HMRC practice, say so as HMRC's view.

> **Exam lens: sources**
> - **Grade:** "Double tax treaties – application of OECD model and supplied extracts from treaties" **1 (core)**.
> - **Traps:** using the Model over a supplied extract (N23 Q1); citing a manual as authority; citing pre-FA 2026 PE sections (old CTA 2009 ss 22–32) for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026 (chapter 23).

## The clock.

No official per-question timing is published. **Derived:** 210 minutes ÷ 100 marks = **2.1 minutes per mark**.

> **Worked example: a time plan for the usual paper** (derived; assumes a 2.30pm start: shift every checkpoint if the start slips)
>
> | Question | Marks | Minutes | Elapsed | Remaining | Clock |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | 1 | 20 | 42.0 | 42.0 | 168.0 | 3.12pm |
> | 2 | 15 | 31.5 | 73.5 | 136.5 | 3.43pm |
> | 3 | 15 | 31.5 | 105.0 | 105.0 | **4.15pm (half time: 50 marks)** |
> | 4 | 20 | 42.0 | 147.0 | 63.0 | 4.57pm |
> | 5 | 20 | 42.0 | 189.0 | 21.0 | 5.39pm |
> | 6 | 10 | 21.0 | 210.0 | 0.0 | 6.00pm |
> | Timer flashes | | | 200.0 | 10.0 | 5.50pm |
>
> Total 100 marks, 210 minutes (checked in Python). Write the checkpoints on your note paper at the start; the on-screen timer is your only clock.

**No reading time.** Reading and planning come out of each question's budget. A sensible habit (the book's suggestion, not a CIOT rule): read the requirement, then the scenario, and jot a list of issues before typing.

**Bank the easy marks** (examiners' message, repeated across M23–M26). On a typical marking guide the first half of a question's marks come quickly: spotting the issues, stating the rules, the standard computation. The last few come slowly: a subtle interaction, a point most miss. Over-running on one question to chase its last two marks costs the first five of the next.

**When the budget runs out, stop.** Leave a line listing what you would do next, flag the question, move on, and come back if time remains.

**The ten-minute warning is for finishing, not starting.** Check every requirement has something in its answer box; copy across any working still in the spreadsheet; add any missing recommendation.

**Breadth beats length.** Ten correct points in two lines each usually outscore four points in a paragraph each. Make each point once, precisely, and move on.

> **Exam lens: timing**
> - **20 marks ≈ 42 minutes; 15 ≈ 31½; 10 ≈ 21** (derived).
> - **Trap:** spending 60 minutes on the first 20-mark question because you know it well.
> - **Trap:** leaving a requirement blank. Even a skeleton list of issues earns some marks.

## Reading the requirement.

Read the requirements before the scenario; they are the marking guide in disguise.

| Signal | What it means | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| "Calculate, with explanations" | A computation with a short reason on each line | All computation questions M23–M26 |
| "Explain" | The rule, its authority, applied to these facts | Discursive questions (PE, migration, hybrids, CFC) |
| "Discuss" | Weigh the options; reach a view | |
| "Recommend" | Name the option and the main reason | **M23 Q2**: the examiners reported that some candidates failed to give the recommendation and lost marks |
| Mark split (e.g. 8/6/6) | Time per part | M26 Q1 (8/6/6); N25 Q4 (4/8/8) |
| "Tax consequences" | **Every tax in the syllabus**, including stamp duty and SDLT | N25 and M26 chief examiner |
| A named tax | **Only that tax** scores | N25 and M26 chief examiner |
| "Ignore transfer pricing" / excluded | Excluded means excluded | M26 Q5: some did TP anyway; many discussed residence or migration of the UK company |
| "Identify further information required" | A short list of precise questions, each tied to a rule | LCG syllabus; tested directly in M26 Q6 |

**Excluded and unrelated topics earn nothing.** DPT in a loan question (M23 Q3: DPT never applied to loan relationships, and for APs beginning on or after 1 January 2026 it is repealed: chapter 30); hybrids with no hybrid trigger (N24 Q5); residence in a PE question (M26 Q5, N25 Q2); holdover when roll-over was asked (M26 Q3); pre-sale loss relief when not asked (M24 Q3).

**Stamp taxes are the classic missed marks.** After N25 Q5 the examiners noted that few candidates considered SDLT, so marks were missed.

**The rubric** (every paper M23–M26):
- "All workings should be shown and made to the nearest month and pound unless the question specifies otherwise."
- "You may assume that [prior year] legislation (including rates and allowances) continues to apply for [next year] and future years." For a 2027 sitting on FA 2026, expect 2026/27 legislation to be assumed to continue (derived from the pattern: year N exams test the Finance Act of year N–1).
- "Candidates answering by reference to more recently enacted legislation or tax cases will not be penalised."
- Scots law candidates may answer by reference to LBTT.

> **Exam lens: requirements**
> - **Habit:** underline (mentally) the verb, the scope words and the exclusions; write the mark split on your note paper.
> - **Traps:** answering the question you hoped for; omitting the recommendation; ignoring stamp taxes when "tax consequences" are asked; adding taxes when one tax is named.

## How the marks are made.

The CIOT publishes suggested answers **with a full marking guide** (at least from N25 and M26). Marks come in halves and wholes: a condition correctly stated, 0.5 or 1; a correct figure, 0.5 or 1; a conclusion, 0.5; "Conditions for rollover relief 1.5" is a typical line.

**Derived:** a 20-mark question needs roughly **20 to 40** distinct, correct points. Each should be short, on its own line or in its own sentence, and easy to find: a marker scanning the answer should be able to tick as they read.

**Habits that follow:**
1. **List every item**, including those needing no adjustment, with a few words of reason. A nil adjustment with a correct reason earns its mark (chapter 7's "four habits").
2. **State the authority** briefly: "TCGA 1992 s 179", "SSE, Sch 7AC". It shows the marker which rule you are applying.
3. **Do not restate the question.** Copying the facts back earns nothing.
4. **Do not pad.** After M26 Q1 the examiners described the CIR administration and PIE answers as adequate but padded with irrelevant detail. General paragraphs before you reach the facts are padding.
5. **Match the verb.** Do not stop at a conclusion when a computation is asked for, or at a computation when an explanation is asked for.

**The second misconception.** *"Advanced Technical depth means writing everything you know."* It does not. Depth here means applying the right rule to these facts, with the conditions, exceptions and interactions the facts raise. After **N24 Q6** the examiners reported higher marks for answers tied to the scenario; after **N25 Q6** they criticised generic anti-avoidance answers to a hybrids question.

**When the question seems wrong.** The chief examiner's advice (M25 and N25 reports): re-read the question; if you still think there is an error, **state it and your assumption, then answer on that basis**. Results are **moderated** and the pass mark may be adjusted for paper difficulty.

**Presentation.** M23 to N25 papers said "Additional marks may be awarded for presentation." **The M26 paper omits this line.** Do not count on marks for neatness; but layout decides whether the marker can find your points.

> **Exam lens: marks**
> - **Evidence:** marking guides at 0.5–1 per point; M24–M26 example scripts banded 40–50, 50–60, 60–70 and 70+.
> - **Trap:** a correct conclusion with no visible reasoning (often half the marks lost).
> - **Trap:** an essay on the regime before applying it.

## Layouts.

The examiners expect conventional layouts and say so when candidates ignore them (after M24 Q2 they reported that some candidates did not use a conventional CA layout). The set this book has taught:

| Topic | Layout | Built in |
|---|---|---|
| CT computation | PBT → adjustments (each with reason and authority) → tax-adjusted trading profit → CAs → other income and gains → total profits → reliefs in statutory order → TTP → CT (marginal relief where relevant) → RDEC set-off → payable; QIP dates | Chapter 7 |
| Capital allowances | Columns: FYA/FE, main pool, special rate pool, single asset pools, allowances; additions, disposals (pool deduction or special balancing charge), WDAs, c/f | Chapter 8 |
| Chargeable gains | Proceeds → costs of disposal → cost (restricted for leases; apportioned for part disposals) → enhancement → indexation to December 2017 → relief (**proceeds not reinvested**) → chargeable gain | Chapters 16, 17 |
| CIR | ANTIE → aggregate tax-EBITDA → 30% → debt cap (ANGIE) → interest capacity (with b/f allowance) → disallowance or reactivation → allocation | Chapter 28 |
| CFC | Company by company: CFC? → entity exemption (**stop** if one applies) → gateway → 25% test and apportionment → creditable tax → charge | Chapter 26 |
| DTR | Columns by source: gross income, allocated deductions, UK CT at R, foreign tax, credit (lower), unrelieved | Chapter 24 |
| Deferred tax | Fixed asset and short-term differences on separate lines; opening, closing, movement; current + deferred = total charge | Chapter 6 |
| Group relief (non-coterminous) | Time line; for each overlap: surrenderable amount, claimant's available profits, relief = lower | Chapter 15 |
| SSE | Conditions as a checklist, each applied, then a conclusion | Chapter 18 |
| Migration | Schedule of assets (book value, market value, exit amount, plan-eligible?), then CT1 − CT2 | Chapter 22 |
| Branch or subsidiary | Options in turn; short UK tax table per option; recommendation | Chapter 25 |

**One unit per table.** £ or £000, never both (M26 Q4 examiners: keep units consistent).

>
> | | £ | £000 |
> |---|---|---|
> | Total profits | 26,000,000 | 26,000.00 |
> | Group relief: TPLC management expenses | (5,475,000) | (5,475.00) |
> | Group relief: TPLC NTLR deficit | (7,670,000) | (7,670.00) |
> | Group relief: Brackenwell post-acquisition loss | (2,600,000) | (2,600.00) |
> | Consortium relief: Helmside | (1,350,000) | (1,350.00) |

Every one of these layouts can be typed as a simple table in the answer box, or built in the spreadsheet and pasted. Practise both, and choose the faster one that keeps its labels.

## What to take away.

- **The paper:** 3 hours 30 minutes; normally six questions of 10, 15 or 20 marks (usually three 20s, two 15s, one 10); 100 marks; pass mark 50%; at least 70% of the CT element from core. Listed groups with no controlling individual. Technical prose and computations, never a letter.
- **The day:** 4 May 2027, 2.30pm (fallback 26 October 2027; check your confirmation). Arrive early; no entry after 30 minutes late; no leaving in the first 45 or last 10 minutes. No watches. Physical calculator. Note paper is not marked.
- **RM Assessment Master:** "Calculations left in the spreadsheet and not copied to the answer box will not be marked"; formulae are invisible. Copy every working across, labelled. The timer flashes 10 minutes before the end: the only warning.
- **Resources:** tax tables (paper and PDF), the OECD Model (2017 articles, PDF only), Croner-i or Tolley legislation (tags, no notes, no pro formas). A supplied extract beats the Model. The 2026 tables show 18%; FA 2026 law is 14%.
- **Time:** 2.1 minutes a mark (derived): 42 / 31½ / 21 minutes. Half time at 4.15pm. Bank the easy marks; move on.
- **Requirements:** calculate, explain, discuss, recommend. "Tax consequences" = every syllabus tax; a named tax = that tax alone. Excluded means excluded.
- **Marks:** 0.5–1 per point; many short precise points, each with its authority; show workings; state any assumption and carry on.
- **Layouts:** conventional, one unit per table.

**So what does this paper reward?** Not what you know, but what you show: the right rule, applied to these facts, laid out where the marker can see it, inside the time.
