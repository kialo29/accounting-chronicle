Chapter twenty one: Stamp taxes for groups.

A bill the gains rules never sent.

On the first of July in Group Year Five, our invented group, Tarnmoor, did something large and quiet. Everything about Tarnmoor is invented for teaching. Its parent, Tarnmoor P L C, is a listed engineering group with its head office in Leeds. That morning it handed its water systems subsidiary to its own shareholders. Tarnmoor Water Systems left the group and listed on its own as Tarnwater P L C.

The corporation tax work had taken months, and it came out clean. The demerger was exempt. The parent's disposal of the shares fell within the shareholding exemption. Even the factory that the group's property company had moved into Tarnmoor Water Systems twenty one months earlier escaped the degrouping charge, because the gains rules switch that charge off for an exempt demerger.

Then Tom Hesketh, the group head of tax, opened a different file. When that factory moved inside the group, nobody paid stamp duty land tax on its value of six million pounds, because group relief applied. But that relief comes with a string. If the company that received the land leaves the group within three years, the relief is withdrawn. Tarnwater had just left. It now owed two hundred and eighty nine thousand, five hundred pounds.

Nobody had done anything wrong. The clawback was the price of the relief, and it had been written into the law all along. It simply lived in a different statute from the one everyone had been reading.

That bill is the misconception this chapter exists to correct. People assume that if the gains rules forgive a group transfer, the stamp taxes forgive it too. They do not. The stamp taxes have their own groups, their own purpose tests and their own clock. So the question for this chapter is this. When shares or land move into, around and out of a large group, what do the stamp taxes charge, what do they forgive, and when do they take the forgiveness back?

This is chapter twenty one, the last chapter of Part Three, on the group. On the Advanced Technical paper, stamp duty and stamp duty land tax are awareness topics, the lightest grade. Yet the examiners keep reporting the same thing. Stamp tax marks sit at the end of property and acquisition questions, and candidates walk past them. The Living Law, chapter thirty, taught the basics. Here we take them into the group. Throughout, the rules are those in force for the financial year twenty twenty six, under the Finance Act twenty twenty six.

Two taxes, one border.

Start with the shape of the system, because the groups rules only make sense once you see the border they guard.

Stamp duty is a tax on documents. Since stamp duty land tax took over land on the first of December, two thousand and three, stamp duty applies only to instruments transferring stock or marketable securities. In a group, that means a stock transfer form for shares. The rate is half a per cent of the consideration, rounded up to the next five pounds. A transfer on sale for a thousand pounds or less pays nothing. The duty is due within thirty days of the document being signed, and interest and penalties run after that. In practice the buyer pays.

Alongside it runs stamp duty reserve tax, which I will call S D R T. It charges the agreement to transfer, which catches paperless trades on the stock market, also at half a per cent. A higher rate of one and a half per cent applies to certain transfers into depositary receipt and clearance systems. If a duly stamped transfer follows within six years, the S D R T is cancelled, so the same sale is not taxed twice. The syllabus grid for this paper gives S D R T no grade of its own, so treat it as background.

Stamp duty land tax, which I will call S D L T, is the tax on land in England and Northern Ireland. It lives in Part four of the Finance Act two thousand and three, which I will call the stamp duty land tax rules. It taxes land transactions, not documents. For non-residential property the rates are nothing on the first one hundred and fifty thousand pounds, two per cent on the next one hundred thousand, and five per cent above two hundred and fifty thousand. A new lease is taxed on the net present value of its rent, discounted at three and a half per cent a year, at one per cent above one hundred and fifty thousand pounds and two per cent above five million. The return is due within fourteen days, and any V A T on the price is part of the consideration.

Now the border. Buy a warehouse and you pay S D L T at up to five per cent. Buy the shares in the company that owns the warehouse and you pay stamp duty at half a per cent. The same economic asset carries two very different prices of entry. That gap is the engine behind almost every rule in this chapter. If a group could move land into a fresh subsidiary free of tax, and then sell the subsidiary's shares, it could turn a five per cent tax into a half per cent one at will. Group relief exists so that genuine reorganisations are not taxed. Its guards and its clawback exist so that it cannot be used as a wrapper.

Stamp duty on Tarnmoor's share deals.

The story gives us five share purchases, and each teaches one point.

On the first of April in Group Year One, Tarnmoor P L C bought Calder Valve Engineering from the Oldroyd family for thirty two million pounds in cash. Stamp duty at half a per cent was one hundred and sixty thousand pounds, paid by the parent within thirty days. It is part of the cost of the shares for chargeable gains. As chapter thirteen explained, it is not an expense of management.

On the first of July in Group Year Two, the parent bought Brackenwell Sensors for twenty two point two million pounds. Stamp duty on the shares was one hundred and eleven thousand pounds. The parent also paid one point eight million pounds for Brackenwell's convertible notes. Transfers of ordinary loan capital are normally exempt from stamp duty. But section seventy nine of the Finance Act nineteen eighty six removes the exemption where, when the transfer is signed, the loan capital carries a right of conversion into shares. H M R C's view is that the right must be one the holder can exercise. Brackenwell's notes gave the holders that right. So the notes cost another nine thousand pounds, and the deal's stamp duty was one hundred and twenty thousand pounds in all.

On the first of March in Group Year Five, the parent bought the forty per cent of Helmside Energy held by Greyfell Utilities, its joint venture partner. It paid in its own new shares, two million of them at eight pounds each. Consideration in shares counts like cash, so stamp duty was half a per cent of sixteen million pounds, eighty thousand pounds. The relief you might expect to apply fails, for reasons the next section explains.

The fourth deal is the sale of Tarnmoor Actuators on the last day of Group Year Six, to Brennock Industries, an invented American buyer. Brennock is not resident in the United Kingdom, but the duty is on the document transferring United Kingdom shares, so Brennock pays. The price was forty eight million pounds in cash, plus an earn-out of up to six million pounds, valued at three million at completion.

Here stamp duty follows what H M R C's manual calls the contingency principle, which it traces to a House of Lords decision, Underground Electric Railways. Duty is fixed when the document is signed. A contingent payment with a stated maximum is charged on the maximum. One with only a minimum is charged on the minimum. One that is wholly unascertainable is not charged at all. Brennock's earn-out is capped at six million pounds, so duty is on fifty four million pounds, which is two hundred and seventy thousand pounds. If the earn-out pays out less, nothing comes back. Charging only the cash, or the earn-out's value, are the two tempting wrong answers.

The fifth deal comes some years later, when the parent buys Ridgeway Holdings from Jess Hartley, the invented founder whose cycle business The Living Law followed, for four point five million pounds. Stamp duty is twenty two thousand, five hundred pounds. Chapter thirty one tells that story.

Reliefs when shares move inside a group.

Stamp duty has three reliefs a group adviser must know, all in the Finance Acts of nineteen thirty and nineteen eighty six.

The first is group relief, in section forty two of the Finance Act nineteen thirty. A transfer between two associated bodies corporate is free of duty. They are associated if one owns at least seventy five per cent of the other's ordinary share capital, directly or indirectly, and is entitled to at least seventy five per cent of its profits and of its assets on a winding up. Those profits and assets tests arrived in two thousand. Relief is denied if, when the transfer is signed, arrangements exist under which a person could obtain control of the transferee company but not of the transferor. That is the stamp duty version of the wrapper problem. The relief is not self-assessed. The transfer goes to H M R C to be adjudicated or stamped as not chargeable. Suppose, for this example, that the parent had moved its Calder shares down to Tarnmoor Engineering for thirty two million pounds, with no sale in prospect. Section forty two would have removed one hundred and sixty thousand pounds of duty.

The second is reconstruction relief, in section seventy five of the Finance Act nineteen eighty six. It covers a company acquiring all or part of another company's undertaking under a scheme for the reconstruction of that other company. The consideration must be non-redeemable shares issued to all the target's shareholders, plus at most the taking over of liabilities. Afterwards the shareholdings must mirror each other. There must be bona fide commercial reasons and no main purpose of avoiding stamp duty, income tax, corporation tax or capital gains tax. The old acquisition relief at half a per cent, in section seventy six, was repealed in twenty twelve.

The third is share-for-share relief, in section seventy seven. It applies when an acquiring company takes the whole issued share capital of a target, paying only with its own shares, issued to the target's shareholders, so that the shareholdings after the deal mirror those before. Since the twenty ninth of June, twenty sixteen, section seventy seven A has also denied relief where, at the time of the transfer, there are disqualifying arrangements. Those are arrangements with a purpose of securing that a particular person, or persons together, obtain control of the acquiring company. Its classic use is putting a new holding company on top of an existing group.

Now Helmside. The parent already held forty five per cent. It bought forty per cent from Greyfell. Northlight, an infrastructure fund, kept its fifteen per cent. The parent did not acquire the whole issued share capital, and only one of the three shareholders received its shares. Section seventy seven was never in play, so the eighty thousand pounds was payable.

Group relief for land, and its guards.

Land moving inside a group meets S D L T, and it meets a rule that makes the tax bite even on a gift.

Under section fifty three of the stamp duty land tax rules, where the buyer is a company connected with the seller, the chargeable consideration is at least the market value of the land. A group company that hands a building to a sister for nothing is still taxed on its full value. But section fifty three keeps the reliefs alive, and the most important is group relief, in Schedule seven.

Group relief exempts a land transaction where the seller and the buyer are companies in the same group. One must be the seventy five per cent subsidiary of the other, or both of a third company, measured by ordinary share capital, profits and assets. That much looks like the stamp duty test.

The guards are where S D L T goes further. Relief is not available if, at the time of the transaction, there are arrangements for someone to obtain control of the buying company but not the selling company. It is not available if the consideration is provided by someone outside the group. It is not available if there are arrangements for the buying company to leave the group. And it is not available unless the transaction is effected for bona fide commercial reasons and not as part of arrangements with a main purpose of avoiding tax. Tax there means more than S D L T. It includes corporation tax, income tax and capital gains tax.

That last guard has teeth, as a recent case shows. The case is Tower One St George Wharf Limited versus H M R C, decided by the Upper Tribunal in twenty twenty four. The tribunal held that group relief was not available. The arrangements around an intra-group transfer had a main purpose of obtaining a corporation tax advantage, a step-up in base cost. It did not help the company that the step had failed to achieve that advantage. The tribunal drew on the loan relationship cases on unallowable purpose, the same line of cases our prologue began with. When the case reached the Court of Appeal in December twenty twenty five, the company no longer argued that group relief applied.

H M R C's view of arrangements to leave the group is also worth knowing. Its manual says that the practical likelihood of the scheme being carried through is not in itself relevant. Yet it also says that it will not infer arrangements merely because land is moved to a group company that holds only property. Moving land around a group is normal. Moving it into a box that is already being sold is not.

The three-year string.

Here is the analogy I find most useful. S D L T group relief is like lending a parking space to a relative. The loan is free, but if the car leaves the family within three years, the space must be paid for, at the price it had on the day you lent it.

The precise rule is in paragraph three of Schedule seven. Relief is withdrawn if the buying company ceases to be a member of the same group as the selling company within three years of the transaction, or later under arrangements made within those three years. It applies only while the buyer, or a company leaving with it, still holds the land or an interest derived from it. The tax charged is the S D L T on the market value at the date of the original transaction, not the value on the day the buyer leaves.

There are exceptions. Relief is not withdrawn if the buyer drops out of the group because the seller leaves it. But since two thousand and eight, a later change in control of the buyer, within the three years, brings the withdrawal back. Nor is relief withdrawn where the groups separate in the course of winding up the seller or a company above it. And it survives where the buyer leaves because another company acquires it in a deal that qualifies for stamp duty reconstruction relief under section seventy five, so long as the buyer is then grouped with that acquiring company.

The buying company pays the clawback. If it is still unpaid six months after it fell due, H M R C can recover it from the seller, or from any company above the buyer in the group.

Two cousins of group relief carry the same kind of string. Reconstruction relief exempts land acquired with an undertaking under a scheme of reconstruction, on conditions much like section seventy five. Acquisition relief caps the tax at half a per cent where a company acquires an undertaking for its own non-redeemable shares, with any cash limited to ten per cent of the shares' nominal value, provided the business is not mainly dealing in land. Suppose, for this example, that land worth ten million pounds came with such an undertaking. Full S D L T would be four hundred and eighty nine thousand, five hundred pounds. Acquisition relief would cap it at fifty thousand. Both reliefs are withdrawn if control of the acquiring company changes within three years.

Three transfers, three outcomes.

Our invented group moved land three times under S D L T group relief, and each move ended differently.

The first move was on the first of April in Group Year Three. Calder transferred its works to Tarnmoor Estates, the group's property company, at their market value of five point six million pounds. Section fifty three made market value the starting point. Group relief took the tax to nothing, saving two hundred and sixty nine thousand, five hundred pounds. Tarnmoor Estates then leased the works back to Calder, and the grant of that lease was itself a land transaction between group companies, so Calder claimed group relief on the rent as well. Tarnmoor Estates never left the group. Three years passed, and the saving became permanent.

The second move was on the first of October in Group Year Three, when Tarnmoor Estates transferred the water systems factory to Tarnmoor Water Systems at its market value of six million pounds. Group relief saved two hundred and eighty nine thousand, five hundred pounds. Then came the demerger, twenty one months later. A direct demerger is a distribution to the parent's shareholders, not an acquisition by another company, so none of the exceptions applied. The relief was withdrawn on the six million pound value fixed at the transfer, and Tarnwater paid two hundred and eighty nine thousand, five hundred pounds.

Was there another way? On this book's reading of the exception just described, an indirect demerger might have kept the relief. In that route a new company acquires the subsidiary in exchange for its own shares, issued to the parent's shareholders, and that acquisition may qualify for section seventy five. The board chose the direct route for reasons chapter nineteen explains, and accepted the S D L T cost as part of the price.

The third move was on the first of February in Group Year Six. Tarnmoor Engineering hived its actuators business down into a new subsidiary, Tarnmoor Actuators, including a factory worth seven point five million pounds. Before claiming relief, Tom tested the arrangements guard. The board minute recorded a decision to run the actuators business as a separate company and to review its options later in the year. There was no buyer, no heads of terms and no agreement to sell. On those facts there were no arrangements for the subsidiary to leave the group, and relief of three hundred and sixty four thousand, five hundred pounds was claimed.

On the last day of Group Year Six, Brennock bought the subsidiary, eleven months after the hive-down. The relief was withdrawn, and Tarnmoor Actuators owed three hundred and sixty four thousand, five hundred pounds. Brennock had seen this coming in due diligence and negotiated an indemnity from Tarnmoor Engineering in the sale agreement. Had a buyer already been lined up on the day of the hive-down, the relief would have been denied at the outset, and claiming it would have been an error.

Add it up. Group relief saved nine hundred and twenty three thousand, five hundred pounds at the time of the three transfers. Six hundred and fifty four thousand pounds of it came back. Only the Calder works saving stuck, because only that building stayed in the group for three years.

Selling the head office and renting it back.

On the first of October in Group Year Six, Tarnmoor Estates sold the group's head office, a freehold, to an unconnected pension fund for fourteen million pounds. It took back a fifteen year lease at a commercial rent of nine hundred thousand pounds a year. Tarnmoor Estates had not opted to tax the building, so no V A T was added to the price.

The pension fund, as buyer, pays S D L T on fourteen million pounds, which is six hundred and eighty nine thousand, five hundred pounds. The leaseback is also a land transaction, with Tarnmoor Estates as the buyer of a new lease. Its rent has a net present value of about ten point three seven million pounds, and the S D L T on that rent would be about one hundred and fifty six thousand pounds.

Sale and leaseback relief, in section fifty seven A of the stamp duty land tax rules, removes that second charge. It exempts the leaseback when four things are true. One party sells a major interest in land to another. The buyer grants a lease back to the seller. The sale is made wholly or partly in return for the leaseback. And the only other consideration for the sale is money or the taking over of debt. One condition matters for groups. If both parties are companies, they must not be in the same S D L T group. Inside a group, group relief does that job instead, as Calder's lease showed.

Corporation tax has its own sale and leaseback rules, in Part nineteen of the Corporation Tax Act twenty ten, which I will call the twenty ten Act. They are also an awareness topic. Chapter one of Part nineteen applies where a company sells land and then pays rent for it. Its deduction for the rent is capped at a commercial rent, so a seller cannot take an inflated price now in return for an inflated, deductible rent later. Any excess is carried forward. Tarnmoor's rent was commercial, so no restriction arises.

Chapter two of Part nineteen aims at a different trick, turning a short lease into capital. It applies where a company assigns or surrenders a lease with fifty years or less to run for a capital sum and takes a new lease of fifteen years or less of the same land. Part of the capital sum is then taxed as income, sixteen minus the new term in years, over fifteen. Suppose, for this example, that Tarnmoor Estates had assigned a short lease for one million pounds and taken a ten year lease back. Six fifteenths, four hundred thousand pounds, would be taxed as income. The gain on the head office sale itself follows chapter sixteen's rules.

Scotland, the deal table and the examiner.

Three practical points close the teaching.

First, Scotland. Land there falls under land and buildings transaction tax, which I will call L B T T, not S D L T, and the exam lets candidates answer by reference to either. Its group relief, in Schedule ten to the Land and Buildings Transaction Tax, Scotland, Act twenty thirteen, has the same shape, clawback included. Revenue Scotland's guidance gives non-residential rates of nothing to one hundred and fifty thousand pounds, one per cent to two hundred and fifty thousand, and five per cent above.

Second, the tax function. Tom's team keeps a register of every intra-group land transfer, with its market value and a three-year diary date. Before any sale, demerger or refinancing, that register is checked. Arrangements are tested on the day of every group transfer, and the evidence is filed. Sale agreements carry indemnities for clawback, as Brennock's did. Earn-outs are drafted knowing that a cap fixes the duty. Intra-group share transfers claiming section forty two go to H M R C's stamp office before anyone treats them as done. And stamp duty is paid within thirty days of signing.

The price model needs the same discipline. A buyer who finds an unindemnified clawback in due diligence will simply take it off the price, so the seller is better off pricing it first.

One new relief belongs on the checklist. The Finance Act twenty twenty six gave a three-year holiday from S D R T for a company first listed on a United Kingdom market on or after the twenty seventh of November, twenty twenty five. There are exclusions for mergers, new holding companies and changes of control. Tarnwater was listing for the first time, and on this book's reading of H M R C's description, none of the exclusions fits a direct demerger.

Third, the examiner. Stamp taxes are tested as add-ons. After the November twenty twenty five sitting the examiners reported that few candidates considered S D L T in a twenty mark group property question. After November twenty twenty three, they noted that some confused stamp duty with S D L T. In November twenty twenty four, a buyer's stamp duty was worth three marks, for the rate, the thirty day deadline and the point that non-residents must pay. The habit to build is simple. Whenever shares or land move, ask which stamp tax applies, at what rate, who pays, and whether a relief or a clawback follows.

What to take away.

Stamp taxes follow their own logic, not the gains rules. A transfer the gains rules forgive can still cost S D L T, and a demerger or a sale of a subsidiary can bring back S D L T that a group transfer saved.

Stamp duty now taxes only documents transferring stock or marketable securities, at half a per cent, rounded up to the next five pounds, due within thirty days. The buyer pays, even a non-resident. Shares given as consideration count. Convertible loan notes lose the loan capital exemption. A capped earn-out is charged on its maximum, so Brennock paid two hundred and seventy thousand pounds on fifty four million pounds.

S D L T taxes land transactions. Non-residential rates are nothing, two and five per cent, with bands at one hundred and fifty thousand and two hundred and fifty thousand pounds. Leases are taxed on rent discounted at three and a half per cent. A transfer to a connected company is taxed on at least market value.

For stamp duty, section forty two of the Finance Act nineteen thirty gives group relief at seventy five per cent of shares, profits and assets, unless arrangements exist for a change of control of the transferee alone. Section seventy five covers reconstructions. Section seventy seven covers share-for-share acquisitions of the whole share capital, subject to the disqualifying arrangements test in section seventy seven A. That is why Helmside cost eighty thousand pounds.

For S D L T, Schedule seven gives group relief on the same seventy five per cent tests, with four guards. They are control arrangements, outside consideration, arrangements to leave, and a main purpose of avoiding any of the listed taxes, corporation tax included, as Tower One shows. Relief is withdrawn if the buyer leaves the group within three years while holding the land, on the market value at the original date.

In our invented case, group relief saved nine hundred and twenty three thousand, five hundred pounds. The demerger clawed back two hundred and eighty nine thousand, five hundred, and the actuators sale three hundred and sixty four thousand, five hundred. Only the Calder works saving of two hundred and sixty nine thousand, five hundred pounds stayed.

Reconstruction relief exempts, and acquisition relief caps the tax at half a per cent. Both are withdrawn on a change of control of the acquiring company within three years.

Sale and leaseback relief exempts the leaseback, but not between members of the same group. Part nineteen of the twenty ten Act caps rent deductions at a commercial rent, and taxes part of the capital sum when a short lease is sold and a lease of fifteen years or less is taken back.

So the answer to the chapter's question is this. Stamp taxes charge the entry ticket for shares and land. They forgive movements inside a group that stay inside it. And they take the forgiveness back when the asset, wrapped in a company, leaves within three years.
