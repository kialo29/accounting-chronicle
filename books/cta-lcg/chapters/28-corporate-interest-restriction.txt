Chapter twenty eight: The corporate interest restriction.

In October twenty fifteen the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development, the O E C D, published its final report on limiting interest deductions. It was Action four of the base erosion and profit shifting project. H M R C's manual still quotes its opening line. It is an empirical matter of fact that money is mobile and fungible.

That sentence is the whole problem in miniature. A pound borrowed in London can be spent in Lisbon. A pound lent by one group company to another is the same pound whichever company signs the loan. So a multinational group can choose where its interest falls, and it will naturally choose the country where a deduction is worth most. Transfer pricing can test whether each loan's terms are what strangers would agree. The unallowable purpose rule can test why a company borrowed. Neither asks the question this chapter answers. How much interest, in total, should the United Kingdom let one group deduct?

This is the third gate from the prologue. Chapter twelve taught the second, the unallowable purpose rule, and chapter twenty seven the first, transfer pricing. The corporate interest restriction is core on the paper. It appeared at four of the seven sittings from May twenty twenty three to May twenty twenty six, and twice it was the heart of a twenty mark question. The Living Law only signposted it, so we build it from the ground up.

The law is in Part ten of the Taxation, International and Other Provisions, Act twenty ten, which I will call the International Act, with the administration in Schedule seven A to the International Act. Finance Act twenty twenty six rebuilt the reporting company rules and added one item to the earnings measure. We apply the law for the financial year twenty twenty six.

Money is mobile and fungible.

The O E C D report identified three ways interest erodes a country's tax base. First, a group can load more of its outside borrowing into high-tax countries. Second, it can use intra-group loans to create deductions larger than anything the group actually pays to outside lenders. Third, it can borrow to fund income that is never taxed.

Each piece of the United Kingdom's rule answers one of those risks. A fixed ratio caps net interest at thirty per cent of earnings taxed in the United Kingdom, so borrowing has to bear some relation to taxable activity. A debt cap stops the United Kingdom deductions exceeding what the whole group really pays to outsiders. And the earnings measure counts only profits within the charge to United Kingdom corporation tax, so debt that funds exempt income buys no capacity.

The rule took effect for periods of account beginning on or after the first of April, twenty seventeen. It was inserted by the Finance Number Two Act twenty seventeen and replaced the worldwide debt cap, an older and narrower rule that compared United Kingdom interest with the group's external interest and nothing else.

Think of the restriction as a ration book. Each year the group receives interest coupons worth thirty per cent of its United Kingdom earnings. Coupons it does not spend can be kept for five years. Interest that cannot find a coupon is not destroyed. It waits in a queue, with no expiry date, until a later year has coupons to spare. Hold on to that picture, because every rule that follows is a detail of how the coupons are counted, who holds the queue, and who fills in the forms.

Two features make this regime different from everything in Part Two of this book. It works at the level of the group, not the company. And its answer, once found, must be pushed back down into individual company returns. A group computes one disallowance and then decides which companies bear it.

The group that counts.

The unit is the worldwide group. It is the ultimate parent and every subsidiary consolidated in its accounts under international accounting standards, wherever they are resident. It is not the seventy five per cent group of group relief, and it is not the gains group. Chapter one showed how many different guest lists a large group keeps. This is the one the accountants write.

Take our invented group, Tarnmoor. Everything about Tarnmoor, its companies, people and numbers, is made up for teaching. Tarnmoor plc is a listed engineering group with its head office in Leeds. Its worldwide group includes its United Kingdom trading, property, finance and holding companies, its subsidiaries in the invented countries of Vallaria and Marrovia, its Irish company, and even its dormant pumps company. Helmside Energy, the hydrogen joint venture in which Tarnmoor holds forty five per cent, sits outside it, because it is not consolidated.

The computation runs for the group's period of account, here Tarnmoor's calendar year. Only United Kingdom group companies contribute to the figures, meaning companies within the charge to corporation tax. The Vallarian company's interest and earnings count for nothing in the United Kingdom measures, though its results are part of the consolidated accounts the group measures come from.

When a company's accounting period does not match the group's period of account, its amounts are cut down to the part that falls in the group's period. The law calls the rest a disregarded period, and the split must be just and reasonable. H M R C's guidance says time apportionment will usually do.

Calder Valve Engineering shows the point. Tarnmoor bought it on the first of April in Group Year One, and Calder kept its thirty first of March year end for a while. Its earnings measure for its year to the following March was four point four million pounds. Nine months of that year fell inside Tarnmoor's period of account, so three point three million pounds went into the Group Year One figures and the rest into Group Year Two. Brackenwell Sensors, bought on the first of July in Group Year Two, counts only from that date. Its losses before then belong to its old owners' world, not Tarnmoor's.

Tax-interest, and the group's net figure.

The restriction bites on net interest. For each United Kingdom company we gather its tax-interest expense amounts and its tax-interest income amounts. The law gives three kinds. The first is loan relationship debits and credits, other than exchange gains and losses and impairment losses and their reversals. The second is derivative contract amounts on contracts whose subject matter is interest rates, price or income indices, currency or corporate debt. The third is the financing cost implicit in other arrangements, namely finance leases, debt factoring and service concession arrangements accounted for as financial liabilities.

That third category matters more than it sounds. H M R C's manual illustrates it with a lease. Where a lessee pays more over the term than the asset's cash price, the difference is a financing cost, and it counts as tax-interest for both lessor and lessee.

Each company nets its expense against its income. Add up every company with a net expense, take away every company with net income, and you have the aggregate net tax-interest expense. That is the figure the restriction tests. In what follows I will often call it simply the group's net interest.

Here is Tarnmoor's in Group Year One. Tarnmoor Finance, the group's treasury company, pays thirty point two five million pounds a year on five hundred and fifty million pounds of listed notes at five point five per cent. It lends on to group companies at six per cent. The interest that Tarnmoor Engineering, the property company and the water company pay it is United Kingdom interest on both sides, so it cancels out when the companies are added up. So does Tarnmoor plc's own borrowing from the finance company. What does not cancel is the seven point two million pounds Tarnmoor Finance receives from the Vallarian subsidiary, because the payer is outside the United Kingdom figures. Add the parent's bank facility, taken out to buy Calder, which cost one point six million pounds in Group Year One including its arrangement fee.

So the group's net interest is thirty point two five, less seven point two, plus one point six. Twenty four point six five million pounds. In Group Year Two the larger bank facility takes it to twenty five point eight two million pounds.

In Group Year Three a small new item arrives. Tarnmoor Engineering took a ten year finance lease of a refurbished machining centre from an outside lessor, as chapter nine described. Its finance charge in the first year is ninety thousand pounds. That is tax-interest. With the bank facility cut to twenty million pounds, the group's net interest in Group Year Three is twenty four point four four million pounds.

Two simplifications are worth saying aloud. The finance company's interest rate swap, which hedges its notes, would also produce tax-interest amounts. We leave its settlements out of every figure in this book, to keep the story readable. And interest a group pays to its own overseas companies counts just as much as interest paid to a bank, because the United Kingdom measure looks only at United Kingdom companies. That is precisely why the debt cap exists, as we will see.

Tax E B I T D A.

The capacity to deduct interest is measured against earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. The United Kingdom version is called tax E B I T D A, and it is built from tax numbers, not accounting numbers. For each United Kingdom company, start with the amounts that make up its taxable total profits. If it has a loss, use the amounts that would make up its profits if it had any. The result can be negative.

Then strip out four families of item.

First, tax-interest itself, both expense and income. Interest cannot count towards the earnings that measure interest.

Second, capital allowances and balancing charges. Full expensing on a new machine does not reduce tax E B I T D A, and a balancing charge does not increase it. This mirrors the depreciation that accounting E B I T D A ignores.

Third, intangibles debits, meaning amortisation, the four per cent write-down and losses on disposal. Realisation credits are removed only so far as they claw back earlier tax debits, which is the cost of the asset less its tax written down value. Amortisation in, amortisation out.

Fourth, reliefs that belong to other periods or other companies, and reliefs Parliament gives as incentives. Losses brought forward or carried back come out. So does group relief. So does a list the law calls qualifying tax reliefs. It includes the research and development expenditure credit and the additional deductions for research intensive companies, qualifying charitable donations, the patent box deduction, the creative sector reliefs and land remediation relief. The reason is simple. A relief that is meant to encourage something should not also shrink a group's interest capacity.

Finance Act twenty twenty six added one more exclusion. Since then the law also leaves out capital expenditure deducted under four special rules in the two thousand and nine Act, which is the Corporation Tax Act two thousand and nine. They cover contributions to flood and coastal erosion projects, preparing waste disposal sites, restoring sites and cemeteries and crematoria. The change has effect for periods of account ending on or after the thirty first of December, twenty twenty one, so it reaches back. A revised return taking account of it had effect if H M R C received it before the first of October, twenty twenty six.

Chargeable gains need care. H M R C's guidance says net chargeable gains in the company's profits count, after any capital losses actually set against them in the period. Capital losses that sit unused add nothing and take nothing away. A gain rolled over, or a transfer at no gain, no loss inside the gains group, contributes nothing, because nothing enters taxable profits.

Now the Tarnmoor numbers. In Group Year One the trading company, Tarnmoor Engineering, has tax E B I T D A of fifty million pounds. The water company has twelve million, the property company thirteen million, Calder three point three million and the Irish company three million. Two companies are negative. The finance company's running costs give it minus six hundred thousand pounds. Tarnmoor plc is minus five point nine million pounds, because its management expenses of eight million pounds count in full and its recharge income from Vallaria is only two point one million. Add them all and the aggregate is seventy four point eight million pounds.

The holding company's negative number surprises students. A management expense is an amount in taxable total profits, so it counts. What does not count is a management expense brought forward from an earlier year. That is a carried-forward amount, and it belongs to the year it arose in.

Group Year Three shows two harder points. Calder sold its process valve know-how and customer contracts to the Vallarian company for six million pounds. Those assets had no tax cost and had never produced a tax debit, so the whole six million pounds is a gain over cost. Nothing is clawed back, so nothing is excluded. The credit raises Calder's taxable profit and raises the group's interest capacity too. Calder's contribution for Group Year Three is eight point six million pounds, after restructuring costs of one point eight million.

The property company's contribution of fourteen point eight million pounds includes six hundred and fifty one thousand, two hundred pounds of net gains. Three hundred thousand of that is the part of its depot gain it kept. Five hundred thousand pounds of the depot gain had been reallocated to Tarnmoor Engineering by election and was matched there by a capital loss, so it adds nothing in either company. The rest is the gain on assigning a lease. Across the group, tax E B I T D A for Group Year Three is eighty seven point seven million pounds.

Thirty per cent, and the debt cap.

Now the coupons. The default method is the fixed ratio. The group's basic interest allowance is the lower of two numbers. One is thirty per cent of aggregate tax E B I T D A. The other is the fixed ratio debt cap.

The debt cap starts from the group's adjusted net group-interest expense. That is the net interest in the consolidated accounts, on broadly the same definitions as tax-interest, so it includes finance lease charges. Because it comes from the consolidated accounts, interest between group companies cancels out. What is left is what the group pays to the outside world. If the United Kingdom companies' net interest exceeds the whole group's external interest, the United Kingdom is being asked to relieve interest that, for the group as a whole, does not exist.

Tarnmoor's external interest in Group Year One is the thirty point two five million pounds on the notes plus the bank facility's one point six million. That is thirty one point eight five million pounds. Thirty per cent of its tax E B I T D A is twenty two point four four million. The lower figure, twenty two point four four million pounds, is its basic allowance. The debt cap is irrelevant for Tarnmoor. It bites on a different kind of group, one with little outside debt that funds its United Kingdom subsidiary with large intra-group loans. Such a subsidiary can be restricted well below thirty per cent.

The debt cap has a refinement that examiners like. A group can carry forward excess debt cap, the amount by which its debt cap exceeds thirty per cent of tax E B I T D A. It goes forward only to the next period, where it is added to that period's debt cap. And it is limited. The carry-forward limit is the excess brought forward plus the total disallowed amount of the current period. In plain terms, a group only banks spare debt cap in a year when the thirty per cent rule is actually restricting it. Tarnmoor generated two point two one million pounds of excess debt cap in Group Year One and four point five one million in Group Year Two. That cushion never mattered, because thirty per cent was always the lower number. The full working is in the reading edition.

Next, the interest allowance is the basic allowance plus any aggregate net tax-interest income, which arises only for a group whose United Kingdom companies are net lenders overall. Tarnmoor has none.

Finally, interest capacity is the interest allowance plus any unused allowance brought forward from earlier periods. Capacity is never less than the de minimis amount of two million pounds a year, adjusted for a longer or shorter period of account. If the group's net interest exceeds its capacity, the excess is the total disallowed amount.

The floor that is not a bonus.

The two million pound de minimis is the most misunderstood number in the regime. Students write that every group gets two million pounds of interest free of restriction and then thirty per cent on top. That is wrong.

The law says interest capacity is the allowance plus any available unused allowance, but never less than two million pounds. It is a floor, not an extra. A group whose thirty per cent figure is twenty two million pounds gets twenty two million pounds of capacity, not twenty four.

The misunderstanding persists because the de minimis behaves like an allowance for small groups. If the group's net interest is under two million pounds, nothing is disallowed, whatever its earnings. Most groups never think about the regime beyond that.

So suppose, for this example, a United Kingdom group with net interest of two point five million pounds and tax E B I T D A of five million pounds. Thirty per cent of its earnings is one point five million. Its capacity is raised to the floor of two million pounds. It loses five hundred thousand pounds of deductions. It does not escape, and its loss is not one million pounds either.

A small group under the floor still has housekeeping to do. It should keep evidence that its net interest is below two million pounds. And it may want to file a return anyway, to bank unused allowance for a later year when its interest rises. Unused allowance only exists in law if a return records it, as we will see.

The group ratio and the other elections.

Thirty per cent is a blunt number. Some groups are genuinely highly geared. A property group or an infrastructure business may borrow heavily from banks against stable income, and its real external interest may be far more than thirty per cent of its earnings. The group ratio method is the safety valve for them.

Under the group ratio, the group's own ratio replaces thirty per cent. The ratio is the qualifying net group-interest expense divided by group E B I T D A. Qualifying net group-interest expense is the external interest figure with some items taken out, notably interest on related party debt, results-dependent securities and equity notes. That stops a group inflating its ratio by borrowing from its own shareholders. Group E B I T D A is the consolidated profit before tax, interest, depreciation and amortisation. If the ratio comes out negative or above one hundred per cent, or group E B I T D A is nil, it is taken as one hundred per cent.

The group ratio percentage is then applied to aggregate tax E B I T D A, and the result is capped by a group ratio debt cap, which works like the fixed ratio debt cap but on the qualifying figure. The method needs an election, made in the interest restriction return for the period.

For Tarnmoor the group ratio is no help. Its group E B I T D A in Group Year One is one hundred and seventy million pounds. Its external interest, all paid to unconnected lenders, is thirty one point eight five million. The ratio is about eighteen point seven four per cent, well under thirty. Applied to the United Kingdom earnings it would give about fourteen million pounds of allowance against the fixed ratio's twenty two point four four million. The pattern repeats in the next two years. An engineering group with modest gearing is exactly the group the fixed ratio was designed for.

Other elections adjust the group figures. The blended group ratio election helps where the group has investors in joint ventures with different gearing. The group E B I T D A chargeable gains election measures gains on a basis closer to the United Kingdom rules, and H M R C's guidance says it is irrevocable. There are elections for an alternative calculation of certain items, for non-consolidated investments and for consolidated partnerships. For the exam, know that they exist, know that each is made through the return, and do not compute them unless asked.

Tarnmoor's first restriction.

Now put it together for Group Year One.

The group's net interest is twenty four point six five million pounds. Its tax E B I T D A is seventy four point eight million, and thirty per cent of that is twenty two point four four million. The debt cap is thirty one point eight five million, so it does not bite. There is no net interest income to add and, in our invented case, nothing brought forward from earlier years. So the interest capacity is twenty two point four four million pounds, comfortably above the two million pound floor.

Twenty four point six five less twenty two point four four. The total disallowed amount is two point two one million pounds. At twenty five per cent, that is five hundred and fifty two thousand, five hundred pounds of extra tax for the year, unless and until the interest comes back.

Group Year Two repeats the pattern. The bank facility grows to fund the purchase of Brackenwell, so net interest rises to twenty five point eight two million pounds. Tax E B I T D A rises to seventy eight point four million, giving an allowance of twenty three point five two million. The disallowance is two point three million pounds. By the end of Group Year Two, four point five one million pounds of interest is waiting in the queue.

Notice what the restriction is really measuring. Tarnmoor's interest has not grown much. Its earnings have. The problem is that the group borrowed in the United Kingdom to fund, among other things, a Vallarian subsidiary. The seven point two million pounds that subsidiary pays back is outside the United Kingdom figures, but so are its earnings. The thirty per cent rule does not ask whether the borrowing was sensible. It asks whether the United Kingdom earnings can carry it.

Who bears the disallowance.

The group has one disallowance. The companies have tax returns. Something must connect them.

In a full interest restriction return, the reporting company allocates the total disallowed amount among the United Kingdom companies. Each allocated amount must be within that company's own net tax-interest expense, and the allocations must add up to the total. A company with net interest income cannot be given any. Each company then leaves the allocated amount of tax-interest expense out of its corporation tax computation.

Within a company there is a default order. Non-trading loan relationship debits go first, then non-trading derivative debits, then trading loan relationship debits, then trading derivative debits, and finally the financing costs of finance leases, factoring and service concessions. A company can elect for a different order.

If there is no reporting company, or no full return, the law does the allocating itself. The disallowance is spread pro rata across the companies with net tax-interest expense. There is one more safety valve. A company that did not consent to the reporting company's appointment can elect to have the pro rata share instead of the reporting company's allocation.

Tarnmoor's reporting company is the parent, Tarnmoor plc, and it allocates the whole disallowance to its own non-trading loan relationship debits. Two point two one million pounds in Group Year One and two point three million in Group Year Two. Why there?

Run the alternative. Pro rata, Tarnmoor Engineering would bear almost a million pounds of the first year's disallowance, the water company nearly two hundred thousand, the property company about three hundred and forty thousand and the parent about seven hundred and ten thousand. In the year itself the group's tax would be the same, because every company pays the main rate and losses flow freely by group relief. The difference is what happens next.

Disallowed interest carries forward at company level, with no time limit. But it is lost if the company's trade or investment business ceases or becomes small or negligible. And it stays with the company. The water company is to be demerged and listed on its own in Group Year Five. Any disallowance parked there would leave the Tarnmoor group with it. Tarnmoor plc, the listed parent, will be there for as long as the group is. So the queue is kept in the one company certain to stay.

The allocation has a knock-on effect, which chapter fourteen recorded. Tarnmoor plc's non-trading debits of eight point eight million pounds in Group Year One are cut by the disallowed two point two one million, so its non-trading deficit is six point five nine million pounds. That is the amount it surrenders to Tarnmoor Engineering as group relief. In Group Year Two the deficit is seven point six seven million pounds. The restriction comes first, and the deficit is what remains.

Coming back.

Group Year Three is the year the queue moves.

Tax E B I T D A jumps to eighty seven point seven million pounds, helped by Calder's six million pound know-how sale. Thirty per cent of that is twenty six point three one million. The group's net interest falls to twenty four point four four million pounds, because the bank facility has been paid down, even after the ninety thousand pounds of finance charge on Tarnmoor Engineering's new lease. The group now has one point eight seven million pounds of spare allowance.

The law says that where a group's interest allowance exceeds its net interest, and companies hold disallowed amounts from earlier periods, those amounts are reactivated, up to the interest reactivation cap. The cap is this year's allowance less this year's net interest. Notice that it is measured against this year's allowance only. Unused allowance brought forward can cover current interest, but it cannot reactivate old disallowances.

Reactivation is not optional. H M R C's guidance says a group must reactivate at the earliest opportunity and cannot save the spare capacity for a later year. A group cannot have both disallowances and reactivations in the same period of account. And reactivation needs a full return, with a statement of allocated reactivations naming the companies.

So in Group Year Three Tarnmoor plc reactivates one point eight seven million pounds of its own queue. The reactivated amount is treated as a tax-interest expense of Tarnmoor plc in that year. It does not count a second time in the group's net interest. Tarnmoor plc's non-trading debits for the year rise from eight point five million pounds to ten point three seven million, and the extra deficit is worth four hundred and sixty seven thousand, five hundred pounds of tax at twenty five per cent. Two point six four million pounds stays in the queue for a later year.

Does any unused allowance arise? H M R C's guidance says the year's spare capacity must first be applied to reactivate, and on the reading this book takes of the statute none is left. All the spare capacity was used. So nothing is banked for the future.

Had there been nothing to reactivate, Tarnmoor would have generated one point eight seven million pounds of unused interest allowance. Unused allowance is available in later periods for up to five years, and the law apportions it by time where a later period straddles the five year point. But it exists only if the group files a full return for the period it arises in, and every period in between. If an abbreviated return election has effect for any of those periods, or no return is made, the unused allowance is nil.

There is a deferred tax question too. Two point six four million pounds of interest waiting to be reactivated is a deductible item. Whether the group recognises an asset for it depends on whether future spare capacity is probable, which is the judgement chapter six described. At the end of Group Year Two Tarnmoor recognised nothing. That remains a judgement, not a rule.

The reporting company and the return.

All of this runs through one document, the interest restriction return, filed by one company, the reporting company. The rules for appointing it were rewritten by Finance Act twenty twenty six, and the story of why is worth knowing.

Under the original rules, a reporting company had to be appointed by notice to H M R C within twelve months of the end of the period of account, authorised by at least half of the eligible companies. An appointment then continued for later periods. Groups that missed the deadline had no reporting company. Without one there could be no full return, so no allocation of their choice, no reactivation and no election. For a while H M R C would appoint a reporting company for a group that had missed the deadline. In June twenty twenty three it announced that it would do so only in limited circumstances. The professional bodies, including the Institute of Chartered Accountants in England and Wales, made representations, and H M R C reconsidered. At the Budget in November twenty twenty five the government announced a legislative fix, which became section sixty one of Finance Act twenty twenty six.

The new rules apply to periods of account ending on or after the thirty first of March, twenty twenty six. A reporting company is appointed for each period of account separately. There is no time limit and no notice to H M R C. The appointment must be authorised by more than half of the eligible companies, meaning United Kingdom group companies at some time in the period that were not dormant throughout it. The details, including the list of companies that authorised it, are to go in the return itself, from a date H M R C sets by regulations. And if a company files a return before it has been properly appointed, a later appointment can regularise it, treated as made just before the return. That regularisation reaches back further, to periods of account ending on or after the thirty first of March, twenty twenty four.

Two wording traps. More than half is not the same as at least half. And the old rules still govern periods ending on or before the thirtieth of March, twenty twenty six. So, as a real calendar example, a group with a thirty first of December, twenty twenty five year end still needed an appointment by notice, within twelve months, from at least half of its companies. A group whose period of account ended on the thirty first of March, twenty twenty six is under the new rules.

Is a return compulsory? Under the new rules, a reporting company appointed by the group may submit a return. One appointed by H M R C must. H M R C's guidance says a return is needed to allocate disallowances to particular companies, to carry forward unused allowance, to reactivate, or to make an election. A group with no disallowance and nothing to carry forward may therefore file nothing at all. A group that wants to keep its options open should file.

If no return has been submitted eighteen months after the end of the period of account, H M R C may appoint a reporting company itself. The filing date is twelve months after the end of the period of account, or three months after an H M R C appointment if later. A return has no effect if it arrives more than thirty six months after the period ends.

There are two kinds of return. A full return contains the whole calculation, the allocations and any elections. An abbreviated return contains only basic information, and is for groups that know they are not restricted. Electing for an abbreviated return has a cost already mentioned. It makes the group's unused allowance for that period nil.

Penalties are flat. A late return costs five hundred pounds if it is within three months of the filing date, and one thousand pounds otherwise. Finance Act twenty twenty six added a new one thousand pound penalty where a company submits a return without having been appointed. There is no penalty if the company is appointed within eighteen months of the end of the period, if it told H M R C unprompted, or if it has a reasonable excuse.

In our invented case, Tarnmoor's head of tax, Tom Hesketh, treats the appointment as part of the year-end routine. The parent's board paper lists every eligible United Kingdom company, each company's board authorises the parent, and the parent files a full return every year. It needs to. It has disallowances to allocate in Group Years One and Two, and a reactivation to claim in Group Year Three. Once the return is in, each company's own corporation tax return must reflect its allocated disallowance or reactivation, and any difference is corrected by amendment.

Infrastructure.

Some businesses are financed almost entirely by debt by design. A toll road, a power network or a hospital built under a public contract borrows against decades of predictable income. Thirty per cent of earnings would cripple them, and the debt is not the kind of base erosion the rule was aimed at. The public infrastructure exemption takes them out.

A company can elect to be a qualifying infrastructure company. Broadly, all or all but an insignificant part of its income must come from public infrastructure assets, and the same must be true of its assets. It must be fully within the charge to United Kingdom tax. Interest it pays to third parties is then left out of tax-interest, provided the lender's recourse is limited to the company's own income, assets, shares or debt. Loans between qualifying infrastructure companies can qualify too. In return, the company's tax E B I T D A is treated as nil.

On H M R C's guidance the election must be made before the end of the accounting period it is to cover. Once made, it cannot be revoked with effect for a period beginning within five years of the start of the first period it covered.

Here is the point that cost marks in May twenty twenty six. When a qualifying infrastructure company sits inside a larger group, both sides of the computation change. Its interest comes out of the group's net interest. Its earnings come out of tax E B I T D A.

Suppose, for this example, that after Tarnmoor takes control of Helmside in Group Year Five, its hydrogen plant met the statutory tests, and Helmside elected. Suppose it paid two point eight million pounds of interest on limited recourse bank debt and had tax E B I T D A of six million pounds. The group's net interest would fall by two point eight million. Its earnings would fall by six million, and its thirty per cent allowance by one point eight million. Net headroom would improve by one million pounds. Whether a real plant meets the tests is a question of fact, and this one is purely an illustration.

Where the restriction meets other rules.

The interest restriction comes last. Transfer pricing first fixes the arm's length amount of each loan and its interest. The unallowable purpose rule then removes debits that serve a tax avoidance purpose. The hybrid rules counteract mismatches. What survives is the tax-interest the restriction tests. Disallowed amounts under those earlier rules are simply not brought into account, so they never reach the group's net interest. The transfer pricing code makes the order explicit from the other side. In deciding whether there is a potential United Kingdom tax advantage, the interest restriction is disregarded.

Then there is the trap the examiners reported after May twenty twenty six. Very few candidates adjusted both figures correctly. The rule is this. A transfer pricing adjustment to interest changes tax-interest, so it moves the group's net interest, but it does not change tax E B I T D A, which excludes interest anyway. A transfer pricing adjustment to a service charge changes ordinary profit, so it does move tax E B I T D A.

Tarnmoor has examples of both. In Group Year One Tarnmoor plc charged the Vallarian company one point six million pounds for head office services that should, at arm's length, have cost two point one million. The five hundred thousand pound adjustment raised the parent's taxable profit, so it raised tax E B I T D A by the same amount, and the allowance by one hundred and fifty thousand pounds. The adjustment cost one hundred and twenty five thousand pounds of tax. It also shrank the disallowance from what would have been two point three six million pounds to two point two one million. Looking ahead, Tarnmoor plc's twenty million pound interest-free loan to its Irish company becomes cross-border when the Irish company migrates in Group Year Four. Transfer pricing then imputes interest at six per cent. That is tax-interest income for the parent. It reduces the group's net interest, six hundred thousand pounds for the second half of Group Year Four and one point two million a year after that, and leaves tax E B I T D A untouched.

Leases are the next join. Since twenty nineteen, lessees have brought most leases onto the balance sheet, and Parliament adjusted this regime to match. Chapter five reported the profession's reading of the result. A lease's financing cost is tax-interest where the lease would have been a finance lease under the old accounting classification, and not where it would have been an operating lease. Tarnmoor Engineering's machining centre lease is a finance lease on any view, which is why its ninety thousand pounds counted in Group Year Three.

Flood defences show the newest exclusion at work. Chapter nine followed the property company's flood wall. Its structures and buildings allowance is a capital allowance, so it never touched tax E B I T D A, and nor did the local authority's grant. Now suppose, for this example, that Tarnmoor Engineering instead contributed three hundred thousand pounds to an Environment Agency flood scheme protecting its works. That contribution is deductible under the special rule for flood and coastal erosion projects. Before Finance Act twenty twenty six it would have cut tax E B I T D A, and so the allowance, by ninety thousand pounds. Now it is excluded.

Two more joins in a sentence each. The finance company exemption in the controlled foreign company rules looks at the same group net interest figure, as chapter twenty six explains. And deferred tax follows any carried-forward disallowance, as we saw.

Finally, the regime has its own anti-avoidance rule. Where one of the main purposes of arrangements is to obtain a tax advantage, and the advantage comes from leaving tax-interest out of account, reducing a restriction, increasing a reactivation or shifting either into a different period, the advantage is counteracted by just and reasonable adjustments. H M R C's guidance adds that the rule does not apply if another rule already neutralises the advantage.

How the examiner tests it.

The corporate interest restriction is core on the twenty twenty six grid, which governs Kian's likely sitting in May twenty twenty seven, and it has appeared at four of the last seven sittings. It falls to non-core only from twenty twenty eight.

May twenty twenty six was the big one. Question one, for twenty marks, combined a merger forming a new worldwide group, the administration of the reporting company and the return, the public infrastructure exemption, and a fixed ratio calculation with transfer pricing adjustments and a qualifying infrastructure company to carve out. The administration was answered adequately but padded. The calculation was poor. Very few candidates adjusted the group's net interest and tax E B I T D A correctly. Some computed the group ratio when told not to. Some wrongly adjusted tax E B I T D A for the disallowed interest.

May twenty twenty four set a calculation inside a twenty mark question about a parent company, with an interest-free loan, transfer pricing and reactivation of a brought-forward disallowance, plus administration. A minority mixed up the group's tax-interest figure with its external interest figure. The same sitting put an allowance brought forward into a computation question. November twenty twenty four gave three and a half marks, inside a question on a newly acquired company, for saying that there was probably no restriction but that a return could protect the position, with a reporting company and an abbreviated return. May twenty twenty three set transfer pricing and the interest restriction together on a loan from an American company, and the examiners noted that time was wasted on diverted profits tax.

The style is technical prose and computations, half a mark to a mark per point. The layout the marker wants follows the statutory steps. Group net interest, company by company. Tax E B I T D A, company by company. Thirty per cent. Debt cap. Allowance, capacity, the two million pound floor. Disallowance or reactivation. Allocation. The reading edition sets out the template.

The traps follow from what we have covered. The two million pounds is a floor, not a bonus. Disallowed interest waits forever, but unused allowance dies after five years and needs full returns. An interest transfer pricing adjustment moves the net interest figure, not the earnings figure. Under Finance Act twenty twenty six the appointment is per period, with no deadline and more than half of the companies. And do not pad. Administration answers lose time, not marks, when they wander.

What to take away.

The corporate interest restriction answers one question. How much net interest should the United Kingdom let a whole group deduct? It is the United Kingdom's version of the O E C D's Action four, and it has applied to periods of account beginning on or after the first of April, twenty seventeen.

The unit is the worldwide group, the ultimate parent and its consolidated subsidiaries, for the group's period of account. Only United Kingdom companies contribute, and a company whose accounting period straddles the group's period contributes the just and reasonable share, usually by time.

The tested figure is the aggregate net tax-interest expense. Tax-interest covers loan relationship amounts, without exchange movements or impairment, interest rate and similar derivative amounts, and the financing cost in finance leases, factoring and service concessions. Interest between United Kingdom companies cancels out. For Tarnmoor it was twenty four point six five million pounds in Group Year One, twenty five point eight two million in Group Year Two and twenty four point four four million in Group Year Three.

The earnings measure is tax E B I T D A, built from taxable profits and excluding interest, capital allowances, intangibles debits and clawback credits, losses from other periods, group relief and qualifying reliefs such as the expenditure credit, charitable donations and the patent box deduction. Since Finance Act twenty twenty six it also excludes capital expenditure deducted under the flood defence and three similar rules. Tarnmoor's was seventy four point eight, seventy eight point four and eighty seven point seven million pounds.

The fixed ratio allowance is the lower of thirty per cent of tax E B I T D A and the debt cap, which is the group's external net interest plus excess debt cap from the previous period. The group ratio is an election for highly geared groups. Interest capacity is the allowance plus unused allowance brought forward, never less than two million pounds.

Tarnmoor lost two point two one million pounds of deductions in Group Year One and two point three million in Group Year Two. Its parent, as reporting company, allocated both to its own non-trading debits, keeping the queue in the company that will stay. In Group Year Three it reactivated one point eight seven million pounds, leaving two point six four million waiting, with no unused allowance created.

Disallowed interest carries forward without limit, unless the business ceases or becomes small or negligible. Unused allowance lasts five years, and only with full returns. Reactivation needs a full return and must be taken at the first opportunity.

Under Finance Act twenty twenty six, for periods of account ending on or after the thirty first of March, twenty twenty six, a reporting company is appointed for each period, with no deadline and no notice, by more than half of the eligible companies. The return is due twelve months after the period ends. Late returns cost five hundred pounds or one thousand pounds, and an unappointed filer can face one thousand pounds.

Transfer pricing and the other interest rules come first. An interest adjustment moves the net interest figure. A services adjustment moves tax E B I T D A. A qualifying infrastructure company comes out of both.

So how much interest should the United Kingdom let one group deduct? About thirty per cent of what the group earns here, never more than it really pays to outsiders, and never less than two million pounds. Everything else is the machinery for counting that fairly across many companies and many years.
