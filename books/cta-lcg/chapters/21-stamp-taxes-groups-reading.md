# Chapter twenty one: Stamp taxes for groups.

## A bill the gains rules never sent.

On 1 July GY5, our invented group, Tarnmoor, did something large and quiet. (Everything about Tarnmoor is invented for teaching.) Its parent, Tarnmoor plc (TPLC), a listed engineering group with its head office in Leeds, distributed its water systems subsidiary to its own shareholders. Tarnmoor Water Systems Ltd (TWS) left the group and listed on its own as Tarnwater plc.

The corporation tax work had taken months, and it came out clean. The demerger was exempt (CTA 2010 s 1076). TPLC's disposal of the TWS shares fell within the substantial shareholding exemption. Even the factory that Tarnmoor Estates Ltd (TES) had moved into TWS 21 months earlier escaped the degrouping charge, because TCGA 1992 s 192(3) switches s 179 off for an exempt demerger (chapters 17 and 19).

Then Tom Hesketh, the group head of tax, opened a different file. When that factory moved inside the group, nobody paid SDLT on its £6.0m value, because group relief applied. That relief comes with a string: if the company that received the land leaves the group within three years, the relief is withdrawn. Tarnwater had just left. It now owed **£289,500**.

Nobody had done anything wrong. The clawback was the price of the relief, and it had been written into the law all along. It simply lived in a different statute from the one everyone had been reading.

That bill is the misconception this chapter exists to correct: **"if the gains rules forgive a group transfer, the stamp taxes forgive it too."** They do not. The stamp taxes have their own groups, their own purpose tests and their own clock. The question for this chapter: **when shares or land move into, around and out of a large group, what do the stamp taxes charge, what do they forgive, and when do they take the forgiveness back?**

This is chapter 21, the last chapter of Part Three (the group). On the LCG Advanced Technical paper, stamp duty and SDLT are **awareness (grade 3)** topics. Yet the examiners keep reporting that stamp tax marks sit at the end of property and acquisition questions, and candidates walk past them. *The Living Law* (TKS), chapter 30, taught the basics (stamp duty, SDRT, SDLT, group relief and clawback). Here we take them into the group. Throughout, the rules are those in force for **FY2026**, under Finance Act 2026.

## Two taxes, one border.

Start with the shape of the system, because the groups rules only make sense once you see the border they guard.

**Stamp duty** is a tax on documents. Since SDLT took over land on **1 December 2003** (the implementation date appointed by Treasury order under FA 2003 Sch 19), stamp duty applies only to instruments relating to **stock or marketable securities** (FA 2003 s 125, restricting FA 1999 Sch 13). In a group that means, in practice, a stock transfer form for shares.

| Stamp duty and SDRT (FY2026) | Rule | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Rate on a transfer on sale | 0.5% of the consideration, **rounded up to the next £5** | FA 1999 Sch 13 |
| Small transfers | Nil where the consideration is £1,000 or less (with certificate) | FA 1999 Sch 13 |
| Timing | Due within **30 days** of execution; interest and penalties after that | Stamp Act 1891 (late stamping) |
| Who pays | In practice the purchaser (including a **non-resident** buying UK shares) | — |
| SDRT | 0.5% on **agreements** to transfer chargeable securities (paperless trades); **1.5%** on certain transfers into depositary receipt and clearance systems; cancelled (repaid) if a duly stamped transfer follows within **6 years** | FA 1986 ss 87, 92, 93, 96 |
| Loan capital | Transfers of ordinary loan capital exempt, **but not** loan capital carrying a conversion right at execution | FA 1986 s 79(4), (5) |

**SDLT** is the tax on land transactions in England and Northern Ireland, in **FA 2003 Part 4**. It taxes transactions, not documents.

| SDLT (non-residential, FY2026) | Band | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Consideration other than rent | £0 – £150,000 | 0% |
| | £150,001 – £250,000 | 2% |
| | Above £250,000 | 5% |
| Rent on a new lease (NPV at the **3.5%** temporal discount rate, FA 2003 Sch 5) | £0 – £150,000 | 0% |
| | £150,001 – £5,000,000 | 1% |
| | Above £5,000,000 | 2% |
| Return | Within **14 days** of the effective date | FA 2003 Part 4 |
| VAT | Part of the chargeable consideration | FA 2003 Sch 4 para 2 |

**The border.** Buy a warehouse and you pay SDLT at up to 5%. Buy the shares in the company that owns the warehouse and you pay stamp duty at 0.5%. The same economic asset carries two very different prices of entry. That gap drives almost every rule in this chapter: if a group could move land into a fresh subsidiary free of tax and then sell the subsidiary's shares, it could turn a 5% tax into a 0.5% one at will ("enveloping"). Group relief exists so that genuine reorganisations are not taxed; its guards and its clawback exist so that it cannot be used as a wrapper.

## Stamp duty on Tarnmoor's share deals.

The story gives us five share purchases, and each teaches one point.

1. **Calder (1 April GY1).** TPLC bought Calder Valve Engineering Ltd (CVE) from the Oldroyd family for **£32.0m** cash. Stamp duty **£160,000**, paid by TPLC within 30 days. It is an incidental cost of acquiring the shares for chargeable gains (TCGA 1992 s 38(2)); as chapter 13 explained, it is **not** an expense of management.
2. **Brackenwell (1 July GY2).** TPLC bought Brackenwell Sensors Ltd (BSL) for **£22.2m**: stamp duty **£111,000**. It also bought BSL's convertible notes from the venture investors for **£1.8m** (chapter 12). Transfers of ordinary loan capital are exempt (FA 1986 s 79(4)), but **s 79(5)** removes the exemption where, at execution, the loan capital carries a right of conversion into shares. HMRC's view (STSM041070, STSM021230) is that the right must be exercisable by the holder. BSL's notes gave the holders that right, so the notes cost a further **£9,000**: **£120,000** in all.
3. **Helmside (1 March GY5).** TPLC bought the 40% of Helmside Energy Ltd held by Greyfell Utilities plc, its joint venture partner, paying **2,000,000 new TPLC shares at £8.00 = £16.0m**. Consideration in shares counts like cash: stamp duty **£80,000**. Share-for-share relief does not apply (next section).
4. **Tarnmoor Actuators (31 December GY6).** Brennock Industries Inc (invented, US) bought Tarnmoor Actuators Ltd (TAL) from Tarnmoor Engineering Ltd (TEL) for **£48.0m** cash plus a cash **earn-out of up to £6.0m** (valued at £3.0m at completion). Brennock is non-resident, but the duty is on the document transferring UK shares, so Brennock pays.
5. **Ridgeway (some years later).** TPLC buys Ridgeway Holdings Ltd from Jess Hartley (invented; the founder whose cycle business *The Living Law* followed) for **£4.5m**: stamp duty **£22,500** (chapter 31).

**Contingent consideration.** HMRC's Stamp Taxes on Shares Manual (STSM021120, citing the House of Lords in *Underground Electric Railways*) applies the **contingency principle**. Duty is fixed at execution:

| Form of contingent consideration | Charged on |
|---|---|
| Stated maximum (with or without a minimum) | The **maximum** |
| Minimum only | The minimum |
| Stated sum that may vary | The stated sum |
| Wholly unascertainable | Nothing |

Duty charged under the principle is **not adjusted** when the contingency resolves, and HMRC's "wait and see" practice for completion-account adjustments does not apply to consideration that depends on future events.

> |---|---|---|---|
> | Calder shares (GY1) | 32,000,000 | 160,000 | TPLC |
> | BSL shares (GY2) | 22,200,000 | 111,000 | TPLC |
> | BSL convertible notes (GY2) | 1,800,000 | 9,000 | TPLC |
> | Helmside 40% (GY5): 2,000,000 × £8.00 | 16,000,000 | 80,000 | TPLC |
> | TAL shares (GY6): 48,000,000 + earn-out maximum 6,000,000 | 54,000,000 | **270,000** | Brennock |

> **The TAL trap.** Duty on the cash alone would be £240,000; on cash plus the earn-out's £3.0m value, £255,000. Both are wrong: the earn-out is capped, so the cap is charged. If Brennock eventually pays only £2.0m of earn-out, nothing comes back.

## Reliefs when shares move inside a group.

Stamp duty has three reliefs a group adviser must know.

**1. Group relief (FA 1930 s 42).** A transfer between **associated bodies corporate** is free of duty. They are associated if one is the beneficial owner of at least **75% of the other's ordinary share capital** (directly or indirectly) and is beneficially entitled to at least **75% of its profits available for distribution and of its assets on a winding up**, or a third body corporate stands in that relationship to both. The profits and assets tests were added by FA 2000. Relief is **denied** if, at execution, **arrangements exist under which a person could obtain control of the transferee but not of the transferor** (the stamp duty version of the wrapper problem; ss 42A and 42B qualify the test). Relief is not self-assessed: the instrument must be **adjudicated or stamped as not chargeable** to count as duly stamped.

*For this example, suppose* (labelled hypothetical, not story) that TPLC had transferred its Calder shares to TEL for £32.0m with no sale in prospect: s 42 would have removed £160,000 of duty.

**2. Reconstruction relief (FA 1986 s 75).** No duty where a company acquires all or part of another company's **undertaking** in pursuance of a **scheme for the reconstruction** of that target, and the consideration is **non-redeemable shares issued to all the target's shareholders** (plus at most the assumption or discharge of liabilities). Afterwards each shareholder of each company must be a shareholder of the other, in the same proportions. Bona fide commercial reasons and no main purpose of avoiding stamp duty, income tax, corporation tax or CGT are required. The old 0.5% acquisition relief (**s 76**) was **repealed by FA 2012**.

**3. Share-for-share relief (FA 1986 s 77).** No duty where an acquiring company acquires the **whole issued share capital** of a target, the consideration consists **only** of the issue of the acquirer's shares to the target's shareholders, the share classes and proportions afterwards **mirror** those before, and the commercial and no-avoidance conditions are met. For instruments executed on or after **29 June 2016**, **s 77A** denies relief where there are **disqualifying arrangements** at execution: arrangements of which it is reasonable to assume that a purpose is to secure that a particular person, or particular persons together, obtain **control** of the acquiring company (CTA 2010 s 1124). FA 2020 amended the definition (instruments executed on or after 22 July 2020) so that a person holding at least 25% of the target throughout the relevant period is left out of account; "relevant merger arrangements" are carved out. HMRC's manual has worked examples on capital reduction demergers (STSM042520–042560). The classic use of s 77 is **inserting a new holding company** on top of a group.

**Helmside.** TPLC already held 45%; it bought 40% from Greyfell; Northlight Infrastructure Fund LP kept its 15%. TPLC did not acquire the whole issued share capital, only one of the three shareholders received consideration shares, and the holdings could not mirror. Section 77 was never in play: **£80,000** payable.

> **Going further: share deals in a group**
> - **Base cost.** Stamp duty paid on an acquisition is an incidental cost (TCGA 1992 s 38(2)). For a holding later sold within the SSE the point rarely matters; for a portfolio stake it does.
> - **Convertibles and options.** Check every debt instrument in a share purchase for conversion rights before assuming the loan capital exemption.
> - **Earn-outs.** A cap fixes the duty at the cap on day one. Commercial terms come first, but the deal model should show the duty on the maximum.
> - **Listed securities.** HMRC's manual notes a market value rule (from 29 October 2018) for certain transfers of listed securities to connected companies: check it on any intra-group transfer of quoted shares outside s 42.
> - **New holding companies.** A s 77 claim needs the s 77A arrangements test documented at execution; the June 2026 distributions consultation's proposals on new holding companies (chapter 19) are **proposals, not law**.

## Group relief for land, and its guards.

Land moving inside a group meets SDLT, and it meets a rule that makes the tax bite even on a gift.

**Connected companies (FA 2003 s 53).** Where the purchaser is a company and the vendor is connected with it (CTA 2010 s 1122), the chargeable consideration is **at least market value** (and, for a lease, at least the rent). A group company that hands a building to a sister for nothing is taxed on its full value. But s 53(4) keeps the reliefs alive, and the most important is group relief.

**Group relief (FA 2003 Sch 7 para 1).** A land transaction is exempt if the vendor and the purchaser are **companies in the same group** at the effective date: one the 75% subsidiary of the other, or both 75% subsidiaries of a third company, measured by **ordinary share capital, profits and assets** (≥ 75% of each).

**The guards (Sch 7 para 2).** Relief is **not available** if:

| Guard | Test at the effective date |
|---|---|
| Control arrangements (para 2(1)) | Arrangements exist for a person (or persons together) to obtain **control of the purchaser but not the vendor** (with a carve-out for an acquisition qualifying for FA 1986 s 75) |
| Outside consideration (para 2(2)(a)) | The consideration is provided or received, directly or indirectly, by a person **outside the group** under arrangements connected with the transaction |
| Leaving arrangements (para 2(2)(b)) | Arrangements exist for the **purchaser to cease to be a member of the same group** as the vendor |
| Purpose (para 2(4A)) | The transaction is not effected for **bona fide commercial reasons**, or forms part of arrangements a main purpose of which is the **avoidance of tax** (SDLT, stamp duty, income tax, **corporation tax**, CGT) |

**The purpose guard has teeth.** In *Tower One St George Wharf Ltd v HMRC* [2024] UKUT 373 (TCC), the Upper Tribunal upheld the FTT: group relief was unavailable because the arrangements around an intra-group transfer had a main purpose of obtaining a **corporation tax** advantage (a step-up in base cost through an inserted step). It did not help the company that the step had failed to deliver the advantage, or that the overall transaction was commercial. The tribunal drew on the unallowable purpose case law under CTA 2009 s 441 (the line of cases the prologue began with). Published commentary reports that the resulting charge was on market value (about £200m) rather than the price paid (about £30m). In the Court of Appeal ([2025] EWCA Civ 1588, 10 December 2025) the company no longer contended that group relief applied; the Court reversed the Upper Tribunal on the s 53/s 54 market value point but held that the s 75A anti-avoidance rule applied to the arrangements (status: case summaries via commentary; the judgment text was not opened).

**HMRC's view of leaving arrangements** (SDLTM23017, reproducing a Tax Bulletin article): a claim is not appropriate if there are arrangements for the purchaser to cease to be a 75% subsidiary of the vendor or a third company, and "the practical likelihood of the scheme being carried through" is not in itself relevant. But HMRC says it will **not** infer such arrangements merely because land moves to a group company that holds only property, and that the full circumstances must be examined. Moving land around a group is normal; moving it into a box that is already being sold is not.

## The three-year string.

The analogy: SDLT group relief is like lending a parking space to a relative. The loan is free, but if the car leaves the family within three years, the space must be paid for, at the price it had on the day you lent it.

**Withdrawal (Sch 7 para 3).** Group relief is withdrawn if the **purchaser ceases to be a member of the same group as the vendor** (a) **within 3 years** of the effective date, or (b) later, in pursuance of or in connection with **arrangements made within those 3 years**, and at that time the purchaser (or a relevant associated company leaving with it) **holds the land** or an interest derived from it. The tax charged is the SDLT that would have been due on the **market value at the original effective date** (or the appropriate proportion), not the value on the day of leaving.

**Exceptions (para 4, 4ZA).**

| Exception | Rule | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Vendor leaves | Not withdrawn where the purchaser leaves because the **vendor** leaves the group (a transaction in shares of the vendor, or of a company above it that is not above the purchaser) | **Para 4ZA (FA 2008 s 96):** a later **change in control of the purchaser** within the 3 years brings the withdrawal back |
| Winding up | Not withdrawn where the groups separate because of anything done for the purposes of, or in the course of, **winding up** the vendor or a company above it | HMRC's manual says para 4ZA does not affect this exception (SDLTM23084) |
| s 75 acquisition | Not withdrawn where the purchaser leaves because another company acquires shares in it (or a company above it) in a transaction qualifying for **FA 1986 s 75** relief, and the purchaser is then in the same group as that acquiring company | Withdrawal if the purchaser later leaves the acquiring company's group within the 3 years (book's summary of para 4; text not opened in full) |

**Recovery (para 5).** The **purchaser** is liable. If the tax is unpaid **6 months** after it became payable, HMRC may recover it from the **vendor** and from any company that at a relevant time was in the same group as the purchaser and **above it** in the group structure.

**Reconstruction and acquisition relief (Sch 7 paras 7–11).** Two cousins of group relief carry a similar string:

| Relief | Conditions | Effect | Withdrawal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reconstruction (para 7) | Acquiring company acquires all or part of a target's undertaking under a **scheme for the reconstruction** of the target; consideration non-redeemable shares to **all** the target's shareholders (plus at most assumption of liabilities); mirror shareholdings; commercial, no avoidance purpose | **Exempt** | **Control of the acquiring company changes within 3 years** (or under arrangements made within them) while it holds the land: tax on market value (paras 9–11; exceptions include intra-group share transfers within FA 1930 s 42) |
| Acquisition (para 8) | Acquiring company acquires all or part of an undertaking for non-redeemable shares issued to the target or its shareholders; other consideration only **cash up to 10% of the nominal value** of those shares and/or assumption of liabilities; the undertaking's main activity a trade **not** wholly or mainly dealing in land | Tax **capped at 0.5%** | As for reconstruction relief |

> **Worked example 21.2: acquisition relief (labelled hypothetical, not story; £)**
>
> | | £ |
> |---|---|
> | Market value of land acquired with the undertaking | 10,000,000 |
> | SDLT without relief: 0 + 2% × 100,000 + 5% × 9,750,000 | 489,500 |
> | SDLT with acquisition relief: 0.5% × 10,000,000 | 50,000 |
> | **Saving** | **439,500** |

## Three transfers, three outcomes.

Our invented group moved land three times under SDLT group relief, and each move ended differently.

**1. Calder's works to TES (1 April GY3).** CVE transferred its works to TES, the group's property company, at no gain, no loss for chargeable gains (chapter 17). For SDLT, s 53 made **market value £5.6m** the starting point; group relief took the tax to nil, saving **£269,500**. TES then leased the works back to Calder. The grant of that lease is itself a land transaction between group companies (TES the vendor, CVE the purchaser), so Calder claimed **group relief on the lease** too (no rent figure is needed here). TES never left the group: three years passed, and the saving became permanent.

**2. The water-systems factory to TWS (1 October GY3).** TES transferred the factory (its cost £4.6m) to TWS at **market value £6.0m**: group relief saved **£289,500**. On **1 July GY5**, 21 months later, the direct demerger took TWS out of the group. A direct demerger is a **distribution** to TPLC's shareholders, not an acquisition by another company, so none of the para 4 exceptions applied. Relief was withdrawn on the £6.0m value fixed at the transfer: Tarnwater paid **£289,500**.

*Was there another way?* On the **book's reading** of para 4 (above), an **indirect demerger** (CTA 2010 s 1077) might have kept the relief: a new company acquires TWS in exchange for its own shares issued to TPLC's shareholders, an acquisition that may qualify for FA 1986 s 75, after which TWS is grouped with the acquirer. The board chose the direct route for the reasons chapter 19 gives, and accepted the SDLT cost as part of the price.

**3. The actuators factory to TAL (1 February GY6).** TEL hived its actuators trade down into new subsidiary TAL, including a factory worth **£7.5m** (TEL's cost £5.0m). Before claiming relief, Tom tested the **leaving arrangements** guard (para 2(2)(b)). In our invented case the board minute recorded a decision to run the actuators business as a separate company and to review its options later in the year; there was **no buyer, no heads of terms and no agreement to sell**. On those facts there were no arrangements for TAL to leave the group, and relief of **£364,500** was claimed.

On **31 December GY6**, 11 months after the hive-down, Brennock bought TAL. Relief was withdrawn: TAL owed **£364,500** (on the £7.5m value at 1 February GY6). Brennock had seen this in due diligence and negotiated an **indemnity from TEL** in the sale agreement. Had a buyer already been lined up on 1 February GY6, relief would have been denied at the outset, and claiming it would have been an error in the return.

> **Worked example 21.3: Tarnmoor's SDLT group relief (invented; £)**
>
> | Transfer | Date | Market value | SDLT relieved | Event | Clawback | Kept |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | CVE works → TES | 1 Apr GY3 | 5,600,000 | 269,500 | none within 3 years | — | 269,500 |
> | Water-systems factory → TWS | 1 Oct GY3 | 6,000,000 | 289,500 | demerger 1 Jul GY5 | 289,500 | — |
> | Actuators factory → TAL | 1 Feb GY6 | 7,500,000 | 364,500 | sale 31 Dec GY6 | 364,500 | — |
> | **Total** | | | **923,500** | | **654,000** | **269,500** |
>
> SDLT on each market value: £0 on £150,000 + 2% × £100,000 (£2,000) + 5% on the excess over £250,000. CVE: £2,000 + 5% × £5,350,000 = £269,500. TWS: £2,000 + 5% × £5,750,000 = £289,500. TAL: £2,000 + 5% × £7,250,000 = £364,500.

> **Exam lens: group relief and clawback**
> - **Grade:** SDLT group, reconstruction or acquisition relief, **awareness (3)**; stamp duty groups of companies, **awareness (3)**.
> - **Past appearances:** **N25 Q5** (20 marks: group property, an intra-group no gain, no loss transfer, s 198, roll-over **and SDLT**; the examiners reported that few candidates considered SDLT, so marks were missed). **M23 Q4** (20 marks: property, a hive-down with the trade, s 198 elections and group roll-over) is the kind of property question in which these marks sit.

## Selling the head office and renting it back.

On **1 October GY6**, TES sold the group's head office (a freehold, in our invented case) to an unconnected pension fund for **£14.0m** and took back a **15-year lease** at a commercial rent of **£900,000 a year**. TES had not opted to tax the building, so no VAT was added to the price.

**The sale.** The pension fund, as purchaser, pays SDLT on £14.0m: **£689,500**.

**The leaseback.** The new lease is also a land transaction, with TES as purchaser. Without relief, TES would pay SDLT on the NPV of the rent.

> **Worked example 21.4: sale and leaseback SDLT (invented; £)**
>
> | | £ |
> |---|---|
> | Price paid by the pension fund | 14,000,000 |
> | Buyer's SDLT: £2,000 + 5% × £13,750,000 | **689,500** |
> | Leaseback rent: £900,000 × 15 years (undiscounted) | 13,500,000 |
> | NPV of rent at 3.5% (Σ 900,000 / 1.035^i, i = 1 to 15) | 10,365,670 |
> | SDLT on rent without relief: 1% × £4,850,000 + 2% × £5,365,670 | 155,813 |
> | **SDLT on the leaseback after s 57A relief** | **nil** |

**Sale and leaseback relief (FA 2003 s 57A).** The leaseback element is exempt where: A transfers or grants to B a **major interest** in land (the sale); B grants a **lease** out of it to A (the leaseback); the sale is entered into wholly or partly in consideration of the leaseback; the only other consideration for the sale is **money** and/or the assumption, satisfaction or release of **debt**; the sale is not a transfer of rights or a pre-completion transaction; and, **if both A and B are bodies corporate, they are not members of the same group** for SDLT group relief. Inside a group, group relief does that job instead, as Calder's lease showed. (The legislation.gov.uk text of s 57A shows duplicated paragraph lettering, probably a consolidation artefact.)

**Corporation tax: CTA 2010 Part 19 (awareness).**

| Chapter | Target | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Ch 1 (ss 834–848): payments connected with transferred land | A seller takes an inflated price now in return for an inflated, deductible rent later | Where land is transferred and the transferor (or an associate) becomes liable to pay rent for it, the **deduction is capped at a commercial rent**; the excess is carried forward under the cumulative "amount E" rules |
| Ch 2 (ss 849–862): new lease after assignment or surrender | Turning a short lease into capital | Where a lease with **50 years or less** to run (rent deductible) is assigned or surrendered for a capital sum and a new lease of **15 years or less** of the same land is granted to the same person or a linked person, part of the capital sum is taxed as income: **all** of it if the new term is 1 year or less, otherwise **(16 − N)/15**, N = the new term in years |
| Chs 3–4 (ss 863–886) | Leased trading assets other than land; capital sums | Commercial rent cap; charge on capital sums up to the payments relieved |

TES's rent of £900,000 is a commercial rent: **no Part 19 Ch 1 restriction**. TES sold a freehold, not a lease, so Ch 2 does not apply.

*For this example, suppose* (labelled hypothetical, not story) that TES had assigned a lease with 40 years to run for **£1,000,000** and taken back a **10-year** lease: (16 − 10)/15 = 6/15, so **£400,000** is taxed as income and £600,000 remains capital.

The chargeable gain on the head office sale itself follows chapter 16's rules.

> **Exam lens: sale and leaseback**
> - **Grade:** SDLT sale and leaseback relief **awareness (3)**; CT sale and leaseback (CTA 2010 Part 19) **awareness (3)**.
> - **Past appearances:** not examined as a main topic in M23–M26; expect a sentence or two inside a property question.
> - **Points to make:** the buyer pays SDLT on the price; the leaseback is relieved under s 57A (not within a group); the CT deduction for rent is capped at a commercial rent; Ch 2 taxes part of the capital sum on a short-lease assignment with a leaseback of 15 years or less.

## Scotland, the deal table and the examiner.

**Scotland.** Land in Scotland falls under **land and buildings transaction tax (LBTT)**, and the exam rubric lets candidates answer by reference to SDLT **or** LBTT. LBTT group relief is in the **Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (Scotland) Act 2013 Sch 10** (relief, withdrawal, recovery), with the same shape as SDLT's, clawback included; its para 9 mirrors the s 75 carve-out. Revenue Scotland's guidance gives non-residential rates of **0% to £150,000, 1% to £250,000 and 5% above** (transactions from 25 January 2019), and lease rent bands of 0% to £150,000, 1% to £2,000,000 and 2% above (from 7 February 2020). For Calder's works, had they been in Scotland, LBTT on £5.6m would have been **£268,500** (relieved in the same way). Wales has its own land transaction tax, outside this paper.

> **Going further: the tax function's tasks**
> - **Register and diary.** Keep a register of every intra-group land transfer (date, market value, SDLT relieved) with a **3-year diary date**. Check it before any sale, demerger, refinancing or listing. Tom's register flagged both clawbacks in the Tarnmoor story before completion.
> - **Test arrangements on the day.** For every group relief claim (stamp duty and SDLT), record the facts on the effective date: any sale process, heads of terms, financing that could pass control, and the purpose of the step. *Tower One* shows that a corporation tax motive can sink SDLT relief.
> - **Price and indemnify.** A buyer that finds an unindemnified clawback in due diligence will take it off the price; Brennock negotiated an indemnity from TEL. Sellers should price it first.
> - **Draft earn-outs knowingly.** A cap fixes the stamp duty at the cap; the deal model should show it.
> - **Stamp on time.** Present s 42 claims for adjudication; pay duty within 30 days of execution.
> - **Demerger checklist.** Direct demergers do not protect SDLT group relief; indirect demergers may (book's reading). Run the SDLT analysis alongside the s 1091 clearance work (chapter 19).
> - **New listings.** **FA 2026 s 85** inserted **FA 1986 s 89C**: an **SDRT** exemption for agreements to transfer a company's chargeable securities for **3 years** from its first listing on a UK regulated market, for companies first listed on or after **27 November 2025**. HMRC's manual (STSM042600, STSM042610) describes exclusions for listings connected with **mergers or takeovers of listed companies** (Exclusion A) and the **insertion of a new holding company** above an already listed company (Exclusion B), and the relief ends on a **change of control**; it does not cover the 1.5% charge. Tarnwater listed for the first time on a direct demerger; **on the book's reading** of HMRC's description, neither exclusion fits, so trades in its shares may enjoy the holiday. SDRT is ungraded for this paper: this is background.
> - **Recent tribunal decision.** *HC-One No.1 Ltd v HMRC* [2026] UKFTT 678 (TC) (FTT) is reported to have applied the para 4(4) winding-up exception to a solvent members' voluntary liquidation of an intermediate parent before a share sale; commentators describe it as fact-specific and possibly subject to appeal (secondary source only).

## How the examiner tests it.

Stamp taxes are tested as add-ons inside larger questions:

- **N25 Q5** (20 marks, group property): the examiners reported that **few candidates considered SDLT**, so marks were missed.
- **N23 Q2** (15 marks: a JV sold with an earn-out, SSE, s 135, then stamp duty for the buyer in a short second part): the examiners noted that **some confused stamp duty with SDLT**.
- **N24 Q5** (20 marks: a US multinational buys a UK SME): the marking guide gave **3 marks** for stamp duty on the UK share purchase (**0.5%, 30 days, non-residents must pay**).

The habit to build: **whenever shares or land move, ask which stamp tax applies, at what rate, who pays, and whether a relief or a clawback follows.** In a question that says "tax consequences", stamp taxes are within the syllabus and earn marks; in a question that names corporation tax only, leave them out.

## What to take away.

- **Stamp taxes follow their own logic, not the gains rules.** A transfer the gains rules forgive can still cost SDLT; a demerger or a sale of a subsidiary can bring back SDLT that a group transfer saved.
- **Stamp duty** taxes only instruments relating to stock or marketable securities (since 1 December 2003): **0.5%**, rounded **up** to the next **£5**, due within **30 days**; the buyer pays, even a non-resident. Shares given as consideration count. **Convertible** loan notes lose the loan capital exemption (FA 1986 s 79(5)). A **capped earn-out** is charged on its **maximum**: Brennock paid **£270,000** on £54.0m.
- **SDLT** taxes land transactions: non-residential **0% / 2% / 5%** (bands £150,000 and £250,000); lease rent on NPV at **3.5%** (0% / 1% / 2%; bands £150,000 and £5m); **connected company** transfers at least at **market value** (s 53), but reliefs survive.
- **Stamp duty reliefs:** **FA 1930 s 42** group relief (75% of shares, profits and assets; no arrangements for control of the transferee alone); **FA 1986 s 75** reconstructions; **s 77** share-for-share acquisitions of the **whole** share capital, subject to **s 77A**. Helmside therefore cost **£80,000**.
- **SDLT group relief** (FA 2003 Sch 7): the same 75% tests, plus four guards (control arrangements, outside consideration, leaving arrangements, a main purpose of avoiding tax, **corporation tax included**: *Tower One*). **Clawback** if the purchaser leaves the vendor's group within **3 years** while holding the land, on **market value at the original date**; exceptions for the vendor leaving (subject to para 4ZA), winding up, and s 75 acquisitions; recovery from the vendor and parent companies after 6 months.
- **In our invented case:** group relief saved **£923,500**; the demerger clawed back **£289,500** and the actuators sale **£364,500**; only the Calder works saving of **£269,500** stayed.
- **Reconstruction relief** exempts; **acquisition relief** caps SDLT at **0.5%**; both are withdrawn on a **change of control of the acquiring company within 3 years**.
- **Sale and leaseback:** s 57A exempts the leaseback (**not** within an SDLT group); CTA 2010 Part 19 caps the rent deduction at a **commercial rent** and, on a short-lease assignment with a leaseback of 15 years or less, taxes **(16 − N)/15** of the capital sum as income.

The answer to the chapter's question: stamp taxes charge the entry ticket for shares and land; they forgive movements inside a group that stay inside it; and they take the forgiveness back when the asset, wrapped in a company, leaves within three years.

Part Four crosses the border of the United Kingdom itself: chapter 22 asks the first question of international tax, where a company lives.
