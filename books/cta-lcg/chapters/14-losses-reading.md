# Chapter fourteen: Losses in the larger company

On 1 July GY2, Tarnmoor plc completed its purchase of Brackenwell Sensors Ltd. Tarnmoor, its companies, its people and its numbers are invented for teaching, as they are throughout this book. Brackenwell was a young developer of industrial sensors, founded by Dr Asha Varma, and it had never made a profit. In the data room sat a schedule headed "tax losses available". The total at the bottom was £8.6m.

At the main rate of 25%, £8.6m of losses looks like £2.15m of tax that will never have to be paid. The sellers' advisers were happy for the buyer to see it that way. Tarnmoor's group head of tax, Tom Hesketh, wrote two words in the margin of his copy: *value at nil*.

This chapter explains why he was right, and what a large group can and cannot do with a loss. It is the last chapter of Part Two. Loss relief, change in company ownership and companies in liquidation or administration are all **core (grade 1)** on the LCG grid; tax avoidance involving carried-forward losses (Part 14B) is **non-core (grade 2)**. The chapter builds on *The Living Law* (TKS), chapter 22, which taught the single-company rules. Here we take them to Advanced Technical depth and into the group.

Most of the law is in the Corporation Tax Act 2010 (CTA 2010), with a few rules in the Corporation Tax Act 2009 (CTA 2009) and one in the Taxation of Chargeable Gains Act 1992 (TCGA 1992). We apply the law for FY2026. Finance Act 2026 made no change to the loss reliefs, Part 5A, Part 7ZA or Part 14, so the framework here is stable.

---

## The 2017 bargain

Every loss rule in this chapter makes more sense once you see the deal that shaped it. Before April 2017, a company's carried-forward trading loss was a narrow thing. It could only be set against future profits of the same trade (CTA 2010 s 45). It could not be used against interest income or gains. It could not be passed to another group company: group relief was for current-year losses only.

In return for that narrowness, the old rules had no general cap outside banking. A company with £100m of old trading losses could wipe out £100m of later trading profits in a single year.

The reform that took effect on 1 April 2017, legislated in the Finance (No. 2) Act 2017, turned both dials at once:

- **Relaxation.** Losses arising in accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2017 can be carried forward against **total profits** (s 45A), not just the same trade, and can be **surrendered to group companies** in later years (Part 5A, group relief for carried-forward losses).
- **Restriction.** Above a **£5m deductions allowance**, carried-forward losses can shelter only **50%** of a company's profits (Part 7ZA).
- **Tighter loss-buying rules.** The change in ownership rules were widened: the window for a major change in a trade grew from 3 years to **5**, and for investment businesses to **8** (Part 14 Chs 2 and 3), and new chapters (2A to 2E) were added.

The logic of the restriction is easy to see. Without it, a large company returning to profit after heavy losses could pay no corporation tax for years. With it, a profitable large company pays some tax every year, while companies whose profits rarely exceed £5m are largely untouched.

Think of carried-forward losses as credit sitting in a bank account. After 2017 you can spend that credit in more shops. But the bank now limits how much a large customer can withdraw in any one year. And if the account changes hands and the business behind it changes, the bank freezes the account. The rest of the chapter is the fine print on each of those three features.

For the exam, almost every loss you meet arose after April 2017. It is therefore a **flexible** loss (total profits, group surrender), a **restricted** loss (Part 7ZA) and a **fragile** loss (Part 14). The examiner tests all three properties together.

## Relief now, in the current year and the year before

The first question for any trading loss is whether it can be used now. **CTA 2010 s 37** lets a company set a trading loss against its **total profits** (trading profits, interest, rents, chargeable gains) of the same accounting period and, if the loss exceeds them, carry the balance back against total profits of accounting periods falling within the **previous 12 months**.

Three features catch candidates:

1. **Current year first.** The carry-back is available only after the current-year claim; you cannot skip the loss year.
2. **All or nothing.** Within each period the relief is the maximum available; you cannot use only part of the loss to leave profits in charge.
3. **Straddling periods.** Where an earlier period falls only partly within the 12 months, its profits are time-apportioned and only the part inside the window is relievable.

Section 37 relief is **not restricted by Part 7ZA**. A current-year claim and a 12-month carry-back are unlimited in amount, which is why a tax function looks at them first.

The temporary three-year carry-back of the pandemic years has **expired**. The examiners reported after November 2023 (Q4) that many candidates still thought it applied. For FY2026 the trading carry-back is 12 months, except in the terminal year (below).

Other kinds of loss have their own rules, and the differences are a favourite trap:

| Amount | Same period | Carry back | Carry forward | Group relief (current year) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trading loss (post-2017) | Total profits (s 37) | 12 months, total profits (s 37) | Total profits by claim within 2 years after the later period, any amount (s 45A) | Yes, in full |
| NTLR deficit (post-2017; chapter 12) | Total profits (CTA 2009 s 463B) | 12 months, **NTLR profits only** (ss 463E–463F) | Total profits, claim within 2 years after the later period (s 463G) | Yes, in full |
| Excess management expenses (chapter 13) | Deducted first from total profits (CTA 2009 s 1219) | **None** | As next period's expenses, by claim within 2 years; priority lost (s 1223) | Only the excess over the company's profit-related threshold (gross profits plus apportioned controlled foreign company (CFC) profits) |
| Capital loss | Gains only | **None** | Gains only | No (s 171A instead; chapter 17) |

One more current-year route matters most of all in a group: the loss-making company can **surrender its current-year loss as group relief** to a company in the same 75% group for the overlapping period (Part 5; chapter 15). Current-year group relief, like s 37, is **outside Part 7ZA**. A pound of loss used now, somewhere in the group, is almost always worth more than a pound carried forward.

## Relief later, by carrying forward

What is not used now is carried forward. For losses of periods beginning on or after 1 April 2017, **s 45A** lets the company claim to set the carried-forward loss against its total profits of a later period. The claim can be **for any amount**, so loss can be held in reserve, and it must be made **within 2 years after the end of the period in which the loss is used** (or later if HMRC allows; CTM04135). Unused carried-forward losses can also be surrendered to group companies under **Part 5A** (chapter 15).

The flexible route has exclusions. Where the trade has become small or negligible, or s 37 relief was not available for the loss in the first place (for example an uncommercial trade), the loss falls under **s 45B** and is set **automatically** against later profits of the **same trade** only. Losses of periods before 1 April 2017 follow **s 45**: same trade, automatic. (Since 2017 a company may claim, within the same 2-year limit, that some or all of an s 45 or s 45B loss is **not** used in a period: CTM04135.) *The Living Law*, chapter 22, told *Leekes Ltd v HMRC* [2018] EWCA Civ 1185 on streaming such losses when trades are combined; you need only the outline here.

Other carried-forward amounts follow the same pattern (table above). Capital losses carry forward against chargeable gains only.

## The fifty per cent rule

**Part 7ZA CTA 2010 (ss 269ZA–269ZZB)** is the loss restriction. It applies only to **carried-forward** amounts:

- carried-forward trading losses (ss 45, 45A, 45B);
- carried-forward NTLR deficits;
- carried-forward management expenses;
- carried-forward UK property losses;
- group relief for carried-forward losses (Part 5A) claimed;
- and, from **1 April 2020**, carried-forward capital losses.

The mechanism: each company, or each group, has a **deductions allowance of £5m** for a 12-month period (reduced proportionately for a shorter one). Carried-forward losses can relieve profits up to the allowance in full; above it, only **50%** of the profits. The ceiling is the **relevant maximum**.

HMRC's manual (CTM05030 onwards) builds the calculation in steps:

1. **Modified total profits**: total profits (s 4(2) step 1) with adjustments (for example excluding Part 9A distributions and losses relieved automatically under s 45(4)(b) and s 45B).
2. Deduct **in-year reliefs** (current-year losses, current-year group relief and similar) to give **qualifying profits**.
3. Deduct the **deductions allowance** to give **relevant profits**.
4. **Relevant maximum** = deductions allowance + 50% × relevant profits.

The company keeps separate tallies for trading profits, non-trading profits and (from April 2020) chargeable gains, and divides its allowance between them (s 269ZB, s 269ZC, s 269ZD; s 269ZF). HMRC's own example: qualifying profits £21m, allowance £5m, relevant profits £16m, relevant maximum £13m (CTM05260).

> **Worked example 14.2: the restriction (invented; not a Tarnmoor story fact; £000)**
>
> | | £000 |
> |---|---|
> | Qualifying profits | 14,000 |
> | Carried-forward trading losses available | 20,000 |
> | Deductions allowance | 5,000 |
> | Relevant profits (14,000 − 5,000) | 9,000 |
> | Relevant maximum: 5,000 + 50% × 9,000 | 9,500 |
> | Losses deducted (lower of 9,500 and 20,000) | (9,500) |
> | **Taxable total profits** | **4,500** |
> | CT at 25% | 1,125 |
> | Losses carried forward (20,000 − 9,500) | 10,500 |

Three things are **outside** the restriction, and the examiner likes all three: current-year relief (including the s 37 carry-back and current-year group relief); **terminal relief** for carried-forward losses under s 45F; and the fact that the restriction **defers** rather than destroys (unused loss simply carries forward).

**Misconception: "a large company can only ever use half its losses."** It cannot use carried-forward losses to cover more than £5m plus half of the excess **in any one year**. Given enough profitable years, every pound of loss can still be used.

## One allowance for the whole group

If every company had its own £5m, groups would multiply companies. So a group has **one** £5m **group deductions allowance** (ss 269ZR–269ZV).

The procedure is the trap:

- **Nomination.** The group nominates one company. The nomination must be **signed on behalf of each group company within the charge to CT**. HMRC's guidance (CTM05180) says it need not be sent to HMRC, but the group must have a compliant nomination and be able to produce it. It continues until replaced by a new nomination, revoked in writing, or the nominated company leaves the group.
- **Group allowance allocation statement (GAAS).** The nominated company files a statement for each of its accounting periods allocating the £5m among members. No member may be given more than £5m (pro rata for short periods). **A member allocated nothing has a deductions allowance of nil** (CTM05140).
- **Deadline.** The GAAS must reach HMRC before the **first anniversary of the filing date** of the nominated company's return for the period; for periods beginning on or after 1 April 2021 the deadline is the latest of that date and 30 days after the close of an enquiry into, or the final determination of an amendment to, a relevant return (or later if HMRC allows) (CTM05200).
- **No statement, no allowance.** If no GAAS is filed in time, **no company in the group** has any deductions allowance for the period.

**In our invented case**, every UK company in the Tarnmoor group signed a nomination of **TPLC** with effect from the start of GY1. As a matter of the group's own practice (not a legal requirement), Tom Hesketh has each new member countersign it on joining, so Calder (from 1 April GY1) and Brackenwell (from 1 July GY2) are on the document. Each year TPLC files the GAAS; in **GY1 to GY4 it allocates the whole £5m to Tarnmoor Engineering Ltd (TEL)**.

Notice the timing: the GAAS is filed long after the year end, when the group knows which companies actually need the allowance. **Allocate with hindsight** to the company whose carried-forward losses meet profits above £5m.

> **Exam lens: loss relief and the deductions allowance**
>
> - **Grade:** loss relief **1** (core).
> - **Past appearances:** N23 Q4 (20 marks: a loss-making retailer; 12-month carry-back only: many candidates applied the expired three-year rule); M24 Q6 (20: b/f losses and NTLR deficit group relief in a two-company computation: losses well done); N25 Q4 (20 marks, 4/8/8: options to relieve a new subsidiary's trading losses and NTLR deficits; examiners criticised irrelevant payment detail when there were losses); **M26 Q4** (20: b/f losses and the £5m allowance: **no restriction in amount, but a nomination and allocation are still needed**; best answers used group relief to keep profits in the 19% band).
> - **Style:** computation plus explanation; 0.5–1 mark per point. Lay out reliefs in statutory order: total profits; current-year claims; carry-back; carried-forward claims within the relevant maximum; group relief; QCDs; TTP.
> - **Traps:** the three-year carry-back; carrying an NTLR deficit back against trading profits; carrying management expenses back; forgetting the GAAS; treating the 50% rule as permanent loss; applying the restriction to current-year or carry-back relief; mixing £ and £000.

## Deficits and expenses in a group

In a listed group the largest losses are often not trading losses but the holding company's interest and running costs.

> **Worked example 14.3: TPLC's non-trading amounts, GY1 (invented; £000)**
>
> | | £000 |
> |---|---|
> | Interest payable to TFL (£120m × 6%) | 7,200 |
> | RCF interest and amortised arrangement fee (1,350 + 250) | 1,600 |
> | NTLR debits | 8,800 |
> | Less CIR disallowance allocated to TPLC (chapter 28) | (2,210) |
> | **NTLR deficit** | **6,590** |
> | Management expenses (after excluding £0.9m capital deal costs; chapter 13) | 8,000 |
> | Less recharge income from TVS at arm's length (chapter 27) | (2,100) |
> | Excess over gross profits | 5,900 |
> | Less CFC chargeable profits apportioned to TPLC from Tarnmoor Capital Ltd (TCM, the group's Marrovian finance company), part of the profit-related threshold (s 105(3A); chapters 13 and 26) | (825) |
> | **Excess management expenses surrenderable** | **5,075** |
> | Carried forward as surplus management expenses (s 1223) | 825 |
> | **Surrendered to TEL as current-year group relief (6,590 + 5,075)** | **11,665** |

The options, in the order the examiner expects (N25 Q4 asked exactly this):

- **NTLR deficit:** against TPLC's own total profits of GY1 (none left); carry back 12 months against **NTLR profits** only (none); carry forward against total profits by claim (then within Part 7ZA); or **surrender in full** as current-year group relief, whatever TPLC's own profits.
- **Excess management expenses:** no carry-back; carry forward as next period's management expenses by claim (within Part 7ZA, s 1219(1A) priority lost); or surrender as current-year group relief **only to the extent they exceed TPLC's profit-related threshold, gross profits plus apportioned CFC profits** (CTA 2010 s 105(3A); chapter 15).

**Order of events.** The CIR comes first. The £2.21m disallowed is not part of the deficit: it is carried forward under TIOPA 2010 Part 10 and can be reactivated later (some of Tarnmoor's was reactivated in GY3, chapter 28).

TPLC surrendered the deficit and the surrenderable management expenses, £11.665m in all, to TEL; the other £825,000 of management expenses carried forward. Current-year group relief is unrestricted and certain; carried forward, the same amounts would face the 50% rule, claim deadlines, and the risk that a later change in ownership would strand them.

> **Going further: the holding company's deficits.** A recurring audit point in large groups is a parent with years of carried-forward NTLR deficits and management expenses that "nobody claimed". Check each year that current-year group relief claims and consents are made (FA 1998 Sch 18 Part 8; chapter 15), that the CIR allocation of disallowed amounts to particular companies (TIOPA 2010 Sch 7A) is chosen with group relief in mind, and that carried-forward balances are tracked for Part 7ZA and Part 14.

## Endings and terminal relief

When a trade ends, two special reliefs open up:

- **Terminal loss relief (s 39).** A loss of the **final 12 months** of trading can be carried back against total profits of the **3 years** before, not just 12 months.
- **Terminal relief for carried-forward losses (s 45F).** On cessation, losses carried forward into the terminal period (under s 45, 45A or 45B) and not relieved there can be set against profits of the terminal period and accounting periods falling within the **3 years ending with it** (total profits for s 45A losses; trade profits for s 45 and s 45B losses). It is **unrestricted by Part 7ZA**; it cannot be set against profits of the period in which the loss was made or any earlier period; it cannot reach any period beginning before **1 April 2017**; the claim is due within **2 years** (CTM04130; FA 2019 Sch 10 refined straddling periods).

**Limit.** On a transfer of trade under common ownership (CTA 2010 Part 22 Ch 1; chapter 19), the predecessor cannot make a terminal claim under s 39 or s 45F; the losses pass to the successor (CTM06065).

Both are claims, and both reward planning: map the 3-year look-back before fixing a cessation date.

**A Tarnmoor point.** In GY3 Calder Valve closed its process valves division, the closure that made Dan Hartley redundant on 30 September GY3 (TKS chapter 9 told it from his side). **Closing a division is not ceasing a trade.** Whether a company carries on one trade or several is a question of fact. In our invented case Calder carries on a single engineering trade and continues to trade, so the closure costs are deductible in the ordinary way, but there is no terminal loss and no s 45F claim.

## Why buying losses was a business

Imagine a company that has run out of business but kept its tax losses. Its shares are worth almost nothing, except to a buyer with profits. The buyer acquires the shell, feeds profitable activity into it, and the old losses shelter the new profits. The seller gets a price for a corpse; the buyer gets a tax saving; the Exchequer pays for both. That trade in **tax shells** is what the change in ownership rules exist to stop. They have existed in some form for decades and now live in **CTA 2010 Part 14**.

| Part 14 chapter | Target | Sections |
|---|---|---|
| Ch 2 | Trading losses: major change in nature or conduct of trade; small or negligible trade | ss 673–676 |
| Ch 2A | Post-2017 carried-forward losses: major change in the **business** (wider) | ss 676AA–676AL |
| Ch 2B, 2D | Gains on assets transferred into the company after the change (pre-change losses; Part 5A) | ss 676BA–676BE; 676DA–676DE |
| Ch 2C | Part 5A surrender of pre-acquisition losses barred for 5 years | ss 676CA–676CI |
| Ch 2E | Transfers of trade after a change | ss 676EA–676EE |
| Ch 3 (chapter 13) | Companies with investment business (significant increase in capital; 8-year window) | ss 677–691 |
| Ch 5A | Shell companies | ss 705A–705G |
| Ch 6 | Recovery of unpaid CT from linked persons | ss 706–718 |
| Ch 7 | Meaning of change in ownership | ss 719–726 |
| Ch 8 | Supplementary (extended assessment time limit; information about share ownership) | ss 727–728 ff |

**Change in ownership (s 719).**

- **Condition A:** a single person acquires more than half the ordinary share capital.
- **Condition B:** two or more persons each acquire at least 5% and together more than half.
- **Condition C:** two or more persons together acquire more than half, ignoring holdings under 5% unless they top up an existing holding.
- Indirect ownership counts (s 723): selling a parent changes the ownership of its subsidiaries. Other interests, such as voting power, can be taken into account where the share capital test gives anomalous results (s 721).
- **Disregards:** insertion of a company between a parent and its subsidiaries (s 724); insertion of a **new top holding company** with mirror-image shareholdings (s 724A, inserted by FA 2014 s 37, for changes on or after 1 April 2014; CTM06355).
- **Group-based trigger for Chs 2A–2E:** in HMRC's summary (CTM06720), an acquisition that brings the company into the group condition with another company can itself be a change in ownership for those chapters (s 719(4A); for Ch 2E only from 1 April 2021).

**Misconception: "when you buy a loss-making company, you buy its losses."** You buy a company that *has* losses. Whether they survive depends on what the company does next.

## A major change in the nature or conduct of a trade

**Chapter 2 (s 673)** applies on a change in ownership if:

- **Condition A:** within **any period of 5 years beginning no more than 3 years before the change**, there is a **major change in the nature or conduct of a trade** carried on by the company; or
- **Condition B:** the change occurs at any time after the scale of the trade has become **small or negligible** and before any significant revival.

(The window was 3 years for accounting periods ending before 1 April 2017, and the 5-year window applies only where both the change in ownership and the major change occur on or after 1 April 2017: CTM06370; F(No.2)A 2017 Sch 4 para 72.)

**Read the window slowly.** It is not "5 years after the change". A change the sellers made two years before the sale, to dress the company up, can count; so can a change the buyer makes four years after.

**Meaning (s 673).** A major change includes a major change in the type of property dealt in or services or facilities provided, and a major change in customers, outlets or markets; it can result from a gradual process. HMRC's view is in **Statement of Practice 10/91** (revised 1996; CTM06380), and CTM06370 reports the cases:

- *Purchase v Tesco Stores Ltd* (1984) 58 TC 46: "major" means more than significant but less than fundamental (as HMRC's manual summarises it).
- *Williams v Peeters Picture Frames Ltd* (1983) 56 TC 436 (HMRC's spelling of the name; other reports give it as *Willis v Peeters Picture Frames Ltd* [1983] STC 453): after a takeover the company sold through a group distributor, which sold on to the company's original customers. The Special Commissioners thought this could be a major change looked at in isolation; on the special facts, as the manual reports it, it was held not to be a major change. The end customers were unchanged; only the route to them changed.

**Consequences.** Losses made before the change cannot be carried forward against profits after it (ss 45, 45A, 45B); a loss made after the change cannot be carried back to a pre-change period (s 37). Where the change falls mid-period, the period is split into two notional accounting periods and amounts are apportioned on a time basis, or another basis if time would be unjust (s 674). Because a later change can disallow losses already used, **Chapter 8 extends HMRC's assessment time limit** (s 727). The tax function's job does not end at completion.

## Brackenwell's £8.6m

> **Worked example 14.4: Brackenwell's pre-change losses (invented; £000)**
>
> | Source | £000 |
> |---|---|
> | Losses brought forward from years before GY1 | 5,300 |
> | Year to 31 December GY1 (standalone SME): trading loss after the ERIS extra deduction 7,210, less 6,510 surrendered for the 14.5% payable credit (chapter 10) | 700 |
> | Year to 31 December GY2: trading loss after taxable RDEC 5,200, split at the change on 1 July GY2 by months (6/12 : 6/12) | |
> | — pre-change notional period 1 January–30 June GY2 | 2,600 |
> | **Pre-change losses** | **8,600** |
> | — post-change notional period 1 July–31 December GY2 (not affected by Ch 2; surrendered to TEL as current-year group relief, chapter 15) | 2,600 |
>
> A day-count apportionment (181/365) would give £2,578,630 before and £2,621,370 after; the story uses months, as the exam rubric ("nearest month") does. Either is a time basis under s 674.

**The analysis.** Tarnmoor's integration plan, agreed before completion, made Brackenwell the group's captive sensor developer and supplier: it would design and make sensors for Calder's and TEL's valves and run down its sales to the outside industrial customers it had served for years. Tom Hesketh's team concluded:

- customers change from many unconnected buyers to two group companies;
- markets change from general industrial sensing to components for the group's own products;
- unlike *Peeters*, the end customers are not the same people;
- so, **on the group's analysis**, a major change in the nature or conduct of the trade, inside the 5-year window (Ch 2, s 673; Condition A).

**Consequence.** None of the £8.6m can be carried forward against Brackenwell's post-change profits. The post-change £2.6m loss of GY2 is untouched and goes to TEL as current-year group relief.

**Even without a major change**, the losses would have been worth little to the group. **Chapter 2C** bars Part 5A surrender of pre-acquisition losses for five years, measured (per HMRC's guidance, CTM06815) from the end of the accounting period in which the change happens: for Brackenwell, from 31 December GY2 to the end of **GY7**. They could only have been used against Brackenwell's own profits, within Part 7ZA.

>

> | Qualifying profits | 4,000 | 4,000 |

The price reflected nothing for the losses (chapter 20), and the group accounts recognised no deferred tax asset for them (chapter 6). Dr Varma, in our invented story, said at the integration meeting that it felt as if the company's history had been written off. In a sense it had: the law treats a company that changes hands and changes course as a new taxpayer for the purpose of its old losses.

## The wider net after 2017

The 2017 reform made losses more useful, so it also made them more attractive to buy. Parliament added chapters to Part 14 (all for changes in ownership on or after 1 April 2017: CTM06715).

- **Chapter 2A** looks for a **major change in the business** of the company, wider than a change in a trade. In HMRC's summary (CTM06750, on s 676AC) it includes a major change in **scale**, the **beginning or ending** of a trade or business, and a change in the **nature of investments**, as well as changes in property dealt in, services, customers, outlets and markets; gradual changes count; transfers between companies related before the change are ignored. Where it applies, it restricts pre-change carried-forward losses of various kinds (trade losses, non-trading deficits, management expenses, property losses) against post-change profits, with windows of 5 or 8 years depending on the relief. **Priority:** Chapter 2A does not apply where Chapter 2 or Chapter 3 can apply instead (CTM06775). Brackenwell is therefore a **Chapter 2** case.
- **Chapters 2B and 2D**: after the change, an asset is moved into the company on a no gain, no loss basis (TCGA s 171) or tax-neutrally (CTA 2009 s 775) and a gain arises within **5 years**: pre-change losses (2B) and Part 5A group relief (2D) cannot shelter it. Chapter 17 covers the parallel Sch 7A rule for pre-entry capital losses.
- **Chapter 2C**: Part 5A surrender of pre-acquisition losses barred for 5 years; applies even if the business is unchanged.
- **Chapter 2E**: restriction where a trade with post-2017 losses is transferred after a change.
- **Chapter 5A (shell companies)**: a company with no trade, investment business or UK property business: NTLR debits and deficits and non-trading intangibles losses restricted after a change.
- **Chapter 6 (recovery of unpaid CT)**: where CT for a pre-change period is unpaid more than **6 months after assessment** and conditions about the company's trade or business are met (s 711), HMRC may assess a **linked person** (a person who controlled the company before the change, and companies they control), within **3 years** of the final determination (s 710).

**Helmside and timing.** On 1 March GY5 TPLC bought Greyfell's 40% and went from 45% to 85% (chapter 18). Whether that is a change in ownership under s 719 is not something we need to decide: Helmside used its last carried-forward loss in GY4, so there was nothing left for Part 14 to restrict.

> **Going further: due diligence on losses.** For each target: (1) schedule losses by type and by period (pre- or post-April 2017); (2) map the integration plan against Ch 2, Ch 2A and Ch 3 windows, including changes the sellers made in the 3 years before; (3) model Ch 2C (5 years) and Part 7ZA using a realistic GAAS allocation; (4) check Chs 2B/2D before moving assets in; (5) price losses at nil unless the trade continues unchanged, and keep any "loss value" out of the headline price (use an earn-out or deferred payment if the seller insists); (6) take an indemnity for Ch 6 linked-person exposure where the target has unpaid pre-change tax.

## Loss refreshing and the purpose rules

Mechanical rules invite mechanical schemes, so two purpose-based rules sit behind Part 14.

**Part 14A: transfer of deductions (CTA 2010 ss 730A–730D; FA 2013 Sch 14).** After a **qualifying change** (defined by reference to CAA 2001 Part 2 Ch 16A, the allowance-buying rules), "deductible amounts" (trade and property expenses, management expenses, NTLR and derivative debits, IFA debits) cannot be used in a s 37 claim, group relief or Part 5A relief if, at the change date, it was **highly likely** they would be so used and a **main purpose** of the change arrangements was to achieve that (s 730C). Section 730D blocks deductions against profits from **profit transfer arrangements** made with that main purpose.

**Part 14B: tax avoidance involving carried-forward losses (ss 730E–730H; FA 2015, for accounting periods beginning on or after 18 March 2015; grade 2).** HMRC's manual (CTM07505) calls the target **"corporate loss refreshing"**: converting old, restricted carried-forward losses into new, flexible in-year deductions. A group company makes a deductible payment to the loss company; the payer gets a fresh deduction usable across the group; the loss company's old losses absorb the matching income. Section 730G denies the carried-forward losses against the arrangement profits where **all five conditions** are met:

| Condition | Content (s 730G) |
|---|---|
| A | Relevant profits arise to the company as a result of the arrangements, and without the section it could deduct carried-forward losses from them |
| B | The company or a **connected** company (s 1122) brings in a deductible amount it is reasonable to assume would not otherwise have arisen |
| C | A **main purpose** of the arrangements is a relevant CT advantage (or relevant CFC charge advantage) |
| D | It is reasonable to assume the **tax value** of the arrangements exceeds their **non-tax value** |
| E | Priority: the banking company rule in Part 7A (s 269CK) does not apply |

**The promoter's pitch (GY3; invented).** A promoter proposed a "loss refresh": TEL would pay Brackenwell a large upfront fee for future development work, deductible at once; Brackenwell would book the income and set its old losses against it; to get round the major change, Brackenwell would restart a small external sales channel.

Tom Hesketh's advice turned on **two locks, either sufficient**:

1. If the group's analysis was right, the major change had **already happened**, and nothing done later undoes it: the losses were dead under Ch 2.
2. If the promoter was right and the losses were alive, **Part 14B** would bar them against the scheme profits: Condition B (TEL's deduction), Condition C (main purpose) and Condition D (a fee paid to refresh losses has tax value far above its non-tax value).

The board declined. Chapter 4 covers the DOTAS hallmarks (a premium fee and confidentiality) and the GAAR.

> **Exam lens: Part 14B and Part 14A**
>
> - **Grade:** tax avoidance involving carried-forward losses (Part 14B) **2** (non-core): working knowledge and recognition.
> - **Past appearances:** none M23–M26.
> - **Style:** short explanation marks: name the rule, list the conditions, apply the tax value v non-tax value test to the facts.
> - **Traps:** confusing Part 14A (transfer of deductions after a qualifying change) with Part 14B (refreshing carried-forward losses); forgetting that Part 14B only matters if the losses would otherwise be usable (Condition A).

## When a company is wound up

Companies in liquidation or administration are **core** and have not been examined standalone recently: easy marks for the prepared.

| Rule | Liquidation | Administration |
|---|---|---|
| Accounting period ends | **Immediately before the winding up starts** (resolution; for a compulsory winding up, presentation of the petition if an order follows) (CTA 2009 s 12) | **Immediately before the day** the company enters administration; also when it leaves (CTA 2009 s 10(1)(i), (j), (2)) |
| Later periods | **12 months** each until completion of the winding up | Normal rules |
| Final-year rate | Rate fixed for the year; else proposed by Budget resolution; else the penultimate year's rate (CTA 2010 s 628) | Same approach (s 630) |
| Assessments | Liquidator may assume a completion date; if wrong, a new AP begins (s 629) | Equivalent (s 631) |
| Repayment interest | £2,000 or less in the final period not taxable (s 633) | Same |
| Beneficial ownership | **Lost** on commencement of winding up (*Ayerst v C & K (Construction) Ltd* [1976] AC 167, 50 TC 651, House of Lords, 21 May 1975) | Not generally thought to be lost (secondary commentary; facts matter) |

**Group relief.** Group relief requires beneficial ownership of the shares in a 75% subsidiary (s 152; CTM80151). When an intermediate holding company goes into liquidation it loses beneficial ownership of its subsidiaries' shares, so, in HMRC's view, its subsidiaries are no longer grouped with it or, through it, with the rest of the group. HMRC's guidance (CTM97750) also treats the appointment of an insolvency practitioner as taking control away from the owners and breaking the group relationship; the "arrangements" rules (s 154) sit behind that view (chapter 15).

**Gains groups survive.** TCGA 1992 **s 170(11)**: a company does not cease to be a member of a gains group merely because of a resolution, order or other act for the winding up of it or another group member (CG45135). Without it, liquidating an intermediate holding company would drop its subsidiaries out of the gains group, with degrouping charges.

> **Worked example 14.6: liquidating an intermediate holding company (invented; not a Tarnmoor story fact)**
>
> A group's intermediate holding company (H) owns two trading subsidiaries (S1, S2). The group resolves to liquidate H to simplify the structure.
>
> - H's accounting period ends immediately before the resolution; then 12-month periods to completion.
> - From the start of the winding up, S1 and S2 can no longer surrender or claim current-year group relief with the rest of the group through H (HMRC's view, based on loss of beneficial ownership).
> - No degrouping charge arises merely because of the liquidation (s 170(11)).
> - Better plan, if losses need to move: transfer S1 and S2 up to the parent (no gain, no loss within the gains group) **before** the winding up starts.

> **Exam lens: companies in liquidation or administration**
>
> - **Grade:** **1** (core).
> - **Past appearances:** N24 Q2 (20 marks: group and consortium relief over two years including a **holding company in liquidation**; the examiners said its impact was missed). Not otherwise seen M23–M26.
> - **Traps:** liquidation v administration period ends (before the winding up *starts* v before the *day* of administration); forgetting that group relief through a liquidating holding company ends while the gains group survives; the final-year rate rules.

## Choosing the claim in a group

The examiner often asks you to minimise tax or "long-term costs". In a group that means choosing among current-year relief, carry-back, group relief and carry-forward, and choosing where each pound of loss lands.

**Value.** In a small group, a pound of loss saves most where it keeps a company's profits below the upper limit (M26 Q4: the best answers used group relief to keep profits in the 19% band; the marginal rate of 26.5% is not "the rate that should be applied in full", as the examiners put it). In a large group the marginal relief limits are divided by each company plus its associated companies. For Tarnmoor the divisor was **9** in GY1 and **10** from GY2:

| | GY1 (divisor 9) | GY2–GY4 (divisor 10) |
|---|---|---|
| Lower limit (£50,000 ÷ divisor) | £5,556 | £5,000 |
| Upper limit (£250,000 ÷ divisor) | £27,778 | £25,000 |

Every profitable Tarnmoor company pays at **25%**; a pound of loss is worth 25p wherever it lands.

The choice then turns on:

1. **Certainty.** Current-year relief and carry-back are unrestricted; carried-forward losses face Part 7ZA, Part 14 and the risk of a cessation that strands them. Use losses early.
2. **Cash.** For very large companies paying instalments in months 3, 6, 9 and 12 (chapter 3), expected current-year group relief reduces the instalments due in the year itself; a carry-back produces cash only after the loss year ends.
3. **The group allowance.** Allocate with hindsight in the GAAS.
4. **Interaction.** The CIR (chapter 28) can disallow interest and change which companies have profits to relieve; deferred tax (chapter 6) depends on whether future profits will absorb carried-forward losses.

> **Going further: the tax function's annual loss tasks**
>
> - Keep the group deductions allowance nomination current as companies join and leave; diarise the GAAS deadline (first anniversary of TPLC's filing date).
> - Track each acquired company's Part 14 windows (Ch 2: any 5 years from 3 years before; Ch 2C: 5 years from the end of the acquisition period) and record the integration plan honestly.
> - Before liquidating any group company, test its effect on group relief and the gains group, and the timing of the accounting period end.
> - On acquisitions: value losses at nil unless the trade will continue unchanged, and discount even then for Ch 2C and Part 7ZA.
> - Ethics: a promoted "refresh" with confidentiality and a premium fee is a DOTAS and GAAR conversation for the board (chapter 4), not just a technical one.

> **Exam lens: how losses are examined overall**
>
> Losses appear in nearly every CT computation question (M23–M26). The paper is technical prose and computations, normally 10/15/20-mark questions, 0.5–1 mark per point, no letter or report format. Lay out loss relief in statutory order, show each claim separately with its time limit, and state what carries forward. Use a consistent unit (£ or £000) in each table.

## What to take away

- **The 2017 bargain:** post-1 April 2017 losses are flexible (total profits; group surrender), restricted (Part 7ZA) and fragile (Part 14).
- **Current year:** s 37 against total profits, then 12 months back; current year first; all or nothing; unrestricted. The three-year carry-back has gone. NTLR deficits carry back only against NTLR profits; management expenses do not carry back; capital losses never touch income.
- **Carry forward:** s 45A against total profits by claim (2 years after the period of use), any amount; s 45B and s 45 same trade, automatic.
- **Part 7ZA:** £5m deductions allowance; above it 50% (worked example: £14m qualifying profits allowed £9.5m relief). Current-year relief, carry-back and s 45F are outside it.
- **Groups:** one £5m allowance; nomination signed for every CT member; GAAS by the first anniversary of the nominated company's filing date; otherwise nil for everyone.
- **Cessation:** s 39 final-year loss back 3 years; s 45F carried-forward losses against the final 3 years, unrestricted, not before April 2017, not the loss-making period. Closing a division is not ceasing a trade.
- **Part 14:** change in ownership under s 719; Ch 2 major change within any 5-year period beginning up to 3 years before the change; Ch 2A wider but gives way to Chs 2 and 3; Ch 2C bars Part 5A surrender for 5 years. Brackenwell's £8.6m was lost to the major change; Helmside's £1.2m was used before TPLC took control.
- **Part 14B:** no carried-forward losses against loss-refreshing profits where tax value exceeds non-tax value.
- **Liquidation:** AP ends immediately before winding up starts, then 12-month periods; beneficial ownership lost (group relief through the company ends, HMRC's view); gains group survives (s 170(11)).

When you buy a loss-making company, you do not buy its losses. You buy a chance to use them, and the law takes that chance away if the business changes.

Losses are only half of the group story. Chapter fifteen, on group relief, consortia and joint ventures, shows how a loss in one company becomes relief in another, and finishes Helmside's story.
