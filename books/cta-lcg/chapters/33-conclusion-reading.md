# Conclusion.

The book began with one loan and two answers. A company called LLC5 borrowed about $4bn from its own group. Transfer pricing asked whether strangers would have lent on those terms, and said yes. The unallowable purpose rule asked why the company borrowed, and took every pound of the deductions away (*BlackRock HoldCo 5, LLC v HMRC* [2024] EWCA Civ 330). The Supreme Court refused permission to go further.

Thirty-two chapters later, that case looks less like a puzzle and more like a summary. A larger company lives under several rules at once, each asking its own question. The adviser's job is to know which questions are being asked, and to ask them all.

This conclusion does four things. It answers the four questions the book set out with. It revisits the debates the book has carried. It looks at what is coming, separating what is law from what is only proposed. And it asks what a large group's tax function will look like in the years after your exam. The law it describes is the law for **FY2026**, and the facts about what is coming are as they stood on **9 October 2026**, when this book was finished.

## One taxpayer or many.

The law's answer has not moved since *Salomon v A Salomon and Co Ltd* [1897] AC 22 (HL). A company is a person distinct from its members. Each company in a group computes its own profits, files its own return and pays its own tax, on its own timetable. There is no group return.

What the law does is build bridges, each with its own guest list; and it refuses to build others. The refusals are where the marks are.

Yet the newest rules lean the other way:

- the **CIR** takes the **worldwide group** as its unit and asks how much interest the whole group may deduct in the UK (TIOPA 2010 Part 10);
- **Pillar Two** measures the group's effective rate **territory by territory**, across all its entities there (F(No.2)A 2023 Parts 3–4);
- for chargeable periods beginning on or after **1 January 2026**, most UK-to-UK dealings are taken out of transfer pricing altogether (TIOPA 2010 s 164A), because inside one country the price between relatives usually changes nothing.

**Answer:** many taxpayers for computing and paying; one business for an increasing number of the limits.

**Answer:** profits are taxed where the activity and the decision-makers are, and the law has built a series of fences against their drifting elsewhere. The latest fence is not a wall at the border. It is a minimum, set by agreement among many countries.

## What the law follows.

All three, in layers:

1. **The accounts first.** CTA 2009 s 46 starts trading profits from GAAP; loan relationships, derivatives and intangibles follow profit or loss. *HMRC v NCL Investments Ltd* [2022] UKSC 9: a properly computed accounting debit counts unless a statute removes it. A change of accounting standard can change taxable profit without a Finance Act (chapter 5).
2. **The deal second.** A share exchange is not a disposal (TCGA s 135); a scheme of reconstruction passes assets across without a charge (s 139); an exempt demerger is not a distribution (CTA 2010 s 1075 onwards). The law respects the form of a commercial transaction if its conditions are met (chapters 18–19).
3. **Purpose last, overriding both.** s 441 CTA 2009 (*BlackRock*, *Kwik-Fit*, *JTI*); the rewritten share exchange test in TCGA s 137 for issues from 26 November 2025, after *Delinian* (formerly Euromoney) won in the Court of Appeal in November 2023; s 139's purpose test reaching income tax; s 31 value shifting; ss 184A–184I; CTA 2010 Part 14B; the treaty principal purpose test; UTPP's tax design condition.

**The trend:** mechanical rules are steadily acquiring purpose tests.

## The tax function to come.

The rules already enacted give a fair picture of a group like Tarnmoor over the next few years.

- **A data function.** Pillar Two returns need figures from every entity in every territory, 15 months after the year end (18 for the first). CbC reports, Master Files and Local Files need the same discipline. If the ICTS regulations are made, transaction data will travel with the return; if HMRC's tagged computations arrive, the computation itself becomes data HMRC's systems read before any person does.
- **A governance function.** The SAO certificate, the published strategy and UTT put the tax function's controls before the board and before HMRC's customer compliance manager (TCRM1000: risk ratings shared with the group). The corporate criminal offence puts its procedures before the courts.
- **A function of specialists, coordinated.** No single person knows every regime in this book at depth. The group head of tax, like our invented Tom Hesketh, knows enough of each to see the issue, ask the right specialist and put the answers into one recommendation for the board.
- **A function that checks.** Much of the first draft of tax work (the research memo, the summary of a new rule, the first pass at a computation) will increasingly come from software, including AI. The CIOT's 2028 learning outcome describes the job that remains: evaluate that output critically; go back to the statute; check the date the law applies from; say whether a point is law, HMRC's view, disputed or proposed.

## The living law.

The book's title is a claim. Corporate tax law is not a finished code. It is a set of answers to questions that keep changing, as groups grow, borders matter in new ways, accounts are rewritten and governments agree new floors. The four questions will still be asked after the rules that answer them today have changed.

So for the exam, keep the law for FY2026, and use it precisely. After the exam, keep the questions. Is this one taxpayer or many? Where was this profit made? What is the law following here? And what must this group do, by when, and what would a good adviser flag?

The last piece of this book is a note on sources: where the law in these chapters lives, what this book could not open, and how to keep up once the exam is behind you.
