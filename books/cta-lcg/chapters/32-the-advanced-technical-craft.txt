Chapter thirty two: The Advanced Technical craft.

One sentence in the instructions.

In the Candidate Instructions for the October twenty twenty six sitting, the Chartered Institute of Taxation printed a sentence that every candidate should learn by heart. In its words, calculations left in the spreadsheet and not copied to the answer box will not be marked.

That sentence arrived with new exam software. From October twenty twenty six, the Institute's papers are sat on R M Assessment Master, which replaced the program candidates had used for years. The new system puts a spreadsheet on the screen, right under the place where you type your answer. It is a gift and a trap. You can add up a capital allowances pool in seconds. And if you leave it there, the marker never sees it.

The sentence is a fair summary of the whole paper. The Advanced Technical paper does not reward what you know. It rewards what you put in front of the marker, in a form the marker can credit, within three and a half hours.

This chapter is about that craft. It is not question practice. You have the Institute's past papers and your own question bank for that. It is the set of habits that turns thirty one chapters of law into marks. The chapter gathers what every Exam lens box in this book has said, adds the facts about the paper and the day, and checks them against the Institute's own documents. The law you will be examined on, for a sitting in twenty twenty seven, is almost certainly the law for the financial year twenty twenty six, under the Finance Act twenty twenty six, which is the law this book teaches.

Chapter two, on where corporate tax law lives, began this work with research in the exam room. Here we finish it. There is no syllabus grade for this chapter, because its subject is the paper as a whole. Every grade quoted here comes from the twenty twenty six syllabus grid, and every comment about past papers comes from the question papers, suggested answers and examiners' reports the Institute publishes for each sitting. Where a fact is my own arithmetic rather than the Institute's statement, I will say so. The question for the chapter is simple. What does this paper reward, and how do you give it that in two hundred and ten minutes?

The shape of the paper.

The paper is the Chartered Tax Adviser Advanced Technical paper, Taxation of Larger Companies and Groups. Until November twenty twenty two it was called Taxation of Major Corporates. The new name, from twenty twenty three, signals where the weight sits now. Groups, consortia, interest, and everything that crosses a border.

It lasts three hours and thirty minutes. There are normally six questions, each worth ten, fifteen or twenty marks, and one hundred marks in all. The pass mark is fifty per cent. Every paper from May twenty twenty three to May twenty twenty six had six questions, and the usual mix was three twenties, two fifteens and one ten. Four of those seven papers ran twenty, fifteen, fifteen, twenty, twenty, ten, in that order.

The Institute describes the scenarios plainly. They will typically be based around multinational groups and larger companies that may be listed, with no individual controlling shareholders. So owner managers, close company loans and profit extraction are not this paper's business. Our invented group, Tarnmoor, a listed engineering group with no controlling shareholder, was built to match that description.

The syllabus has three elements. Corporation tax, including corporation tax on chargeable gains. Capital allowances. And stamp duty and stamp duty land tax at awareness level. At least seventy per cent of the corporation tax element comes from core material.

Core means grade one on the syllabus grid. Non-core is grade two, and awareness is grade three. Grade one topics are examined at depth, with computations. Grade two needs working knowledge and the main computation. Grade three needs recognition. On the twenty twenty six grid, which is the grid this book is built on, controlled foreign companies, transfer pricing, the corporate interest restriction and hybrid mismatches are all core.

The Institute also tells you what kind of adviser it is testing. Candidates, it says, are not expected to have deep knowledge of all tax areas but should be able to identify what the potential tax issues are. That is the job of a corporate tax trainee in a large firm. Spot the issue, explain it precisely, and know enough to coordinate the specialists.

The style follows from that. Requirements say calculate, with explanations, or explain, or discuss, and sometimes recommend. No question on this paper between twenty twenty three and twenty twenty six asked for a letter, a report or an email. That format belongs to the Application and Professional Skills paper. Here you write technical prose and computations, and roughly half the marks are computational.

The day itself.

Your likely sitting is the fourth of May, twenty twenty seven, at half past two in the afternoon, with results in July. The fallback is the twenty sixth of October, twenty twenty seven, also at half past two, with results in January twenty twenty eight. The Institute's timetable shows this paper in that afternoon slot alongside Cross Border and Environmental Taxes. One line on the Institute's exam entry page gives the twenty eighth of October for the autumn sitting, so check your own entry confirmation before you book the day off.

You sit the paper in a test centre, at a Windows computer with one twenty four inch monitor, in a room of perhaps twenty to twenty five terminals. Your Candidate Information email arrives about three weeks before the exam. Print it. Write your legislation username and password on it, and nothing else, because no other notes are allowed on that sheet.

Arrive at least thirty minutes early. The Institute recommends an hour. If you are more than thirty minutes late you will not be admitted. You may not leave in the first forty five minutes or the last ten.

What you may bring is short. Pens, pencils and an eraser. Your own physical calculator, basic or scientific, whose memory may be checked. Ear plugs. Your printed candidate email. Still water or a non-fizzy drink, and quiet snacks without nuts. No watches of any kind. That last rule matters, because the clock you will rely on is the one on the screen.

You will find two sheets of note paper on the desk, and you can ask for more. Nothing on that paper is marked, and it stays in the room. Use it for a plan, a timeline, or a quick list of issues. Never use it for working you want credit for.

When the paper opens, spend a minute on the resources before the first question. Log in to the legislation, open the tax tables on screen, and check that both respond. If anything fails, tell the invigilator at once, while there is still time to fix it.

None of this is glamorous. But a candidate who arrives flustered, with a legislation password that will not work, has lost marks before reading a word. A work subscription to the legislation products will not log in on the day. Buy the student product early, and practise on it until finding a Part of an Act takes seconds.

Inside R M Assessment Master.

The screen has two halves. On the left sits the question scenario, with any exhibits shown as small thumbnails you can open. The requirements appear at the bottom of the scenario and again at the top right. Under the requirements on the right is the answer box. Below the answer box is a spreadsheet, a version of SpreadJS that behaves much like Excel.

The spreadsheet is a calculator with columns. It is not part of your answer. You must copy your working into the answer box, with control C and control V. And when you copy it, think about what the marker will see. The examiners cannot see the formulae behind your cells. A cell that shows four hundred and twelve thousand pounds tells them nothing about where that number came from. So every working must be shown in words and figures. Proceeds, less cost, less indexation, equals the gain. Not just the gain.

There is also an on-screen calculator, and a flag function that lets you mark a question or a part to return to. And there is a timer. Ten minutes before the end, it turns red and flashes. That is the only time warning you will get.

The Institute says candidates must have practised using the software before they reach the test centre, and it puts a practice test on its website before each sitting. Use it more than once. Find out how a table pastes into the answer box, how long that takes, and how it looks once pasted. Discover that now, not at a quarter to six on the day of the exam.

Here is the misconception this chapter most wants to kill. It runs like this. The marker will see my working because the computer holds it. The marker sees only the answer box. A perfect computation sitting in the spreadsheet scores nothing. A rough computation, copied across with a one-line reason beside each figure, can score most of the marks even if the final number is wrong.

The examiners have said exactly that, in another context. After the November twenty twenty three sitting, on the senior accounting officer question, they told candidates to show their workings, because marks can be earned even when the conclusion is wrong. They said it again after May twenty twenty five, on a transfer pricing computation. The software change makes their advice sharper. Working that is not visible is working that does not exist.

So build a habit. Do the arithmetic wherever is quickest. Then, before you move to the next requirement, copy it into the answer box, label each line, and check that it pasted cleanly.

The tax tables.

Behind an open book icon in the software are three resources. The tax tables, the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development's Model Tax Convention, and links to the legislation. The rules are strict. In the Institute's words, candidates cannot access anything other than Online Legislation and the Tax Tables. The Model sits inside the software alongside them. Nothing else comes in.

The tax tables are on your desk in hard copy and also on screen as a P D F. For this paper they give the corporation tax rates, the European definition of a small or medium sized enterprise with notes for research and development and transfer pricing, the research and development expenditure credit rate, the enhanced support rates for research and development intensive companies, the capital allowance rates, the interest rates of His Majesty's Revenue and Customs, H M R C, including those on instalments, the lease percentage table for short leases, the retail prices index for indexation, and the stamp duty and stamp duty land tax rates.

Two things follow. You need not memorise a rate the tables give you. And you must know exactly what the tables do not give you. They do not give the order of the steps in the expenditure credit. They do not tell you whether notional tax is at twenty five or nineteen per cent. They do not tell you when a research and development claim must be notified. They do not give the conditions for any relief. Those are your marks.

Now the trap for twenty twenty seven. As at October twenty twenty six, the Institute's tables page offers only the twenty twenty six tables. Those reflect the Finance Act twenty twenty five, with a main pool writing down allowance of eighteen per cent. A paper set on the Finance Act twenty twenty six uses fourteen per cent, for chargeable periods beginning on or after the first of April, twenty twenty six, and it has the new forty per cent first-year allowance. The twenty twenty seven tables, when they appear, should show the new figures. Until then, practise with fourteen per cent, and know how a hybrid rate works for a period that straddles the change. Chapter eight did the arithmetic.

Practise with the tables you will actually have. Learn where each figure sits on the page, so that finding the lease percentage for a lease with twenty seven and a half years left is a ten-second job, not a two-minute hunt.

Legislation and the treaty.

The legislation comes from Croner-i or from Tolley's Digital Library. Both are student products bought in advance. You cannot make notes in either. You may highlight, and you may use the products' tags and folders. A tag may hold a section number or a topic name. The Institute's own example pairs the corporate rescue exception with section three six one D of the two thousand and nine Act. A tag must not reproduce a pro forma or a formula, and invigilators may check.

So the legislation is a map, not a crib. Its value is speed. In the exam you will not have time to read a Part of an Act from the start. You will have time to open the right section, confirm a condition or a time limit, and cite it.

Build your tags around the regimes this book has taught. In the Taxation, International and Other Provisions, Act twenty ten, which I will call the International Act, tag Part two for double tax relief, Part four for transfer pricing, Part six A for hybrids, Part nine A for controlled foreign companies, and Part ten with Schedule seven A for the corporate interest restriction. In the Corporation Tax Act two thousand and nine, tag the branch exemption in section eighteen A, the loan relationship rules in Part five with the unallowable purpose rule in section four four one, intangible assets in Part eight, research and development in Part thirteen, and the timing rules for remuneration. In the Corporation Tax Act twenty ten, tag group relief in Part five, the loss restriction in Part seven Z A, change of ownership in Part fourteen, and Part twenty two. In the Taxation of Chargeable Gains Act nineteen ninety two, which I will call the Gains Act, tag section one seven one, section one seven nine, the leases schedule and the substantial shareholding exemption in Schedule seven A C.

Practise on the product you will use on the day, because each publisher lays out the Acts and its search differently.

Then the treaty. The O E C D Model is available only as an on-screen P D F, with no hard copy, and the version published for the exam contains the twenty seventeen articles. It is a fallback. When a question supplies an extract from a real or invented treaty, the extract is the treaty for that question. After the November twenty twenty three sitting, the examiners reported that some candidates ignored the supplied treaty article and used the Model text instead. The question turned on a tie-breaker, and the supplied article used place of effective management. Read the extract, quote its article number, and apply its words to the facts.

One more point about sources. The paper rewards statute. H M R C's manuals are its view, not law, and you will not have them in the room. If you rely on a known H M R C practice, say so, briefly, as H M R C's view.

The clock.

Two hundred and ten minutes for one hundred marks gives about two point one minutes a mark. The Institute publishes no official timing, so that figure is simple arithmetic, but it is the right starting point. A twenty-mark question is worth about forty two minutes. A fifteen-mark question about thirty one and a half. A ten-mark question about twenty one.

There is no separate reading time. Reading and planning come out of each question's budget. A sensible habit is to spend the first few minutes of each question reading the requirement, then the scenario, and jotting a list of issues on the note paper before you type.

Turn the budget into checkpoints before the day. Take the usual order, twenty, fifteen, fifteen, twenty, twenty, ten, and suppose the paper starts at exactly half past two. Question one should end at twelve minutes past three. Halfway, after question three, you should be at a quarter past four with fifty marks attempted. Question five should end at thirty nine minutes past five. The timer flashes at ten to six, and the paper ends at six. The full table is in the reading edition. If the start slips, shift every checkpoint with it.

The most valuable timing rule is the one the examiners keep repeating. Bank the easy marks on every question. On a typical marking guide, the first half of a question's marks come quickly. They are for spotting the issues, stating the rules and doing the standard computation. The last few marks come slowly. They are for a subtle interaction, or a point most candidates miss. Over-running on one question to chase its last two marks costs you the first five marks of the next.

So when the budget for a question runs out, stop. Leave a line in the answer box listing what you would have done next. Flag the question. Move on. If time remains at the end, come back.

The ten-minute warning is for finishing, not for starting. When the timer flashes, make sure every requirement has something in its answer box, copy across any working still in the spreadsheet, and give any recommendation you have not yet given.

One last timing point. The paper rewards breadth across the requirement more than depth on one point. A candidate who makes ten correct points in two lines each will usually outscore one who makes four points in a paragraph each. That is not an argument for shallow answers. It is an argument for making each point once, precisely, and moving on.

Reading the requirement.

Every question ends with requirements, and the requirements are the marking guide in disguise. Read them before the scenario. Then read the scenario knowing what you are looking for.

Notice the verbs. Calculate, with explanations, means a computation in which each line carries a short reason. Explain means prose, with the rule, its authority and its application to these facts. Discuss means weigh the options and reach a view. Recommend means name an option and give the main reason. After the May twenty twenty three sitting, the examiners reported that some candidates failed to give the recommendation and lost marks. A recommendation is one sentence. Never leave it out.

Notice the mark split. A twenty-mark question split eight, six and six, as in May twenty twenty six, tells you how long to spend on each part. Do not write three pages for a part worth three marks.

Notice the scope words. The examiners have drawn a clear line. Where a question asks for the tax consequences, consider every tax in the syllabus, including stamp duty and stamp duty land tax. Where it names a tax, nothing else scores. Stamp taxes are the classic missed marks. After the November twenty twenty five property question, the examiners noted that few candidates considered stamp duty land tax, so those marks were lost.

Notice what is excluded. In May twenty twenty six, a question on an overseas construction contract said transfer pricing was excluded. Some candidates discussed it anyway. Many discussed the residence or migration of the United Kingdom company, which the question was not about. In May twenty twenty three, candidates wasted time on diverted profits tax in a question about a loan, although that tax never applied to loan relationships, and for periods beginning on or after the first of January, twenty twenty six, it no longer exists at all. In November twenty twenty four, some raised hybrids where nothing in the facts was hybrid. Each of those paragraphs earned nothing, and each one cost time.

Notice when the question asks you to identify further information. The syllabus says a candidate should be able to identify the information still needed, and the May twenty twenty six deferred tax question tested it. A short list of precise questions, each tied to a rule, earns marks.

And notice the rubric on the front of the paper. All workings should be shown and made to the nearest month and pound unless the question specifies otherwise. The rubric also tells you to assume that the previous year's legislation continues to apply. Candidates answering by reference to more recently enacted legislation or tax cases will not be penalised. Candidates who answer by Scots law may use land and buildings transaction tax instead of stamp duty land tax.

How the marks are made.

The Institute publishes suggested answers with a full marking guide. Read a few and the pattern is obvious. Marks come in halves and wholes. A condition correctly stated is half a mark or one mark. A correct figure is half a mark or one mark. A correct conclusion is another half. A set of conditions for roll-over relief might be worth one and a half.

That tells you how to write. On that arithmetic, a twenty-mark question needs somewhere between twenty and forty distinct, correct points. Each should be short, in its own sentence or line, and easy to find. A marker scanning your answer should be able to tick as they read.

Several habits follow.

List every item in a computation, including the ones that need no adjustment, with a few words of reason. A nil adjustment with a correct reason earns its mark.

State the authority briefly. Section one seven nine of the Gains Act, or the substantial shareholding exemption in Schedule seven A C. The section number shows the marker you know which rule you are applying.

Do not restate the question. The examiners have said so repeatedly. Copying the facts back earns nothing.

Do not pad. After May twenty twenty six, the examiners described the interest restriction administration answers as adequate but padded with irrelevant detail. General paragraphs about a regime, written before you reach the facts, are padding.

Do not stop at a conclusion when a computation is asked for, and do not stop at a computation when an explanation is asked for.

Here is the second misconception. It says that Advanced Technical depth means writing everything you know. It does not. Depth on this paper means applying the right rule to these facts, with the conditions, the exceptions and the interaction that the facts raise. The examiners have praised, again and again, answers tied to the scenario. After the November twenty twenty four sitting they reported that higher marks went to answers that did exactly that.

What if the question seems wrong? The chief examiner's advice, after the May and November twenty twenty five sittings, was to re-read the question first. If you still think there is an error, state it, state your assumption, and answer on that basis. A sensible assumption, clearly stated, is never penalised for being an assumption. Results are moderated, and the pass mark may be adjusted for a paper's difficulty, so one strange question will not sink you on its own.

What about presentation? The papers from May twenty twenty three to November twenty twenty five said additional marks may be awarded for presentation. The May twenty twenty six paper dropped that line. Do not count on marks for neatness. But layout still decides whether the marker can find your points, and a marker who cannot find a point cannot give the mark.

Layouts.

The examiners expect conventional layouts, and they say so when candidates do not use them. After the May twenty twenty four capital allowances question, they noted that some candidates did not use a conventional layout. Here is the set this book has taught, in one place.

The corporation tax computation runs from profit before tax, through each adjustment with its reason, to the tax-adjusted trading profit. Then capital allowances, other income and gains, total profits, reliefs in their statutory order, taxable total profits, corporation tax with any marginal relief, the expenditure credit set off, and the amount payable with its instalment dates. Chapter seven built it.

Capital allowances go in columns. First-year allowances and full expensing, main pool, special rate pool, single asset pools, and a column of allowances claimed. Chapter eight built it.

A chargeable gain runs proceeds, costs of disposal, cost, enhancement, indexation to December twenty seventeen, any relief, and the chargeable gain. Roll-over relief shows the proceeds not reinvested. Chapters sixteen and seventeen built it.

The corporate interest restriction follows its statutory steps. Aggregate net tax-interest expense, aggregate tax E B I T D A, which means tax earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, thirty per cent of it, the debt cap, the interest capacity, the disallowance, and its allocation. Chapter twenty eight built it.

A controlled foreign company is attacked in order, company by company. Is it a controlled foreign company at all? Does an exemption apply? If so, stop. Which gateway lets profits through? Then the twenty five per cent test, apportionment, creditable tax and the charge. Chapter twenty six built it.

Double tax relief goes in columns by source, with deductions allocated and the credit limited to the lower of the foreign tax and the United Kingdom tax on that income. Chapter twenty four built it.

Deferred tax separates fixed asset differences from short-term differences, shows the opening and closing balances and the movement, and ends with the total tax charge. Chapter six built it.

Group relief across non-coterminous periods is a time line. Each overlapping period has its surrenderable amount, the claimant's available profits, and relief at the lower figure. Chapter fifteen built it.

Then one rule that cuts across all of them. Keep one unit in each table. Pounds, or thousands of pounds, never both. The examiners raised it after the May twenty twenty six group question. Take a computation you already know, Tarnmoor Engineering's for Group Year Two in our invented case. Total profits are twenty six million pounds. Group relief takes away seventeen point oh nine five million. Taxable total profits are eight point nine oh five million, and the corporation tax is two million, two hundred and twenty six thousand, two hundred and fifty pounds. In thousands that last figure has a decimal. That is fine. Switching units halfway down the table is not. The reading edition shows both versions side by side.

Every one of these layouts can be typed as a simple table in the answer box, or built in the spreadsheet and pasted. Practise both, and choose the one you can do fastest without losing the labels.

What the examiners keep saying.

Read seven sets of examiners' reports, from May twenty twenty three to May twenty twenty six, and nine messages come back again and again.

One. Answer the requirement asked. Time spent on excluded or unrelated topics earns nothing.

Two. Do not restate the question, and do not pad with general detail.

Three. Bank the easy marks on each question. Over-running for the last marks rarely pays.

Four. Show your workings. Marks are available even when the final figure is wrong.

Five. Use a conventional capital allowances layout.

Six. Give a recommendation when one is asked for.

Seven. Use the supplied treaty extract, not the O E C D Model, when an extract is given.

Eight. Tax consequences means every tax in the syllabus. A named tax means only that tax.

Nine. Keep pounds and thousands of pounds consistent.

Behind those messages sit the topic traps, and the Exam lens boxes in this book have collected them. A handful deserve repeating, because they have cost candidates marks at more than one sitting.

Roll-over relief is chargeable on the proceeds not reinvested, not on the gain. Short lease status is judged on the unexpired term at the date of the transaction, and an assignment is not a grant. Indexation cannot create or increase a loss, and a capital loss is never set against income. A no gain, no loss transfer passes on the transferor's cost, not market value.

On controlled foreign companies, the twenty five per cent apportionment threshold is not the control test, and once one exemption applies you stop. After May twenty twenty six, the examiners also criticised answers for not mentioning obvious points, such as how the controlled foreign company legislation works. Two lines on how the charge works and who pays it are almost free marks.

On the interest restriction, a transfer pricing adjustment to interest reduces the net tax-interest expense but not tax E B I T D A, and a disallowance never changes tax E B I T D A.

On depreciatory transactions and value shifting, the November twenty twenty four examiners put it crisply. Depreciatory transactions do not consider motive and only apply to losses. Value shifting is motive based.

On marginal relief, the examiners after May twenty twenty six warned that whilst the marginal rate of tax between the thresholds is twenty six point five per cent, this is not the rate that should be applied in full.

On double tax relief, after November twenty twenty five, they reported that nearly all candidates failed to realise that the royalties can be treated as one source and the foreign tax credits aggregated.

And on the compliance questions, which are among the most common on this paper, the traps are counting. Count associated companies on the right day. Apply the large group enquiry rules to a member of a large group. Use the accounts for the year ending in the parent's financial year for the tax strategy thresholds. Test the senior accounting officer rules on companies incorporated in the United Kingdom, whatever their residence.

Learning from the papers.

The Institute publishes, for each sitting, the question paper, suggested answers with a marking guide, and the examiners' report. From May twenty twenty four it has also published example candidate scripts, banded by mark, from forty to fifty, through fifty to sixty and sixty to seventy, to over seventy. They are the most useful study material you will find. Read a script that scraped a pass next to one that scored over seventy on the same question. The difference is rarely knowledge. It is usually coverage, precision and layout.

The papers also tell you what the examiners like to set. Across the seven papers from May twenty twenty three to May twenty twenty six, every sitting had a corporation tax computation of fifteen or twenty marks, often ending in instalments and a current and deferred tax charge. Capital allowances appeared standalone or inside nearly every computation. Property gains, leases and group roll-over came five times. The shareholding exemption and reorganisations came five times. Groups and consortia six times. International questions on residence, permanent establishments, the branch exemption or migration have come at every sitting since November twenty twenty four. Controlled foreign companies four times, including two full twenty-mark questions. Transfer pricing five times. The interest restriction four times. Compliance and governance eight times.

They also have favourite combinations, and this book was built to mirror them. A computation with capital allowances, losses, instalments and deferred tax. Transfer pricing with the interest restriction. A permanent establishment with the branch exemption, incorporation of the branch and a controlled foreign company. A property disposal with roll-over, the fixtures election, capital allowances and stamp duty land tax. The sale of a subsidiary with the degrouping charge, the shareholding exemption, the change of ownership rules and an enquiry. Chapter thirty one, on the Ridgeway deal, showed the method for a question that touches many regimes at once.

Some core topics have not been examined between twenty twenty three and twenty twenty six. Demergers, transactions in securities, liquidations, the corporate criminal offence and the general anti-abuse rule among them. Do not read silence as safety. A core topic can be set at any sitting.

Around the paper sits the Joint Programme. You must pass the Professional Responsibilities and Ethics exam before you sit an Advanced Technical paper. The Tax Knowledge and Skills paper must come before, or at the same sitting, and the Institute recommends sitting it first. A pass on the Advanced Technical paper is valid for seven sessions. If you fail the specialist paper, the programme lets you switch to another specialist paper on the same route, subject to your employer.

The pass rates are encouraging. They ran from fifty nine to seventy four per cent across the seven sittings, and the May twenty twenty six figure was the highest, at seventy four. After May twenty twenty four the chief examiner reported that Joint Programme candidates had the highest pass rates. That is reassurance, not a promise. Individual questions, such as the controlled foreign company apportionment in May twenty twenty five, have been answered poorly.

Two things to watch before your sitting. As at October twenty twenty six, the Institute had published no twenty twenty seven grid and no twenty twenty seven tax tables. When they appear, check every grade this book relies on, and check the rates. And from May twenty twenty eight the qualification changes. This paper continues, with four to six questions. The interest restriction, hybrids and intangible assets fall to non-core, and gains on leases leave the syllabus. A new learning outcome asks candidates to critically evaluate and communicate the output of information generated by other parties or by Artificial Intelligence. None of that applies to a twenty twenty seven sitting. It does show where the profession is heading.

What to take away.

The paper is three and a half hours, normally six questions of ten, fifteen or twenty marks, usually three twenties, two fifteens and one ten. One hundred marks, a pass mark of fifty, and at least seventy per cent of the corporation tax element from core topics. Scenarios are listed groups with no controlling individual. The style is technical prose and computations, never a letter.

Your likely sitting is the fourth of May, twenty twenty seven, at half past two, with the twenty sixth of October as the fallback. Arrive early. No watches. Bring a physical calculator. The note paper is not marked.

On R M Assessment Master, calculations left in the spreadsheet and not copied to the answer box will not be marked, and the examiners cannot see formulae. Copy every working across, with labels. The timer flashes ten minutes before the end, and that is the only warning.

The resources are the tax tables, the O E C D Model as an on-screen P D F, and Croner-i or Tolley legislation with tags but no notes. A supplied treaty extract beats the Model. The twenty twenty six tables show eighteen per cent. The law for your sitting is fourteen.

Budget two point one minutes a mark. Forty two minutes for twenty marks, thirty one and a half for fifteen, twenty one for ten. Halfway is a quarter past four. Bank the easy marks, then move on.

Read the requirement first. Calculate, explain, discuss, recommend. Tax consequences means every tax, a named tax means that tax alone. Excluded means excluded.

Marks come in halves and wholes, so write many short, precise points, each with its authority. Show workings, because a wrong answer with visible working still scores. State any assumption and carry on.

Use the conventional layouts, and one unit per table.

So what does this paper reward? Not what you know, but what you show. The right rule, applied to these facts, laid out where the marker can see it, inside the time.
