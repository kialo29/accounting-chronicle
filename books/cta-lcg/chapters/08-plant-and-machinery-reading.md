# Chapter eight: Plant and machinery for larger companies

*In our invented group, Tarnmoor, the capital expenditure committee meets early in GY2 with a list of purchases for Tarnmoor Engineering Ltd (TEL), the main trading company. New computer-controlled machining centres for the valve factory. Chillers and a new electrical system for the test hall. Test rigs that will be hired out to customers. A second-hand milling line bought from a competitor that is closing a site. Tom Hesketh, the group's head of tax, reads the list and asks the engineers the same question about every line: is it new, or has somebody used it before?*

That question is worth millions of pounds. On the answer turns whether a machine is written off for tax in the year it is bought, or drips into relief over a decade and more. And there is a second question Tom asks, which the engineers find stranger still: what will we do with it when we have finished with it? The most generous relief in the code comes with a receipt that must be handed back on the way out.

This chapter takes plant and machinery allowances to Advanced Technical depth, for **FY2026** (1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027), under the Capital Allowances Act 2001 (CAA 2001) as amended by Finance Act 2026. Every syllabus row this chapter covers is **core (grade 1)**, except "Additional VAT liabilities and rebates (ss 234–240)", which is **non-core (grade 2)**. *The Living Law* (TKS), chapter 12, built the basic machine: plant as the tools of the trade rather than the setting in which it is carried on; the main and special rate pools; the AIA; full expensing. Here we go further: the special balancing charge, the 40% FYA, the hybrid rate, the group's single AIA, cars, long-life and short-life assets, software, hire purchase, part use and business entertainment, and how allowances are claimed and given effect. Buildings (SBA), fixtures, long funding leases and successions belong to chapter 9; R&D allowances to chapter 10.

## Why the tax code ignores depreciation

A company that buys a machine for £1m does not charge £1m against its profit that year. Its accounts spread the cost over the machine's useful life as depreciation. Depreciation is an estimate: it depends on the directors' view of useful lives, residual values and impairment, and two honest companies with identical machines can depreciate them quite differently. A tax system that followed depreciation would let every board choose its own timetable of relief.

So the tax code refuses it. Depreciation, amortisation and impairment of plant are capital and are added back in the computation. In their place CAA 2001 gives statutory allowances at rates Parliament sets. The swap makes relief uniform (the same machine earns the same relief in any company) and hands the government a lever: by changing the rates, Parliament can pull investment forward or push it back.

The last few years show the lever at full stretch:

| Date | Event | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Spring Budget 2023 (15 March 2023) | Full expensing announced: 100% FYA for companies' new main-rate plant, 50% FYA for special rate, from **1 April 2023 to 31 March 2026**, replacing the temporary super-deduction | announced; enacted F(No.2)A 2023 (CAA ss 45S–45T) |
| Autumn Statement, 22 November 2023 | Full expensing made **permanent**; uncapped; cars, leasing and second-hand assets excluded | policy paper; end date removed by FA 2024 |
| Budget, 26 November 2025 | Main pool WDA **18% → 14%**; new **40% FYA** reaching plant for leasing and unincorporated businesses | policy paper; enacted **FA 2026 ss 28–29** |

Read together, the policy is clear: generous to new investment, less generous to old pools. The government's policy paper says the rate cut affects businesses with pools of historic main rate expenditure (pre-dating the super-deduction or full expensing) and spending that does not qualify for an FYA.

For a large group, every asset has to be **sorted**: new or used; main rate or special rate; owned, hired or leased out; kept or sold. Each answer sends the asset down a different road. A good capital allowances computation is a sorting machine, and it is only as good as the fixed asset register behind it.

## What counts, and when

**Qualifying activities** (CAA 2001 s 15) include a trade, an ordinary UK property business (how Tarnmoor Estates Ltd (TES) claims for plant in the buildings it lets), the management of a company with investment business (how Tarnmoor plc (TPLC) claims for its head office equipment), and an employment (irrelevant for companies).

**Qualifying expenditure** (s 11): capital expenditure on the provision of plant or machinery wholly or partly for the purposes of the qualifying activity, the person owning the plant as a result. Each word matters:
- *capital*: revenue repairs are simply deducted;
- *provision*: includes installation, and alterations to a building incidental to installation (s 25);
- *owns*: hence the special rules for hire purchase (s 67) and lessee-provided plant (s 70);
- *plant*: still case law. As TKS chapter 12 explained, plant is the apparatus with which the business is carried on, not the setting in which it is carried on. The statute fences off buildings and structures (ss 21–23, with List C saving items), which chapter 9 covers.

**Timing (s 5).** Capital expenditure is incurred when the obligation to pay becomes **unconditional**, even if payment is due later (s 5(1)–(2)). Refinements:

| Rule | Effect | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership by the period end; obligation unconditional within **one month** after the end | Treated as incurred immediately before the end of the period | s 5(4) |
| Amount not due until **more than 4 months** after the obligation becomes unconditional | Incurred on the date it is required to be paid | s 5(5) |
| Obligation made unconditional earlier than normal commercial practice, mainly to accelerate relief | Incurred on the date it is required to be paid | s 5(6) |
| Hire purchase | Unpaid capital instalments treated as incurred when the plant is brought into use | s 67(3) |

The examiner tested timing with plant built in stages in M24 Q6.

**Use for other purposes, and connected parties.** Plant already owned and then brought into a qualifying activity gives deemed qualifying expenditure when brought into use (s 13), but **no FYA** (s 46, general exclusion 8). Plant bought from a **connected person** (s 214) gets **no AIA or FYA** (s 217), and the buyer's qualifying expenditure is capped at the seller's disposal value (s 218). A machine sold from one Tarnmoor company to another therefore carries no AIA and no FYA for the buyer, however new it is.

## Full expensing and the 50% allowance

**The law (CAA 2001 s 45S).** An FYA is available on expenditure:
- incurred **on or after 1 April 2023**;
- by a **company within the charge to corporation tax**;
- on plant or machinery that is **unused and not second-hand**;
- not incurred under **disqualifying arrangements** (s 45T: broadly, arrangements with a main purpose of securing the allowance);
- not excluded by s 46.

The rate is **100%** for main rate expenditure ("full expensing") and **50%** for **special rate expenditure** (s 52(3) Table), with the balance of a 50% asset going to the special rate pool. There is **no cap**.

**General exclusions (s 46)** include: (1) the final chargeable period of the qualifying activity; (2) cars; (6) plant for **leasing** (including hire), except, for s 45S, an **excluded lease of background plant or machinery for a building** (s 46(4A)); (8) s 13/13A/14 cases.

**Special rate expenditure** (s 104A): thermal insulation, **integral features** (s 33A: electrical and lighting systems, cold water, space or water heating, powered ventilation, air cooling or purification, and the rest of the list), **long-life assets**, cars that are not main-rate cars, cushion gas and solar panels.

**No double relief (s 52A).** The AIA and an FYA cannot both be claimed on the same expenditure, nor two FYAs. A company may claim less than the full FYA (s 52(4)); unclaimed expenditure is relieved through the pool.

> **Worked example: TEL's GY2 first-year allowances (invented)**
>
> | Item | Cost £ | Treatment | Allowance £ | To pool £ |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | New machining centres (main rate, own use) | 9,000,000 | Full expensing 100% (s 45S) | 9,000,000 | — |
> | New chillers and electrical systems (special rate) | 1,200,000 | 50% FYA (s 45S) | 600,000 | 600,000 to special rate pool |
> | New test rigs for hire to UK customers (main rate, leasing) | 800,000 | Not full expensing (leasing); 40% FYA (s 45U) | 320,000 | 480,000 to main pool |
> | Second-hand milling line (unconnected seller) | 500,000 | No FYA (second-hand); no AIA allocated to TEL | — | 500,000 to main pool |

The analogy from *The Living Law*: the main pool is a bath draining at 14% a year that never quite empties. Full expensing tips the whole bucket out at once. But a bucket emptied at once raises a question the bath never had to answer: what happens if you sell the bucket?

> **Exam lens: full expensing and FYAs**
> - **Grade:** first year qualifying expenditure, other allowances and charges, special rate expenditure: all **core (1)**.
> - **Past papers:** M26 Q2 (15 marks: new versus second-hand plant; FE not available on second-hand); M24 Q6 (20 marks: FE and instalment timing of plant built in stages; integral features); N24 Q4 (20 marks: used vans, cars, integral features); M23 Q1 (balancing charge on FE plant).
> - **Traps:** second-hand plant gets no FE; integral features and long-life assets get 50%, not 100%; leased plant gets no FE (but see the 40% FYA); cars never get FE.
> - **Style:** "Calculate, with explanations": half a mark to a mark per item, for the treatment and a one-line reason.

## The receipt you hand back

**The misconception:** "selling a fully expensed machine just reduces the main pool." It does not. A disposal of an asset that had full expensing triggers a **special balancing charge**.

**Why.** Under the pool system the clawback (a disposal value off the pool) is as slow as the relief. Full expensing gave the relief in one year. If the proceeds merely went into the pool, the company would repay over many years a relief it enjoyed in one, and a company with a large old pool might never feel the proceeds at all. So Parliament built a charge that matches the speed of the relief.

**The law.**
- **s 59A (main rate, full expensing):** where a company has had an s 45S FYA on non-special-rate expenditure and must bring a disposal value into account, it is liable to a balancing charge for that period equal to the **relevant proportion of the disposal value**. Relevant proportion = expenditure that had the s 45S FYA ÷ total expenditure on the asset that has been the subject of that or any other FYA, or allocated to a pool. The company's TDR for the pool is reduced by the charge, so the proceeds are not counted twice.
- **s 59B (special rate, 50%):** the same, but the FYA expenditure is **halved** in the numerator: half the disposal value (for an asset wholly within s 45S) is a charge, and the other half reduces the special rate pool.
- **s 59C:** arrangements with a main purpose of avoiding or reducing a s 59A/59B charge are disregarded.
- As for any disposal value, the amount brought in cannot exceed the original qualifying expenditure (s 62).
- The 40% FYA (s 45U) has **no** special balancing charge: ss 59A–59B refer only to s 45S.

> **Worked example: a split asset (hypothetical, not a story fact)**
>
> A company buys a new main-rate machine for £500,000, sets £200,000 against the AIA (allocated to the main pool) and claims full expensing on £300,000. It later sells the machine for £250,000.
>
> | | £ |
> |---|---|
> | Disposal value | 250,000 |
> | Relevant proportion 300,000 / 500,000 | 3/5 |
> | Special balancing charge (s 59A) | 150,000 |
> | Disposal value deducted from main pool (250,000 − 150,000) | 100,000 |
>
> N24 Q4 part 2 (5 marks) turned on exactly this split; the examiners called it the harder part of the question.

> **Worked example: Calder's GY3 sale of process-valve plant (invented)**
>
> On 1 October GY3 Calder Valve Engineering Ltd (CVE) sells the plant of its process-valve business to Tarnmoor Vallaria SA (TVS, resident in the invented country of Vallaria) for £1.4m, alongside the £6.0m intangibles sale (chapters 11 and 27). The disposal values (each below original cost) split as follows. Calder's 9-month AP runs 1 April–31 December GY3.
>
> | Plant | Disposal value £ | Original relief | Relevant proportion | Special balancing charge £ | Pool deduction £ |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | Machines bought new, fully expensed | 900,000 | 100% FE (s 45S) | 1 | 900,000 | — |
> | Heavy test bed (long-life asset, so special rate) | 200,000 | 50% FYA (s 45S) | ½ (s 59B) | 100,000 | 100,000 (special rate pool) |
> | Older plant (pre-FE or second-hand) | 300,000 | Main pool | — | — | 300,000 (main pool) |
> | **Total** | **1,400,000** | | | **1,000,000** | |
>
> The charges (£1.0m) are taxable in Calder's 9-month AP; at 25% they cost **£250,000**. TVS is a connected company, so the price must be at arm's length (TIOPA 2010 Part 4; chapter 27). TVS is outside UK tax, so s 218 is irrelevant to it.

## The 40% first-year allowance

Full expensing has a hole: plant for leasing is excluded, and so is anything bought by a business outside corporation tax. Before 2026, a company buying new plant to lease out got only the AIA (if any was left) and then the main pool.

**The law (CAA 2001 s 45U, inserted by FA 2026 s 29).** A **40%** FYA on expenditure incurred **on or after 1 January 2026** on plant or machinery that is **unused and not second-hand** and **not special rate expenditure**, not incurred under disqualifying arrangements (s 45V) and not excluded by s 46. Key features:
- **any business** (no "company" condition);
- **leasing allowed** (s 46(4B)): the lessee must use the plant wholly or almost wholly to earn income within the charge to tax, or be UK resident and not use it to a significant extent to earn untaxed non-UK income; DTR-relieved income counts as outside the charge (s 46(4C)). In short: plant leased into the UK tax net qualifies; plant leased overseas does not;
- cars excluded (general exclusion 2);
- the **60% balance goes to the main pool** (GOV.UK guidance);
- **no special balancing charge** on disposal: proceeds come off the main pool.

**Commencement (real-calendar example).** A leasing company with a year ending **31 March 2026** buys a new machine to lease to a UK customer. If the obligation to pay became unconditional on **20 December 2025**, there is no FYA at all (full expensing excludes leasing; s 45U had not started). If it became unconditional on **5 January 2026**, the company has 40%. Section 5 decides which.

**In our invented case,** TEL's £0.8m of new test rigs are hired to UK utility customers on two-year hires. Full expensing is barred (leasing); the 40% FYA applies: **£320,000** now and **£480,000** to the main pool. The hires are short, so TEL as lessor keeps the allowances; a long, finance-type lease could have been a **long funding lease** moving allowances to the lessee (chapter 9).

**The three FYAs by whom they serve:** full expensing is for companies investing in their own new main-rate plant; the 50% FYA is its special rate companion; the 40% FYA is for everyone else's new main-rate plant, including plant leased into the UK.

## 14%, and the hybrid rate

**FA 2026 s 28** amends CAA 2001 s 56(1): main pool WDA **14%** for **corporation tax chargeable periods beginning on or after 1 April 2026** (income tax: 6 April 2026). The special rate (s 104D) stays at **6%**.

**Hybrid rate (FA 2026 s 28(3)–(6))** for a chargeable period straddling 1 April 2026:

X = (18% × BRD / CP) + (14% × ARD / CP), using **days**, **rounded up** to 2 decimal places,

where BRD = days before 1 April 2026, ARD = days on or after, CP = days in the period.

> **Worked example: hybrid rates (real-calendar, computed in Python)**
>
> | Chargeable period | BRD | ARD | Unrounded % | Hybrid rate |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | 1 Jan 2026 – 31 Dec 2026 | 90 | 275 | 14.9863 | **14.99%** |
> | 1 Oct 2025 – 30 Sep 2026 | 182 | 183 | 15.9945 | **16.00%** |
> | 1 Jul 2025 – 30 Jun 2026 | 274 | 91 | 17.0027 | **17.01%** |
>
> On a main pool of £1,000,000, the calendar-2026 company's WDA is **£149,900**.

**Short and long periods.** The WDA is proportionately reduced for a chargeable period of less than 12 months (s 56(3)): Calder's 9-month AP to 31 December GY3 gets 9/12 × 14% = **10.5%**. The AIA is likewise proportioned: a 9-month period's maximum is **£750,000**.

The rest of the pool mechanics are as TKS chapter 12 taught: a pool of **£1,000** or less may be written off (small pools allowance); a balancing allowance on the main pool arises only in the final chargeable period.

**In our invented case**, every story period is run on FY2026 rules, so Tarnmoor's pools use 14% with no hybrid rate. TEL's main pool brings forward **£39.5m**, mostly pre-full-expensing spend. After additions and disposals it stands at **£40.18m**; 14% gives **£5,625,200**. At 18% it would have been £7,232,400: the policy paper's "historic pool" in the flesh.

> **Exam lens: rates and the 2027 tables**
> - **Grade:** other allowances and charges **1**.
> - **Trap for 2027 sittings:** the **2026 CIOT tax tables show 18%** (FA 2025). Papers set on **FA 2026** use **14%**, with the 40% FYA (s 45U). The 2027 tables are not yet published: check them when they appear. Hybrid rates are rounded **up**, by **days**.
> - **Style:** state the rate and, for a straddling period, show the day-weighting.

## One annual investment allowance for the whole group

The AIA (ss 38A, 51A) gives 100% relief on up to **£1,000,000** a year (proportioned for short or long periods). For companies it matters less than before, because full expensing gives 100% on new main-rate plant without limit. It still covers ground full expensing does not: second-hand plant; special rate expenditure (where 100% beats 50%); fixtures acquired with a second-hand building. **Cars** are excluded (s 38B), as is expenditure in the final chargeable period.

**The group rule (s 51C).** Where a company is a **parent undertaking** (Companies Act 2006 s 1162) of other companies in a financial year, the parent and its subsidiaries are entitled to **only one AIA** between them for their chargeable periods **ending in that financial year**. A company is treated as the parent's subsidiary for this purpose if the parent holds that status at the end of the subsidiary's chargeable period. Groups and companies under common control that are "related" share one AIA under ss 51D–51E (with ss 51F–51G). The companies allocate the AIA **as they think fit**.

**Acquisitions and changing year ends:**
- A target's period that **ended before** the acquisition is outside the group. Calder's year to 31 March GY1 ended the day before TPLC bought it: Calder kept its own £1m AIA for that year.
- Calder's year to **31 March GY2** ended while TPLC was its parent: it shares the **GY2** group AIA.
- A company with **two periods ending in the same financial year** draws on the same single AIA: Calder's year to 31 March GY3 **and** its 9-month AP to 31 December GY3 both end in GY3, alongside every other group company's GY3 period.

**Where should it go?** Every Tarnmoor company pays 25%, so the question is timing. The AIA adds most relief this year where **no FYA is available at all**, and most of all where the alternative is the 6% special rate pool.

> **Worked example: the group AIA for GY2 (invented)**
>
> | Company | Chargeable period ending in GY2 | Expenditure | AIA allocated £ | Why |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | TES | Year to 31 December GY2 | Fixtures in the office bought in GY2 (s 198 figure £0.4m: chapter 9) | 400,000 | Not new: no FYA available |
> | CVE | Year to 31 March GY2 | Second-hand plant | 600,000 | Not new: no FYA available; Calder's period ends 9 months earlier, so its relief reduces instalments falling due sooner |
> | TEL | Year to 31 December GY2 | — | nil | New main-rate plant has FE; special rate plant has the 50% FYA; its £0.5m second-hand milling line competes with Calder's on equal terms |
> | **Total** | | | **1,000,000** | |

The allocation is a group decision that must reach each company's return; record it centrally each year.

> **Exam lens: the AIA in groups**
> - **Grade:** Annual Investment Allowance **1**.
> - **Past papers:** M26 Q4 (AIA and instalment timing in a two-company group); M24 Q6 (FE and instalment timing, two-company computation).
> - **Traps:** one AIA per group, not per company; the AIA is not available for cars; allocate it to expenditure with no FYA, special rate first; time-apportion for short periods.

## Cars and long-life assets

**Cars.** "Car" (s 268A) excludes motorcycles, vans and vehicles not commonly used or suitable for private use. A **main-rate car** (s 104AA) has CO2 emissions **not exceeding 50g/km**, is electric, or was first registered before 1 March 2001; every other car is special rate. Cars get **no AIA, no full expensing, no 50% and no 40% FYA**. The exception: a **new zero-emission car** has a **100% FYA** for expenditure to **31 March 2027** (CT) (FA 2026 s 30): coming next, that window closes.

> *Hypothetical:* a new car at 120g/km costing £40,000 goes to the special rate pool: WDA 6% = **£2,400** (at 14% it would have been £5,600). A new van, not being a car, can be fully expensed.

**Long-life assets (LLA) (ss 90–102).** Plant with an expected useful economic life, when new, of **at least 25 years** (s 91). LLA expenditure is special rate expenditure (s 104A). **De minimis (s 99):** a company's LLA expenditure in a chargeable period is not treated as such if it is within **£100,000**, but for a company that limit is:
- **divided by 1 + the number of associated companies** (s 99(4), (4A); CTA 2010 ss 18E–18J);
- **reduced** for a period shorter than a year, **not increased** for a longer one (s 99(3));
- **all or nothing**: exceed it, and *all* the period's LLA expenditure is LLA (s 100).

> **Worked example: the LLA trap (hypothetical, not a story fact)**
>
> In GY2 each Tarnmoor company has 9 associated companies, so its LLA limit is £100,000 / 10 = **£10,000**. Suppose TEL also bought a new heat-treatment furnace for £300,000 with a 30-year life.
>
> | | Treated as main rate (wrong) £ | Treated as LLA, special rate (right) £ |
> |---|---|---|
> | FYA | 300,000 (100%) | 150,000 (50%) |
> | WDA on balance at 6% | — | 9,000 |
> | Relief in GY2 | 300,000 | 159,000 |
>
> Misclassification overstates first-year relief by **£141,000** on one asset.

The examiner hides long-life assets in a passing sentence about useful lives.

## Short-life assets and software

**Short-life assets (ss 83–86).** An election puts an asset in its own single asset pool, so that a sale or scrapping below TWDV gives an immediate balancing allowance. For CT the election is due **within 2 years** after the end of the chargeable period of the expenditure and is **irrevocable**. If the asset is still held at the **8th anniversary** of the end of that period, the balance moves to the main pool. Not available for cars, special rate expenditure, plant for leasing (with exceptions) or s 13/14 cases. With full expensing the election matters less: it earns its keep on main-rate assets that cannot be fully expensed, such as second-hand computer equipment beyond the AIA. A sale above TWDV gives a balancing charge.

**Software (CAA 2001 ss 71–72).** Capital expenditure on software, or a right to use it, is treated as plant; a capital sum for a sub-licence is a disposal value. But most company software is also an intangible fixed asset under CTA 2009 Part 8 (chapter 11). The interface:

| Software | Regime | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Treated in the accounts as part of the cost of related hardware | Excluded from Part 8 (except royalties): capital allowances | CTA 2009 s 813 |
| Other capital software | Part 8, unless the company **elects** for capital allowances | CTA 2009 s 815 |

HMRC's guidance (CIRD25180) says the s 815 election must be in writing, identify the expenditure, and be made **within 2 years** after the end of the AP in which the expenditure was incurred; it is **irrevocable**. Why elect? Capital allowances may give faster relief than amortisation, which the intangibles regime generally follows. Realisation is then dealt with mainly through capital allowances (s 815(4)–(5)).

> **Exam lens: special assets**
> - **Grade:** cars, short life assets, long life assets, computer software, special rate expenditure: all **core (1)**.
> - **Past papers:** M24 Q2 (15 marks: hotel, thermal insulation, cars on HP over 50g/km); N23 Q4 (SLAs in a CT computation); M25 Q2 (cars, HP plant, cloud/software costs in R&D).
> - **Traps:** dividing the LLA limit by the associated companies; LLA gets 50% not 100%; cars in the AIA or FE column; vans are not cars.

## Hire purchase and plant provided by lessees

**Hire purchase (s 67).** A person incurring capital expenditure under a contract providing that it **shall or may become the owner** of plant on performing the contract is treated as the owner while entitled to the benefit of the contract. When the plant is **brought into use**, all capital expenditure still to be incurred under the contract is treated as incurred **at that time**: the **whole capital element** enters the computation at once. The interest element is not capital expenditure: it is a finance cost relieved over the contract.

**Modern twist (s 67(2A)–(2C)).** Where the contract is (or would be) a lease under GAAP, s 67 applies only if it is accounted for as a **finance lease** (or, for a right-of-use lessee, would be). Operating leases stay out; long leases of plant fall to the long funding lease rules (chapter 9).

The sorting questions apply at the date the asset is brought into use: new main-rate plant (full expensing for a company); second-hand (AIA or pool); a car (car rules: over 50g/km, special rate pool).

**Plant provided by a lessee (s 70).** A lessee required by its lease to provide plant that is not a fixture is treated as owner while using it for its qualifying activity; no disposal value when the lease ends; the lessor brings in a disposal value on a later disposal.

> **Exam lens: hire purchase**
> - **Grade:** hire purchase etc and plant provided by lessee **1**.
> - **Past papers:** M26 Q2 (HP vans: examiners named the HP capital element as an error); M24 Q2 (cars on HP); M25 Q2 (HP plant).
> - **Trap:** spreading the capital element over the instalments; claiming the interest as capital.

## Part use and business entertainment

**Partly qualifying use (ss 205–208).** Where plant is used partly for other purposes, an FYA or AIA is reduced to a **just and reasonable** amount (s 205), and the asset goes into a **single asset pool** (ss 206–207) so that WDAs and balancing adjustments are reduced in the same way.

**Employees' private use: HMRC's view (CA27100).** Where an employer provides an asset to a director or employee as part of the remuneration package, HMRC accepts the cost as incurred for the business's qualifying activity, and the company's claim is not restricted; the employee is taxed on the benefit. The manual adds that where the asset is blatantly incongruous with the business's commercial needs, allowances may be restricted on grounds of personal choice, citing *G H Chambers (Northiam Farms) Ltd v Watmough* (as cited in CA27100; citation not re-verified).

**Business entertainment (s 269).** Use of plant for **business entertainment** (which includes hospitality of any kind provided in connection with the business) is treated as use **not** for the qualifying activity. Exceptions: where providing entertainment is the function of the trade (a hotel, an events business), and hospitality provided only for staff; **directors count as employees** (s 269(5)).

> *Hypothetical:* Tarnmoor buys a launch on a lake near its head office, used on 60 days a year: 40 customer days and 20 staff summer events. The customer days are business entertainment, so allowances are reduced to a just and reasonable proportion, here about **one third** (20/60), in a single asset pool. Read the facts: a hotel's televisions are not restricted; the same televisions in a manufacturer's hospitality suite are. The profit-computation side of entertaining is in chapter 7.

> **Exam lens: part use**
> - **Grade:** assets provided or used only partly for qualifying activities **1**; use of plant or machinery for business entertainment **1**.
> - **Past paper:** M24 Q2 (hotel: televisions, paintings, kitchen equipment).
> - **Trap:** applying individuals' private-use restrictions to a company's employee-benefit assets.

## Giving effect, claiming, and VAT adjustments

**Giving effect.**

| Qualifying activity | How allowances and charges are given | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Trade | Allowance an expense, charge a receipt, in calculating trade profits (so CAs can create or increase a trading loss for s 37, s 45A or group relief) | CAA 2001 s 247 |
| Property business | Expense / receipt of the property business (TES) | s 248 |
| Company with investment business | Deducted from that business's income, any excess treated as management expenses (TPLC: adds to excess management expenses surrendered to TEL, chapters 13 and 15) | s 253; CTA 2009 s 1233 |

**Claims.** A company claims capital allowances in its company tax return (FA 1998 Sch 18 Part IX). A claim can be made, amended or withdrawn **only by amending the return** (para 81). The time limit (para 82) is the **last** of: the first anniversary of the filing date (in practice about 2 years after the end of a 12-month AP, CA11140); **30 days after an enquiry is completed**; and other dates where the return is amended after an enquiry or determination.

**Case: *HMRC v Dundas Heritable Ltd* [2019] UKUT 208 (TCC).** A pub and bar operator filed its returns for the years to 31 March 2012 and 2013 years late (on 3 February 2015 and 26 November 2015), with CA claims inside them. HMRC opened enquiries in time and then disallowed the claims as out of time. The Upper Tribunal dismissed HMRC's appeal: the opening of the enquiries brought the late claims within the para 82 enquiry limb. HMRC's own enquiry cured the lateness. The practical lesson is not to rely on a lucky enquiry: the window is statutory and the claim lives in the return, so a group that wants to claim less (for example, to preserve losses) must amend within the window.

**VAT adjustments (ss 234–240; grade 2).** An **additional VAT liability** (for example under the capital goods scheme) is treated as further qualifying expenditure in the chargeable period in which it accrues, and attracts the AIA or an FYA if the original expenditure did. An **additional VAT rebate** is a disposal value (or added to one). The CGS thresholds changed from **29 July 2026** (SI 2026/765), and computer equipment was removed from the scheme. Principle: allowances follow the net cost the business bears.

> **Exam lens: giving effect and claims**
> - **Grade:** giving effect to allowances and charges **1**; Part 1 CAA 2001 **1**; VAT adjustments **2**.
> - **Past papers:** M24 Q1 and M26 Q4 (investment companies, where CAs on management assets feed management expenses); N25 Q4 (CAs in a loss-making new subsidiary; R&D allowances often missed).
> - **Trap:** treating an investment company's allowances as a trading deduction.

## Planning, and how the examiner tests it

> **Going further: the tax function's year**
> 1. **Timing.** Expenditure is incurred when the obligation becomes unconditional (s 5), so a purchase committed just before a year end can bring a year's relief forward, subject to the 4-month rule (s 5(5)) and the anti-acceleration rule (s 5(6)). For a very large company paying QIPs, relief in the current year reduces the instalments still to come: update the forecast when a large purchase is committed (chapter 3).
> 2. **Sorting at procurement.** New rather than second-hand; main rather than special rate; bought rather than leased; useful life under 25 years. Buying reconditioned plant to save cash can forfeit full expensing worth far more than the saving.
> 3. **AIA allocation.** Allocate the group's single AIA deliberately each financial year, to expenditure with no FYA, special rate first.
> 4. **Claiming less.** Allowances are not compulsory. A company that is loss-making, or whose carried-forward losses would be restricted above the £5m deductions allowance (chapter 14), may claim less and keep expenditure in the pool; in a group, compare with surrendering a CA-driven loss as group relief (chapter 15). Any change must be made within FA 1998 Sch 18 para 82.
> 5. **Interactions.** Capital allowances are excluded from **tax-EBITDA** for the CIR (TIOPA 2010 s 407), so full expensing does not reduce interest capacity (chapter 28). Full expensing creates large deferred tax liabilities (NBV far above TWDV: chapter 6). An intra-group or cross-border disposal of FE plant needs a transfer pricing check and a special balancing charge together.
> 6. **Records.** Tag every addition in the fixed asset register: new or second-hand; main or special rate; owned, HP, leased in or leased out; useful life; claim history (FE, 50%, 40%, AIA, pool). A disposal ten years later can then be sorted without archaeology.

> **Exam lens: capital allowances overall**
> - **Frequency:** standalone M24 Q2 (15), M26 Q2 (15), N23 Q3(b); embedded in nearly every computation (M23 Q1, M24 Q6, N24 Q4, M25 Q2, N25 Q1, M26 Q4) and in property questions (M23 Q4, N25 Q4, N25 Q5).
> - **What the examiners said:** M24 Q2: "some did not use a conventional CA layout"; M26 Q2: errors on the HP capital element; N24 Q4: the FE disposal split was the harder part; M23 Q1: disposal of FE plant gives a balancing charge.
> - **Style:** "Calculate, with explanations"; 0.5–1 mark per item; no letter format.
> - **Layout:** columns for FYA/FE, main pool, special rate pool, single asset pools, allowances; additions with and without FYAs; disposals (pool deduction or special balancing charge); WDAs; c/f; £ throughout (never mix £ and £000).
> - **Traps:** second-hand plant gets no FE; HP capital element in full when brought into use; cars excluded from AIA and FYAs (except new zero-emission cars to 31 March 2027); FE disposals give a balancing charge; LLA limit divided by associated companies; **14% not the 2026 tables' 18%**.

## What to take away

- The tax code ignores depreciation and gives statutory allowances; every allowance depends on **sorting**: new or used, main or special rate, owned, hired or leased, kept or sold.
- **Full expensing (s 45S):** companies; new and unused; 100% main rate, 50% special rate; from 1 April 2023; permanent; not for second-hand plant, cars, leasing (except background plant), or the final period.
- **Special balancing charge (ss 59A–59C):** disposal value × FE expenditure ÷ total; half for 50% assets, the other half off the special rate pool. Calder's GY3 sale: **£1.0m** of charges.
- **40% FYA (s 45U):** from 1 January 2026; new main-rate plant; any business; leasing into the UK allowed; 60% to the main pool; no special balancing charge.
- **WDA 14%** for CT periods beginning on or after 1 April 2026; hybrid by days, rounded up (calendar 2026: **14.99%**); special rate **6%**.
- **AIA £1m**, one per group for chargeable periods ending in the financial year (s 51C); allocate to expenditure with no FYA, special rate first.
- **Cars:** over 50g/km special rate; no AIA or FYA except new zero-emission cars to 31 March 2027. **LLA:** 25 years; £100,000 ÷ (1 + associates); all or nothing. **SLA:** 2-year election; 8-year cut-off. **Software:** plant, but Part 8 unless s 815 election within 2 years.
- **HP:** whole capital element when brought into use. **Part use and entertainment:** just and reasonable reduction, single asset pool.
- **Giving effect:** trade expense; property business expense; investment business, then management expenses. **Claims:** in the return; para 82 window (*Dundas Heritable*).
- **TEL GY2 (invented):** total allowances **£16,000,000** (FE £9.0m; 50% FYA £0.6m; 40% FYA £0.32m; main pool WDA £5,625,200; special rate WDA £396,000; SBA £58,800).

The SBA in that total is the bridge to the next chapter. Plant is the apparatus; chapter 9 turns to the setting itself (buildings and the fixtures inside them) and to what happens to allowances when assets are leased or a whole trade changes hands.
