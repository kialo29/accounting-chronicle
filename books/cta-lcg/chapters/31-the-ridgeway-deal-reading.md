# Part Five: Putting It Together.

# Chapter 31: The Ridgeway deal.

## Completion day.

On **1 April**, some years after the events of the last thirty chapters, Tarnmoor plc (TPLC) bought a bicycle company. The price was **£4.5m** in cash. The seller was Jess Hartley; the company was **Ridgeway Holdings Ltd (RH)**, the parent of **Ridgeway Cycles Ltd (RC)**. Our invented group, Tarnmoor, its people and its numbers are invented for teaching, as they have been throughout this book. So is Jess, whose story *The Living Law* (TKS) told. Ireland, which will matter later, is real, as are its 12.5% trading rate and the UK–Ireland treaty.

By Tarnmoor's standards it was a small deal. Tarnmoor's revenue runs to more than £1bn a year; Ridgeway's taxable profit is about £600,000. Yet when Tom Hesketh, the group head of tax, opened the file that morning, the list of tax points ran to two pages. That is the question for this chapter: **why does a small acquisition by a large group touch almost every rule in this book, and how do you find them all, in order, without missing the ones that carry marks?**

This is chapter 31, the first chapter of Part Five. It is the capstone. It teaches **no new rule**: every rule it uses was taught earlier, and each time the owning chapter is named. What it adds is the **method, the order and the interactions**: what the LCG syllabus narrative calls the interaction of taxes and corporate transformations, and the way most 20-mark questions are built. It also closes the last open thread of *The Living Law*: the election Jess never made for her Dublin showroom.

Statutes: Corporation Tax Act 2009 (**CTA 2009**), Corporation Tax Act 2010 (**CTA 2010**), Taxation (International and Other Provisions) Act 2010 (**TIOPA 2010**), Taxation of Chargeable Gains Act 1992 (**TCGA 1992**), and the Corporation Tax (Instalment Payments) Regulations 1998 (SI 1998/3175). As always, the numbers apply the law for **FY2026** (Finance Act 2026), whatever year the story has reached.

**Story time.** The Ridgeway year is "some years later": after GY7, not numbered. In this chapter **RY** means the Ridgeway year, and RC's accounting periods are **AP1** (1 April RY – 31 March RY+1), **AP2** (1 April RY+1 – 31 March RY+2) and **AP3** (1 April RY+2 – 31 March RY+3). No calendar year is attached to any of them.

## The road to Ridgeway.

*The Living Law* followed Jess Hartley from a cycle business she ran herself, through incorporation and a holding company structure, to a group of two companies: **RH owns 100% of RC**, and Jess owns 100% of RH, with a **base cost of £162,000**. Along the way the group bought and later sold Wharfe Wheels (TKS chapter 24). RC opened a second unit in Skipton, and then a **showroom in Dublin**.

The Dublin showroom is a **branch**: a permanent establishment (PE) of RC in Ireland, not a separate company. *The Living Law*, chapter 26, gave its results:

| Branch year | Result (£) | Treatment (no election) |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | (80,000) | Loss relieved against RC's UK profits at once |
| Year 2 | (30,000) | Loss relieved against RC's UK profits at once |
| Year 3 | 120,000 | Taxed in the UK with credit for Irish tax |
| Year 4 | 200,000 | Irish tax at 12.5%: £25,000; UK CT with credit |

Jess never made the **s 18A CTA 2009** election; *The Living Law*, chapter 26, left the question open. *The Living Law*, chapter 31, imagined a **hypothetical £4m offer** for the group "if a buyer came back tomorrow"; no sale happened. This is the real sale, at its own price: **£4.5m**.

**RC today (invented).** UK trading profit about **£400,000** a year; Dublin branch profit about **£200,000**. RC's year ends **31 March**, so the deal falls on the **first day of an AP** (a change of owner never ends an AP by itself: CTA 2009 s 10). That simplifies several timing tests below.

> **Worked example 31.1: RC's corporation tax before the deal (invented; £)**
>
> | | £ |
> |---|---|
> | UK trading profit | 400,000 |
> | Dublin branch profit (part of RC's trading profit; no election) | 200,000 |
> | **TTP** | **600,000** |
> | CT at 25% (main rate: far above the upper limit) | 150,000 |
> | Less DTR: lower of Irish tax £25,000 and UK tax on the branch profit £50,000 (TIOPA 2010 s 42) | (25,000) |
> | **CT payable** | **125,000** |
> | Total tax, Ireland + UK | 150,000 |

**The seller's side, in one paragraph.** Jess is an individual selling shares. Her gain before costs is £4,500,000 − £162,000 = **£4,338,000**, charged to capital gains tax, with business asset disposal relief if she still meets its conditions (*The Living Law*, chapters 18 and 31). No corporation tax, no SSE, nothing for the buyer to compute. **LCG does not examine it**: note it in one line and move on.

## Walking every floor.

An Advanced Technical question is a **building inspection**. A good surveyor does not wander the house looking at whatever catches the eye: she walks **every floor, in the same order, with the same checklist**, and records what she finds on each, including the floors where she finds nothing. The checklist is what stops her missing the damp in the cellar while admiring the roof.

**Tom's checklist for a share acquisition** (the order this chapter follows):

| 3. Thresholds | Associated companies (two timings); QIP status (large / very large / grace); knock-on for every group company | 1, 3 |

| 6. Special features | Here: the foreign branch (s 18A election; s 140; transfer to TIL; CFC) | 25, 26 |

**Two exam rules shape the walk.** After the N25 and M26 sittings the chief examiner explained that "tax consequences" means **every tax in the syllabus**, including stamp duty and SDLT; a **named** tax means only that tax. And **excluded means excluded**: if the question says ignore TP, a paragraph on it earns nothing (chapter 32).

> **Misconception: "a small acquisition is a small tax job."** It is not. The size of the target barely matters; the size of the group it joins decides the compliance load. RC goes from a two-company family business to a member of a listed group of **eleven** companies that count as associated, a **worldwide group** for the CIR, an **MNE group** for Pillar Two, a group with no **TP SME exemption** and a group with an **SAO**. Almost every compliance setting changes on completion day, and some change a year later. The tax on RC's profits hardly moves; everything around it does.

## The price of the paper.

**Stamp duty** (chapter 21). TPLC buys shares, so the charge is stamp duty on the stock transfer form: **0.5%**, rounded **up** to the nearest £5, payable by the **buyer**; the instrument must be presented for stamping **within 30 days** of execution, or interest and penalties run.

> **Worked example 31.2: stamp duty (invented)**
>
> £4,500,000 × 0.5% = **£22,500** (a multiple of £5: no rounding). Payable by TPLC. Cash consideration only: no contingent element, so no "stated maximum" question (chapter 21).

- **No SDLT**: RC's Skipton property stays inside RC; only shares change hands.
- **No stamp duty group relief** (FA 1930 s 42): the transferor is an individual, and relief needs a 75% relationship between transferor and transferee.
- **Base cost**: the £22,500 is an incidental cost of acquisition (TCGA 1992 s 38), not deductible for CT. TPLC's base cost in RH is £4,522,500. It will rarely matter: after 12 months a later sale should be within the **SSE** (TCGA 1992 Sch 7AC; chapter 18).

**TPLC's other costs** follow the line chapter 13 drew for Calder after *Centrica Overseas Holdings Ltd v HMRC* [2024] UKSC 25: costs of appraising whether to invest at all can be management expenses; once the board decides to acquire this target, the costs of acquiring it are **capital** (CTA 2009 s 1219(3)(a)). **HMRC's view** (CTM08190, CTM08260) puts the boundary, at the earliest, at the decision to acquire a particular target. Tom asks for the date of the board decision, because the invoices fall either side of it.

**The contract** (chapter 20). A buyer of shares inherits the company's past, so tax that belongs to Jess's years must stay Jess's. Tarnmoor's diligence looks hardest at:
1. **Branch attribution**: the profit RC has attributed to Dublin; Irish and UK figures must agree.
2. **Payroll**: employment taxes are where owner-managed companies most often slip.
3. **Returns and enquiries**: nothing open with HMRC.

The **tax deed** makes Jess liable for tax arising from pre-completion periods, with payments treated as a reduction in the price.

**VAT** (outside LCG): RC might join Tarnmoor's VAT group; a decision for another adviser.

> **Exam lens: the transaction**
> - **Grades:** stamp duty on stocks and shares **3** (awareness); companies with investment business (deal costs) **1**.
> - **Past appearances:** **N24 Q5** gave **3** of its 20 marks to stamp duty on a share purchase (0.5%, 30 days, buyer pays); **M23 Q6** (10 marks) was entirely acquisition costs.
> - **Traps:** deducting stamp duty; charging SDLT on a share purchase; giving stamp duty group relief on a purchase from an individual; missing stamp duty altogether when "tax consequences" are asked (chapter 32).

## Counting the family again.

The third floor is where most candidates lose marks. **Associated companies** (chapter 3; CTA 2010 s 18E): one controls the other, or both are under common control; overseas companies count; a company carrying on no trade or business is ignored.

**Is RH a passive holding company? (CTA 2010 s 18F.)** RH controls RC, so it would count. But s 18F treats certain holding companies as carrying on no business, so they are ignored under s 18E(3). The company must:

| Condition (s 18F) | RH (invented facts) |
|---|---|
| Carry on a business of making investments, and no trade | Yes: it only holds the RC shares |
| Have one or more 51% subsidiaries | Yes: RC (100%) |
| Be **passive** throughout the AP: no assets other than shares in its 51% subsidiaries | Yes, provided it holds **no cash** and makes **no loans** |
| …and no income other than dividends which it **redistributes** to its shareholders (the redistribution condition) | Yes, provided any dividend from RC is paid straight up to TPLC in the same period |

**In our invented case RH meets the conditions.** The integration plan therefore says: RH keeps no bank balance, lends nothing, and passes on any dividend in the same period. Break any of those and RH starts to count.

**Tarnmoor's companies that count** (after the Tarnwater demerger, the sale of TAL and the strike-off of dormant Tarnmoor Pumps): TPLC, TEL, CVE, TES, TFL, BSL, HEL, TVS, TCM, TIL = **10**. RC makes **11**.

**The two timings** (chapter 3):

| Purpose | When associates count | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Small profits rate and marginal relief | Associated **at any time** in the AP | CTA 2010 s 18E; CTM03580 |
| QIP thresholds (£1.5m large, £20m very large, £10m grace limit) | **As at the day before the AP begins** | HMRC's reading of SI 1998/3175 reg 3: CTM92530, COM95001 |

> **Worked example 31.3: RC's instalment status, AP1 to AP3 (invented; HMRC's counting dates)**
>
> | | AP1 | AP2 | AP3 |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Associates for marginal relief (divisor) | 10 (11): limits £4,545 / £22,727 | 10 (11) | 10 (11) |
> | QIP counting date | 31 March RY (owned via RH, passive; Jess owns no other company that counts) | 31 March RY+1 | 31 March RY+2 |
> | QIP divisor | **1** | **11** | **11** |
> | Large threshold (£) | 1,500,000 | **136,364** | 136,364 |
> | Very large threshold (£) | 20,000,000 | **1,818,182** | 1,818,182 |
> | Grace limit £10m ÷ divisor (£) | 10,000,000 | **909,091** | 909,091 |
> | Augmented profits (forecast) (£) | 600,000 | 400,000 (after the election) | 400,000 |
> | Large? | **No** | **Yes**, but **grace**: not large in the previous 12 months and profits ≤ £909,091 (reg 3(5)) | **Yes**: large in AP2, and the grace year counts as a year of being large |
> | Payment | 9 months and 1 day after the AP: **1 January RY+2** | 1 January RY+3 | **QIPs** on forecast CT of about £100,000: each 3/12 of it, **4 × £25,000**, in months 7, 10, 13, 16 (14 October RY+2; 14 January, 14 April, 14 July RY+3) |
>
> The marginal relief divisor changes nothing in practice: RC's profits were already far above the upper limit, so it already paid at 25%. (Checked in Python: 1,500,000/11 = 136,363.6; 20,000,000/11 = 1,818,181.8; 10,000,000/11 = 909,090.9.)

**Status of the AP3 conclusion.** CTM92530 states the grace test (profits ≤ £10m, divided, and not large in the previous 12 months, reg 3(5)); HMRC's manual and practitioners' worked examples treat the grace year as a year in which the company **was** large, so the grace is available only once. The regulation's current text was not opened for this book: cite reg 3(5) and HMRC's reading.

**The ripple through the group.** For their AP containing 1 April RY, Tarnmoor's 31 December companies have **11** associates for marginal relief (RC is associated for part of the AP). For QIPs their count changes only from the AP beginning the following **1 January**: divisor 11, large £136,364, very large £1,818,182. A £600,000 business has moved every instalment threshold in the group.

> **Exam lens: thresholds**
> - **Grades:** calculation of liability **1**; administration and payment of tax **1**.
> - **Past appearances:** **N24 Q5** gave **4** marks to filing and instalments for a newly acquired company.
> - **Traps:** one timing for both purposes; counting a passive holding company or a dormant company; forgetting that overseas companies count; giving a very large company a year of grace (there is none: CTM92520, CTM92800); forgetting that the £10m grace limit is divided too.
> - **Layout:** a short table per AP: counting date, divisor, thresholds, status, payment dates.

## What the shares bring with them.

**The target's history** (floor 2) is short here, and a nil answer with a reason still scores.

- **Trading losses**: none. The Dublin losses were relieved long ago against RC's own profits. CTA 2010 Part 14 (chapter 14's Brackenwell analysis) has nothing to restrict.
- **Capital losses**: none, so the pre-entry loss rules (TCGA 1992 Sch 7A; chapter 17) are silent.
- **Degrouping**: if RH and RC had moved an asset between them at no gain, no loss within the last six years, s 179 would be the worry. It is not. When the principal company of a group (RH) becomes a member of another group, the two groups are treated as the same (TCGA 1992 s 170(10)), so neither company leaves a group at all. Even if one did, RH and RC leave Jess's ownership **together**, RC remaining RH's subsidiary throughout (the "associated companies leaving together" exception, s 179(2); chapter 17).

**Group regimes switch on** (floor 4).

| Regime | What happens | Authority (chapter) |
|---|---|---|
| Group relief | RC and RH join TPLC's group relief group on completion (1 April RY); no relief before then. RC's AP (to 31 March) and the 31 December companies' APs overlap only partly: relief is limited by the **overlapping period** on each side. The same arrangements analysis as chapter 15 applies to the heads of terms with Jess: group relief with Tarnmoor companies starts only on completion | CTA 2010 ss 139–142, 154 (15) |
| Deductions allowance | RH and RC countersign TPLC's **standing nomination**; RC has no losses, so nothing turns on it now, but a company left out gets no allowance | CTA 2010 ss 269ZR ff (14) |
| AIA | One £1m for all members' chargeable periods **ending in the same financial year to 31 March** (parent test at the end of each period). RC's AP1 ends 31 March RY+1: it shares the allowance with the 31 December RY periods of Tarnmoor's companies, which ended three months earlier (nine months into RC's AP1). RC gets whatever the group allocates | CAA 2001 s 51C; Interpretation Act 1978 (8) |
| R&D | RC is now linked to a large enterprise: not an SME for any purpose. Any claim is under the merged scheme's **expenditure credit** (20%, taxable, set against the liability); ERIS is out of reach; HMRC's view is that a company taken over is large for the whole AP of the takeover (here the AP begins on completion anyway) | CTA 2009 Part 13; CIRD92000 (10) |

> **Exam lens: target history and group regimes**
> - **Grades:** use of losses **1**; groups and companies leaving groups **1**; group relief **1**; R&D **1**; capital allowances **1**.
> - **Traps:** restricting losses that do not exist; missing the overlapping-period limit on group relief for non-coterminous APs; treating RC as still an SME for R&D.

## The compliance settings change.

Floor 5 is the one candidates under-sell. **N24 Q5** (20 marks) set exactly this scenario: a US multinational bought a UK SME, and candidates had to explain its new obligations. The marking guide gave **6.5** marks to transfer pricing alone.

| Setting | RC and RH after completion | Authority (chapter) |
|---|---|---|
| **Transfer pricing** | In Jess's hands RC was a small enterprise: TP did not apply (TIOPA 2010 s 166). Size is measured across **linked enterprises**; linked to Tarnmoor, RC is large: **TP applies from completion**. Dealings with UK group companies are within the **UK-to-UK exemption** (s 164A; chargeable periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026), **but** s 164A does not apply to activities in relation to which s 18A exemption adjustments are made (below) | TIOPA 2010 ss 164A, 166, 172 (27) |
| CbC, master file, local file | RC enters TPLC's CbC report and master file; the Dublin PE's figures appear under Ireland; local file for material cross-border dealings | SI 2016/237; SI 2023/818 (27) |
| **SAO** | Turns on **UK incorporation** (both are English). A company qualifies for a financial year only if it was a group member at the **end of its preceding** financial year. RC's previous FY ended 31 March RY, the day before completion: **first SAO year = AP2** (1 April RY+1 – 31 March RY+2); Nadia Kerr's certificate due **9 months** after that FY (31 December RY+2). | Tax strategy | Covered by TPLC's published group strategy (published by 31 December each year) | FA 2016 Sch 19 (4) |
| UTT | RC and RH are members of a 51% group above the £200m UK turnover test: notify uncertain treatments over the **£5m** threshold in their returns (unlikely at this size, but the question must be asked) | FA 2022 Sch 17 (4) |

> **Exam lens: joining a large group**
> - **Grades:** SAO **1**; tax strategy **1**; CCO **1**; UTT **3**; TP **1**; CIR **1**; MTT/DTT **3**.
> - **N24 Q5 (20 marks)** marking guide (as recorded in chapter 20): filing and instalments **4**, CbC **3**, TP **6.5** (SME exemption lost; master and local file), CIR **3.5** (probably no restriction, but file to protect the position; reporting company; abbreviated return), stamp duty **3**. The examiners reported that most candidates found TP, but some wrote at length on it and ignored easier points, and some raised hybrids with nothing in the question to trigger them.
> - **Traps:** the SAO year of joining (the test looks at the end of the **preceding** FY); assuming UK-to-UK TP applies (s 164A) or, conversely, forgetting its carve-outs; applying the SAO to a company because it is UK resident rather than UK incorporated.

## The Dublin question.

Floor 6 is the one Jess left unfinished. Chapter 25 owns the rules; three options are on the table:

1. **Elect** under CTA 2009 s 18A;
2. **Incorporate** the branch into a new Irish subsidiary of RC (TCGA 1992 s 140);
3. **Transfer** the branch to Tarnmoor Ireland Ltd (TIL).

**The election (s 18A).** Once it takes effect, exemption adjustments remove the profits **and losses** of **every** foreign PE of the company. The branch's profit is measured as the **UK–Ireland treaty**'s business profits article would attribute it (Ireland is a full treaty territory: s 18R). Irish tax on exempt profits is **not creditable** (TIOPA 2010 s 18(3A)).

| Feature | Rule | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | All foreign PEs of the company, present and future, in every country | s 18A |
| Start | The **relevant day**: the start of the company's **next** AP after the election is made | s 18F(1)–(2); INTM281020 |
| Revocation | Possible only **before** the relevant day; afterwards irrevocable (ends only if the company ceases to be UK resident) | s 18F(6)–(8) |
| Guard rail | Profits that would pass the CFC gateway are not exempt unless a CFC entity exemption (as adapted) applies | ss 18G–18I |
| Transition | **Opening negative amount**: 6-year look-back | ss 18J–18N; INTM284020–284040 |

**The opening negative amount.** A company cannot take UK relief for branch losses and then elect just as the branch turns profitable. HMRC's guidance (INTM284020): look back over the **6 years** before the first AP the election covers; net PE losses **not matched by subsequent profits** in that period form the total opening negative amount, however the losses were relieved (sideways, carried back or group relief). Until it is matched by later PE profits, those profits are not exempt (INTM284030).

> **Worked example 31.4: RC's look-back (invented; £)**
>
> | Branch year | Result | Cumulative |
> |---|---|---|
> | Year 1 | (80,000) | (80,000) |
> | Year 2 | (30,000) | (110,000) |
> | Year 3 | 120,000 | 10,000 |
> | Year 4 | 200,000 | 210,000 |
> | Later years (about £200,000 a year) | profits | rising |
>
> The £110,000 of losses was matched within the next year, with £10,000 to spare, and every year since has been profitable. **Whatever the 6-year window ending 31 March RY+1 catches, the net result inside it is a profit: no opening negative amount.** (If the window no longer reaches Years 1–2, it contains only profits; if it does, the later profits match them.) No streaming election is needed: there is only one territory.

> **Worked example 31.5: RC with and without the election, a full year from AP2 (invented; £)**
>
> | | No election | Election |
> |---|---|---|
> | UK trading profit | 400,000 | 400,000 |
> | Dublin branch profit | 200,000 | exempt (s 18A) |
> | **TTP** | **600,000** | **400,000** |
> | CT at 25% | 150,000 | 100,000 |
> | DTR (Irish tax; s 42 limit £50,000) | (25,000) | none: s 18(3A) |
> | **UK CT** | **125,000** | **100,000** |
> | Irish tax at 12.5% | 25,000 | 25,000 |
> | **Total tax** | **150,000** | **125,000** |
> | **Saving before interactions** | | **25,000** |

**Test the £25,000 against the rest of the building.** A capstone answer checks each interaction:

1. **CIR** (chapter 28). Tax-EBITDA is built from the company's taxable profits, so once the branch is exempt its £200,000 leaves RC's taxable profits and the group's aggregate tax-EBITDA (this book's reading of TIOPA 2010 s 406: exempt PE profits never enter TTP). At 30% that is **£60,000** of interest capacity. If the group is restricted, or is still reactivating disallowed interest brought forward, that capacity is worth up to **£15,000** of tax (£60,000 × 25%); if the group has capacity to spare, nothing.
2. **Pillar Two** (chapter 30). Without the election, the UK CT on the branch profit is allocated to the PE under the GloBE rules' allocation of covered taxes (OECD Model Rules Art 4.3.2, as implemented; secondary sources), so the PE bears tax at the UK rate. With the election, it bears only Ireland's 12.5%, below 15%. On a **simplified** view (no substance-based income exclusion; the PE blended with TIL in the Irish computation at the same rate): (15% − 12.5%) × £200,000 = **up to £5,000** a year of top-up. Who collects it depends on **Irish law, which this book does not teach**; either way it is a group cost.
3. **TP** (chapter 27). s 164A does not apply to activities for which s 18A exemption adjustments are made: if TPLC charges RC for services used in Dublin, that charge must be at arm's length.
4. **The guard rail** (ss 18G–18I). A retail showroom is far from the anti-diversion rule; its profits are also within the low profits exemption (£500,000, non-trading income ≤ £50,000) that s 18I borrows from the CFC code.
5. **Attribution** (chapters 23–24). Without the election, the split between Skipton and Dublin only moved the credit around; with it, every pound attributed to Dublin is taxed at 12.5%, not 25%. HMRC will test the split: document it on arm's length principles.

| Election: net annual effect (£) | Best case | Worst case |
|---|---|---|
| UK CT saved | 25,000 | 25,000 |
| Lost interest capacity (60,000 × 25%) | — | (15,000) |
| Pillar Two top-up (simplified) | — | (5,000) |
| **Net saving** | **25,000** | **5,000** |

The election never costs money on these facts while the branch is profitable; its real price is the loss of UK relief for **any future** branch loss of RC, anywhere.

## Two other doors.

**Option 2: incorporate under TCGA 1992 s 140** (chapter 25).

| s 140 condition | RC → new Irish subsidiary |
|---|---|
| UK resident company carrying on a trade outside the UK through a PE | Yes |
| Transfers the trade with all its assets, or all except cash | Can be arranged |
| To a **non-UK resident** company | Only if the new company is genuinely managed and controlled in Ireland (chapter 22): decisions taken in Leeds would make it UK resident and the relief would fail |
| Consideration wholly or partly securities | Shares issued to RC |
| Transferor holds **≥ 25%** of the ordinary share capital | 100% |
| Result | Net gain postponed × securities ÷ total consideration; crystallises if RC disposes of the shares, or the subsidiary disposes of the assets within 6 years |

Postponement is all it gives. The branch's goodwill and customer relationships are intangible fixed assets, dealt with under CTA 2009 Part 8 (chapter 11), not the gains rules. Part 8 has its own **parallel postponement claim** for the transfer of a non-UK trade (CTA 2009 ss 827–830): the transfer must include all the trade's assets, or all except cash; the consideration must include shares or securities in the transferee; the transferor must then hold at least 25% of its ordinary share capital; and the transfer must meet a genuine commercial purpose test (s 831). The postponed credit returns if RC disposes of the shares, or if the subsidiary realises the assets within 6 years (s 829). The branch's plant leaves RC's pools with balancing adjustments (chapter 8).

> **Worked example 31.6: the new subsidiary through the CFC order of attack (labelled alternative, not story)**
>
> | Step | Analysis | Result |
> |---|---|---|
> | CFC? | Non-UK resident company controlled by UK persons (RC, TPLC) | **Yes** |
> | Exempt period | Not available: a newly formed company meets the initial condition only if it carried on a business before, or is an acquisition vehicle (HMRC's description, INTM224200) | — |
> | Excluded territories (Ch 11) | Ireland's place on the SI 2012/3024 list was **not confirmed** for this book (secondary sources report Ireland was removed from the pre-2013 list in 2002): do not rely on it | Not relied on |
> | Low profits (Ch 12) | Profits about £200,000 ≤ £500,000; non-trading income ≤ £50,000 | **Exempt: stop** |

> | (For completeness) Tax exemption (Ch 14) | Irish tax 12.5% < 75% × 25% = 18.75% | Fails |

>
> TIOPA 2010 Part 9A: ss 371LB (low profits), 371NB (tax exemption); chapter 26.

So the subsidiary reaches the **same 12.5%** as the elected branch by a longer road: a transfer, a new company, residence management, **TP on every bicycle Skipton sells to Dublin** (RC is no longer an SME), an annual CFC review, and Irish rules on dividends paid to a UK parent (not taught here). Its one advantage is that RC stays free of an irrevocable election.

**Option 3: transfer the branch to TIL.**

- **s 140 fails**: if RC took new TIL shares, it would hold a tiny fraction of a much larger company, far below **25%**; cash consideration fails the securities condition anyway.
- **No s 171 no gain, no loss**: TIL has been non-UK resident since 30 June GY4 (chapter 22); the Dublin assets would not be chargeable assets in its hands (s 171(1A)).
- **Intangibles**: a cross-border related-party transfer is at an **arm's length** value (FA 2026 Sch 6; chapter 11), so realisation credits arise now.
- Commercially, it moves a bicycle showroom into a valve distributor. **Reject.**

**EU-derived relief.** Ireland, unlike the invented Vallaria of chapter 25, is a member State, so **TCGA 1992 s 140C** (transfer of a PE business in a member State to a company resident in a member State) is at least in view. Chapter 25 explained that its post-Brexit wording was not confirmed (non-core, grade 2). Tom does not rely on it.

> **Exam lens: the branch**
> - **Grade:** core (1): "Company residence and chargeable profits of non-UK resident companies and concept of permanent establishment/branch"; CFCs **1**; ss 140A–140L non-core (2).
> - **Past appearances:** **M23 Q2** (15: stay non-exempt, elect or incorporate; recommend; "Some failed to give the recommendation and lost marks"); **N25 Q2** (15: PE, credit relief, the election, incorporation; few saw that the incorporation gain could be postponed); **M26 Q5** (20: PE, branch taxation and DTR, the election and its timing, subsidiary, CMC, CFC, dividend exemption; TP excluded).
> - **Traps:** treating the election as per branch; forgetting it starts from the **next** AP; ignoring the opening negative amount (or computing one where later profits have matched the losses); giving credit for Irish tax on exempt profits; forgetting the 25% condition in s 140; discussing the UK company's own residence in a PE question (M26 Q5).
> - **Layout:** options in turn; a short table of UK tax with and without the election, all in £; the look-back table; recommendation naming the option and the main reason.

## The recommendation.

Tom's paper to the boards of TPLC and RC, in our invented case, **recommends the election**, for three reasons:

1. It removes the UK top-up on the Dublin profit: **up to £25,000 a year**, and at least £5,000 after the CIR and Pillar Two interactions.
2. Its cost fits the facts: RC has no other foreign branches and no plans for any; if Tarnmoor ever wants a loss-making venture abroad it can put it in another company (the election belongs to a company, not a group).
3. Unlike incorporation it needs no transfer, no new company and no annual CFC review.

**Timing.** RC makes the election during **AP1**; the relevant day is **1 April RY+1**, the start of AP2. AP1's Dublin profit is still taxed in the UK with credit (UK CT £125,000 for AP1); the exemption runs from AP2 (UK CT £100,000 a year). While the heads of terms were being drafted, Tom noticed that an election made by Jess's RC **before** completion would have had a relevant day of 1 April RY and exempted AP1 as well. He chose not to ask for it: it would have been an irrevocable choice made before Tarnmoor's due diligence had finished, for a prize of £25,000. In our invented case the board minute records that reasoning; the election is made after board approval, and the minute and a copy of the election go on the permanent file.

**Coming next (proposed, not law).** Draft legislation for the Finance Bill 2026-27 (13 July 2026) would make the foreign branch exemption **compulsory for APs beginning on or after 1 January 2027** and replace the opening negative amount rules (ss 18J–18O would be omitted, per the draft) with restrictions on carrying forward PE losses (chapter 25). Under **FA 2026**, which the 2027 sittings test, it is still an **election**. RC has no branch losses to carry forward, so its answer would not change if the draft became law. Check the Autumn Budget (28 October 2026) and the Bill as introduced.

**Year end.** If Tarnmoor later moves RC to 31 December, as it did with Calder, re-run the election timing and the instalment tests, because both key off the dates of APs (HMRC: a change of accounting date after the election does not move the relevant day; chapter 25).

> - **Day 1:** present the stock transfer form and pay stamp duty within 30 days (£22,500); RH and RC countersign the deductions allowance nomination; add both to the CIR authorisation list, the master file and CbC data, and the Pillar Two data set; brief RH's directors on the s 18F conditions.
> - **During AP1:** board approval and election under s 18A (relevant day 1 April RY+1); attribution study for Dublin; CCO risk assessment for the Irish showroom; agree RC's AIA allocation for the year to 31 March RY+1.

> - **SAO:** RC and RH within Nadia Kerr's certificate from AP2 (due 31 December RY+2).
> - **Group accounts:** purchase price allocation and deferred tax finalised within the IFRS 3 measurement period.
> - **People:** diary the retention bonus payment (1 April RY+2) and the s 1288 consequences.
> - **Ethics:** using an election Parliament offers, for the purpose for which it was offered, is ordinary tax management, not avoidance; documenting the reasons protects the next finance director.

## The group accounts.

Floor 7 is not tax law, and it is where the examiner's tax accounting marks hide. Under IFRS 3, Tarnmoor's consolidated accounts record RC's identifiable assets at **acquisition-date fair value**. The valuers identify a **brand** and **customer relationships**, never recognised in RC's own accounts, worth **£0.8m** between them.

**A share purchase changes nothing in RC's tax position**: RC built the brand itself (no tax cost), and TPLC's purchase of shares gives no step-up. So in the group accounts the assets' carrying amount exceeds their tax base by £0.8m: a **taxable temporary difference** (IAS 12 para 19). Chapter 6 owns the rules.

> **Worked example 31.8: deferred tax on the fair value uplift (invented; £)**
>
> | | £ |
> |---|---|
> | Fair value of brand and customer relationships (group accounts) | 800,000 |
> | Tax base (internally generated; share purchase gives no step-up) | nil |
> | Taxable temporary difference | 800,000 |
> | **Deferred tax liability at 25%** | **200,000** |
> | Entry | Dr Goodwill 200,000; Cr Deferred tax liability 200,000 (IAS 12 para 66) |
> | Deferred tax on goodwill itself | None (para 15(a)): goodwill is a residual |
>
> As the group accounts amortise the customer relationships, the DTL unwinds through the deferred tax credit, so the non-deductible amortisation does not distort the group ETR. If the brand is given an indefinite life and not amortised, its part of the DTL remains. RC's own (FRS 101) accounts and tax return are unaffected.

Two further points. IFRS 3 allows a **measurement period** (up to one year from acquisition) to finalise the fair values, so the DTL and goodwill can move during it. And any Pillar Two top-up the Dublin PE might bear creates **no deferred tax**: the IAS 12 exception (para 4A) applies (chapter 6).

**Planning: document the purchase price allocation.** The split between net assets, identified intangibles and goodwill drives the deferred tax, impairment testing and, later, the gain on any sale. Tom files the valuers' report with the acquisition papers, alongside the board minute on the election.

> **Exam lens: deferred tax on an acquisition**
> - **Grade:** deferred tax **1**.
> - **What scores:** the temporary difference and why it arises (no tax step-up on a share deal); the rate and the figure; where the debit goes (goodwill); no DTL on goodwill.
> - **Traps:** recognising the DTL in the subsidiary's own accounts; charging it to profit or loss at acquisition; using 19% because the subsidiary is small (the rate is the one expected to apply: 25% for RC).

## Keeping Jess.

Floor 8 is people. Tarnmoor wants Jess to stay, so in our invented case she remains **managing director of RC for two years**, on a salary of **£90,000** a year, with a **retention bonus of £60,000** payable **24 months after completion** if she is still employed. **RC pays both**, because it is RC's trade she serves. Chapter 20 explained why that matters: if TPLC paid, it would be paying for services to another company and might get no deduction.

The salary is an ordinary trading deduction. The bonus is a timing puzzle under **CTA 2009 s 1288** (chapter 7): remuneration charged in the accounts but **not paid within 9 months** after the end of the period of account is deductible only in the period in which it is paid.

> **Worked example 31.9: when the retention bonus is deductible (invented; £)**
>
> Assumption: RC accrues the bonus evenly over the service period, £30,000 in each of AP1 and AP2. Payment date: **1 April RY+2**, the first day of AP3.
>
> | | AP1 (to 31 Mar RY+1) | AP2 (to 31 Mar RY+2) | AP3 (to 31 Mar RY+3) |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Accounts charge | 30,000 | 30,000 | — |
> | Paid within 9 months of the period end? | No: paid 12 months after (deadline 31 Dec RY+1) | Yes: paid 1 day after (deadline 31 Dec RY+2) | — |
> | **Tax deduction** | **nil** | **30,000** | **30,000** (AP1's accrual, when paid) |
> | Tax value at 25% | — | 7,500 | 7,500 |
>
> Total deduction £60,000 either way; s 1288 only moves £30,000 of it into AP3. In a computation prepared within the 9 months, assume unpaid and amend later if it is paid in time (s 1289(3)). The AP1 add-back is a short-term timing difference: a deferred tax asset of £7,500 in RC's AP1 accounts.

**An adviser's check.** The bonus must genuinely be **pay for continued service**: conditional on employment, at a commercial level. If the facts showed it was really part of the **price for Jess's shares**, RC would be paying a capital sum on its shareholder's behalf and would get no deduction (CTA 2009 s 54: wholly and exclusively). The service condition and the market level of the pay are the evidence. The same applies to any compensation if Jess left early: **HMRC's view** (BIM38380–BIM38390; chapter 20) is that a payment connected with a change of ownership is unlikely to be wholly and exclusively for the trade.

> **Exam lens: people on an acquisition**
> - **Grade:** CT relief for expenses relating to employment including remuneration **1**; general calculation rules (Part 20 CTA 2009) **1**.
> - **Past appearances:** **M23 Q6** (10 marks: due diligence, legal fees, a loan arrangement fee, compensation for loss of office, retention bonuses; candidates generally did well); s 1288 in **N25** (bonuses paid after the period end).
> - **Traps:** deducting an accrued bonus in the period of accrual when it is paid more than 9 months later; letting the parent pay for a subsidiary's employee; ignoring the purpose question for change-of-control payments.

## How the examiner would mark it.

Set this deal as a **20-mark** question: about **42 minutes** at roughly 2.1 minutes a mark. Marking guides give **0.5–1 mark per point**, so a 20-mark answer needs roughly **20–40** distinct, findable points (chapter 32).

**Order of work.**
1. **Bank the quick marks** (one line each, with a reason): stamp duty £22,500, buyer, 30 days; the associated company count and the two timings; no QIPs in AP1 (divisor 1), grace in AP2 (divisor 11; £909,091), QIPs from AP3; SAO from AP2; TP switched on (SME exemption lost); CIR and Pillar Two membership.
2. **Score the nils**: no losses to restrict; no degrouping charge; no SDLT. A correct nil with its reason is a point like any other.
3. **The branch**, where the recommendation marks live: the three options in turn; the tax table with and without the election (all in £); the opening negative amount test and its result; the s 140 conditions and why the TIL route fails; the CFC position of a subsidiary; then **recommend**, naming the option and the main reason.
4. **Accounts and people** if the requirement reaches them: the DTL and its debit; the s 1288 timing.

**Two disciplines.** Do not restate Jess's history: the marker knows it. And if the question names corporation tax only, Jess's CGT and the stamp duty are outside it, however much you know about them.

> **Exam lens: the multi-issue question**
> - **Grade:** application across the grid; the LCG narrative's "interaction of taxes" and "corporate transformations".
> - **Style:** technical prose and short computations; no letter or report format; "Explain", "Calculate, with explanations", "Recommend".
> - **Favourite combinations it mirrors:** sale or purchase of a subsidiary + SSE + change of ownership + enquiries (M24 Q3); PE + branch exemption + incorporation + CFC (M23 Q2, N25 Q2, M26 Q5); a newly acquired company's obligations (N24 Q5).
> - **Traps:** padding (M26 Q1 examiners: adequate but padded); hybrids or DPT with no trigger (N24 Q5; chapter 30: DPT is repealed for APs beginning on or after 1 January 2026); missing the recommendation (M23 Q2); mixing £ and £000 in one table.

## What to take away.

- **A small acquisition is not a small tax job**: the size of the group the target joins sets the compliance load.
- **Walk every floor in order**: transaction, history, thresholds, group regimes, governance and compliance, special features, accounts, people, recommendation.
- **Stamp duty** £4.5m × 0.5% = **£22,500**, paid by TPLC within 30 days; part of the cost of the shares; no SDLT; no group relief from an individual.
- **RH** is a **passive holding company** (CTA 2010 s 18F): no assets but subsidiary shares, no income but redistributed dividends. Keep it passive.
- **RC's associates**: 10 (divisor 11) for marginal relief at once; for QIPs counted the day before each AP: **divisor 1 in AP1** (not large), **11 in AP2** (large at £136,364, but **grace**: profits ≤ £909,091 and not large before), **QIPs from AP3** (about 4 × £25,000).
- **Nothing to restrict**: no losses, no capital losses, no degrouping charge.
- **Switched on at completion**: group relief (overlapping periods), the deductions allowance nomination, a share of the single AIA, RDEC only; **TP** (SME exemption lost; s 164A for UK-to-UK dealings); CbC and master file; UTT; CCO procedures reaching Dublin; CIR worldwide group and reporting company authorisation; Pillar Two. **SAO from AP2.**
- **Dublin**: elect under **s 18A** during AP1, effective from **1 April RY+1**; **no opening negative amount** (losses £110,000 matched by Years 3–4 profits £320,000); UK CT **£125,000 → £100,000**, saving **up to £25,000** a year, perhaps as little as **£5,000** after lost interest capacity (up to £15,000) and simplified Pillar Two top-up (up to £5,000). Irrevocable after the relevant day; covers all future PEs of RC. **s 140** incorporation (with the parallel intangibles claim, CTA 2009 s 827) only postpones gains and creates a CFC (low profits exemption today); **transfer to TIL** fails the 25% test and s 171. The compulsory exemption in the draft Finance Bill 2026-27 is **proposed, not law**.
- **Group accounts**: fair value uplift **£0.8m** → **DTL £200,000**, debited to goodwill; none on goodwill.
- **Jess**: MD for two years at £90,000; retention bonus **£60,000** paid by RC on 1 April RY+2: **£30,000 deductible in AP2, £30,000 in AP3** (s 1288).

**So why does a small deal touch every rule in the book?** Because a company joining a group inherits the group's thresholds, regimes and obligations, some at once and some one period later. The answer to Jess's last open question is **yes, elect**: under the law the 2027 sittings test it is still a choice, and on these facts it is a good one.

One chapter remains. Chapter 32 turns from the law to the craft: how to put all of this in front of a marker, in three and a half hours, in a form the marker can reward.
